IB Business Management

Examination Tips & Advice

How to read command terms, manage your time across Paper 1, Paper 2 and Paper 3, and structure answers that apply properly to the case study or stimulus in front of you.

1. Know your assessment structure

IB Business Management is assessed across three exam papers plus an Internal Assessment. Knowing exactly what each paper is based on and how it is marked helps you prepare — and pace — correctly.

ComponentBased onTimeStructureMarksWeighting
Paper 1 (SL & HL) A pre-released case study statement, extended with unseen material in the exam 1h 30 Section A: compulsory structured questions. Section B: one extended-response question, choice of two. 30 SL 35% / HL 25%
Paper 2 (SL) Unseen stimulus material about a real business 1h 30 Section A: compulsory structured questions. Section B: one question, choice of two. 40 35%
Paper 2 (HL) Unseen stimulus material about a real business 1h 45 Section A: compulsory structured questions. Section B: one question, choice of two. 50 30%
Paper 3 (HL only) Unseen stimulus on social entrepreneurship 1h 15 Three compulsory questions, including one extended response 25 25%
Internal Assessment Independent research project on a real organisation (up to 1800 words) ~20 hours — — SL 30% / HL 20%

A basic four-function calculator is permitted (no graphic display calculator needed).

2. Know your command terms

IB command terms are organised into four levels of increasing sophistication — the higher the level, the more developed your answer needs to be, and the more marks are usually available.

LevelCommand termsWhat it wants
1 — Knowledge Define, State, List, Identify, Outline A short, accurate fact, definition or summary — no explanation needed.
2 — Application Explain, Describe, Apply, Calculate, Distinguish Show how a concept, formula or tool works, and apply it directly to the business in the case study or stimulus.
3 — Analysis Analyse, Examine A chain of reasoning — tool or theory → effect → impact — built specifically around that business, not generic theory.
4 — Evaluation Evaluate, Discuss, Justify, Recommend, To what extent Weigh up more than one option or viewpoint using evidence from the case study/stimulus, then reach a supported judgement.

3. Manage your time by the mark

Work on roughly one minute per mark, and always read the whole case study or stimulus before answering — later, higher-mark questions usually depend on details given earlier.

  • On Paper 1, use your five hours of pre-release research time to understand the context thoroughly — you cannot take notes into the exam, but the unseen material builds directly on that context.
  • On Paper 2, work through the stimulus material in the order it's presented — questions are usually sequenced to build on what came before.
  • Save extra time for Section B and the final evaluative question on each paper — they carry the most marks per minute spent.
  • Always show full working for calculations — method marks are available even with a slip in arithmetic.

4. Structuring your answers

Application is everything

The single biggest difference between a good and an average Business Management answer is application. Never write a generic textbook answer — always name the organisation, quote a figure or detail from the case study/stimulus, and explain how your point applies specifically to that organisation.

Evaluative (Evaluate / Discuss / Justify / Recommend / To what extent) questions

  • Viewpoint or option one — explain a benefit or argument, applied to the organisation in the case study/stimulus.
  • Viewpoint or option two — explain a drawback or counter-argument, equally applied to the organisation.
  • Judgement — recommend a course of action or conclusion, justified by what matters most for this organisation (its size, sector, objectives, finances or stakeholders).

6. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Writing a generic, textbook-style answer with no reference to the specific organisation in the case study or stimulus.
  • Describing a tool (e.g. listing a SWOT's four headings) instead of using it to answer the question asked.
  • Giving one-sided evaluations, or an evaluation with no final, justified recommendation.
  • Skipping the working on calculation questions.
  • Running out of time on the final, highest-mark question because too long was spent on earlier, lower-mark parts.

7. Final checklist before you submit

  • I've answered the command term, not just the topic.
  • Every point I've made is applied to the named organisation in the case study or stimulus.
  • My calculations show full working, not just the final figure.
  • My evaluative answers cover more than one side and end with a clear, justified recommendation.
  • I've checked I haven't missed a question or part-question.
