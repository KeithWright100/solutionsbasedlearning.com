2. Business objectives and stakeholders

IB Business Management Revision Guide · Unit 1: Introduction to business management · Topics · Topic 2 of 3

Learning Intention

To understand how vision and mission statements, objectives and corporate social responsibility guide a business, and how stakeholders influence and conflict over decisions.

Syllabus Statement

1.3 Business objectives: vision and mission statements; common objectives (growth, profit, protecting shareholder value, ethical objectives); strategic and tactical objectives; corporate social responsibility (CSR). 1.4 Stakeholders: internal and external stakeholders; conflict between stakeholders.

Success criterion 1

Vision and mission statements

A vision statement sets out where the organisation wants to be in the long term (its aspirations). A mission statement explains its purpose: what it does, for whom, and its core values, now. Both give direction and a shared sense of purpose, and help with motivation, decision-making and public image.

Limitations: they can be vague, generic marketing slogans, and are meaningless unless they are reflected in actual decisions.

Real-world example: IKEA’s vision is “to create a better everyday life for the many people”: it guides decisions on low prices, flat-pack design and sustainability.

Success criterion 2

Objectives: strategic and tactical

Aims are general long-term goals; objectives are specific, measurable targets that help achieve them, ideally SMART (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound).

  • Growth: increasing sales, market share or size (economies of scale, security).
  • Profit: rewards owners and funds investment; the main objective of most private firms.
  • Protecting shareholder value: maintaining dividends and share price to keep investors’ confidence.
  • Ethical objectives: acting in a morally correct way, e.g. fair wages, environmental protection, honest marketing.

Strategic objectives are long-term goals for the whole organisation, set by senior management (e.g. become market leader in Asia within five years). Tactical objectives are short- to medium-term goals that help achieve the strategy, set by middle managers (e.g. increase sales in Vietnam by 10% next year). Operational objectives are day-to-day targets.

Success criterion 3

Corporate social responsibility (CSR)

CSR is the extent to which a business considers the interests of society and the environment, going beyond what the law requires. Firms may adopt CSR and ethical objectives because of pressure from consumers and staff, to build their brand and gain a competitive advantage, to attract investors (ESG investing), or because of the owners’ values.

Benefits of CSRDrawbacks of CSR
Improved corporate image and customer loyaltyHigher costs (e.g. fair trade inputs, cleaner technology)
Attracts and retains motivated staffMay reduce short-term profit and shareholder returns
Reduces risk of bad publicity and legal actionRisk of being accused of “greenwashing” if claims are exaggerated
Access to ethical investorsHard to measure; may conflict with other objectives

Exam tip: CSR links directly to the key concepts of ethics and sustainability. Changing attitudes mean that what counts as socially responsible changes over time.

Success criterion 4

Internal and external stakeholders

A stakeholder is any individual or group with an interest in, or affected by, the activities of a business.

Success criterion 5

Stakeholder conflict

Stakeholders often have conflicting interests: shareholders want higher profit, but employees want higher pay; customers want low prices, but suppliers want high prices; expansion may please shareholders but anger the local community. Managers must decide whose interests to prioritise, often using stakeholder mapping.

Ways to resolve conflict: communication and consultation, compromise and negotiation (e.g. with trade unions), sharing profits (profit-related pay, share ownership), and setting objectives that balance interests. Some interests overlap: a successful, growing business can benefit almost every stakeholder.

Mini case: A mining company plans to open a new mine. Shareholders expect higher profits and the government welcomes jobs and tax revenue, but local residents and an environmental pressure group fear pollution and loss of farmland.

Key terms

Vision statement
A statement of what an organisation aspires to be in the long term.
Mission statement
A statement of an organisation’s purpose and values.
Strategic objective
A long-term goal for the whole organisation.
Tactical objective
A short- to medium-term goal that helps achieve a strategic objective.
Corporate social responsibility
Considering the interests of society and the environment beyond legal requirements.
Stakeholder
A person or group with an interest in, or affected by, a business.
Internal stakeholder
A stakeholder who is part of the organisation, such as employees, managers and shareholders.
External stakeholder
A stakeholder outside the organisation, such as customers, suppliers and the government.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a business should adopt ethical objectives. (10 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Marked with five criteria, each worth 2 marks (10 in total):
  • A: knowledge and understanding of relevant tools, techniques and theories.
  • B: application to the case study or stimulus (use names, figures and facts from it).
  • C: reasoned arguments, balanced, with a justified conclusion.
  • D: structure (introduction, logical paragraphs, conclusion) and relevant terminology.
  • E: individuals and societies: consider the perspectives of different stakeholders.
  • Benefits: brand image, loyalty, staff motivation, investor appeal, risk reduction.
  • Costs: higher costs, lower short-term profit, greenwashing risk, stakeholder conflict.
  • Judgement linked to the market (e.g. consumers who value sustainability) and the time frame.

Model answer: Ethical objectives can strengthen a firm’s brand and loyalty, help it recruit and keep staff, and reduce the risk of scandals and fines. However, they usually raise costs and may reduce profits and dividends in the short term, causing conflict with shareholders. In markets where consumers value ethics, such as fashion or food, the long-term benefits are likely to outweigh the costs; in price-sensitive markets the case is weaker unless ethical behaviour also reduces costs.
