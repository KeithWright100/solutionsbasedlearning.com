Success criterion 1

Profit and cash flow

Profit is total revenue minus total costs over a period; revenue is recorded when a sale is made, even if the customer pays later. Cash flow is the movement of money into (inflows) and out of (outflows) the business. A profitable business can run out of cash (for example if customers pay late or it buys too much stock), and a loss-making business can have cash (for example from a loan). Most businesses fail because they run out of cash, not because they are unprofitable.

Net cash flow = inflows − outflows. Closing balance = opening balance + net cash flow, and becomes next month’s opening balance. Negative figures are shown in brackets. Forecasts help plan finance (such as arranging an overdraft before February), support loan applications and set targets, but they are only as good as the estimates behind them.

Exam tip: Paper 2 often asks you to construct or complete a forecast. Check that every closing balance carries forward correctly.

Success criterion 4

Investment, profit and cash flow; dealing with cash flow problems

Investment in new assets uses cash now, but should increase profit and cash inflows later. Rapid growth (overtrading) can cause cash shortages because the business buys stock and pays staff before customers pay.

StrategyExamplesLimitations
Reduce cash outflowsDelay payments to suppliers (more trade credit); cut costs; lease rather than buy; reduce stock (JIT)May harm supplier relations or quality
Improve cash inflowsChase debtors; offer discounts for prompt payment; debt factoring; cash-only sales; sell unused assetsLower revenue per sale; may lose customers

Key terms

Cash flow
The movement of money into and out of a business.
Cash inflow
Money received by a business.
Cash outflow
Money paid out by a business.
Net cash flow
Cash inflows minus cash outflows in a period.
Cash flow forecast
A prediction of future cash inflows and outflows.
Working capital cycle
The time between paying for inputs and receiving cash from sales.
Overtrading
Expanding too quickly without enough working capital.
Exam-style question

Explain two ways a business could improve its cash flow position. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 4 marks: two developed points (or one fully developed point for a 2-mark question), each explained and applied to the case.
  • Use the command term: "explain" needs cause and effect, not a list.
  • e.g. chase debtors or shorten credit terms; negotiate longer credit with suppliers; reduce stock; overdraft.

Model answer: The business could offer a small discount for prompt payment so that debtors pay sooner, increasing cash inflows. It could also negotiate longer credit periods with suppliers, delaying cash outflows.
