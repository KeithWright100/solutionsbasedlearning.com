5. Investment appraisal

IB Business Management Revision Guide · Unit 3: Finance and accounts · Topics · Topic 5 of 6

Learning Intention

To use payback period and average rate of return (HL: net present value) to decide whether an investment is worthwhile.

Syllabus Statement

3.8 Investment appraisal: payback period; average rate of return (ARR). HL: net present value (NPV) using discount factors.

Success criterion 1

Payback period

Example

A machine costs $100 000. Expected net cash flows: Year 1 $30 000, Year 2 $40 000, Year 3 $40 000, Year 4 $30 000.

Cumulative: Y1 $30 000, Y2 $70 000, Y3 $110 000. Payback falls in Year 3: $30 000 still needed at the start of Year 3; monthly cash flow in Year 3 = $40 000 ÷ 12 = $3 333; 30 000 ÷ 3 333 = 9 months. Payback = 2 years 9 months.

Payback is simple and focuses on liquidity and risk (a shorter payback is safer), but it ignores cash flows after payback and the time value of money.

Success criterion 2

Average rate of return (ARR)

Example

ARR = (total returns − capital cost) ÷ years of use ÷ capital cost × 100.

Total net cash flows = 30 + 40 + 40 + 30 = $140 000. Total profit = 140 000 − 100 000 = $40 000. Annual profit = 40 000 ÷ 4 = $10 000. ARR = 10 000 ÷ 100 000 × 100 = 10%.

ARR considers all cash flows and gives a percentage that can be compared with the interest rate or other projects, but it ignores the timing of cash flows and the time value of money.

Success criterion 3

HLNet present value (NPV)

Money received in the future is worth less than money today (it could have earned interest, and inflation reduces its value). Discount factors convert future cash flows into present values.

NPV = sum of present values − initial cost. A positive NPV means the investment earns more than the discount rate, so it is financially worthwhile. NPV accounts for the time value of money but depends on choosing the right discount rate and is harder to explain to non-specialists.

Success criterion 4

Evaluating investment decisions

All methods depend on forecast cash flows, which are estimates and may be wrong, especially over long periods. Qualitative factors matter too: the business’s objectives and strategy, the impact on staff and morale, CSR and environmental effects, the state of the economy, risk, and how competitors may react.

Key terms

Investment appraisal
Methods used to judge whether an investment is financially worthwhile.
Payback period
The time taken for net cash inflows to repay the initial cost.
Average rate of return
The average annual profit from an investment as a percentage of its cost.
Net present value
HL: the sum of discounted future cash flows minus the initial cost.
Discount factor
HL: a number used to convert a future cash flow into its present value.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether investment appraisal should be the main factor in a decision to buy new automated machinery. (10 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Marked with five criteria, each worth 2 marks (10 in total):
  • A: knowledge and understanding of relevant tools, techniques and theories.
  • B: application to the case study or stimulus (use names, figures and facts from it).
  • C: reasoned arguments, balanced, with a justified conclusion.
  • D: structure (introduction, logical paragraphs, conclusion) and relevant terminology.
  • E: individuals and societies: consider the perspectives of different stakeholders.
  • Payback, ARR (and NPV for HL) results and what they show.
  • Limitations: forecasts, time value, risk.
  • Qualitative factors: staff, redundancies, quality, CSR, strategy; conclusion.

Model answer: Investment appraisal gives a clear financial view: a short payback and an ARR above the cost of borrowing suggest the machinery is worthwhile. However, the figures rely on uncertain forecasts of savings and output. Automation may also lead to redundancies, affecting morale and the firm’s reputation, while improving quality and consistency. The financial results should be the starting point, but managers must weigh them against these qualitative factors and the firm’s long-term strategy.
