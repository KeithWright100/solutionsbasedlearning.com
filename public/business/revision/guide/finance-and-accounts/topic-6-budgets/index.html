6. HL: Budgets and variance analysis

Success criterion 1

HLWhy budget?

A budget is a financial plan for the future, usually for a year, setting out expected revenues and costs for the organisation or a department. Budgets help with planning, coordination between departments, control of spending, motivation (targets), and accountability (managers responsible for budgets).

Limitations: they are based on forecasts; rigid budgets may prevent sensible spending; managers may “pad” budgets or spend up to the limit; setting them takes time; imposed budgets can demotivate.

Success criterion 2

HLCost and profit centres

A cost centre is a part of the business for which costs are identified and recorded (e.g. the HR department, IT support). A profit centre is a part of the business for which both costs and revenues, and so profit, can be identified (e.g. each branch of a restaurant chain, each product line).

They help managers see which parts of the business perform well, improve accountability and motivation, and support decisions on where to invest or cut. However, allocating indirect costs between centres can be arbitrary and may cause rivalry between departments.

Variance = actual − budgeted figure. A favourable (F) variance increases profit (revenue above budget or costs below budget). An adverse (A) variance reduces profit (revenue below budget or costs above budget).

Exam tip: An adverse cost variance is not always bad: higher raw material costs may simply reflect higher sales. Always look for the reason behind a variance.

Success criterion 4

HLUsing variances

Variances highlight where performance differs from the plan, so managers can investigate causes (price changes, efficiency, demand) and take action. They support management by exception: focusing attention on significant variances. However, variances only show what happened, not why, and depend on realistic budgets in the first place.

Key terms

Budget
HL: a financial plan for future revenue and spending.
Cost centre
HL: a part of a business for which costs are recorded.
Profit centre
HL: a part of a business for which costs and revenues are recorded.
Variance
HL: the difference between a budgeted and an actual figure.
Favourable variance
HL: a variance that increases profit.
Adverse variance
HL: a variance that reduces profit.
Exam-style question

Discuss the usefulness of budgets for a business with several branches. (10 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Marked with five criteria, each worth 2 marks (10 in total):
  • A: knowledge and understanding of relevant tools, techniques and theories.
  • B: application to the case study or stimulus (use names, figures and facts from it).
  • C: reasoned arguments, balanced, with a justified conclusion.
  • D: structure (introduction, logical paragraphs, conclusion) and relevant terminology.
  • E: individuals and societies: consider the perspectives of different stakeholders.
  • Planning, coordination, control, motivation; branches as profit centres.
  • Variance analysis to compare branches.
  • Limitations: forecasts, inflexibility, allocation of overheads; conclusion.

Model answer: Treating each branch as a profit centre with its own budget lets head office compare performance, hold branch managers accountable and investigate adverse variances. Budgets also help coordinate stock and staffing. However, budgets are based on forecasts that may be wrong, local conditions differ, and allocating shared costs can seem unfair. Budgets are most useful if branch managers are involved in setting them and if they can be adjusted when conditions change.
