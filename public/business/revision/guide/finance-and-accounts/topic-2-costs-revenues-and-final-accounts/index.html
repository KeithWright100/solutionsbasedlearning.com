Success criterion 1

Costs and revenues

CostMeaningExamples
Fixed costsDo not change with output in the short runRent, salaries, insurance
Variable costsChange directly with outputRaw materials, packaging, piece-rate wages
Semi-variable costsHave a fixed and a variable elementPhone bill with line rental plus calls; salary plus commission
Direct costsCan be linked to a specific productMaterials for that product
Indirect costs (overheads)Cannot be linked to one product; sharedHead office rent, marketing, administration
Costs and revenueTotal cost = fixed costs + (variable cost per unit × output). Total revenue = price × quantity sold. Profit = total revenue − total cost.

Revenue streams are the different sources of income: product sales, subscriptions, advertising, licensing, commission, rental income, sponsorship and donations (for non-profits). Multiple revenue streams spread risk.

Net assets = total assets − total liabilities = total equity. Working capital = current assets − current liabilities (here $120 000).

Intangible assets are non-physical assets with value: goodwill (the value of reputation and customer base, recorded when a business is bought for more than its net assets), patents, copyrights, trademarks and brand names. They are hard to value accurately.

Success criterion 5

HLDepreciation

HLTwo methods

Straight-line: (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life. A $50 000 machine with a $10 000 residual value and 5-year life depreciates by $8 000 a year.

Units of production: depreciation per unit = (cost − residual value) ÷ total expected units. If the machine is expected to make 400 000 units, depreciation = $0.10 per unit; producing 90 000 units in a year gives $9 000.

Key terms

Fixed costs
Costs that do not change with output in the short run.
Variable costs
Costs that change directly with output.
Indirect costs
Overheads that cannot be linked to a single product.
Revenue stream
A source of income for a business.
Gross profit
Sales revenue minus cost of sales.
Profit for the period
Profit after interest and tax.
Statement of financial position
A summary of assets, liabilities and equity at a point in time.
Working capital
Current assets minus current liabilities.
Intangible asset
A non-physical asset of value, such as goodwill or a patent.
Depreciation
HL: the fall in the value of a non-current asset over time.
Exam-style question

Explain two reasons why a bank would want to see a company’s final accounts before lending to it. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 4 marks: two developed points (or one fully developed point for a 2-mark question), each explained and applied to the case.
  • Use the command term: "explain" needs cause and effect, not a list.
  • e.g. profitability (can it pay interest?), liquidity and working capital, existing debt (gearing), assets available as collateral.

Model answer: The statement of profit or loss shows whether the company makes enough profit to cover interest payments. The statement of financial position shows existing debts and assets, so the bank can judge whether the firm can repay and what collateral is available.
