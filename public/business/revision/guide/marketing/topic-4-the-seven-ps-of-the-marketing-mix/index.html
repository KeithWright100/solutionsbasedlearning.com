4. The seven Ps of the marketing mix

IB Business Management Revision Guide · Unit 4: Marketing · Topics · Topic 4 of 5

Learning Intention

To understand and evaluate each element of the extended marketing mix: product, price, promotion, place, people, processes and physical evidence.

Syllabus Statement

4.5 The seven Ps of the marketing mix: product (the product life cycle; extension strategies; the relationship between the PLC and the marketing mix; the BCG matrix; branding and packaging); price (cost-plus, penetration, skimming, psychological, loss leader, price discrimination, dynamic and competitive pricing); promotion (above and below the line, promotional mix, social media marketing); place (distribution channels); people; processes; physical evidence.

Success criterion 2

Branding and packaging

A brand is a name, symbol, design or reputation that distinguishes a product. Strong brands create brand awareness, brand loyalty and brand value, allowing premium prices and easier product launches. Types include family (corporate) branding, product branding, own-label (retailer) branding and co-branding.

Packaging protects the product, gives information (ingredients, instructions, legal requirements), supports the brand image and attracts customers. Sustainable packaging is increasingly important to customers and regulators.

Success criterion 4

Promotion and place

Above-the-line promotion uses mass media (TV, radio, newspapers, cinema, online display) to reach a wide audience. Below-the-line promotion is targeted and controlled by the business: sales promotions, direct marketing, personal selling, public relations, sponsorship, loyalty schemes and trade fairs. The promotional mix combines methods within a budget. Social media marketing is cheap, interactive and targeted (influencers, user-generated content), but negative comments spread quickly and results are hard to control.

Place is how products reach customers. Distribution channels: zero-level (direct to the customer, e.g. online), one-level (via a retailer), two-level (wholesaler then retailer), and via agents or brokers for international trade. Longer channels give wider reach but reduce control and profit per unit. E-commerce has made direct channels much more common.

Success criterion 5

People, processes and physical evidence

  • People: staff who deliver the service; training, appearance and attitude shape the customer’s experience.
  • Processes: the systems used to deliver the service (ordering, payment, delivery, complaints) — efficient processes improve satisfaction.
  • Physical evidence: the tangible aspects customers see: premises, decor, uniforms, websites, packaging and receipts, which reassure customers about an intangible service.

Exam tip: For services (hotels, banks, schools), use all 7 Ps; for physical goods the first 4 Ps are usually enough. Always link the mix to the target market and the stage of the life cycle.

Key terms

Marketing mix
The combination of the 7 Ps used to market a product.
Product life cycle
The stages a product passes through from launch to decline.
Extension strategy
An action to delay the decline of a product.
BCG matrix
A tool that classifies products by market share and market growth.
Penetration pricing
Setting a low price to gain market share.
Price skimming
Setting a high initial price for a new product.
Above-the-line promotion
Promotion through mass media.
Below-the-line promotion
Targeted promotion controlled by the business.
Distribution channel
The route a product takes from producer to customer.
Physical evidence
Tangible aspects that support a service.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a soft drinks company should use social media marketing rather than traditional above-the-line promotion for a new product. (10 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Marked with five criteria, each worth 2 marks (10 in total):
  • A: knowledge and understanding of relevant tools, techniques and theories.
  • B: application to the case study or stimulus (use names, figures and facts from it).
  • C: reasoned arguments, balanced, with a justified conclusion.
  • D: structure (introduction, logical paragraphs, conclusion) and relevant terminology.
  • E: individuals and societies: consider the perspectives of different stakeholders.
  • Define both; target market (young people).
  • Social media: cheap, targeted, interactive v hard to control, short attention, negative feedback.
  • Above the line: wide reach, credibility v cost; conclusion: integrated mix.

Model answer: Social media marketing lets the company target young drinkers cheaply, use influencers and track engagement, and content can go viral. However, results are unpredictable and negative comments spread fast. TV advertising reaches a much wider audience and builds credibility for a mass-market drink, but it is expensive and hard to target. For a new drink aimed at young people, social media is likely to be the main channel, supported by some above-the-line activity.
