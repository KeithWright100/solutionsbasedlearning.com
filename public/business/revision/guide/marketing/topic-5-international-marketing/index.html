Success criterion 3

HLAdapting the marketing mix

Firms choose between a global (standardised) marketing mix, which gives economies of scale and a consistent brand, and a localised (adapted) mix that suits local tastes, culture, laws and income. Many use glocalisation: a global brand with local adaptations.

Real-world example: McDonald’s sells the McAloo Tikki burger in India, where many customers do not eat beef, while keeping its global brand, layout and service processes.

Key terms

International marketing
HL: marketing products in more than one country.
Exporting
HL: selling products made in one country to customers in another.
Licensing
HL: allowing a foreign firm to make a product in return for a fee.
Glocalisation
HL: adapting a global product or brand to suit local markets.
Exchange rate risk
HL: the risk that changes in exchange rates will affect revenue or costs.
Exam-style question

Discuss the opportunities and threats for a medium-sized food company entering an overseas market. (10 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Marked with five criteria, each worth 2 marks (10 in total):
  • A: knowledge and understanding of relevant tools, techniques and theories.
  • B: application to the case study or stimulus (use names, figures and facts from it).
  • C: reasoned arguments, balanced, with a justified conclusion.
  • D: structure (introduction, logical paragraphs, conclusion) and relevant terminology.
  • E: individuals and societies: consider the perspectives of different stakeholders.
  • Opportunities: growth, economies of scale, spreading risk, PLC extension.
  • Threats: culture, regulation, exchange rates, competition, cost.
  • Method of entry and conclusion.

Model answer: Entering a new market offers growth beyond a saturated home market and spreads risk. However, food tastes and regulations vary strongly between cultures, so products may need costly adaptation; exchange rate changes may reduce profits; and local competitors know the market better. Entering through a joint venture with a local distributor could reduce these risks while building local knowledge.
