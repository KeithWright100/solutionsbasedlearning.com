3. Market research

Success criterion 1

Why carry out market research?

Market research is the collection and analysis of information about customers, competitors and market trends. It reduces risk when launching products, identifies gaps and customer needs, tests reactions to the marketing mix, measures customer satisfaction and helps forecast sales.

Success criterion 2

Primary and secondary research

Primary methodAdvantagesDisadvantages
Surveys / questionnairesLarge samples; quantitative; cheap onlineLow response rates; poorly designed questions
InterviewsIn-depth; can probe answersTime-consuming; interviewer bias
Focus groupsDiscussion reveals opinions and reactionsSmall, possibly unrepresentative group; dominant members
ObservationShows real behaviourDoes not explain why; time-consuming

Primary (field) research collects new data for a specific purpose: up to date, relevant and confidential, but expensive and slow. Secondary (desk) research uses existing data: academic journals, media articles, government publications (census, trade statistics), market analyses (commercial reports) and company reports. It is quick and cheap, but may be out of date, not specific and available to competitors.

Success criterion 3

Sampling methods

MethodMeaningAdvantageDisadvantage
RandomEveryone in the population has an equal chance of selectionUnbiasedNeeds a full list of the population
QuotaA set number from each group (e.g. 50 men, 50 women)Quick, reflects key groupsSelection within groups may be biased
StratifiedRandom selection from each segment in proportion to its sizeRepresentativeNeeds detailed data on segments
ClusterRandom selection of whole groups (e.g. selected cities)Cheap for spread-out populationsClusters may not be typical
ConvenienceWhoever is easiest to reachQuick and cheapLikely to be unrepresentative
SnowballingRespondents recommend othersReaches hard-to-find groupsSimilar people; bias

Sampling error happens when the sample does not represent the population; larger, well-chosen samples reduce it.

Success criterion 4

Qualitative, quantitative and ethical research

Quantitative research collects numerical data that can be analysed statistically (how many, how often). Qualitative research explores opinions, feelings and motives (why), using interviews and focus groups. Ethical considerations: informed consent, confidentiality and data protection, not misleading respondents, protecting children and vulnerable people, and honest reporting of results.

Key terms

Collecting and analysing information about markets, customers and competitors.
Primary research
Collecting new data for a specific purpose.
Secondary research
Using data that already exists.
Focus group
A small group discussing their views on a product.
Sample
A group selected from a population to take part in research.
Quota sampling
Selecting a set number of people from each group.

Qualitative research

Exam-style question

Discuss whether a start-up should rely on secondary research rather than primary research before launching a new product. (10 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Marked with five criteria, each worth 2 marks (10 in total):
  • A: knowledge and understanding of relevant tools, techniques and theories.
  • B: application to the case study or stimulus (use names, figures and facts from it).
  • C: reasoned arguments, balanced, with a justified conclusion.
  • D: structure (introduction, logical paragraphs, conclusion) and relevant terminology.
  • E: individuals and societies: consider the perspectives of different stakeholders.
  • Define both; methods and examples.
  • Secondary: cheap, quick v general, dated. Primary: specific, current v cost, time, sampling issues.
  • Conclusion for a start-up with limited funds.

Model answer: Secondary research is cheap and quick, which suits a start-up with limited funds, and government and industry data can show the size and growth of the market. However, it is not specific to the new product and may be out of date. Primary research, such as a small survey or focus group of the target market, gives direct feedback on the product and price. The best approach is to start with secondary research and then carry out low-cost primary research to test the idea.
