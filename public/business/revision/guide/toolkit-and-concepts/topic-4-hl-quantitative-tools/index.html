4. HL: Critical path analysis, contribution and simple linear regression

IB Business Management Revision Guide · Toolkit, concepts and assessment · Topics · Topic 4 of 4

Learning Intention

To use critical path analysis, contribution analysis and simple linear regression to support business decisions.

Syllabus Statement

  • Business management toolkit (HL only)
  • Critical path analysis
  • Contribution (make or buy, special orders, contribution costing and dropping products)
  • Simple linear regression (scatter diagrams, line of best fit, correlation, extrapolation)

Success criterion 1

HLCritical path analysis

Steps: list activities, their durations and dependencies; draw the network; calculate earliest start times (working forwards, taking the largest) and latest finish times (working backwards, taking the smallest); the critical path has zero float. CPA helps plan resources and shows which activities to monitor, but relies on accurate time estimates and becomes complex for large projects.

Success criterion 2

HLContribution analysis

Because fixed costs must be paid anyway, short-term decisions often depend on contribution (price − variable cost).

  • Special orders: accept an order at a lower-than-usual price if it covers variable costs (positive contribution) and there is spare capacity, as long as it does not upset regular customers.
  • Make or buy: compare the variable cost of making with the price of buying in (plus qualitative factors).
  • Dropping a product: a product that makes a loss after fixed costs are allocated may still make a positive contribution; dropping it could reduce total profit, because its share of fixed costs must then be paid by other products.
HLSpecial order exampleNormal price $20, variable cost $12, spare capacity. A customer offers $15 each for 2 000 units. Contribution = (15 − 12) × 2 000 = $6 000, so profit rises by $6 000 if fixed costs do not change.
Success criterion 3

HLSimple linear regression

A scatter diagram plots two variables. Correlation can be positive or negative, strong or weak (or none). The line of best fit (y = a + bx) can be used to interpolate within the data or extrapolate beyond it. Correlation does not prove causation, outliers can distort the line, and extrapolation is risky.

Key terms

Critical path
HL: the longest sequence of dependent activities; it sets the minimum project time.
Float
HL: the time an activity can be delayed without delaying the project.
Earliest start time
HL: the earliest an activity can begin.
Latest finish time
HL: the latest an activity can finish without delaying the project.
Special order
HL: a one-off order, often at a lower price.
Correlation
HL: the strength and direction of a relationship between two variables.
Extrapolation
HL: extending a trend or line beyond the data to forecast.
Exam-style question

Explain two limitations of critical path analysis for a construction project. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 4 marks: two developed points (or one fully developed point for a 2-mark question), each explained and applied to the case.
  • Use the command term: "explain" needs cause and effect, not a list.
  • e.g. relies on estimated durations (weather, delays); complex for large projects; does not guarantee quality; needs constant updating.

Model answer: Activity times are estimates, and in construction weather or late deliveries can change them, so the critical path may shift. Large projects have hundreds of activities, making the network complex and costly to update.
