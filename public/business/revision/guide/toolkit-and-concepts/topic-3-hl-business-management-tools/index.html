HLPorter and Hofstede

Hofstede’s dimensions: power distance (acceptance of unequal power), individualism v collectivism, masculinity v femininity (competition v cooperation and quality of life), uncertainty avoidance, long-term v short-term orientation, and indulgence v restraint. They help explain cultural clashes and adapt management and marketing internationally.

Key terms

Decision tree
HL: a diagram showing decisions, chance events, probabilities and expected values.
Expected value
HL: the weighted average of possible outcomes, using probabilities.
Force field analysis
HL: a tool that weighs driving and restraining forces for a change.
Gantt chart
HL: a bar chart showing tasks against time.
Ishikawa diagram
HL: a fishbone diagram showing the causes of a problem.
Porter’s generic strategies
HL: cost leadership, differentiation and focus.
Hofstede’s cultural dimensions
HL: six dimensions for comparing national cultures.
Exam-style question

Discuss the usefulness of a decision tree for a business deciding whether to launch a new product. (10 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Marked with five criteria, each worth 2 marks (10 in total):
  • A: knowledge and understanding of relevant tools, techniques and theories.
  • B: application to the case study or stimulus (use names, figures and facts from it).
  • C: reasoned arguments, balanced, with a justified conclusion.
  • D: structure (introduction, logical paragraphs, conclusion) and relevant terminology.
  • E: individuals and societies: consider the perspectives of different stakeholders.
  • Explain construction, expected values, net gains.
  • Strengths: structured, quantitative, considers risk.
  • Limitations: estimated probabilities and outcomes, ignores qualitative factors; conclusion.

Model answer: A decision tree sets out the options, the possible outcomes and their probabilities, so managers can compare expected net gains and think about risk in a structured way. However, the probabilities and financial outcomes are estimates and may be biased, and the tree ignores qualitative factors such as brand image, staff morale and competitor reactions. It is most useful as one input to a decision, alongside market research and managers’ judgement.
