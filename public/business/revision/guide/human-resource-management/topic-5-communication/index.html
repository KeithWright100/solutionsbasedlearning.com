5. Communication

Success criterion 1

Why communication matters

Communication is the transfer of information from a sender to a receiver through a medium (channel), with feedback to confirm understanding. Effective communication improves coordination, decision-making, motivation and customer service; poor communication causes mistakes, conflict, low morale and lost sales.

Success criterion 2

Formal and informal communication

Formal communication uses official channels set by the organisation (reports, meetings, memos, emails, the intranet), following the chain of command. Informal communication happens outside official channels (conversations, social media, the “grapevine”); it is fast and builds relationships, but can spread rumours.

MethodAdvantagesDisadvantages
Face-to-face / meetingsInstant feedback; body language; good for sensitive issuesTime-consuming; no permanent record

Exam tip: Paper 1 often asks you to explain barriers to communication in the case study: name the barrier, explain why it blocks understanding, and link it to the people in the case.

Key terms

Communication
The transfer of information between a sender and a receiver.
Formal communication
Communication through official channels set by the organisation.
Informal communication
Communication outside official channels, such as the grapevine.
Barrier to communication
Anything that prevents a message from being received and understood.
Information overload
Receiving more information than can be processed effectively.
Exam-style question

Explain two barriers to communication that may arise when a business expands into several countries. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 4 marks: two developed points (or one fully developed point for a 2-mark question), each explained and applied to the case.
  • Use the command term: "explain" needs cause and effect, not a list.
  • e.g. language and cultural differences; time zones and distance; longer chains of command.

Model answer: Language differences mean instructions may be misunderstood or translated wrongly, causing mistakes. Time-zone differences make it harder to hold meetings and get quick feedback, so decisions are delayed.
