Success criterion 1

HLCrisis management and contingency planning

A crisis is an unexpected event that threatens the business: a product recall, fire, cyberattack, natural disaster, pandemic or scandal. Crisis management is how the business responds during the crisis to limit damage. Effective crisis management needs transparency, good communication with stakeholders, speed and control.

Contingency planning prepares in advance for possible crises: identifying risks, assessing their likelihood and impact, and planning responses (backup systems, alternative suppliers, insurance, communication plans). It costs time and money, and not every crisis can be predicted, but it reduces the impact and recovery time.

Success criterion 2

HLResearch and development and innovation

Research and development (R&D) is the process of discovering and developing new products and processes. It creates competitive advantage, a USP, higher prices and growth, but is expensive, risky (many projects fail) and slow to pay off.

TypeMeaningExample
Product innovationNew or improved goods and servicesElectric vehicles
Process innovationNew ways of producing or deliveringRobotic warehouses
Positioning innovationChanging the perception or target market of an existing productRepositioning a cheap brand as premium
Paradigm innovationA radical change in how a whole industry worksStreaming replacing DVDs

Intellectual property (IP) protection: patents (inventions), copyright (creative works), trademarks (brand names and logos) protect the returns from R&D. Pure (innovative) creativity creates completely new ideas; adaptive creativity improves existing ideas. Links to the key concept of creativity.

Success criterion 4

HLEthical implications

Data and technology raise ethical questions: privacy and consent for personal data, surveillance of employees (digital Taylorism), bias in AI algorithms, job losses from automation, and responsibility for data breaches. Businesses must balance the benefits of data with legal obligations (such as data protection laws) and trust.

Exam-style question

Discuss whether a retailer should use artificial intelligence and big data to personalise its marketing. (10 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Marked with five criteria, each worth 2 marks (10 in total):
  • A: knowledge and understanding of relevant tools, techniques and theories.
  • B: application to the case study or stimulus (use names, figures and facts from it).
  • C: reasoned arguments, balanced, with a justified conclusion.
  • D: structure (introduction, logical paragraphs, conclusion) and relevant terminology.
  • E: individuals and societies: consider the perspectives of different stakeholders.
  • Benefits: targeted offers, higher sales, better forecasting and stock control.
  • Costs and risks: investment, privacy, data breaches, bias, customer trust, regulation.
  • Ethics and stakeholders; conclusion.

Model answer: AI and big data let the retailer analyse purchase histories and send personalised offers, which can raise sales and loyalty and improve stock planning. However, systems are expensive, data breaches could damage trust and lead to fines, and customers may find heavy personalisation intrusive. If the retailer is transparent about data use and invests in cybersecurity, the benefits are likely to outweigh the risks.
