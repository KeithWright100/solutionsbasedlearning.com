Success criterion 1

Contribution

Contribution

Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit. Total contribution = contribution per unit × quantity sold (or total revenue − total variable costs). Contribution first pays fixed costs; what is left is profit: profit = total contribution − fixed costs.

Example: price $25, variable cost $15, fixed costs $40 000. Contribution per unit = $10.

Success criterion 2

Break-even quantity and margin of safety

Calculations

Break-even quantity = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit = 40 000 ÷ 10 = 4 000 units. Break-even revenue = 4 000 × $25 = $100 000.

If the firm sells 6 000 units: profit = (6 000 × 10) − 40 000 = $20 000. Margin of safety = 6 000 − 4 000 = 2 000 units (or 2 000 ÷ 6 000 × 100 = 33%).

Success criterion 3

Target profit and target price

Targets

Target profit output = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution per unit. To make $30 000 profit: (40 000 + 30 000) ÷ 10 = 7 000 units.

Target price = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ quantity + variable cost per unit. To make $30 000 profit selling 5 000 units: (40 000 + 30 000) ÷ 5 000 + 15 = $29.

Success criterion 5

Evaluating break-even analysis

BenefitsLimitations
Simple and quick; visualAssumes all output is sold
Shows the effect of changes in price or costs (what-if analysis)Assumes straight lines: no bulk discounts or price changes
Supports business plans and loan applicationsHard to apply to firms with many products
Shows the margin of safety and riskData are only estimates; ignores qualitative factors

Key terms

Contribution per unit
Selling price minus variable cost per unit.
Break-even quantity
The output at which total revenue equals total cost.
Margin of safety
Actual output minus break-even output.
Target profit output
The output needed to earn a set level of profit.
Target price
The price needed to earn a set profit at a given output.
Break-even chart
A graph showing total revenue, total cost and fixed cost against output.
Exam-style question

Explain two limitations of break-even analysis for a restaurant. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 4 marks: two developed points (or one fully developed point for a 2-mark question), each explained and applied to the case.
  • Use the command term: "explain" needs cause and effect, not a list.
  • e.g. many menu items with different contributions; not all output sold; costs and prices are not constant.

Model answer: A restaurant sells many dishes with different prices and variable costs, so a single contribution per unit is only an average. Demand varies by day and season, and food may be wasted, so the assumption that everything produced is sold does not hold.
