3. Location and the reorganisation of production

IB Business Management Revision Guide · Unit 5: Operations management · Topics · Topic 3 of 6

Learning Intention

To understand the factors that affect location decisions and the ways businesses reorganise production.

Syllabus Statement

5.4 Location: reasons for a specific location of production; ways of reorganising production, nationally and internationally (outsourcing/subcontracting, offshoring, insourcing, reshoring).

Success criterion 1

Factors affecting location

FactorType
Cost and availability of land and premisesQuantitative
Labour costs and availability of skillsQuantitative / qualitative
Proximity to market (customers) or raw materialsQuantitative
Infrastructure: transport, power, internetQualitative
Government incentives, taxes and regulationsQuantitative / qualitative
Clustering (external economies of scale)Qualitative
Political stability, culture, language, quality of lifeQualitative
Trade barriers (being inside a trading bloc)Quantitative

Some firms are bulk-reducing (inputs heavier than outputs, e.g. steel) and locate near raw materials; others are bulk-increasing (e.g. soft drinks) and locate near customers. Many services must be near their customers.

Success criterion 2

Reorganising production

ApproachMeaningAdvantagesDisadvantages
Outsourcing / subcontractingUsing an external firm to carry out an activityLower costs, specialist expertise, focus on core activitiesLess control over quality; dependence; job losses
OffshoringMoving production or services to another countryLower costs; access to new marketsQuality, communication, ethical and reputation risks
InsourcingBringing an outsourced activity back in-houseControl and quality; protects knowledgeHigher costs
ReshoringBringing offshored activities back to the home countryShorter, more resilient supply chains; “made at home” imageHigher labour costs

Exam tip: Location questions reward balance: weigh quantitative factors (costs) against qualitative ones (skills, infrastructure, ethics), and say which matters most for this business.

Key terms

Outsourcing
Using an external organisation to carry out a business activity.
Subcontracting
Passing part of a job to another firm.
Offshoring
Relocating business activities to another country.
Insourcing
Bringing previously outsourced activities back in-house.
Reshoring
Bringing activities back to the home country.
Clustering
Firms in the same industry locating near each other.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a technology company should offshore its customer service centre. (10 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Marked with five criteria, each worth 2 marks (10 in total):
  • A: knowledge and understanding of relevant tools, techniques and theories.
  • B: application to the case study or stimulus (use names, figures and facts from it).
  • C: reasoned arguments, balanced, with a justified conclusion.
  • D: structure (introduction, logical paragraphs, conclusion) and relevant terminology.
  • E: individuals and societies: consider the perspectives of different stakeholders.
  • Define offshoring; cost savings v quality, language, reputation.
  • Stakeholders: staff, customers, shareholders.
  • Conclusion considering alternatives (outsourcing, automation).

Model answer: Offshoring to a lower-cost country can cut wage and premises costs considerably and allow 24-hour service across time zones. However, language and cultural differences may lower service quality, customers may react badly, and existing staff will lose their jobs, harming morale and reputation. If service quality can be protected through training and monitoring, the cost savings may justify offshoring; if the brand depends on premium service, keeping it at home may be wiser.
