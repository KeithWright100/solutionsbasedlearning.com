IB Geography

Examination Tips & Advice

How to read command terms, manage your time across Paper 1 and Paper 2, and structure full-mark answers.

Know your command terms

Every command term you'll see in an IB Geography question is tied to one of four assessment objectives, and that objective tells you how far your answer needs to go. AO1 terms are checking that you know the content. AO2 terms want you to actually use that knowledge — applying it to the data, diagram or case in front of you, or breaking down how and why something happens. AO3 terms expect you to weigh things up and reach a reasoned judgement or a properly constructed argument, not just describe or explain in isolation. AO4 works differently again: rather than having its own list of command terms, it's assessed through the skills you bring to any answer — reading and constructing maps, graphs and diagrams accurately, and handling case-study evidence and data appropriately. As a general rule, the level of demand rises as you move from AO1 through to AO3, so treat the assessment objective behind a command term as your guide to how much depth an examiner is expecting.

ObjectiveTypical command termsWhat it's really asking for
AO1 — Knowledge and understanding Classify, Define, Describe, Determine, Estimate, Identify, Outline, State Show that you know and understand the content — a correct, focused answer is enough on its own, with no analysis or judgement required.
AO2 — Application and analysis Analyse, Distinguish, Explain, Suggest Go beyond stating facts — apply your knowledge directly to the specific data, diagram or scenario given, or break down how and why a process or pattern occurs.
AO3 — Synthesis and evaluation Compare, Compare and contrast, Contrast, Discuss, Evaluate, Examine, Justify, To what extent Bring evidence, perspectives or options together and reach a supported judgement or argument, rather than describing or explaining each part on its own.

Command terms aren't spelled out anywhere else in the syllabus content itself, so it's worth learning this classification directly — it's the fastest way to judge how much depth a question wants before you start writing.
