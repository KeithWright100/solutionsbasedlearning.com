Central Bank Challenge

You have just been appointed governor of the central bank. For the next two years (eight quarters), news will hit the economy. Diagnose each problem, then set the interest rate to keep inflation near its 2% target without pushing unemployment up or the economy into recession.

1. Diagnose the problemIs it demand-pull inflation, cost-push inflation, falling demand, or a supply-side boost? A correct diagnosis earns bonus points.
2. Set the interest rateRaise, hold or cut. Higher rates slow spending and inflation; lower rates boost spending and jobs. But policy works with a time lag.
3. Hit your targetsEach quarter scores you on inflation (1–3%), unemployment (5% or below) and growth (above 1%), plus points for choosing the right type of policy. Watch out for conflicts between objectives!

    Exam tip: the transmission mechanism. Higher interest rates → borrowing costs more and saving pays more → consumers and firms spend and invest less → aggregate demand falls → lower inflation, but slower growth and possibly higher unemployment. Higher rates can also raise the exchange rate, making imports cheaper (lower inflation) but exports dearer. In an evaluation, mention time lags (often 12–24 months), conflicts between objectives (especially with cost-push inflation), and confidence: if households and firms are pessimistic, a rate cut may not boost spending much.
