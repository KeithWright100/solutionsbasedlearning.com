💧 Micro-Hydro Mission

Around the world, millions of people in remote villages still have no electricity. How can a small river light up a whole village?

Last-mile communities are the villages furthest from the national electricity grid. They are often remote, with poor road access, so it is too expensive to build power lines all the way to them. Micro-hydro is a small hydroelectric system that uses a local stream to make electricity for one community.

Round 3: Case study — Long Tanid, Malaysian Borneo

🌴 Long Tanid

Long Tanid is a small village in the rainforest of Malaysian Borneo. Like many last-mile communities, it used diesel generators that were costly, noisy and only ran some of the time.

In 2022, the charity Green Empowerment and its local partner TONIBUNG worked with community members to install a micro-hydro system. It now gives 11.6 kW of power, 24 hours a day, to 54 households.

The project was funded by Seacology. In return, the community promised to protect 1,379 acres of rainforest in the river’s watershed.

A community-elected energy committee runs the system. Households pay a small fee (a tariff), which pays for repairs and maintenance.
