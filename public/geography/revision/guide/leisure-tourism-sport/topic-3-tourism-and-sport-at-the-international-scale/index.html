3. Tourism and sport at the international scale

IB Geography Revision Guide · Option E: Leisure, Tourism and Sport · Topics · Topic 3 of 4

Learning Intention

To understand niche tourism, the role of TNCs, tourism as a development strategy and hosting international events.

Syllabus Statement

Niche tourism in Iceland; TNCs and budget airlines; tourism and development in the Maldives; international sporting events and Rio 2016 compared with London 2012.

Success criterion 1

Niche tourism: Iceland: Mass tourism and niche tourism

TypeFeaturesExamples
Mass tourismLarge numbers of tourists, standardised packages, popular resortsBeach resorts in Spain, cruise ships
Niche tourismA specialist product for a smaller market with particular interests; often higher value per visitorAdventure, wildlife, heritage, dark, wine, medical, movie location tourism

Adventure tourism involves physical challenge and some risk (trekking, rafting, glacier hiking). It has grown because of rising incomes, social media, better equipment and the search for unique experiences. Movie location tourism draws fans to filming sites: The Lord of the Rings in New Zealand and Game of Thrones in Northern Ireland and Iceland.

Success criterion 1

Niche tourism: Iceland: Iceland’s national strategy

After the 2008 banking crisis, Iceland’s three main banks collapsed and the krona lost much of its value. A weaker currency made Iceland cheaper to visit, and the 2010 eruption of Eyjafjallajökull, which disrupted flights across Europe, gave Iceland worldwide publicity. The government and businesses promoted tourism through the “Inspired by Iceland” campaign.

AdventureGlacier hiking, ice caves, whale watching, northern lights tours

HeritageViking sagas, Þingvellir (site of the world’s oldest parliament, a UNESCO site)
Success criterion 1

Niche tourism: Iceland: Impacts

Foreign visitors rose from fewer than 0.5 million in 2010 to over 2 million by 2018, far outnumbering Iceland’s population of under 400,000. Tourism became the largest export sector, helping the economy recover, but brought overcrowding at sites such as the Golden Circle, rising housing costs in Reykjavík and damage to fragile environments.

Success criterion 2

TNCs and budget airlines: TNCs in tourism

Transnational corporations such as hotel chains (Marriott, Hilton, Accor), tour operators (TUI), cruise lines and online platforms (Booking.com, Airbnb) open up new destinations by investing, marketing and connecting places to global markets. Budget airlines such as Ryanair and easyJet transformed European travel after the EU deregulated aviation in the 1990s.

  • Low fares through no-frills service, online booking, one aircraft type, quick turnarounds and charges for extras.
  • Use of cheaper secondary airports (for example Girona, Charleroi, Bergamo), sometimes helped by local subsidies.
  • Many people now take short city breaks several times a year.

Success criterion 2

TNCs and budget airlines: Evaluating TNC involvement

TNCs bring capital, expertise and global marketing that small places could not provide, but profits often flow back to headquarters in other countries (economic leakage), and decisions about routes and hotels are made far away. Local control and long-term sustainability depend on how governments regulate and tax them.

Success criterion 3

Tourism and development: the Maldives: Tourism as a development strategy

A national development strategy is a government plan to improve the economy and quality of life. Tourism is attractive because it earns foreign currency, creates jobs, encourages investment in infrastructure and can use natural assets that a country already has. However, it is vulnerable to shocks (pandemics, disasters, terrorism) and can cause leakage and social and environmental problems.

Success criterion 3

Tourism and development: the Maldives: The Maldives

The Maldives is a chain of about 1,200 coral islands in 26 atolls in the Indian Ocean. Its natural capital (white beaches, coral reefs, clear water and marine life) supports high-value luxury tourism. Using a “one island, one resort” model, resorts are built on separate uninhabited islands. Tourism is the country’s most important industry, contributing around a quarter of GDP directly and far more indirectly, and visitor numbers reached around 2 million a year in the 2020s.

Success criterion 4

Hosting global events: Why countries bid to host

  • Political: international prestige and “soft power”; showing that a country has arrived on the world stage (Beijing 2008, Qatar 2022).
  • Economic: tourism, investment, jobs, and a deadline that speeds up infrastructure projects (transport, housing, urban regeneration).
  • Cultural: national pride, showcasing culture, and inspiring people to take part in sport.
Success criterion 4

Hosting global events: Costs and risks

Hosting the Olympics or World Cup typically costs tens of billions of US dollars, and most Games have gone over budget. Risks include debt, unused venues, displacement of residents, environmental damage, security threats and negative publicity. Because of these costs, fewer cities now bid: several European cities withdrew from the 2022 and 2024 Winter and Summer Olympic races, and the IOC changed its rules to encourage the use of existing venues.

TypeCosts
EconomicThe Games cost about US$13 billion; Brazil was in recession and the state of Rio de Janeiro declared a financial emergency weeks before the Games; some public workers went unpaid

Success criterion 5

Rio 2016 and London 2012: Rio 2016: benefits and legacy

  • New metro line 4, bus rapid transit routes and the regeneration of the old port area (Porto Maravilha).
  • Global media exposure and around 1 million visitors during the Games.
  • The Games ran successfully and Brazil won a record medal haul at the time.
  • But many venues fell into disuse after the Games, and the athletes’ village struggled to sell flats, so the legacy is widely judged as weak.

Key terms

Niche tourism
Tourism focused on a specialist interest for a smaller market.
Mass tourism
Large-scale, standardised tourism to popular destinations.
Adventure tourism
Travel involving physical challenge and some risk.
Movie location tourism
Travel to places where films or TV series were made.
TNC
A transnational corporation operating in many countries.
Budget airline
A low-cost airline offering cheap, no-frills flights.
Secondary airport
A smaller airport away from a city’s main hub.
Economic leakage
Money from tourism that leaves the host country.
National development strategy
A government plan to improve the economy and quality of life.
Natural capital
Natural assets such as reefs, beaches and wildlife.
Green tax
A tax on tourists used for environmental projects.
Leakage
Tourist spending that leaves the host country.
Bid
A city or country’s application to host an event.
Legacy
The long-term effects of hosting an event.
Regeneration
Improving a run-down area through investment.
Favela
An informal settlement in a Brazilian city.
Displacement
Forcing people to leave their homes.
Brownfield site
Previously developed land that can be reused.
