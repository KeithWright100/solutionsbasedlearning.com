1. Global interactions and global power

Success criterion 1

Globalization indices: Globalization and global interactions

Globalization is the growing interconnection and interdependence of people, places and economies around the world, through flows of goods, services, money, people, information and ideas. Global interactions are the connections and exchanges that make this happen, from trade and investment to tourism, migration, the internet and international politics.

Globalization is uneven: some places are highly connected “switched-on” hubs, while others are partly or almost entirely “switched off”. Indices try to measure how far each country takes part.

Success criterion 1

Globalization indices: The KOF Globalisation Index

The KOF Globalisation Index is published each year by the KOF Swiss Economic Institute at ETH Zurich. It scores almost every country from 1 to 100 using over 40 variables, grouped into three dimensions:

DimensionExamples of what is measured
EconomicTrade in goods and services, foreign direct investment, trade partners, tariffs and trade regulations
SocialInterpersonal (international phone calls, tourism, migrants), informational (internet use, patents), cultural (trade in cultural goods, international brands)
PoliticalEmbassies, membership of international organisations, UN peacekeeping missions, international treaties

Each dimension is split into de facto globalization (actual flows and activities, such as how much a country really trades) and de jure globalization (the policies and conditions that make flows possible, such as low tariffs or membership of trade agreements). Small, open European economies such as Switzerland, the Netherlands and Belgium usually score highest; isolated or conflict-affected states score lowest.

Success criterion 1

Globalization indices: KOF compared with the Kearney index

KOF indexKearney index
Who and whenKOF Swiss Economic Institute, from 2002 and still updated each yearA.T. Kearney and Foreign Policy magazine, 2001–2007; no longer published
What it measuredEconomic, social and political globalization, de facto and de jureEconomic integration, personal contact, technological connectivity and political engagement
CoverageNearly every country, back to 1970About 60–70 countries

Evaluation: indices make comparison over time and between countries easy, but they use national averages that hide differences within countries (connected cities, isolated rural areas), they depend on the variables chosen and how they are weighted, data for poorer countries may be missing or unreliable, and some flows (illegal trade, informal remittances) are hard to measure.

Success criterion 2

Superpowers: USA and China: What makes a superpower?

A global superpower is a state with the ability to project its influence anywhere in the world, through economic strength, geopolitical and military power, and cultural influence (“soft power”). Hard power uses force or payment (military strength, sanctions, aid); soft power attracts and persuades (culture, brands, values, education). Superpowers usually also hold key positions in global institutions, such as permanent seats on the UN Security Council.

Success criterion 2

Superpowers: USA and China: Evaluating superpower status

The USA remains the only all-round superpower, with unmatched military reach, the dominant currency and global cultural influence. China is an emerging superpower: its economic power rivals the USA, and it is expanding its geopolitical reach, but its military reach and soft power are more limited. Many geographers describe a shift from a unipolar world (after the end of the Cold War in 1991) towards a more multipolar world, with India, the EU and Russia as other powerful players.

Success criterion 3

Global groups and lenders: OPEC and energy power

OPEC (the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries) was founded in 1960 and is based in Vienna. Its members, including Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Iran, Kuwait, the UAE, Venezuela and Nigeria, hold most of the world’s proven oil reserves. By agreeing production quotas, OPEC influences the global oil price and so the energy policies of importing countries. Its power was shown in the 1973 oil embargo, when prices quadrupled. Since 2016, OPEC+ has added Russia and other producers. Its influence is limited by US shale oil, the shift to renewables and disagreements between members.

Key terms

Globalization
The growing interconnection and interdependence of the world’s people, places and economies.
KOF index
An annual index scoring countries’ economic, social and political globalization from 1 to 100.
De facto globalization
Actual flows and activities, such as trade or migration.
De jure globalization
Policies and conditions that make flows possible, such as low tariffs.
Superpower
A state that can project its influence anywhere in the world.
Hard power
Influence through military force or economic pressure.
Soft power
Influence through attraction: culture, values and brands.
Belt and Road Initiative
China’s global programme of infrastructure investment.
Multipolar world
A world with several centres of power.
G7
A group of seven leading high-income democracies.
G20
A forum of 19 major economies plus the EU and African Union.
OPEC
An organisation of oil-exporting countries that coordinates production.
IMF
The International Monetary Fund, a global lender to countries in crisis.
NDB
The New Development Bank set up by the BRICS countries.
