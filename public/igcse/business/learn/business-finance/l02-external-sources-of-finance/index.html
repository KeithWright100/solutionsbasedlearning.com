2. External sources of finance

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 3 Business finance · 3.1 Business finance – sources · Lesson 2 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand the external sources of finance and choose the most suitable source.

Syllabus Statement

External sources of finance: overdraft and trade payables; loan capital, share capital, including stock market flotation (public limited companies); venture capital; crowdfunding.

Success criterion 1

Short-term external sources

SourceWhat it isAdvantagesDisadvantages
OverdraftThe bank allows the business to spend more than is in its account, up to an agreed limitFlexible; only pay interest on the amount overdrawn; quick to arrangeHigh interest; the bank can ask for it to be repaid at any time
Trade payables (trade credit)Suppliers let the business buy now and pay later, e.g. in 30 or 60 daysNo interest; the business can sell the goods before paying for themDiscounts for paying quickly are lost; late payment damages supplier relationships
Success criterion 2

Long-term external sources

SourceWhat it isAdvantagesDisadvantages
Loan capitalA sum borrowed from a bank, repaid with interest over a fixed periodLarge sums; fixed repayments help planning; owners keep controlInterest must be paid; often needs security (collateral); repayments due even if trading is poor
Share capitalSelling shares (part-ownership) in a limited company. A plc can sell shares to the public through a stock market flotationNo interest; never has to be repaid; flotation can raise very large sumsOwners lose some control; profits shared as dividends; flotation is expensive and risks takeover
Venture capitalInvestment from specialists in new or growing businesses with high potential, in exchange for sharesLarge sums plus expert advice and contactsInvestors take a share of ownership and want a high return
CrowdfundingRaising small amounts from a large number of people, usually online, in return for rewards or sharesTests public interest; no bank needed; good publicityTakes time; the target may not be reached; ideas can be copied
Success criterion 3

Choosing the right source

When recommending a source, consider: the amount needed, what it is for (short or long term), the cost (interest or loss of ownership), the type of business (only companies can sell shares; only plcs can float on the stock market), and how much control the owners want to keep.

Activity: best source?

Choose the most suitable source of finance.

  • A shop needs $2,000 for three weeks until customers pay
  • A sole trader wants $30,000 for a van, repaid over five years
  • A large company wants to raise $50 million to open 100 stores
  • An inventor wants to test whether people will buy a new gadget
  • A firm’s bank balance is temporarily negative while waiting for payment
  • A partnership wants to buy new machinery but keep full control

Key terms

Overdraft
An agreement allowing a business to spend more than it has in its bank account, up to a limit.
Trade payables
Money owed to suppliers for goods bought on credit.
Loan capital
Money borrowed for a fixed period and repaid with interest.
Share capital
Money raised by selling shares in a company.
Venture capital
Finance provided by investors to new or growing businesses in return for a share of ownership.
Crowdfunding
Raising finance from a large number of people, usually online.
Exam-style question

Analyse one disadvantage to a private limited company of raising finance by selling shares. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a disadvantage: e.g. loss of control / profits shared.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. new shareholders get voting rights.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so the original owners may be outvoted on important decisions.

Model answer: Selling shares means the original owners give up part of the ownership of the company. The new shareholders receive voting rights and a share of profits as dividends, so the original owners may be outvoted on important decisions and receive a smaller share of future profits.
