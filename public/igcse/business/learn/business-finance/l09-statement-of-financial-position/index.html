9. The statement of financial position

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 3 Business finance · 3.4 Financial documents · Lesson 9 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand the main features of a statement of financial position and interpret it.

Syllabus Statement

The purpose of statements of financial position: main features – current and non-current assets, current and non-current liabilities, capital employed; interpret a statement of financial position (learners will not be required to construct a statement of financial position).

Success criterion 1

Assets and liabilities

A statement of financial position (balance sheet) shows what a business owns (assets) and what it owes (liabilities) on a particular date.

ItemMeaningExamples
Non-current assetsOwned for more than one yearBuildings, machinery, vehicles, equipment
Current assetsOwned for less than one year; will turn into cash soonInventory (stock), trade receivables (money owed by customers), cash
Current liabilitiesOwed and due within one yearTrade payables (owed to suppliers), overdraft, tax due
Non-current liabilitiesOwed and due after more than one yearLong-term bank loans, mortgages

Success criterion 2

Capital employed

Example: Lotus Café Ltd at 31 December$
Non-current assets150,000
Current assets60,000
Current liabilities(40,000)
Net current assets (working capital)20,000
Non-current liabilities(50,000)
Net assets120,000
Share capital80,000
Retained profit40,000
Total equity120,000

Capital employed = total equity + non-current liabilities

For Lotus Café: capital employed = 120,000 + 50,000 = $170,000. This is the total long-term finance invested in the business, used to calculate ROCE (lesson 10).

Success criterion 3

Interpreting the statement

Managers, investors and lenders use the statement to judge the business’s financial health:

  • Are current assets greater than current liabilities? If not, the business may struggle to pay short-term debts (liquidity, lesson 11).
  • How much is financed by borrowing (non-current liabilities) compared with owners’ equity? High borrowing means high interest and risk.
  • Is inventory very high? Cash may be tied up in stock.
  • Comparing with last year shows whether the business is growing and how it is financed.

Key terms

Statement of financial position
A financial statement showing assets, liabilities and equity on a particular date.
Non-current assets
Assets kept for more than one year, such as buildings and machinery.
Current assets
Assets that will be turned into cash within a year, such as inventory, receivables and cash.
Current liabilities
Debts due within one year, such as trade payables and overdrafts.
Capital employed
Total equity plus non-current liabilities.
Exam-style question

Explain the difference between current assets and non-current assets, using examples. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: current assets are held for less than a year / easily turned into cash.
  • 1 mark: non-current assets are held for more than a year.
  • 1 mark for appropriate examples of each, e.g. inventory or cash; machinery or buildings.

Model answer: Current assets are items the business expects to turn into cash within a year, such as inventory and money owed by customers. Non-current assets are kept and used for more than a year to run the business, such as machinery, vehicles and buildings.
