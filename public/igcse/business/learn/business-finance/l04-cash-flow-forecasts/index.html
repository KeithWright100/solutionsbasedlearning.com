4. Cash-flow forecasts

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 3 Business finance · 3.2 Cash-flow forecasting · Lesson 4 of 11

Learning Intention

To calculate and interpret cash-flow forecasts.

Syllabus Statement

Calculation and interpretation of cash-flow forecasts: cash inflows, cash outflows, net cash flow, opening and closing balances.

Success criteria

Success criterion 1

Inflows and outflows

A cash-flow forecast predicts the money coming into and going out of a business over future months.

  • Cash inflows: cash sales, payments from credit customers, loans received, owner’s capital, sale of assets.
  • Cash outflows: paying suppliers, wages, rent, electricity, loan repayments, interest, buying equipment.

Activity: inflow or outflow?

Classify each item.

  • Cash sales
  • Rent
  • A bank loan received
  • Wages
  • Money from selling an old van
  • Loan repayments

Success criterion 2

Calculating the forecast

Net cash flow = total inflows − total outflows

Closing balance = opening balance + net cash flow

The closing balance of one month becomes the opening balance of the next month.

Try it

In April, inflows are $10,000 and outflows are $11,200. The opening balance is $1,500 (March’s closing balance). Calculate April’s net cash flow and closing balance.

Show answer

Net cash flow = 10,000 − 11,200 = ($1,200). Closing balance = 1,500 + (−1,200) = $300.

Success criterion 3

Using and improving cash flow

A forecast helps a business plan ahead: it shows when cash will be short so an overdraft can be arranged in advance, it supports loan applications to banks, and it helps managers decide when to buy expensive equipment. Forecasts are only predictions, so unexpected events can make them wrong.

Ways to improve cash flow: chase customers to pay sooner or offer less credit; ask suppliers for longer credit; reduce stock levels; delay buying equipment or lease it instead; arrange an overdraft; sell unused assets.

Key terms

Cash-flow forecast
A prediction of a business’s cash inflows and outflows over future months.
Cash inflow
Money coming into a business.
Cash outflow
Money going out of a business.
Net cash flow
Total inflows minus total outflows for a period.
Closing balance
The cash a business has at the end of a period: opening balance plus net cash flow.
Exam-style question

A business has an opening balance of $3,000. In June its cash inflows are $12,500 and its cash outflows are $16,000. Calculate its closing balance for June, and explain one way it could improve its cash flow. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: net cash flow = 12,500 − 16,000 = ($3,500).
  • 1 mark: closing balance = 3,000 − 3,500 = ($500).
  • 1 mark for a method: e.g. ask customers to pay more quickly.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. cash arrives sooner so the business can pay its bills without an overdraft.

Model answer: Net cash flow = 12,500 − 16,000 = −$3,500. Closing balance = 3,000 − 3,500 = −$500. The business could ask its customers to pay sooner, for example within 14 days instead of 30. Cash would arrive earlier, so it could pay its suppliers and staff without needing an expensive overdraft.
