8. The statement of comprehensive income

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 3 Business finance · 3.4 Financial documents · Lesson 8 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand the main features and uses of a statement of comprehensive income.

Syllabus Statement

The purpose of statements of comprehensive income: main features – sales, cost of sales, gross profit, expenses, operating profit; the use of statements of comprehensive income in decision making (learners will not be required to construct an income statement); the nature of profit and its importance.

Success criterion 1

Main features

A statement of comprehensive income (income statement) shows a business’s revenue, costs and profit over a period, usually one year.

Example: Lotus Café Ltd, year ending 31 December$
Sales revenue200,000
Cost of sales(120,000)
Gross profit80,000
Expenses (rent, salaries, marketing, utilities)(50,000)
Operating profit30,000
  • Cost of sales: the direct cost of the goods sold, e.g. ingredients or stock bought for resale.
  • Expenses (overheads / other operating expenses): costs of running the business not directly linked to each item sold.
Success criterion 2

Calculating profit

Gross profit = revenue − cost of sales

Operating profit = gross profit − other operating expenses

Try it

Revenue is $450,000, cost of sales is $270,000 and expenses are $135,000. Calculate gross profit and operating profit.

Show answer

Gross profit = 450,000 − 270,000 = $180,000. Operating profit = 180,000 − 135,000 = $45,000.

You will not be asked to construct a full statement, but you may need to calculate missing figures and interpret them.

Success criterion 3

The nature and use of profit

Profit is the reward for the risk taken by the owners. It is important because it:

  • provides a return to owners (drawings or dividends);
  • can be retained to finance growth (an internal source of finance);
  • shows the business is successful, which attracts investors and lenders;
  • is a measure used to compare performance over time and with competitors.

Using the statement in decisions: if gross profit is falling, the business may look for cheaper suppliers or raise prices; if operating profit is falling while gross profit is steady, it should control its expenses (e.g. rent, salaries). Banks and investors use the statement to decide whether to lend or invest.

Key terms

Statement of comprehensive income
A financial statement showing revenue, costs and profit over a period.
Cost of sales
The direct costs of producing or buying the goods sold.
Gross profit
Revenue minus cost of sales.
Expenses
Costs of running the business other than cost of sales, such as rent and salaries.
Operating profit
Gross profit minus other operating expenses.
Exam-style question

A business has revenue of $600,000, cost of sales of $380,000 and other operating expenses of $150,000. Calculate its operating profit. (2 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: gross profit = 600,000 − 380,000 = $220,000.
  • 1 mark: operating profit = 220,000 − 150,000 = $70,000.

Model answer: Gross profit = 600,000 − 380,000 = $220,000. Operating profit = 220,000 − 150,000 = $70,000.
