3. The importance of cash

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 3 Business finance · 3.2 Cash-flow forecasting · Lesson 3 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand why cash is vital and how cash differs from profit.

Syllabus Statement

The importance of cash to a business: to pay suppliers, overheads and employees; to prevent business failure (insolvency); the difference between cash and profit.

Success criterion 1

Why cash matters

Cash is the money a business has available to spend immediately, in notes, coins and its bank account. A business needs cash to pay:

  • suppliers for raw materials and stock;
  • overheads such as rent, electricity, insurance and interest;
  • employees their wages and salaries.

If suppliers are not paid, they may stop supplying; if staff are not paid, they leave. Cash is often described as the lifeblood of a business.

Success criterion 2

Insolvency

A business is insolvent when it cannot pay its debts when they are due. Creditors (people the business owes money to) can take it to court and force it to close. Many businesses fail not because they are unprofitable but because they run out of cash.

Common causes of cash-flow problems: customers paying late, too much money tied up in stock, unexpected costs, falling sales, expanding too quickly (overtrading), and poor planning.

Success criterion 3

Cash and profit are different

Profit is revenue minus total costs over a period. Cash is the money actually in the bank at a point in time. A profitable business can be short of cash, and a loss-making business can have cash.

Example: profitable but short of cash

A furniture maker sells tables worth $20,000 in May. Its costs are $14,000, so it makes a profit of $6,000. But the customers have 60 days’ credit, so the $20,000 does not arrive until July, while the $14,000 of wages and materials must be paid in May. In May the business has a profit but a cash shortage of $14,000.

Activity: affects cash, profit or both?

Does each event mainly affect cash now, or profit?

  • Taking out a $10,000 bank loan
  • Selling goods for cash
  • A customer pays a bill from last month
  • Paying this month’s wages
  • The owner puts $5,000 of savings into the business
  • Paying rent for the shop

Key terms

Cash
Money available to spend immediately, in hand or in the bank.
Profit
Revenue minus total costs over a period of time.
Insolvency
Being unable to pay debts when they are due.
Overheads
Costs that do not change with output, such as rent and insurance.
Creditor
A person or business that is owed money.
Exam-style question

Explain why a profitable business might still fail. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: it may run out of cash / have cash-flow problems.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. customers are given credit so cash from sales arrives later than costs must be paid.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. it cannot pay suppliers or staff, becomes insolvent and is forced to close.

Model answer: A profitable business might still fail if it runs out of cash. For example, if it gives customers 60 days to pay, the money from sales arrives after it has had to pay its suppliers and staff. It may then be unable to pay its debts on time, become insolvent and be forced to close even though it was making a profit.
