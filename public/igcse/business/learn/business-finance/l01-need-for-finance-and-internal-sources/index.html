1. The need for finance and internal sources

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 3 Business finance · 3.1 Business finance – sources · Lesson 1 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand why businesses need finance and the internal sources available.

Syllabus Statement

The need for finance: short-term needs, long-term needs, to start up or expand. Internal sources of finance: personal savings, retained profit, selling assets.

Success criteria

I can…

Tick each one when you are confident. Your ticks are saved to your account.

Success criterion 1

Why businesses need finance

Every business needs money (finance) to pay for what it needs.

NeedExamples
Start-upPremises, equipment, first stock, marketing before any sales are made
Short-term needsDay-to-day running costs (working capital): paying suppliers, wages, bills; covering a gap before customers pay
Long-term needsNon-current assets that last many years: buildings, machinery, vehicles, new technology
ExpansionOpening new branches, entering new markets, buying another business

A good rule is to match the source to the need: short-term needs should use short-term finance, and long-term needs should use long-term finance.

Success criterion 2

Internal sources of finance

Internal sources come from within the business or its owner.

SourceWhat it isAdvantagesDisadvantages
Personal savingsThe owner’s own money, often used by sole traders to start upNo interest or repayment; owner keeps controlLimited amount; owner risks losing their savings
Retained profitProfit kept in the business rather than paid to ownersNo interest; no loss of control; no repaymentOnly available if the business is profitable; owners get less now; new businesses have none
Selling assetsSelling things the business owns but no longer needs, e.g. old machinery or unused landRaises cash from items not being usedAssets can only be sold once; may not get a good price; the asset may be needed later
Success criterion 3

Choosing internal finance

Internal sources are usually the cheapest because there is no interest to pay and no loss of control. However, they are often not enough, especially for a new business or a big expansion, which is why many businesses also use external finance (next lesson).

Activity: which need?

Is each a short-term or long-term finance need?

  • Paying this month’s wages while waiting for customers to pay
  • Buying a new delivery van
  • Buying extra stock for the Christmas rush
  • Building a new factory
  • Paying an unexpected electricity bill
  • Buying another business

Key terms

Finance
Money needed to start, run or expand a business.
Internal finance
Money raised from within the business or its owners.
Retained profit
Profit kept in the business rather than paid to the owners.
Asset
Something owned by a business, such as machinery or buildings.
Working capital
Money available to pay day-to-day running costs.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a business should use retained profit to finance the purchase of new machinery. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge: e.g. no interest, no repayment, no loss of control; must have enough profit.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. no interest keeps costs low so future profits are higher; but shareholders/owners receive less and cash for emergencies is reduced.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: Retained profit is a cheap source because there is no interest to pay and nothing to repay, so costs stay low and the owners keep full control. However, the business may not have enough retained profit to pay for expensive machinery, and using it means less money is available for emergencies or for paying the owners. If the business has built up enough retained profit, it is the best source because it avoids interest, but if not it may need a loan as well.
