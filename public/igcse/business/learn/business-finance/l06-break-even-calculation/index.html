6. Break-even: the concept and calculation

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 3 Business finance · 3.3 Costs and break-even analysis · Lesson 6 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand break-even and calculate the break-even level of output.

Syllabus Statement

The concept of break-even and calculation of break-even (from formula or diagram): break-even level of output.

Success criteria

I can…

Tick each one when you are confident. Your ticks are saved to your account.

Success criterion 1

What is break-even?

The break-even point is the level of output where total revenue = total costs. The business makes neither a profit nor a loss. Below break-even it makes a loss; above it, a profit.

Knowing the break-even point helps a business decide whether a product or a new business idea is worth pursuing, set prices and sales targets, and support a loan application.

Success criterion 2

Contribution per unit

Each unit sold contributes something towards paying the fixed costs.

Contribution per unit = selling price per unit − variable cost per unit

A café sells sandwiches for $5; ingredients and packaging cost $2. Each sandwich contributes $3 towards fixed costs. Once fixed costs are covered, the contribution becomes profit.

Success criterion 3

The break-even formula

Break-even output = fixed costs ÷ (selling price per unit − variable cost per unit)

Worked example

A business has fixed costs of $4,000 a month. It sells each item for $16 and the variable cost is $6.

Contribution per unit = 16 − 6 = $10. Break-even output = 4,000 ÷ 10 = 400 units a month.

Check: revenue at 400 units = 16 × 400 = $6,400; total costs = 4,000 + (6 × 400) = $6,400. ✔

Try it

Fixed costs are $9,000. Price is $25 and variable cost is $10 per unit. Calculate the break-even output. How many units would give a profit of $3,000?

Show answer

Break-even = 9,000 ÷ (25 − 10) = 600 units. For $3,000 profit: (9,000 + 3,000) ÷ 15 = 800 units.

Always give your answer in units, and round up to the next whole unit if the answer is not a whole number, because selling fewer units would still make a loss.

Key terms

Break-even point
The level of output where total revenue equals total costs.
Contribution per unit
Selling price minus variable cost per unit.
Break-even output
Fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit.
Profit
Revenue greater than total costs.
Loss
Total costs greater than revenue.
Exam-style question

A business has fixed costs of $12,000. Its product sells for $30 and has a variable cost of $18 per unit. Calculate the break-even level of output. (2 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: contribution = 30 − 18 = $12.
  • 1 mark: break-even = 12,000 ÷ 12 = 1,000 units.

Model answer: Contribution per unit = 30 − 18 = $12. Break-even output = 12,000 ÷ 12 = 1,000 units.
