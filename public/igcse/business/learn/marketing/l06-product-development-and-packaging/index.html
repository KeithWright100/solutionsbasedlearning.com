6. Product: new products, goods and services, and packaging

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 4 Marketing · 4.3 The marketing mix · Lesson 6 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand how new products are developed, the difference between goods and services, and the importance of packaging.

Syllabus Statement

Product: development of a new product/service; the difference between goods and services; packaging and its importance.

Success criterion 1

Developing a new product

The marketing mix (the 4Ps) is the combination of Product, Price, Place and Promotion used to sell a product. This lesson starts with Product.

  1. Generating ideas: from market research, staff, customers or competitors.
  2. Screening: rejecting ideas that are too costly or unlikely to sell.
  3. Concept testing / market research: asking customers what they think.
  4. Developing a prototype: a working model to test design and safety.
  5. Test marketing: selling in a small area.
  6. Launch: full release with promotion.

Developing new products is risky and expensive, but successful new products bring growth, a competitive advantage and protection against falling sales of older products.

Success criterion 2

Goods and services

FeaturesExamples
GoodsTangible: physical products you can touch and storePhones, cars, food, clothes
ServicesIntangible: you cannot touch or store them; produced and consumed at the same timeHaircuts, banking, insurance, education, hotel stays

Because services are intangible, their quality depends heavily on the staff who provide them and on the customer’s experience, so businesses invest in training and customer service.

Success criterion 3

Packaging and its importance

Packaging is part of the product. It is important because it:

  • protects the product during transport and storage;
  • attracts attention on the shelf and helps the product stand out from competitors;
  • gives information: ingredients, instructions, safety warnings, legal requirements;
  • builds the brand image, e.g. luxury packaging for premium products;
  • makes the product convenient to carry, open and use.

Increasingly, customers expect packaging to be environmentally friendly (recyclable, less plastic), although this can increase costs.

Key terms

Marketing mix
The combination of product, price, place and promotion used to sell a product.
Prototype
A working model of a new product used for testing.
Goods
Tangible products that can be touched and stored.
Services
Intangible products, such as banking or haircuts.
Packaging
The container or wrapping that protects and promotes a product.
Exam-style question

Explain one reason why packaging is important for a new chocolate bar. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a reason: e.g. attracts attention / protects / informs.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. bright, eye-catching packaging makes it stand out on a crowded shelf.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so more customers notice and try it, increasing sales.

Model answer: Packaging attracts attention. A new chocolate bar sits on a shelf next to many well-known brands, so bright, distinctive packaging helps it stand out. More customers will notice and try it, which increases sales and helps the new product become established.
