3. Using data in market research

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 4 Marketing · 4.1 Market research · Lesson 3 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand qualitative and quantitative data, social media research and the reliability of data.

Syllabus Statement

The use of data in market research: qualitative and quantitative data; the role of social media in collecting market research data; the importance of the reliability of market research data.

  • “The seats are uncomfortable”

Success criterion 2

Social media and market research

Social media is now a major source of market research data:

  • Online polls and surveys can reach thousands of followers quickly and cheaply.
  • Comments, reviews and likes show what customers think in real time.
  • Analytics show who engages with posts (age, location, interests).
  • Businesses can monitor what people say about competitors.

However, followers are not always typical customers, people may give joke or extreme answers, and fake reviews exist.

Success criterion 3

Reliability of data

Data is reliable if it is accurate and representative, so that decisions based on it are sound. Data may be unreliable because of:

  • Small or unrepresentative samples, e.g. only asking friends.
  • Biased or leading questions, e.g. “Don’t you agree our product is great?”.
  • Out-of-date information.
  • People giving answers they think the researcher wants, or not being honest.

Decisions based on unreliable data can be costly, such as launching a product few people really want.

Key terms

Quantitative data
Numerical information that can be measured and counted.
Qualitative data
Information about opinions, feelings and reasons.
Sample
The group of people chosen to take part in research.
Bias
When data is distorted so it does not give a true picture.
Reliability
How accurate and trustworthy data is.
Exam-style question

Explain one reason why market research data collected through social media may be unreliable. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a reason: e.g. the sample is not representative.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. only followers of the business answer, who may already like the brand / be young.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so the results may not reflect the views of the whole target market, leading to poor decisions.

Model answer: Social media data may come from an unrepresentative sample, because only people who already follow the business answer the poll. They are likely to already like the brand and may mostly be young, so the results may not reflect the views of all potential customers, which could lead the business to make a poor decision.
