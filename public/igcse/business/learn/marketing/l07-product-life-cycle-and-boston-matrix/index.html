7. The product life cycle and the Boston matrix

Success criterion 1

The product life cycle

The product life cycle shows the stages a product passes through from its development to its withdrawal.

PhaseWhat happensSales and profit
DevelopmentResearch, design and testingNo sales; high costs; loss
IntroductionLaunched; few customers know itLow sales; heavy promotion; usually a loss
GrowthMore customers buy itSales rise quickly; profits begin; competitors appear
MaturityMost potential customers have bought itSales peak and level off; strong competition; highest profits
DeclineTastes change or better products appearSales and profits fall; product may be withdrawn
Success criterion 2

Extension strategies

An extension strategy is used in maturity or early decline to extend the life of a product:

  • new versions, flavours, sizes or features;
  • new packaging or a new advertising campaign;
  • selling in new markets (other countries or customer groups);
  • finding new uses for the product;
  • price cuts or special offers.
Success criterion 3

The Boston matrix

Most businesses sell a range of products called a product portfolio. The Boston matrix helps managers review the portfolio by placing each product according to its market share and the growth of its market.

A balanced portfolio uses profits from cash cows to fund question marks and stars, which will become the cash cows of the future, while dogs are reviewed and may be withdrawn.

Activity: which Boston category?

Classify each product.

  • A market-leading cola in a mature drinks market
  • The best-selling electric car in a fast-growing market
  • A new energy drink with a tiny share of a booming market
  • A DVD player with low sales in a shrinking market
  • A top-selling app in a rapidly growing sector
  • A long-established, best-selling washing powder

Key terms

Product life cycle
The stages a product passes through: development, introduction, growth, maturity and decline.
Extension strategy
An action taken to extend the life of a product, such as a new version or new market.
Product portfolio
The range of products sold by a business.
Boston matrix
A model classifying products by market share and market growth.
Cash cow
A product with a high market share in a low-growth market.
Exam-style question

Explain one extension strategy a business could use for a soft drink in the maturity phase of its life cycle. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a strategy: e.g. launch a new flavour / sugar-free version.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. this attracts new customers such as health-conscious buyers and renews interest.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so sales rise again instead of falling into decline.

Model answer: The business could launch a sugar-free version of the drink. This would attract health-conscious customers who did not buy the original and create new interest among existing customers, so sales rise again and the product stays in maturity longer instead of moving into decline.
