2. Methods of market research

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 4 Marketing · 4.1 Market research · Lesson 2 of 11

Learning Intention

To compare primary and secondary market research methods.

Syllabus Statement

Methods of market research: primary research (survey, questionnaire, focus group, observation, test marketing); secondary research (internet, market reports, government reports).

Success criterion 1

Primary research

Primary research (field research) collects new information first-hand for a specific purpose.

MethodWhat it involvesStrength
Survey / questionnaireA set of questions asked face to face, by phone, post or onlineCan reach many people; easy to analyse if questions are closed
Focus groupA small group discusses a product in depth, led by a researcherDetailed opinions and reasons
ObservationWatching how customers behave, e.g. counting passers-by or watching shoppersShows what people actually do, not what they say
Test marketingSelling a product in a small area before a full launchReal sales data; reduces risk
Success criterion 2

Secondary research

Secondary research (desk research) uses information that already exists, collected by someone else.

SourceExamples
InternetCompetitors’ websites, online reviews, news articles, social media
Market reportsReports by research companies on the size and trends of a market
Government reportsPopulation census, income and employment statistics

Activity: primary or secondary?

Classify each method.

  • Asking shoppers questions in the street
  • Reading a government census report
  • Running a focus group of teenagers
  • Looking at competitors’ prices online
  • Counting cars passing a possible shop site
  • Buying a market report on the coffee industry

Success criterion 3

Comparing primary and secondary research

TypeAdvantagesDisadvantages
PrimaryUp to date; specific to the business’s needs; the business owns it and competitors do not have itExpensive and time-consuming; small samples may be biased or unrepresentative
SecondaryCheaper and quicker; often covers large samples or whole populationsMay be out of date; not specific to the business; also available to competitors; may be biased

Many businesses start with cheap secondary research to understand the market, then use primary research to answer specific questions.

Key terms

Primary research
Collecting new information first-hand for a specific purpose.
Secondary research
Using information that has already been collected by others.
Questionnaire
A set of questions used to collect information from people.
Focus group
A small group of people who discuss a product in depth.
Test marketing
Selling a product in a limited area before a full launch.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a small business should use primary or secondary market research. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of both types.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. primary research is specific and up to date but costly for a small business; secondary is cheap but may not be relevant to its local market.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: Primary research, such as a local survey, would give up-to-date information about the business’s own customers, which competitors would not have. However, it takes time and money, which a small business may lack. Secondary research, such as government population data and competitors’ websites, is cheap and quick, but it may be out of date and not specific to the local area. A small business should start with secondary research to understand the market, then carry out a small, low-cost survey of local customers to answer its specific questions.
