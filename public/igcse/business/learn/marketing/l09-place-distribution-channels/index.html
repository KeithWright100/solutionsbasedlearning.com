9. Place: distribution channels

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 4 Marketing · 4.3 The marketing mix · Lesson 9 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand the methods of distribution, including retailers and e-tailers.

Syllabus Statement

Place – distribution channels: methods of distribution – retailers, e-tailers (e-commerce).

Success criteria

Success criterion 1

Distribution channels

Place is about how and where products reach customers. A distribution channel is the route a product takes from the producer to the consumer.

ChannelExample
Producer → consumer (direct)A farm shop, a factory outlet, a business’s own website
Producer → retailer → consumerA drinks maker sells to supermarkets
Producer → wholesaler → retailer → consumerA wholesaler buys in bulk and sells to many small shops

Each intermediary (wholesaler or retailer) adds a profit margin, so longer channels usually mean higher prices, but they help products reach more customers.

Success criterion 2

Retailers and e-tailers

MethodAdvantagesDisadvantages
Retailers (shops)Customers can see, touch and try products; immediate purchase; personal service; good for impulse buysHigh costs of rent and staff; limited opening hours; limited to local customers
E-tailers (e-commerce)Open 24/7; reach national and global customers; lower costs (no shop); wide range; easy price comparisonDelivery costs and delays; customers cannot try products; returns; security worries; strong competition

Many businesses use both (“clicks and bricks”), for example ordering online and collecting in store.

Activity: retailer or e-tailer advantage?

Is each an advantage of a retailer (shop) or an e-tailer (online)?

  • Customers can try clothes on before buying
  • Open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week
  • Staff give personal advice
  • Can sell to customers in other countries
  • Customers take the product home immediately
  • No expensive high-street rent

Success criterion 3

Choosing the right channel

A business should consider: the type of product (fresh food needs short channels; expensive items may need demonstrations), the target market and where they shop, the cost of each channel, the size of the business, and what competitors do.

Key terms

Place
How and where a product is made available to customers.
Distribution channel
The route a product takes from producer to consumer.
Retailer
A business that sells products to consumers, usually in a shop.
E-tailer
A business that sells products to consumers online.
Wholesaler
A business that buys in bulk from producers and sells to retailers.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a clothing business should sell only online rather than through its own shops. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of e-tailing and retailing.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. online cuts rent and staff costs and reaches more customers; but customers cannot try clothes on, so returns rise.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: Selling only online would remove the high costs of shop rent and staff, and the business could reach customers across the country and abroad 24 hours a day. However, customers like to try clothes on, so online sales can lead to many returns, and the business would face strong online competition and delivery costs. Shops also give a place for customers to see the brand. On balance, the business should combine both, with a strong website and a smaller number of shops, rather than selling only online.
