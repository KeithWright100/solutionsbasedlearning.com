8. Price: pricing strategies

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 4 Marketing · 4.3 The marketing mix · Lesson 8 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand the main pricing strategies and when they are used.

Syllabus Statement

Price: the main pricing strategies and when they might be applied – cost plus, penetration, competition, skimming, promotional.

Success criterion 1

Pricing strategies

StrategyHow it worksWhen usedDrawbacks
Cost plusAdd a percentage markup to the cost per unitSimple; ensures each item covers its costIgnores competitors and what customers will pay
PenetrationSet a low price to enter a market and gain market share quickly, then raise it laterNew products in competitive mass marketsLow profit at first; customers may leave when price rises
CompetitionSet prices similar to competitorsMarkets with many similar productsLittle profit if costs are high; no price advantage
SkimmingSet a high price at launch, then lower it over timeNew, innovative or high-tech products with little competitionSales may be low; high price attracts competitors
PromotionalTemporarily lower prices, e.g. “buy one get one free”, salesClearing stock, boosting sales, attracting new customersLower profit; customers may wait for offers
Success criterion 2

Cost-plus pricing

Cost-plus price = cost per unit + (cost per unit × markup %)

Worked example

A candle costs $4.00 to make. The business adds a 75% markup.

Markup = 75% × $4.00 = $3.00. Price = 4.00 + 3.00 = $7.00.

Try it

A phone case costs $6. The business uses a markup of 150%. What is the selling price?

Show answer

Markup = 1.5 × 6 = $9. Price = 6 + 9 = $15.

Success criterion 3

Choosing a strategy

The right strategy depends on the stage of the product life cycle, the level of competition, how unique the product is, the firm’s objectives (market share or profit) and its costs.

Activity: which strategy?

Choose the most suitable pricing strategy.

  • A new games console with unique features
  • A new streaming service entering a crowded market
  • A petrol station next to two others
  • A shop clearing winter coats in spring
  • The latest top-of-the-range smartphone
  • A new supermarket brand of cereal wanting quick market share

Key terms

Cost-plus pricing
Adding a percentage markup to the cost of producing a product.
Penetration pricing
Setting a low price to enter a market and win market share.
Price skimming
Setting a high price for a new product, then lowering it over time.
Competition pricing
Setting a price similar to competitors’ prices.
Promotional pricing
Temporarily reducing prices to increase sales.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a business launching a new, innovative smartwatch should use price skimming. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of skimming (and an alternative, e.g. penetration).
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. early adopters will pay a premium so development costs are recovered quickly and a quality image is created; but high prices limit sales and attract rivals.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: Price skimming would let the business charge a high price to early adopters who want the latest technology, recovering its high development costs quickly and giving the watch a premium image. However, a high price limits the number of buyers, and high profits may attract competitors who launch cheaper versions. Penetration pricing would win market share faster but with lower profit. Because the watch is innovative and has little competition at first, skimming is the better choice, with the price lowered as rivals enter the market.
