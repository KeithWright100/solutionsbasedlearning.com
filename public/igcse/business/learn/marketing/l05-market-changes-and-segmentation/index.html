5. Responding to market changes and market segmentation

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 4 Marketing · 4.2 The market · Lesson 5 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand how businesses respond to market changes and use segmentation to target customers.

Syllabus Statement

Responding to changes in the market: changing customer needs; changing customer/consumer spending patterns; increased competition. How businesses use market segmentation to target customers: identifying market segments – location, demographics, lifestyle, income, age.

Success criterion 1

Changing needs and spending patterns

Markets change constantly. Customer needs change with fashion, health concerns, technology and environmental awareness. Spending patterns change with incomes, the economy and how people shop, for example the shift to online shopping and mobile payments.

Businesses respond by developing new products, changing existing ones, selling through new channels (e.g. online), and adjusting prices. A restaurant might add plant-based dishes and a delivery service.

Success criterion 2

Increased competition

When new competitors enter a market, a business may lose customers. It can respond by:

  • cutting prices or offering promotions;
  • improving quality or customer service;
  • adding new features or products (differentiation);
  • increasing promotion and building its brand;
  • building loyalty with rewards schemes.
Success criterion 3

Market segmentation

Market segmentation divides a market into groups of customers with similar characteristics. Each segment can then be targeted with a suitable product, price and promotion.

Segment byMeaningExample
LocationWhere customers live (country, region, city, rural)Different menus for different countries
DemographicsPopulation characteristics: gender, family size, religion, ethnicityProducts for families with young children
LifestyleInterests, activities and valuesSports drinks for fitness fans

Activity: how is it segmented?

Which segmentation method is the main one used?

  • A toy shop for under-5s
  • A luxury watch costing $20,000
  • Hiking boots for outdoor adventurers
  • A snack flavour sold only in Thailand
  • Smartphones designed for older people
  • A discount supermarket chain

Benefits of segmentation: marketing is more effective and less wasteful, customer needs are met more closely, and gaps can be spotted. Drawback: developing different products and campaigns for each segment is costly.

Key terms

Spending patterns
How and on what customers spend their money.
Differentiation
Making a product different from competitors’ products.
Market segmentation
Dividing a market into groups of customers with similar characteristics.
Market segment
A group of customers with similar characteristics.
Demographics
Characteristics of a population, such as gender, family size and religion.
Exam-style question

Explain one benefit to a business of using market segmentation. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a benefit: e.g. marketing can be targeted.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. the product and promotion can be designed to meet the needs of a specific group.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so sales increase and money is not wasted advertising to people who will not buy.

Model answer: Segmentation lets a business target its marketing at a specific group, such as teenagers. It can design products and adverts that meet that group’s needs and place them where the group will see them, so sales are likely to rise and less money is wasted on advertising to people who are unlikely to buy.
