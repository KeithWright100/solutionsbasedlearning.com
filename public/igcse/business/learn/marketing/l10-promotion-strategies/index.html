10. Promotion: strategies and public relations

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 4 Marketing · 4.3 The marketing mix · Lesson 10 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand promotion strategies, above and below the line promotion and public relations.

Syllabus Statement

Promotion: promotion strategies for different market segments – advertising, sponsorship, product trials, special offers, branding; above the line and below the line promotion techniques; public relations – improving company image/brand.

Success criterion 1

Promotion strategies

Promotion is communicating with customers to inform them about a product and persuade them to buy it.

StrategyWhat it isPurpose
AdvertisingPaid messages in media such as TV, radio, newspapers, billboards, onlineReaches many people
SponsorshipPaying to be linked with an event, team or programmeBuilds brand awareness and image
Product trialsFree samples or free trial periodsLets customers try with no risk
Special offersDiscounts, BOGOF, competitions, couponsBoosts short-term sales
BrandingCreating a name, logo and image that customers recognise and trustBuilds loyalty and allows higher prices

The strategy should suit the market segment: social media influencers for teenagers, magazine adverts for older readers, sponsorship of football for sports fans.

Success criterion 2

Above and below the line

TypeExamplesAdvantagesDisadvantages
Above the line (ATL)Mass media advertising paid for by the business: TV, radio, cinema, newspapers, billboardsReaches a huge audience; builds awarenessExpensive; hard to target; hard to measure
Below the line (BTL)All other promotion the business controls directly: special offers, sponsorship, direct mail, point-of-sale displays, free samples, eventsEasier to target; cheaper; quick resultsReaches fewer people; effects may be short-term

Success criterion 3

Public relations

Public relations (PR) is managing the image and reputation of the business with the public. Examples: press releases, media interviews, charity donations, community events, responding to complaints and managing a crisis (e.g. a product recall).

PR is often cheaper than advertising and can be more trusted, because news stories seem more independent than adverts. However, the business cannot fully control what the media says.

Key terms

Promotion
Communicating with customers to inform and persuade them to buy.
Above the line promotion
Paid advertising through mass media such as TV and newspapers.
Below the line promotion
Promotion not using mass media, such as offers, sponsorship and direct mail.
Sponsorship
Paying to be associated with an event, team or programme.
Public relations
Activities to create and maintain a positive image of the business.
Exam-style question

Analyse one advantage of below the line promotion for a small business. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for an advantage: e.g. cheaper / easier to target.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. a leaflet drop or local offer reaches customers in its area.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so it gains sales without the high cost of TV advertising, which it could not afford.

Model answer: Below the line promotion is cheaper and easier to target. A small café could hand out vouchers to people working nearby, reaching exactly the customers likely to visit. This increases sales without the very high cost of mass media advertising, which a small business could not afford.
