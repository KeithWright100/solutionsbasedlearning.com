4. The importance of marketing

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 4 Marketing · 4.2 The market · Lesson 4 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand why marketing matters and key ideas about markets and orientation.

Syllabus Statement

Importance of marketing: satisfying customer needs; building customer relationships; keeping customer loyalty; market orientation and product orientation; market share and analysis; niche and mass marketing.

Success criterion 1

Why marketing matters

Marketing is identifying, anticipating and satisfying customer needs profitably. It helps a business to:

  • satisfy customer needs, so customers buy the product;
  • build customer relationships through good service, communication and after-sales care;
  • keep customer loyalty, e.g. loyalty cards and rewards, so customers buy again instead of switching to rivals. Keeping customers is cheaper than finding new ones.
Success criterion 2

Market and product orientation

ApproachMeaningAdvantagesDisadvantages
Market orientationResearch customer needs first, then develop products to meet themProducts more likely to sell; responds to changeResearch is costly; may limit truly new inventions
Product orientationDevelop the product first (based on the firm’s skills or technology), then find customersCan create innovative, high-quality productsRisk that customers do not want the product

Activity: market or product oriented?

Classify each business approach.

  • A café surveys customers before changing its menu
  • An engineer invents a new gadget, then looks for buyers
  • A clothing brand follows social media trends to design ranges
  • A firm makes the best-quality drill it can, whatever customers say
  • A toy firm runs focus groups with children before launching
  • A tech firm launches a product built around its new patent

Success criterion 3

Market share and niche vs mass marketing

Market share (%) = (business’s sales ÷ total market sales) × 100

Try it

A brand sells $6 million of trainers in a market worth $40 million. What is its market share?

Show answer

6 ÷ 40 × 100 = 15%.

Market analysis means studying market size, growth, market shares and trends to spot opportunities and threats.

ApproachMeaningAdvantagesDisadvantages
Mass marketingSelling the same product to the whole marketLarge sales; economies of scaleHigh competition; heavy advertising costs
Niche marketingSelling to a small, specialised part of the marketLess competition; can charge higher prices; loyal customersSmall sales; vulnerable if tastes change

Key terms

Marketing
Identifying and satisfying customer needs profitably.
Customer loyalty
When customers keep buying from the same business.
Market orientation
Basing decisions on customer needs found through research.
Product orientation
Developing products first and then finding customers.
Niche market
A small, specialised part of a larger market.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a new business should target a niche market or a mass market. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of niche and mass markets.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. a niche has less competition so a small firm can charge premium prices; mass market offers higher sales but strong competition from big firms with economies of scale.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: A niche market has fewer competitors, so a new business can become known as a specialist and charge higher prices, which helps it survive with lower sales. However, the market is small and sales could fall if tastes change. A mass market offers much larger potential sales, but the new business would compete with large, established firms that have bigger budgets and lower costs. A new business with limited finance should usually target a niche first, then grow into wider markets once it is established.
