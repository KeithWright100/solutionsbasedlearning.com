5. Legal controls over employment

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.2 Recruitment and selection · Lesson 5 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand how equal opportunities and minimum wage laws affect businesses and employees.

Syllabus Statement

Legal controls over employment and their effects: equal opportunities – gender, race, disability, religion, sexual preference, age; minimum wage laws.

Success criterion 1

Equal opportunities

Equal opportunities laws make it illegal to discriminate (treat someone unfairly) against employees or job applicants because of their gender, race, disability, religion, sexual orientation or age. This applies to recruitment, pay, training, promotion and dismissal.

For example, a business cannot refuse to interview an applicant because of their religion, pay women less than men for the same work, or reject an older applicant because of age. Businesses may have to make reasonable adjustments for disabled employees, such as ramps or special equipment.

Success criterion 2

Minimum wage laws

A minimum wage is the lowest hourly rate of pay that employers are legally allowed to pay. Many countries set different rates for different ages. It protects low-paid workers from exploitation and gives them enough to live on.

Worked example

The minimum wage rises from $8.00 to $8.50 an hour. A café employs 6 workers at the minimum wage for 30 hours a week each.

Extra cost per week = $0.50 × 30 hours × 6 workers = $90. Over 52 weeks that is $4,680 a year.

Key terms

Discrimination
Treating a person unfairly because of a characteristic such as gender, race or age.
Equal opportunities
Giving everyone the same chance regardless of gender, race, disability, religion, sexual orientation or age.
Minimum wage
The lowest hourly rate of pay an employer can legally pay.
Exploitation
Treating workers unfairly, for example paying very low wages.
Reasonable adjustment
A change made so that a disabled person can do a job.
Exam-style question

Discuss the impact of an increase in the minimum wage on a small restaurant. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge: e.g. higher wage costs; more motivated staff.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. profit margins fall unless prices rise; motivated staff give better service and stay longer, cutting recruitment costs.
  • Up to 2 marks for a judgement: e.g. depends on how many staff are on the minimum wage and whether customers will accept higher prices.

Model answer: Higher minimum wages increase the restaurant’s costs, because many waiting and kitchen staff are low paid, so profit falls unless it raises menu prices, which could lose customers to cheaper rivals. It might cut staff hours, reducing service quality. However, better-paid staff may be more motivated and loyal, so service improves and the restaurant spends less on recruiting and training. Overall, the impact is likely to be negative in the short term for a small restaurant with tight margins, but it depends on how many staff earn the minimum and whether competitors face the same rise.
