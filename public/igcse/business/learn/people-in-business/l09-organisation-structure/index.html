9. Organisation structure

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.5 Organisation structure and employees · Lesson 9 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand organisational charts and the key terms used to describe them.

Syllabus Statement

Organisational charts for different types of business: hierarchical and flat; centralised and decentralised. Roles and responsibilities of employees in terms of compliance and accountability: span of control, chain of command, delegation.

Success criterion 1

Organisational charts and key terms

An organisational chart shows the structure of a business: the job roles, who is responsible to whom, and how communication flows.

TermMeaning
Chain of commandThe line of authority from the top of the business to the bottom, showing who reports to whom
Span of controlThe number of employees who report directly to one manager
DelegationPassing authority to make decisions or do tasks to someone lower in the hierarchy; the manager remains accountable
AccountabilityBeing responsible for the results of decisions and tasks
Levels of hierarchyThe number of layers of management in the structure
Success criterion 2

Hierarchical and flat structures

StructureFeaturesAdvantagesDisadvantages
Hierarchical (tall)Many levels, narrow spans of controlClose supervision; clear promotion ladder; clear rolesSlow communication and decisions; messages distorted; high management costs
FlatFew levels, wide spans of controlFast communication; lower management costs; staff have more responsibility (motivating)Managers can be overloaded; fewer promotion opportunities; less supervision

Activity: tall or flat?

Is each a feature of a tall (hierarchical) or flat structure?

  • Many layers of management
  • Wide spans of control
  • Messages take a long time to pass down
  • Workers are trusted with more decisions
  • Many promotion opportunities
  • Lower management costs

Success criterion 3

Centralised and decentralised

In a centralised organisation, important decisions are made by a small number of senior managers at head office. In a decentralised organisation, decision-making authority is delegated to managers in branches, regions or departments.

TypeAdvantagesDisadvantages
CentralisedConsistent decisions and brand across all branches; experienced senior managers decide; tight control of costsSlow to respond to local needs; branch managers may be demotivated
DecentralisedFaster, local decisions that suit local customers; motivates branch managers; senior managers free to planLess consistency; branches may make poor decisions or compete with each other

Key terms

Organisational chart
A diagram showing the structure of a business and the lines of authority.
Span of control
The number of employees directly responsible to a manager.
Chain of command
The line of authority through which instructions pass from the top to the bottom.
Delegation
Passing authority to a subordinate to carry out a task or decision.
Decentralisation
Passing decision-making authority from head office to managers lower down or in branches.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a growing business should change from a hierarchical to a flat organisational structure. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of both structures.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. removing layers speeds communication and cuts costs; but wider spans of control may overload managers.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: A flat structure would remove layers of management, so messages pass more quickly and decisions are made faster, and the business saves on managers’ salaries. Workers would get more responsibility, which can be motivating. However, managers would have wider spans of control, so they may be overloaded and unable to supervise properly, and there would be fewer promotion opportunities. If the staff are skilled and well trained, a flatter structure is a good choice, but if the business relies on close supervision, it should keep some hierarchy.
