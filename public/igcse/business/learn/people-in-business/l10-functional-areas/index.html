10. The functional areas of a business

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.5 Organisation structure and employees · Lesson 10 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand the roles of human resources, finance, marketing and production, and how they work together.

Syllabus Statement

The different functional areas within a business: human resources – workforce planning, recruitment and selection, training, health and safety, staff welfare, employment issues, industrial relations, disciplinary and grievance procedure, dismissal, unfair dismissal and redundancy; finance – wages/salaries, cash-flow forecasting, budgets and accounting; marketing – market research, product planning, pricing, sales promotion, advertising, customer service, public relations, packaging and distribution; production – manufacturing the product, designing new products, quality control and stock control.

Success criterion 2

Dismissal, unfair dismissal and redundancy

  • Dismissal (being sacked or fired) is when an employee’s contract is ended because of misconduct (e.g. theft, violence, repeated lateness) or incompetence. Businesses should follow a disciplinary procedure: verbal warning, written warning, final warning, then dismissal.
  • Unfair dismissal is ending someone’s employment for a reason that is not legally allowed, e.g. because of pregnancy, race, joining a trade union, or without following the correct procedure. The employee can take the business to an employment tribunal.
  • Redundancy is when the job is no longer needed, e.g. because of falling sales, new technology or a branch closing. It is not the employee’s fault, so they usually receive redundancy pay.
  • A grievance procedure is the formal way for an employee to raise a complaint about their treatment at work.

Activity: dismissal, unfair dismissal or redundancy?

  • An employee is caught stealing from the till
  • A factory replaces 20 workers with robots
  • A worker is sacked for becoming pregnant
  • A branch closes because sales have fallen

  • An employee is sacked for joining a trade union

Success criterion 3

How the departments work together

The functional areas are interdependent. For example, if marketing launches a new product:

  • Production must design and make it in the right quantity and quality;
  • Finance must budget for the launch and check it is affordable;
  • HR may need to recruit and train extra staff.

Poor communication between departments causes problems, such as marketing promising customers a delivery date that production cannot meet.

Key terms

Human resources
The department responsible for managing the workforce.
Dismissal
Ending an employee’s contract because of misconduct or incompetence.
Unfair dismissal
Ending a contract for a reason not allowed by law, or without the correct procedure.
Redundancy
Losing a job because the job is no longer needed.
Grievance procedure
The formal process for an employee to raise a complaint.
Exam-style question

Explain the difference between dismissal and redundancy. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: dismissal is because of the employee’s misconduct or incompetence.
  • 1 mark: redundancy is because the job is no longer needed.
  • 1 mark for development/example: e.g. redundancy is not the employee’s fault, so they usually receive redundancy pay, e.g. when a branch closes.

Model answer: Dismissal is when an employee loses their job because of something they have done, such as theft or poor performance. Redundancy is when the job itself is no longer needed, for example because a branch closes. Redundancy is not the employee’s fault, so they usually receive redundancy pay.
