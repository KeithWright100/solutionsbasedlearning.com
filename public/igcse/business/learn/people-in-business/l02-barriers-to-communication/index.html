2. Barriers to communication

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.1 Internal and external communication · Lesson 2 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand how barriers to communication arise and how they can be removed.

Syllabus Statement

Barriers to communication: how communication barriers arise; problems of ineffective communication and how they can be removed.

Success criteria

Success criterion 1

How barriers arise

A barrier to communication is anything that stops a message being received or understood correctly.

BarrierExample
Language and jargonTechnical words or a different language the receiver does not understand
Noise and distractionsA noisy factory floor, interruptions, too many emails
Wrong mediumDetailed instructions given verbally; urgent news sent by letter
Too many layersLong chains of command distort the message as it passes down
Attitudes and relationshipsThe receiver does not trust or like the sender, or is not interested
Technology failureInternet down, email in the spam folder, poor phone signal
Distance and time zonesStaff in other countries work at different times
Success criterion 2

Problems of ineffective communication

When barriers are not removed, the business suffers:

  • Mistakes and waste: wrong orders are made or delivered, raising costs.
  • Slow decisions: managers do not get information in time.
  • Low motivation: staff feel ignored, so productivity falls and staff turnover rises.
  • Unhappy customers: wrong information leads to complaints and lost sales.

Activity: name the barrier

Choose the main barrier in each situation.

  • An accountant explains “liquidity ratios” to new sales staff
  • Instructions shouted across a busy kitchen
  • A 20-page safety procedure explained in a quick phone call
  • A Thai supplier receives an order written only in German
  • A manager’s email is lost among 200 others
  • Urgent news of a fire sent to staff by post

Success criterion 3

Removing barriers

  • Use simple, clear language and avoid jargon; translate where needed.
  • Choose the right medium for the message (written for detail, face to face for sensitive news).
  • Check for feedback: ask the receiver to confirm or repeat back.
  • Use flatter structures or direct communication to cut layers.
  • Provide training and reliable IT, and schedule calls to suit time zones.

Key terms

Barrier to communication
Anything that stops a message being received or understood correctly.
Jargon
Technical language that may not be understood by everyone.
Chain of command
The route through which instructions pass down an organisation.
Staff turnover
The rate at which employees leave a business.
Noise
Anything that interferes with a message, such as distractions.
Exam-style question

Explain one way a business could reduce barriers to communication with its employees. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a method: e.g. avoid jargon / ask for feedback / use the right medium.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. asking staff to repeat back instructions shows whether they understood.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so mistakes can be corrected before they cost the business money.

Model answer: The business could ask for feedback after giving instructions, for example asking workers to repeat back what they need to do. This shows the manager whether the message has been understood, so any confusion can be cleared up before mistakes are made.
