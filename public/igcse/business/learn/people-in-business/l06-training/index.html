6. Training

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.3 Training · Lesson 6 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand the types of training, their purpose, and their benefits and limitations.

Syllabus Statement

Importance of training to a business and workers: different types of training and the main purpose (induction, on-the-job, off-the-job); compliance with health and safety laws; benefits and limitations of training.

Success criterion 1

Types of training

TypeWhat it involvesBenefits / limitations
InductionIntroduces new employees to the business: the site, colleagues, rules, policies and health and safetyNew staff settle in quickly and become productive sooner
On-the-jobLearning while doing the job, often by watching and copying an experienced workerBenefits: cheap; relevant to the actual job; worker still producing. Limitations: bad habits can be passed on; the trainer’s output falls
Off-the-jobTraining away from the workplace, e.g. college courses, conferences, online coursesBenefits: expert trainers; new skills and qualifications. Limitations: expensive; worker not producing; may leave for a better job

Activity: which type of training?

Classify each example.

  • A new employee is shown the fire exits on their first day
  • A trainee chef watches the head chef prepare dishes
  • A manager attends a two-day leadership course at a college
  • Meeting the team and reading the staff handbook
  • A new cashier is supervised on the till
  • An accountant studies online for a professional qualification

Success criterion 2

Health and safety

Businesses have a legal duty to protect the health and safety of employees and customers. Training helps businesses comply with health and safety laws, for example training in safe lifting, using machinery, food hygiene, fire procedures and wearing protective equipment.

Good health and safety training reduces accidents and absence, avoids fines and legal claims, and protects the business’s reputation.

Success criterion 3

Benefits and limitations of training

ForBenefitsLimitations
BusinessMore productive, skilled and flexible workforce; fewer mistakes and accidents; better customer service; motivated staff stay longerCostly; lost output during training; trained staff may be “poached” by rivals
WorkersNew skills and qualifications; more confidence; better chance of promotion and higher payTime away from home for off-the-job training; extra pressure

Key terms

Induction training
Training that introduces new employees to the business.
On-the-job training
Training while doing the job, usually guided by an experienced worker.
Off-the-job training
Training away from the workplace, such as a college course.
Health and safety
Laws and practices that protect people from harm at work.
Productivity
Output per worker in a given time.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a hotel should use on-the-job or off-the-job training for new receptionists. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of both types.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. on-the-job is cheap and specific to the hotel’s booking system; off-the-job gives expert training in customer service but costs more and staff are away from work.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: On-the-job training would be cheaper, and receptionists would learn the hotel’s own booking system and procedures from experienced staff while still serving guests. However, they could pick up bad habits and the trainer’s work would slow down. Off-the-job training could give expert customer-service skills and qualifications, but it is expensive and the receptionists would be away from the desk. On balance, on-the-job training is most suitable for new receptionists because the skills are specific to the hotel, possibly with a short off-the-job course later.
