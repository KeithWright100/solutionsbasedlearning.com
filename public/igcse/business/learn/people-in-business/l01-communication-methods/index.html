1. Communication in business

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.1 Internal and external communication · Lesson 1 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand why good communication matters and compare the methods businesses use.

Syllabus Statement

Importance of good communication and the problems of ineffective communication: the different communication methods used, including information technology (IT) and the benefits and limitations of each method.

Success criterion 1

Why communication matters

Communication is the passing of information from a sender to a receiver through a medium (method), ideally with feedback to confirm the message was understood.

Good communication means instructions are followed correctly, mistakes and waste are reduced, employees feel involved and motivated, and customers receive accurate information. Ineffective communication can cause errors, delays, low morale, poor decisions and unhappy customers.

Success criterion 2

Internal and external communication

Internal communication takes place between people inside the business, for example a manager emailing a team, or a staff meeting. External communication is between the business and people outside it, such as customers, suppliers, banks and the government.

Activity: internal or external?

Decide whether each is internal or external communication.

  • A manager briefs staff at the start of a shift
  • A shop emails customers about a sale
  • A notice on the staff room board
  • A business sends an order to a supplier
  • A team chat on the company intranet
  • A press release to a newspaper

Success criterion 3

Communication methods and IT

Businesses choose a method depending on the message, the audience, the cost and how quickly a reply is needed.

MethodBenefitsLimitations
Face to face / meetingsImmediate feedback; body language helps understandingTime-consuming; no permanent record
Telephone / video callQuick; can reach people far away; video shows body languageNo written record; depends on technology
Letter / memo / reportPermanent record; good for detail and legal mattersSlow; no immediate feedback
EmailFast, cheap, can send to many people with attachmentsMessages may be ignored or misread; overload
Intranet / messaging appsInstant; good for teams and sharing documentsCan be distracting; security risks
Social media / website (external)Reaches many customers cheaply; two-wayNegative comments are public; must be managed

Key terms

Communication
Passing information from a sender to a receiver.
Internal communication
Communication between people within the business.
External communication
Communication between the business and outside groups.
Medium
The method used to send a message.
Feedback
A response from the receiver showing the message was understood.
Exam-style question

Analyse one benefit to a business of using email to communicate with its employees. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a benefit: e.g. fast / cheap / reaches many people.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. the same message reaches every employee at once.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so everyone has the same information and there is a written record to refer back to.

Model answer: Email is fast and can be sent to every employee at once. This means everyone receives the same information at the same time, and there is a written record they can check later, which reduces mistakes caused by misunderstanding.
