4. The recruitment and selection process

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.2 Recruitment and selection · Lesson 4 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand internal and external recruitment and the stages of selection.

Syllabus Statement

Internal and external recruitment: job advertisement, shortlisting, interviewing.

Success criterion 1

The stages

Recruitment is attracting suitable people to apply for a job. Selection is choosing the best person from the applicants.

  1. Identify a vacancy (someone leaves, the business grows, a new skill is needed).
  2. Write the job description and person specification.
  3. Decide on internal or external recruitment and advertise the job.
  4. Applicants send application forms / CVs.
  5. Shortlist the best applicants.
  6. Interview (and possibly test) the shortlisted candidates.
  7. Select and appoint, then check references and offer a contract.
Success criterion 2

Internal and external recruitment

MethodAdvantagesDisadvantages
Internal (from inside the business)Cheaper and quicker; the person already knows the business; motivates staff through promotionNo new ideas; creates another vacancy; may cause jealousy
External (from outside the business)Wider choice of candidates; brings new skills and ideasMore expensive (adverts, agencies) and slower; candidate needs induction

Internal vacancies are advertised on staff noticeboards, the intranet or by email. External vacancies are advertised on job websites, social media, newspapers, in job centres or through recruitment agencies.

Activity: internal or external?

Is each an advantage of internal or external recruitment?

  • The candidate already knows how the business works
  • Brings in fresh ideas from other companies
  • Cheaper, as no expensive adverts are needed
  • A much wider pool of candidates
  • Promotion opportunities motivate existing staff
  • Can find skills no current employee has

Success criterion 3

Advertising, shortlisting and interviewing

A job advertisement should include the job title, main duties, pay, location, the skills needed and how to apply. It must be placed where suitable candidates will see it.

Shortlisting means selecting the applicants who best match the person specification. Interviews let the business question candidates face to face (or by video), judge their communication skills and personality, and let the candidate ask questions. Interviews may be combined with skills tests, presentations or trial shifts, because some people perform well in interviews but not in the job.

Key terms

Recruitment
The process of attracting people to apply for a job.
Selection
Choosing the most suitable applicant for a job.
Internal recruitment
Filling a vacancy with someone already employed by the business.
External recruitment
Filling a vacancy with someone from outside the business.
Shortlisting
Choosing the best applicants to invite to interview.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a hotel should recruit a new manager internally or externally. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge: advantages/disadvantages of each method.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. an internal candidate knows the hotel’s systems so needs little training; an external candidate may bring ideas from a competitor.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement: e.g. it depends whether a suitable internal candidate exists and whether the hotel needs change.

Model answer: Recruiting internally would be cheaper and quicker, and an existing supervisor already knows the hotel’s staff and systems, so would need little training; promotion would also motivate other staff. However, it creates another vacancy and brings no new ideas. Recruiting externally gives a wider choice and could bring experience from other hotels, but costs more in advertising and the new manager needs time to settle in. If the hotel is performing well and has a strong supervisor, internal recruitment is best; if it needs to change, an external manager would be better.
