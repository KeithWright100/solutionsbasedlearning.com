3. Types of employment and recruitment documents

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.2 Recruitment and selection · Lesson 3 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand the different types of employment and the documents used in recruitment.

Syllabus Statement

Types of employment: full-time, part-time, job share, casual/seasonal/temporary. Recruitment documents: job description, person specification, application form, curriculum vitae (CV)/résumé.

Success criterion 1

Types of employment

TypeWhat it meansBenefits to businessDrawbacks to business
Full-timeUsually 35–40+ hours a weekCommitted staff available all weekHigher wage costs; less flexible
Part-timeFewer hours, e.g. 15–25 a weekCover busy times; lower cost; attracts parents and studentsHarder to communicate; may be less committed
Job shareTwo people share one full-time jobKeeps skilled staff who want fewer hours; two sets of ideasMust communicate well; extra training costs
Casual / seasonal / temporaryWork only when needed, e.g. summer, Christmas, a fixed contractVery flexible; staff only paid when neededLess trained and loyal; quality may suffer
Success criterion 2

Which type suits which job?

Businesses choose the type of employment that matches demand. A hotel in a beach resort may employ seasonal staff in the holiday season, a supermarket uses part-time staff in the evenings and at weekends, and a hospital needs many full-time nurses for shifts. Workers benefit too: part-time and job-share contracts suit people with childcare or study commitments.

Success criterion 3

Recruitment documents

DocumentWho writes itPurpose
Job descriptionWritten by the businessDescribes the job: title, duties, responsibilities, hours, pay, location
Person specificationWritten by the businessDescribes the ideal person: qualifications, skills, experience, qualities (essential and desirable)
Application formDesigned by the business, filled in by the applicantEvery applicant gives the same information, so they are easy to compare
CV / résuméWritten by the applicantA summary of the applicant’s education, qualifications, experience and interests

The job description and person specification are used to write the job advertisement and to shortlist applicants by comparing their applications and CVs against the essential criteria.

Key terms

Part-time employment
Working fewer hours than a full-time employee.
Job share
Two people sharing the duties and hours of one full-time job.
Job description
A document describing the duties and responsibilities of a job.
Person specification
A document describing the qualifications, skills and qualities needed for a job.
Curriculum vitae (CV)
A document written by an applicant summarising their education, skills and experience.
Exam-style question

Explain one reason why a business uses a person specification when recruiting. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a reason: e.g. identifies the qualities and skills needed.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. applicants can be compared against the essential criteria.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so the most suitable candidates are shortlisted, saving time and reducing the risk of a poor appointment.

Model answer: A person specification sets out the qualifications, skills and qualities the ideal candidate needs. The business can compare each application against these essential criteria, so only suitable applicants are shortlisted. This saves time and reduces the risk of employing someone who cannot do the job.
