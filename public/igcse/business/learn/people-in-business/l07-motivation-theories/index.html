7. The importance of motivation and motivation theories

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.4 Motivation and rewards · Lesson 7 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand why motivation matters and the theories of Taylor, Maslow and Herzberg.

Syllabus Statement

The importance of motivation in the workplace: attracting employees, retaining employees, productivity; motivational theories of Herzberg, Maslow and Taylor.

Success criterion 1

Why motivation matters

Motivation is the desire or willingness to work hard and achieve goals. Motivated employees:

  • are easier to attract, because a business known as a good employer receives more applications;
  • are more likely to stay (retention), reducing labour turnover and the costs of recruiting and training replacements;
  • work harder and make fewer mistakes, so productivity and quality rise;
  • have lower absenteeism and give better customer service.
Success criterion 2

Taylor, Maslow and Herzberg

TheoryKey ideasWhat it means for businesses
F.W. Taylor (scientific management)Workers are motivated mainly by money. Break jobs into simple tasks, train workers in the most efficient method and pay them by results (piece rate).Explains bonus and piece-rate systems; ignores social and personal needs
Abraham Maslow (hierarchy of needs)People have five levels of needs: physiological (pay for food and shelter), safety (job security), social (friendship, teamwork), esteem (recognition, status) and self-actualisation (reaching full potential). Once one level is met, the next motivates.Different workers are at different levels, so need different rewards
Frederick Herzberg (two-factor theory)Hygiene factors (pay, working conditions, security, supervision) do not motivate, but cause dissatisfaction if poor. Motivators (achievement, recognition, responsibility, interesting work, promotion) create real motivation.Suggests job enrichment and recognition rather than only pay

Maslow’s needs are often drawn as a pyramid, with physiological needs at the bottom and self-actualisation at the top.

Success criterion 3

Applying the theories

Activity: which theory?

Which theory best supports each idea?

  • Pay factory workers for each item they produce
  • Social events help satisfy workers’ need to belong
  • Poor working conditions cause dissatisfaction but good ones do not motivate
  • A secure contract meets workers’ safety needs
  • Giving workers more responsibility motivates them
  • Break the job into simple, repetitive tasks

Key terms

Motivation
The desire or willingness to work hard to achieve goals.
Labour turnover
The rate at which employees leave a business and need to be replaced.
Hierarchy of needs
Maslow’s five levels of human needs, from physiological to self-actualisation.
Hygiene factors
Herzberg’s factors, such as pay and conditions, that cause dissatisfaction if poor.
Motivators
Herzberg’s factors, such as recognition and responsibility, that motivate workers.
Exam-style question

Using Herzberg’s two-factor theory, explain why increasing pay may not motivate employees in the long term. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for identifying pay as a hygiene factor.
  • 1 mark for explaining that hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction but do not motivate.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. long-term motivation comes from motivators such as recognition, responsibility and achievement.

Model answer: Herzberg says pay is a hygiene factor. A pay rise removes dissatisfaction, but once workers are used to the new level it does not make them work harder. To motivate employees in the long term, the business needs motivators such as giving them more responsibility, recognition for good work and the chance of promotion.
