8. Financial and non-financial methods of motivation

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 2 People in business · 2.4 Motivation and rewards · Lesson 8 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand the financial and non-financial methods businesses use to motivate employees.

Syllabus Statement

How businesses motivate employees: financial methods (remuneration, bonus, commission, promotion, fringe benefits); non-financial methods (job rotation, job enrichment, autonomy).

Success criterion 1

Financial methods

MethodWhat it isEffect
RemunerationThe total payment for work: wages (paid hourly or weekly, often for manual work) or salaries (a fixed annual amount paid monthly)A basic level of pay attracts staff and meets physiological needs
BonusAn extra payment for reaching a target, e.g. at the end of the yearRewards good performance
CommissionA percentage of the value of sales made, common for sales staffEncourages more sales, but can lead to pushy selling
PromotionMoving to a higher position with more pay and responsibilityRewards ability; meets esteem needs
Fringe benefitsNon-cash extras: company car, health insurance, staff discounts, free meals, pensionHelp attract and keep staff

Worked example: commission

A sales assistant earns a basic $1,200 a month plus 5% commission on sales. In March she sells $14,000 of goods.

Commission = 5% × $14,000 = $700. Total pay = $1,200 + $700 = $1,900.

Success criterion 2

Non-financial methods

MethodWhat it isEffect
Job rotationWorkers move between different tasks at the same levelReduces boredom; workers become multi-skilled and can cover absences
Job enrichmentGiving workers more challenging tasks and responsibilityUses workers’ abilities; links to Herzberg’s motivators and Maslow’s esteem needs
AutonomyGiving workers freedom to decide how to do their workShows trust; encourages creativity and ownership

Activity: financial or non-financial?

Classify each method.

  • A $500 bonus for meeting a sales target
  • Letting a team decide its own work schedule
  • Free private health insurance
  • Moving a worker between the till, stockroom and deli
  • Commission of 3% on sales
  • Giving a worker responsibility for training new staff

Success criterion 3

Choosing the right method

The best method depends on the job and the person. Commission suits sales staff whose output is easy to measure; it does not suit a nurse. Job rotation suits repetitive factory work. Highly skilled professionals may value autonomy and enrichment more than small bonuses. Money motivates in the short term, but non-financial methods often have longer-lasting effects (Herzberg). Many businesses combine both.

Key terms

Remuneration
The total payment an employee receives for their work.
Commission
Payment based on a percentage of the value of sales made.
Fringe benefits
Rewards given in addition to pay, such as a company car or discounts.
Job rotation
Moving workers between different tasks at the same level.
Job enrichment
Giving workers more challenging and responsible work.
Exam-style question

A salesperson earns a basic salary of $1,500 a month plus 4% commission on sales. In June she sold $30,000 of goods. Calculate her total pay for June, and explain one disadvantage of paying commission. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: commission = 4% × $30,000 = $1,200.
  • 1 mark: total pay = $1,500 + $1,200 = $2,700.
  • 1 mark for a disadvantage: e.g. staff may pressure customers into buying / income is unpredictable.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. this can damage customer relationships and the firm’s reputation, reducing repeat sales.

Model answer: Commission = 4% × $30,000 = $1,200, so total pay = $1,500 + $1,200 = $2,700. One disadvantage is that salespeople may pressure customers into buying things they do not need, which could lead to complaints and damage the firm’s reputation, reducing repeat business.
