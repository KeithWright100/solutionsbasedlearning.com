4. Primary, secondary and tertiary sectors

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 1 Business activity and influences on business · 1.3 Classification of businesses · Lesson 4 of 10

Learning Intention

To classify business activity into the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors.

Syllabus Statement

Primary, secondary and tertiary activities: primary sector – extracting raw materials from the earth; secondary sector – converting raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods; tertiary sector – provision of a wide variety of services.

Success criterion 1

The three sectors

Every business activity can be placed in one of three sectors.

SectorWhat it doesExamples
PrimaryExtracting raw materials from the earth or seaFarming, fishing, mining, forestry, oil drilling
SecondaryConverting raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods (manufacturing and construction)Car factories, bakeries, steel works, house builders
TertiaryProviding services to consumers and other businessesBanks, shops, hotels, transport, hospitals, schools

Activity: which sector?

Place each business in its sector.

  • A rice farm in Thailand
  • A factory making mobile phones
  • An online bank
  • A copper mine
  • A company that builds houses
  • A hotel chain
  • A delivery company
  • A furniture maker

Success criterion 2

The chain of production

The sectors are interdependent: they rely on each other. A chain of production shows the stages a product passes through. For example, for a wooden table:

  • Primary: trees are cut down by a forestry company.
  • Secondary: the wood is made into a table in a furniture factory.
  • Tertiary: the table is transported, advertised, insured and sold in a shop or online.

If one stage has a problem (for example a storm destroys a forest), businesses in the other sectors are affected too: costs rise and products may be delayed.

Success criterion 3

How the sectors change over time

In less developed economies most people work in the primary sector, especially farming. As countries industrialise, the secondary sector grows as factories open. In developed economies the tertiary sector employs the most people, as incomes rise and people spend more on services such as leisure, health and finance.

The decline of the secondary sector in developed countries is called deindustrialisation. It happens partly because manufacturing moves to countries with lower labour costs.

Key terms

Primary sector
Businesses that extract raw materials from the earth or sea.
Secondary sector
Businesses that manufacture goods or construct buildings from raw materials.
Tertiary sector
Businesses that provide services.
Chain of production
The stages a product passes through from raw material to the consumer.
Deindustrialisation
The decline in the importance of the secondary sector in an economy.
Exam-style question

Explain one reason why the tertiary sector grows as a country becomes more developed. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a reason, e.g. rising incomes.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. people have more disposable income to spend on services such as holidays, restaurants and healthcare.
  • 1 mark for a consequence: e.g. so more businesses and jobs are created in the tertiary sector.

Model answer: As a country develops, incomes rise, so people have more money left after paying for essentials. They spend more on services such as tourism, eating out and financial services, so demand for these services grows and more tertiary businesses open, employing more people.
