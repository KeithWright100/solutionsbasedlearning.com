1. Business aims and objectives

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 1 Business activity and influences on business · 1.1 Business objectives · Lesson 1 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand the financial and non-financial objectives a business can have, and why they change.

Syllabus Statement

Businesses can have several objectives: financial aims and objectives (survival, profit, sales, market share, financial security) and non-financial aims and objectives (social objectives, personal satisfaction, challenge, independence and control). Why business aims and objectives change as businesses evolve: in response to market conditions, technology, performance, legislation, internal reasons.

Success criterion 1

Aims and objectives

An aim is a long-term goal or intention of a business, for example “to become the most popular café in town”. An objective is a shorter-term, measurable target that helps the business achieve its aim, for example “to increase sales by 10% in the next 12 months”.

Good objectives are often described as SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic and Time-bound. Clear objectives give everyone in the business a target, help managers make decisions, and allow the owners to measure success.

Success criterion 2

Financial and non-financial objectives

Objectives can be grouped into two types.

Financial objectiveWhat it means
SurvivalStaying in business, especially in the first year or during a recession.
ProfitMaking revenue greater than costs; the main reward for the owners.
SalesIncreasing the value or number of goods sold.
Market shareIncreasing the business’s percentage of total sales in its market.
Financial securityHaving enough cash and savings to cope with unexpected problems.
Non-financial objectiveWhat it means
Social objectivesBenefiting the community or the environment, e.g. using recycled materials or supporting local charities.
Personal satisfactionThe enjoyment an owner gets from running their own business.
ChallengeThe motivation of setting up and growing something new.
Independence and controlBeing your own boss and making your own decisions.

Activity: financial or non-financial?

Decide whether each is a financial or non-financial objective.

  • Raise profit by 5% this year
  • Being your own boss
  • Reach a 20% share of the local market
  • Using only fair-trade coffee beans
  • Survive the first 12 months of trading
  • The thrill of starting something new

Success criterion 3

Why objectives change

Objectives are not fixed. As a business evolves, its objectives change. A new sole trader may focus on survival; once established, the owner may aim for profit and then growth or market share.

ReasonExample
Market conditionsA new competitor opens nearby, so the objective switches from growth back to survival.
TechnologyNew online ordering lets the business aim for higher sales beyond its local area.
PerformanceA year of high profits allows the business to set a target to open a second branch.
LegislationA new environmental law means the business sets a target to cut packaging waste.
Internal reasonsA new manager or owner has different priorities, e.g. more focus on social objectives.

Key terms

Aim
A long-term goal or intention of a business.
Objective
A short-term, measurable target that helps a business achieve its aim.
Market share
The percentage of total sales in a market made by one business.
Survival
Continuing to trade, rather than closing down.
Social objective
A target to benefit society or the environment rather than to make money.
Exam-style question

Explain one reason why a business may change its objectives as it grows. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for identifying a reason, e.g. improved performance / changes in the market.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. once survival is secure, the owner can focus on growth.
  • 1 mark for further development or context: e.g. so it may set a target to open a new outlet or increase market share.

Model answer: As a business grows its performance improves, so it no longer needs to focus on survival. Once it is making a regular profit, the owner can set a new objective such as increasing market share, for example by opening a second branch in another town.
