10. What makes a business successful?

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 1 Business activity and influences on business · 1.8 What makes a business successful? · Lesson 10 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand how business success is measured and why some businesses fail.

Syllabus Statement

Measuring success in business: revenue, market share, customer satisfaction, profit, growth, owner/shareholder satisfaction, employee satisfaction. Reasons for business failure: cash flow problems/lack of finance, not competitive, failure to adapt to changes in the market.

Success criterion 1

Measuring success

There is no single measure of success. Businesses use several:

MeasureHow it is shown
RevenueThe value of sales: price × quantity sold
Market shareThe business’s sales as a percentage of total market sales
Customer satisfactionMeasured by surveys, reviews, repeat purchases and complaints
ProfitRevenue minus total costs
GrowthIncrease in size: more outlets, employees, sales or market share
Owner/shareholder satisfactionHigh profits, rising dividends and share price
Employee satisfactionLow staff turnover and absence, good feedback from staff surveys
Success criterion 2

Calculating market share and different views of success

Worked example: market share

Market share (%) = (business’s sales ÷ total market sales) × 100

A juice company sells $3 million of juice in a market worth $20 million. Market share = (3 ÷ 20) × 100 = 15%.

Try it

A phone maker’s sales are $45 million in a market worth $180 million. What is its market share?

Show answer

(45 ÷ 180) × 100 = 25%.

Different stakeholders judge success in different ways. Shareholders focus on profit and dividends; employees on pay, job security and working conditions; customers on quality and value; the local community on jobs and the environment. A social enterprise may judge success by its social impact rather than profit.

Success criterion 3

Why businesses fail

Many new businesses close within their first few years. The main reasons are:

ReasonExplanation
Cash flow problems / lack of financeThe business cannot pay suppliers, staff or loans when they are due, even if it is profitable on paper.
Not competitiveRivals offer lower prices, better quality, better service or better locations, so customers go elsewhere.
Failure to adapt to changes in the marketThe business does not respond to new technology or changing tastes, e.g. shops that did not move online.

Activity: why did it fail?

Choose the main reason for each failure.

  • A builder could not pay suppliers while waiting for customers to pay
  • A café closed after a cheaper chain opened next door
  • A video rental shop ignored the growth of streaming
  • A start-up could not get a bank loan to buy stock
  • A camera maker kept making film cameras as phones improved
  • A shop’s prices were much higher than online rivals

Key terms

Revenue
The money received from selling goods or services: price × quantity.
Profit
Revenue minus total costs.
Market share
A business’s sales as a percentage of the total sales in the market.
Stakeholder
A person or group with an interest in a business.
Cash flow
The movement of money into and out of a business.
Exam-style question

A bakery sold $120,000 of bread in a local market worth $800,000. Calculate the bakery’s market share. Then explain one reason, other than market share, why the owner might judge the bakery to be successful. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for the method: (120,000 ÷ 800,000) × 100.
  • 1 mark for the answer: 15%.
  • 1 mark for another measure, e.g. customer satisfaction / profit.
  • 1 mark for development, e.g. lots of repeat customers and good reviews show customers value the product, which supports long-term sales.

Model answer: Market share = (120,000 ÷ 800,000) × 100 = 15%. The owner might also judge success by customer satisfaction: if customers return regularly and leave good reviews, this shows they value the bread, which will help the bakery keep its sales in the future even if competitors open.
