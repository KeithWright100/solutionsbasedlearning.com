8. Government objectives and policies

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 1 Business activity and influences on business · 1.6 Government objectives and policies · Lesson 8 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand how government spending, taxation, legislation, trade policy and interest rates affect business.

Syllabus Statement

Government spending: to provide public service; taxation and constraints on public spending. How governments can affect business activity: infrastructure provision; legislation; trade policy – membership of trading blocs, tariffs. The effect of interest rates on businesses and consumer spending.

Success criterion 1

Government spending and taxation

Governments spend money to provide public services such as education, healthcare, defence, policing and roads. These services also help businesses: educated, healthy workers are more productive.

Most of this spending is paid for by taxation: taxes on incomes (income tax), on spending (VAT or sales tax) and on business profits (corporation tax). Public spending is constrained because governments cannot raise taxes too high without reducing spending and incentives, and borrowing must eventually be repaid with interest. Higher taxes on businesses reduce their profits; higher taxes on consumers reduce the money they have to spend.

Success criterion 2

Infrastructure, legislation and trade policy

Governments affect business activity in several ways.

MethodExamplesEffect on business
Infrastructure provisionRoads, railways, ports, airports, electricity and internet networksLower transport costs and faster deliveries; can open up new markets
LegislationLaws on employment, health and safety, consumer protection, competition and the environmentProtects workers and consumers, but compliance raises business costs
Trade policyMembership of trading blocs; tariffs (taxes on imports); quotasTrading blocs give tariff-free access to members; tariffs protect domestic firms from imports but raise the cost of imported materials

Activity: which type of government action?

Classify each government action.

  • Building a new high-speed railway
  • Introducing a law on food labelling
  • Placing a 20% tariff on imported steel
  • Joining a free trade agreement
  • Rolling out faster broadband to rural areas
  • Raising health and safety standards in factories

Success criterion 3

Interest rates

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing money and the reward for saving. It is usually set by the country’s central bank.

Effect on…Higher interest ratesLower interest rates
BusinessesLoans and overdrafts cost more, so firms invest less in new machinery or expansion; profits fall for firms with large debtsBorrowing is cheaper, so firms are more likely to invest and expand
Consumer spendingLoans and mortgages cost more and saving is more attractive, so consumers spend less, especially on expensive items like cars and furnitureBorrowing is cheaper and saving less attractive, so consumer spending rises

Key terms

Public services
Services provided by the government, such as schools, hospitals and the police.
Infrastructure
The basic facilities a country needs, such as roads, railways and communication networks.
Legislation
Laws passed by the government.
Tariff
A tax on imported goods.
Interest rate
The cost of borrowing money and the reward for saving.
Exam-style question

Analyse the impact of a rise in interest rates on a business that sells new cars. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a direct effect: e.g. borrowing becomes more expensive for consumers.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. many customers buy cars using loans, so fewer can afford to buy.
  • 1 mark for the consequence for the business: e.g. so sales and revenue of the car dealer fall.

Model answer: A rise in interest rates makes borrowing more expensive. Many customers buy new cars with loans, so monthly repayments rise and some customers decide to delay buying. As a result, the car dealer is likely to sell fewer cars and its revenue and profit will fall.
