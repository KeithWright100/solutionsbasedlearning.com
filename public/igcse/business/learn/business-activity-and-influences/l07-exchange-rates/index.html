7. Exchange rates

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 1 Business activity and influences on business · 1.5 Business and the international economy · Lesson 7 of 10

Learning Intention

To calculate exchange rates and analyse how changes affect importers and exporters.

Syllabus Statement

Exchange rate calculation. The impact of exchange rate changes: on international competitiveness; on importers and exporters.

Success criterion 1

Calculating with exchange rates

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another, for example £1 = $1.25. Businesses that trade internationally must convert prices.

Worked example

Exchange rate: £1 = $1.25.

  • A UK firm sells a jacket for £40. Price in the USA = 40 × 1.25 = $50.
  • A US supplier charges $500 for materials. Cost in pounds = 500 ÷ 1.25 = £400.

Rule: to change from the currency on the left of the rate (£) to the right ($), multiply; to go from right to left, divide.

Try it

£1 = €1.15. (a) What is the price in euros of a £200 product? (b) What is the cost in pounds of a €690 component?

Show answers

(a) 200 × 1.15 = €230. (b) 690 ÷ 1.15 = £600.

Success criterion 2

Appreciation, depreciation and competitiveness

When a currency rises in value against another, it appreciates (gets stronger). When it falls, it depreciates (gets weaker).

How an appreciation changes export prices

The pound rises from £1 = $1.25 to £1 = $1.50. A £40 jacket now costs US buyers 40 × 1.50 = $60 instead of $50. UK exports become more expensive, so they are less competitive abroad.

A useful memory aid is SPICED: a Strong Pound means Imports are Cheaper and Exports are Dearer. The opposite happens when a currency depreciates: exports become cheaper and more competitive, but imports become more expensive.

Success criterion 3

The impact on importers and exporters

How a change affects a business depends on whether it mainly sells abroad (exports) or buys from abroad (imports).

BusinessCurrency appreciatesCurrency depreciates
ExporterExports dearer: may lose sales, or must cut its foreign price and lose profit marginExports cheaper: more competitive, sales and revenue may rise
Importer (e.g. buys raw materials)Imports cheaper: costs fall, so profits may rise or prices can be cutImports dearer: costs rise, so profits fall or prices must rise

Activity: good news or bad news?

The Thai baht appreciates. Is this good or bad news for each business?

  • A Thai rice exporter selling to Japan
  • A Thai car dealer importing cars from Germany
  • A Thai hotel that relies on foreign tourists
  • A Thai factory buying components from China
  • A Thai furniture maker selling to the USA
  • A Thai family buying a holiday in Europe

Key terms

Exchange rate
The price of one currency in terms of another.
Appreciation
A rise in the value of a currency against another currency.
Depreciation
A fall in the value of a currency against another currency.
International competitiveness
The ability of a business to sell its products abroad at attractive prices.
Importer
A business that buys goods or services from another country.
Exam-style question

A UK business exports bicycles to the USA for £300 each. The exchange rate changes from £1 = $1.20 to £1 = $1.40. Calculate the change in the US price of one bicycle, and explain one likely effect on the business. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: original US price 300 × 1.20 = $360.
  • 1 mark: new US price 300 × 1.40 = $420, so an increase of $60.
  • 1 mark: the bicycles are now less price competitive in the USA.
  • 1 mark: so US demand / sales and revenue may fall.

Model answer: Before: 300 × 1.20 = $360. After: 300 × 1.40 = $420. The US price rises by $60. Because the bicycles are now more expensive for American customers, they are less competitive against rival brands, so the business is likely to sell fewer bicycles in the USA and its export revenue may fall.
