5. Decisions on location

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 1 Business activity and influences on business · 1.4 Decisions on location · Lesson 5 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand the factors that influence where a business locates or relocates.

Syllabus Statement

The main factors influencing location decisions and relocation of a business: proximity to market, labour, materials and competitors; nature of the business activity; the impact of the internet on location decisions – e-commerce and/or fixed premises; legal controls and trade blocs.

Success criterion 1

Proximity to market, labour, materials and competitors

Choosing the right location can reduce costs and increase sales. Moving to a new site is called relocation.

Being close to…Why it matters
MarketBeing close to customers increases sales and reduces delivery costs. Shops and cafés need lots of passing customers.
LabourBusinesses need workers with the right skills at an affordable wage. Call centres may locate where wages are lower.
MaterialsBusinesses using heavy or bulky raw materials locate near them to cut transport costs, e.g. a sawmill near a forest.
CompetitorsSome businesses locate near competitors to attract the same customers (e.g. restaurants on one street); others avoid them.
Success criterion 2

The nature of the business activity

Different sectors have different location needs.

  • Primary businesses must locate where the natural resources are: mines where the minerals are, farms where the soil and climate are suitable.
  • Secondary businesses (manufacturers) often locate near materials, a large labour force, cheap land or good transport links such as ports and motorways.
  • Tertiary businesses that serve the public, such as shops, hairdressers and banks, usually locate close to their customers in town centres or shopping malls.

Activity: what matters most?

Choose the most important location factor for each business.

  • A coffee shop
  • A steel works using iron ore and coal
  • A software company needing programmers
  • A shoe shop
  • A fish-canning factory
  • A call centre for a bank

Success criterion 3

The internet, legal controls and trade blocs

The internet: businesses that sell through e-commerce do not need expensive premises in town centres. They can locate in cheaper warehouses with good transport links. Some businesses combine both: fixed premises (a shop customers can visit) and an online store (“clicks and bricks”).

Legal controls: governments use planning laws to control where businesses can build, for example keeping factories out of residential areas. Governments may also offer grants or lower taxes to attract businesses to areas with high unemployment.

Trade blocs: a business that locates inside a trade bloc (such as the EU or ASEAN) can sell to all member countries without paying tariffs. This is why many multinationals open factories inside trade blocs.

Key terms

Relocation
Moving a business to a new site.
Proximity
Being close to something, such as customers or materials.
E-commerce
Buying and selling goods and services online.
Planning laws
Government rules about where businesses can build.
Trade bloc
A group of countries that agree to trade with each other with few or no barriers.
Exam-style question

Discuss the factors a supermarket chain should consider when choosing the location of a new store. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of factors: e.g. proximity to market, competitors, planning laws.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. a large local population means more customers and higher revenue; nearby competitors may reduce market share.
  • Up to 2 marks for evaluation: a judgement on which factor is most important, e.g. proximity to the market because supermarkets rely on frequent visits.

Model answer: The supermarket should consider proximity to its market: a site in a busy residential area means many customers can shop there regularly, raising revenue. It should also look at competitors, because if a rival supermarket is already nearby, sales may be split. Planning laws matter, since the local government must allow a large store and car park. Overall, nearness to the market is the most important factor, because a supermarket depends on frequent, local customers, but this must be balanced with the cost of land and the level of competition.
