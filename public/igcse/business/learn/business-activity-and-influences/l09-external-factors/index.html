9. External factors affecting business

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 1 Business activity and influences on business · 1.7 External factors · Lesson 9 of 10

Learning Intention

To analyse how social, technological, environmental and political factors affect business decisions.

Syllabus Statement

The external factors affecting business decisions: social, technological, environmental, political.

Success criterion 1

The four external factors

External factors are things outside the business that it cannot control but that affect its decisions. They can be remembered as STEP.

FactorWhat it includesExample
SocialChanges in society: tastes, fashions, lifestyles, population (e.g. an ageing population), attitudes to healthGrowing demand for vegan food
TechnologicalNew inventions and ways of doing things: the internet, apps, automation, AICustomers expect online ordering and contactless payment
EnvironmentalConcern for the planet: climate change, pollution, waste, sustainabilityCustomers prefer businesses that use recyclable packaging
PoliticalGovernment decisions and political stability: laws, taxes, regulations, trade dealsA new tax on sugary drinks
Success criterion 2

Opportunities and threats

Each external factor can be an opportunity (a chance to increase sales or profit) or a threat (something that could reduce sales or raise costs), depending on the business.

Activity: which STEP factor?

Classify each external change.

  • More people choosing to work from home
  • Development of self-service checkouts
  • Customer pressure to cut plastic packaging
  • A new law raising the minimum age to buy energy drinks
  • An ageing population
  • Growth of social media advertising
  • More frequent flooding caused by climate change
  • An election leading to a change of government

For example, a social trend towards healthy eating is a threat to a fast-food chain but an opportunity for a salad bar.

Success criterion 3

Responding to external change

Businesses that adapt quickly can gain a competitive advantage. Possible responses include:

  • developing new products (e.g. healthier menu options)
  • investing in new technology (e.g. an app for online orders)
  • changing suppliers or materials (e.g. switching to recycled packaging)
  • changing prices or marketing

When evaluating a response, consider the cost of the change, how quickly it can be made, whether customers will value it, and what competitors are doing. A business that fails to adapt may lose customers, as you will see in 1.8.

Key terms

External factor
Something outside a business that it cannot control but that affects its decisions.
STEP
Social, technological, environmental and political factors.
Social factor
A change in society, such as tastes, lifestyles or population.
Sustainability
Meeting today’s needs without damaging the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
Competitive advantage
Something that makes a business better than its rivals in the eyes of customers.
Exam-style question

Discuss how a fast-food business might respond to growing customer concern about healthy eating and the environment. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge: e.g. social and environmental factors; new products, recyclable packaging.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. healthier options attract new customers and protect sales; recycled packaging improves its image.
  • Up to 2 marks for evaluation: a judgement weighing costs against benefits, e.g. healthier products may be more expensive to make but protect long-term sales.

Model answer: The business could add healthier options such as salads and grilled food, which would keep health-conscious customers and attract new ones, protecting sales. It could also switch to recyclable packaging, improving its image with customers who care about the environment. However, these changes raise costs and healthier food may not appeal to all of its existing customers. Overall, responding is important because the trend is long term; if it does nothing, competitors who adapt will take its market share.
