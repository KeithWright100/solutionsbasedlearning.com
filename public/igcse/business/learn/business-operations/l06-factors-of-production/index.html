6. Factors of production

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 5 Business operations · 5.3 Factors of production · Lesson 6 of 7

Learning Intention

To understand the four factors of production and how their relationships change.

Syllabus Statement

Changing relationships between enterprise, capital, land, and labour: difference between capital-intensive and labour-intensive activities.

Activity: which factor?

  • The espresso machine
  • The baristas
  • The plot of land the shop stands on

Success criterion 2

Changing relationships

Businesses combine the factors in different proportions, and the mix changes over time:

  • Technology means capital replaces labour in many tasks (self-service checkouts, robots, online banking).
  • Rising wages encourage firms to invest in capital; cheap labour in some countries encourages labour-intensive methods.
  • Land and natural resources are becoming scarcer and more expensive, encouraging efficient use and recycling.
  • Enterprise drives change: entrepreneurs find new ways to combine the factors, such as app-based businesses that need little land.
Success criterion 3

Capital-intensive and labour-intensive activities

A capital-intensive activity uses a high proportion of capital compared with labour (e.g. an automated car plant, a power station). A labour-intensive activity uses a high proportion of labour (e.g. fruit picking, a hotel, a school).

The choice depends on the cost of labour and capital, the size of output, the type of product (personal services need people) and the availability of finance and skills. Many businesses are becoming more capital intensive over time as technology improves.

Key terms

Factors of production
The resources needed to produce goods and services: land, labour, capital and enterprise.
Land
Natural resources used in production.
Capital
Man-made resources, such as machinery and buildings, used in production.
Enterprise
The skill and risk-taking of the entrepreneur who organises production.
Capital-intensive activity
Production using much more capital than labour.
Exam-style question

Explain one reason why many businesses are becoming more capital intensive. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a reason: e.g. improved technology / rising labour costs.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. machines can do tasks faster and more accurately than people.
  • 1 mark for consequence: e.g. so average costs fall and productivity rises.

Model answer: Technology has improved and become cheaper, so machines can now do many tasks faster and more accurately than workers, such as self-service checkouts. This raises productivity and lowers average costs in the long run, especially as wages rise, so businesses replace some labour with capital.
