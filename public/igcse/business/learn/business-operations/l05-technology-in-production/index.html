5. The impact of technology in production

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 5 Business operations · 5.2 Production · Lesson 5 of 7

Learning Intention

To understand the use of robotics and how businesses balance cost, productivity, quality and flexibility.

Syllabus Statement

Impact of technology in production: use of robotics; balancing cost, productivity, quality and flexibility.

Success criterion 1

Technology and robotics

Technology in production includes robots on production lines, computer-aided design (CAD) to design products on screen, computer-aided manufacture (CAM) where computers control machines, 3D printing, and automated warehouses.

Robots can work 24 hours a day without breaks, carry out dangerous or repetitive tasks (welding, painting, lifting) and produce with great precision.

Success criterion 2

Benefits and drawbacks

BenefitsDrawbacks
Higher productivity: faster, continuous outputHigh initial cost of buying and installing
Consistent, high quality with fewer errorsMaintenance and repair costs; breakdowns stop production
Lower labour costs in the long runJob losses can harm morale and the firm’s reputation
Safer: robots do dangerous jobsStaff need training; technology becomes outdated quickly
Can produce complex designs (CAD/CAM)Less flexible for small, varied orders unless reprogrammed

Activity: benefit or drawback?

Classify each effect of introducing robots.

  • Robots can work through the night
  • A robot breaks down and the line stops
  • Welding is done with perfect precision
  • Workers fear for their jobs
  • Fewer workplace injuries
  • Buying the robots costs $2 million

Success criterion 3

Balancing cost, productivity, quality and flexibility

When choosing production methods and technology, a business must balance four factors that can conflict:

FactorAimPossible trade-off
CostKeeping average costs lowCheaper materials may lower quality
ProductivityMaximising output per worker or machineFaster output can increase defects
QualityMeeting customers’ standards consistentlyExtra checks raise costs and slow output
FlexibilityBeing able to change products and quantities quicklyHighly automated lines are cheap per unit but hard to change

The right balance depends on the market: a luxury watchmaker prioritises quality; a budget mass producer prioritises low cost and productivity; a fashion firm needs flexibility.

Key terms

Robotics
The use of programmable machines to carry out tasks in production.
CAD
Computer-aided design: designing products using computer software.
CAM
Computer-aided manufacture: using computers to control production machinery.
Flexibility
The ability to change products or output levels quickly.
Trade-off
Giving up some of one thing to gain more of another.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a car manufacturer should replace more of its workers with robots. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of robotics.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. robots raise productivity and consistency and cut long-run labour costs; but they are expensive, may break down and job losses hurt morale and reputation.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement balancing cost, productivity, quality and flexibility.

Model answer: Robots would increase productivity because they work continuously, and they weld and paint with great precision, improving quality and reducing long-run labour costs. They also make the factory safer. However, they are very expensive to buy and maintain, breakdowns can stop the line, and replacing workers could lower morale and damage the firm’s reputation. Because car manufacturing is high volume and needs consistent quality, more robots are justified, but the firm should retrain staff for maintenance roles to limit job losses.
