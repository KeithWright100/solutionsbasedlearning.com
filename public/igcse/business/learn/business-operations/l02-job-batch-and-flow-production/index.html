2. Production processes: job, batch and flow

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 5 Business operations · 5.2 Production · Lesson 2 of 7

Learning Intention

To compare job, batch and flow production and their impact.

Syllabus Statement

Production processes: different types – job, batch, flow; the impact of different types of production.

Success criterion 1

Three types of production

MethodWhat it isExamples
JobOne-off, unique products made to the customer’s specificationA wedding dress, a bridge, a custom website, a portrait
BatchA group (batch) of identical products is made together, then the machinery is changed for the next batchA bakery making 200 white loaves then 200 brown; clothing in different sizes
Flow (mass)Continuous production of large quantities of identical products on a production lineCars, soft drinks, mobile phones, chocolate bars
Success criterion 2

Advantages and disadvantages

MethodAdvantagesDisadvantages
JobHigh quality; meets exact customer needs; high prices; motivating, skilled workSlow; high labour costs; no economies of scale
BatchSome variety; some economies of scale; flexibleMachines idle when changed between batches; stock of each batch must be stored; repetitive work
FlowVery low average costs (economies of scale); fast; consistent quality; often automatedVery expensive to set up; little variety; boring for workers; a breakdown stops the whole line

Success criterion 3

The impact of the production method

The method affects a business’s costs, prices, quality, flexibility and workforce. Job production suits small businesses serving individual customers who will pay high prices; flow production suits large firms selling standard products to mass markets at low prices. As a business grows, it often moves from job to batch to flow production.

Key terms

Job production
Making one-off, unique products to a customer’s requirements.
Batch production
Making a group of identical products together before switching to another batch.
Flow production
Continuous production of large numbers of identical products.
Production line
A sequence of workstations through which products move during flow production.
Specification
The exact requirements of a customer for a product.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a furniture maker should change from job production to batch production. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of job and batch production.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. batch production lowers average costs and speeds up output; but it loses the unique, customised products customers may pay a premium for.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: Batch production would let the furniture maker produce groups of identical chairs and tables, so average costs fall through some economies of scale and output rises, allowing lower prices and more sales. However, customers who want unique, made-to-measure furniture may be lost, along with the higher prices they pay, and workers may find batch work less satisfying. If demand for standard designs is growing, batch production makes sense, but the business could keep job production for premium custom orders.
