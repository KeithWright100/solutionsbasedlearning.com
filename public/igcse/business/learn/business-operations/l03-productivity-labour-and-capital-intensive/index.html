3. Labour and capital intensive production and productivity

Success criterion 2

Calculating productivity

Productivity measures how efficiently inputs are turned into output.

Labour productivity = total output ÷ number of workers (in a time period)

Worked example

A factory with 40 workers makes 2,000 chairs a month. Productivity = 2,000 ÷ 40 = 50 chairs per worker per month. After training, 40 workers make 2,400 chairs: productivity = 60, a 20% improvement.

Try it

A call centre has 25 staff who handle 3,000 calls a day. Calculate productivity. If staff numbers rise to 30 and calls rise to 3,300, has productivity improved?

Show answer

3,000 ÷ 25 = 120 calls per worker. 3,300 ÷ 30 = 110 calls per worker, so productivity has fallen.

Success criterion 3

Impact of productivity improvements

Higher productivity means more output from the same inputs, so average costs fall. The business can lower prices to become more competitive, or keep prices and earn more profit; it may also be able to pay higher wages. Productivity can be improved by training, better technology, motivation, better organisation (e.g. lean production) and investment in capital.

However, improvements can be costly (new machines, training) and may lead to job losses if fewer workers are needed.

Key terms

Labour intensive
Production using a high proportion of labour compared with capital.
Capital intensive
Production using a high proportion of machinery compared with labour.
Productivity
Output per worker (or per input) in a given time.
Efficiency
Producing output using as few resources as possible.
Automation
Using machines and computers to carry out tasks instead of people.
Exam-style question

A factory has 50 workers who produce 7,500 units a week. Calculate labour productivity, and explain one benefit to the business of increasing productivity. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: productivity = 7,500 ÷ 50 = 150 units per worker per week.
  • 1 mark for a benefit: e.g. lower average cost per unit.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. so it can cut prices to win customers or increase its profit margin.

Model answer: Labour productivity = 7,500 ÷ 50 = 150 units per worker per week. If productivity increases, each worker produces more, so the cost of each unit falls. The business can then lower its prices to win customers from competitors or keep prices the same and earn a higher profit.
