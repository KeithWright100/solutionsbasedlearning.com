4. Lean production: JIT and Kaizen

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 5 Business operations · 5.2 Production · Lesson 4 of 7

Learning Intention

To understand lean production, just-in-time and Kaizen.

Syllabus Statement

Principles of lean production: just-in-time (JIT); Kaizen; the importance of using resources effectively.

Success criterion 1

Lean production

Lean production aims to reduce waste of all kinds — materials, time, space, energy and effort — while maintaining quality. Using resources effectively lowers costs, improves quality and helps the environment.

Types of waste include: producing too much, holding too much stock, waiting time, unnecessary movement, defects that must be fixed, and over-complicated processes.

Success criterion 2

Just-in-time (JIT)

Just-in-time means stock (raw materials and components) arrives exactly when it is needed for production, and products are made only when ordered. Little or no stock is held.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Less money tied up in stock, improving cash flowRelies on suppliers delivering on time; one late delivery stops production
Less storage space needed, cutting rentCannot meet a sudden rise in demand
Less waste from stock going out of date, damaged or stolenLoses bulk-buying discounts; more frequent deliveries cost more
Encourages close relationships with reliable suppliersVulnerable to strikes, bad weather or transport problems

Activity: advantage or disadvantage of JIT?

Classify each point.

  • Less cash tied up in stock
  • A supplier strike halts production
  • Smaller warehouse needed
  • Cannot cope with a sudden surge in orders
  • Less stock goes out of date
  • Bulk-buying discounts are lost

Success criterion 3

Kaizen

Kaizen is a Japanese term meaning continuous improvement. All employees are encouraged to suggest small, regular improvements to how work is done, often in Kaizen groups (quality circles).

  • Many small changes add up to big gains in productivity and quality.
  • Workers feel valued and involved, raising motivation.
  • Changes are cheap compared with major investment.

However, staff need training and time for meetings, and the gains from small steps eventually become smaller. Some workers may resist change.

Key terms

Lean production
An approach to production that aims to minimise waste of all resources.
Just-in-time (JIT)
A stock-control system where materials arrive exactly when needed, so little stock is held.
Kaizen
Continuous improvement through small, regular changes suggested by all employees.
Waste
Any use of resources that does not add value to the product.
Stock (inventory)
Raw materials, work-in-progress and finished goods held by a business.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a restaurant should use just-in-time stock control. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of JIT.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. fresh food is used before it spoils, reducing waste and freeing cash; but a late delivery means dishes cannot be served.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: JIT would suit a restaurant because fresh ingredients would arrive daily, so less food goes out of date and is wasted, and less cash is tied up in stock or storage space. However, if a supplier is late, the restaurant cannot serve some dishes, upsetting customers, and it would struggle if there was an unexpectedly busy night. Overall JIT is suitable for perishable ingredients if the restaurant has reliable local suppliers, but it should keep a small buffer stock of key items.
