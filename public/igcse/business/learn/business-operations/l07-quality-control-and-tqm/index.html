7. Quality: quality control and TQM

Edexcel IGCSE Business · 5 Business operations · 5.4 Quality · Lesson 7 of 7

Learning Intention

To understand quality, quality control, total quality management and competitive advantage.

Syllabus Statement

The concept of quality and its importance in: the production of goods and the provision of services (quality control, total quality management (TQM)); allowing a business to gain a competitive advantage.

Success criterion 1

What is quality?

Quality means meeting customers’ expectations: the product is fit for purpose, reliable, safe and good value. For goods, quality includes design, durability and reliability. For services, it includes speed, friendliness, accuracy and the overall customer experience.

Poor quality leads to complaints, returns, refunds, waste, lost customers and a damaged reputation.

Success criterion 2

Quality control and TQM

ApproachHow it worksAdvantagesDisadvantages
Quality controlInspectors check products at the end of production (or at stages) and remove faulty itemsFaults are found before reaching customersExpensive inspectors; waste of faulty products; workers may not take responsibility for quality
Total quality management (TQM)Quality is the responsibility of every employee at every stage, aiming for zero defects and continuous improvementFewer defects and less waste; motivated, involved staff; strong reputationNeeds training and commitment from all staff; takes time to introduce

Activity: quality control or TQM?

Classify each statement.

  • An inspector checks every tenth item at the end of the line
  • Every worker checks their own work before passing it on
  • Faulty items are found and thrown away after production
  • The aim is zero defects through continuous improvement
  • A separate department is responsible for quality
  • Quality is part of the whole company culture

Success criterion 3

Quality and competitive advantage

A competitive advantage is something that makes customers choose one business instead of its rivals. High quality can provide this because it:

  • builds a strong reputation and brand loyalty, so customers return and recommend the business;
  • allows premium prices and higher profit margins;
  • reduces costs of waste, returns and complaints;
  • helps the business win contracts and meet legal standards.

In services, quality often depends on staff training and customer service, so businesses use customer feedback, mystery shoppers and service targets.

Key terms

Quality
Meeting or exceeding customers’ expectations.
Quality control
Checking products, usually at the end of production, to find and remove faults.
Total quality management (TQM)
An approach where every employee is responsible for quality at every stage.
Zero defects
The aim of producing products with no faults.
Competitive advantage
Something that makes a business more attractive to customers than its rivals.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether a food manufacturer should introduce total quality management (TQM) instead of quality control. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for knowledge of QC and TQM.
  • Up to 2 marks for analysis: e.g. TQM reduces waste and defects because problems are prevented at every stage; but it needs training and commitment, which costs time and money.
  • Up to 2 marks for a justified judgement.

Model answer: With TQM every worker would check quality at each stage, so problems are prevented rather than found at the end, reducing waste of food ingredients and the risk of unsafe products reaching customers. Staff would also feel more involved and motivated. However, TQM requires training for all workers and a change in culture, which is expensive and takes time, and some staff may resist. Quality control is simpler but more food is wasted. For a food manufacturer, where safety and reputation are vital, TQM is worth the investment, possibly keeping some final inspections as well.
