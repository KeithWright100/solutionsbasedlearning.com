IGCSE Business

Examination Tips & Advice

How to read command words, manage your time, and structure case-study and extended answers so every point is properly rewarded.

Extended answers

Develop your ability to answer 6, 8 and 12 mark exam questions

1. Know your command words

Business questions are almost always built around a case study (a named business and its context). Each part asks you to do something different with that same case — know what each command word wants before you write.

Command wordWhat it wants
Define / State / IdentifyA short, accurate fact or definition — no explanation needed.
CalculateShow your formula and working, not just the final figure — method marks are available.
Explain / DescribeSay what something is or does, and how it applies to the case-study business.
AnalyseA chain of reasoning applied to the business in the case — cause → effect → impact on that business specifically.
Justify / RecommendPick a side or option and support it with reasons directly linked to the business’s situation.
Discuss / Evaluate / To what extentWeigh up more than one option or viewpoint for the business, then reach a justified recommendation.

2. Manage your time by the mark

Work on roughly one minute per mark. Short-answer parts should be answered briefly and precisely; save your time for the final evaluative part of each question, which usually carries the most marks.

  • Read the whole case study carefully before answering — later questions often depend on details given early on.
  • Always show full working for calculations (e.g. profit, break-even, cash flow) — you can earn method marks even with a slip in arithmetic.
  • Don’t spend evaluation-question time repeating facts from earlier, shorter parts of the question.

3. Structuring your answers

Applying to the case study

The single biggest difference between a good and an average Business answer is application. Never write a generic business-studies answer — always name the business, its product, market or the numbers given, and explain how your point applies specifically to that business.

Evaluative (discuss / evaluate / to what extent / recommend) questions

  • Option or viewpoint one — explain a benefit or argument, applied to the case-study business.
  • Option or viewpoint two — explain a drawback or the counter-argument, equally applied to the business.
  • Judgement — recommend a course of action or conclusion, justified by what matters most for this business (e.g. its size, market, finances, objectives).

4. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Writing a textbook-style answer with no reference to the specific business in the case study.
  • Giving one-sided evaluations, or an evaluation with no final recommendation.
  • Skipping the working on calculation questions.
  • Confusing similar terms (e.g. profit vs profitability, cash vs profit, revenue vs profit) — get the definitions exactly right.
  • Running out of time on the final, highest-mark question because too long was spent on earlier short-answer parts.

5. Final checklist before you submit

  • I’ve answered the command word, not just the topic.
  • Every point I’ve made is applied to the named business in the case study.
  • My calculations show full working, not just the final figure.
  • My evaluative answers cover more than one side and end with a clear recommendation.
  • I’ve checked I haven’t missed a question or part-question.
