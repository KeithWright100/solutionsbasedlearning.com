6. Presenting rural fieldwork data

Learning Intention

Success criterion 1

Example: a student’s located divided bar graphs

This student carried out a traffic survey, tallying the number and type of vehicles passing a point in both directions over three minutes at each village. They drew a divided bar graph for each village — the height shows the total number of vehicles and the colours show the type — then placed the graphs on a hand-drawn map to make a located divided bar graph map.

  • Take care: keep the same vertical scale on every bar so they can be compared.
  • Pie charts and divided bars can also show the proportion of high- and low-order services or land uses. To draw a pie chart: percentage × 3.6 = degrees. Limitation: percentages hide the actual numbers.
Success criterion 1

Proportional symbols maps

Example data for practice only — use your own group’s results in the exam.

  • Show data and location together, e.g. the number of services or traffic count in each village.
  • Strength: very visual; easy to see which village is largest and where it is. Limitation: circles can overlap and it is hard to read exact values.
Success criterion 1

Annotated photos and word clouds

  • Annotated photographs — labels describe and explain what the photo shows (e.g. “7-Eleven: low-order convenience service on the main road”). Strength: shows the real place and supports numerical data. Limitation: the photographer chooses what to show, so it can be selective.
  • Word clouds — present qualitative interview data; the size of each word shows how often it was mentioned. Strength: quickly shows common themes. Limitation: loses the context and meaning of what people said.
Success criterion 2

Choose the right technique

Activity: choose the best technique

Which technique best presents each data set?

  • Comparing the proportion of high- and low-order services in three villages side by side
  • Showing the total traffic count at each village on a map of the national park
  • Showing the words shopkeepers used most often in interviews
  • Showing what a typical high-order service looks like in Ban Khao Daeng
  • Showing the land use of all buildings in one village as percentages
Success criterion 3

Evaluating presentation

Exam tip: when asked to “evaluate” a technique, give a strength and a limitation and link both to your actual data.

Key terms

Pie chart
A circle divided into sectors showing percentages.
Divided bar graph
A bar split into sections showing percentages.
Proportional symbols map
A map with symbols sized in proportion to the data.
Word cloud
A picture of words sized by how often they occur.
Annotation
A label that describes and explains.
Exam-style question

Explain why a located bar graph is a suitable technique for presenting rural fieldwork data. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: located bar graphs show data on a map at the location where it was collected.
  • 1 mark: this allows spatial patterns to be seen, e.g. the number of services in each village.
  • 1 mark: easy to compare settlements / identify the relationship with distance or size.

Model answer: A located bar graph places a bar for each site directly on a map, at the place where the data was collected. This shows the spatial pattern clearly, for example how the number of services changes between villages and towns, and makes it easy to compare settlements of different sizes.
