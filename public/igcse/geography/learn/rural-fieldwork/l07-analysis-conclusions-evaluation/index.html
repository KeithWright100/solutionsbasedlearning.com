7. Analysis, conclusions and evaluation

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Fieldwork · Presenting, analysing and evaluating · Lesson 7 of 7

Learning Intention

To present, analyse and evaluate fieldwork data.

Syllabus Statement

Paper 2, Section B fieldwork: investigating rural environments &mdash; the relationship between settlement size and the provision of services.

Success criteria

Success criterion 1

Analysing the data

Describe using GCSE A: a general statement, specific data as evidence, and any anomaly. Then explain it using settlement hierarchy theory.

Example: the student’s traffic data

Describe: Sites 1 (Ban Phu Yai Noi) and 2 (Ban Khao Daeng) had the most vehicles, 15 each in three minutes, almost double Site 3 (Ban Khung Tanot) with 8. Site 1 had the most private cars and songthaews; Site 2 had the most motorbikes and was the only site with buses. Site 3 was the only site with bicycles.

Explain: More traffic suggests more activity and a larger sphere of influence, so more people travel into the larger villages to use their services. Buses at Ban Khao Daeng suggest it is better connected. Bicycles at Ban Khung Tanot may suggest shorter distances between homes and services in a small village. The student found a positive relationship between the village with the highest population (Site 1) and the total number of vehicles.

Possible anomalies

  • A smaller village with more services than expected because it is on the main road or near a tourist attraction (e.g. a beach or cave) — tourists add customers beyond the resident population.
  • Ban Khao Daeng had the same traffic total as Ban Phu Yai Noi — perhaps because it is on a main route used by tourists visiting the national park, not just by residents.
  • A traffic count affected by a bus or school run at the time we counted.
  • Census population figures that are out of date.
Success criterion 2

Conclusions

Activity: does the evidence support the hypothesis?

Decide whether each finding supports or does not support “the larger the settlement, the more services”.

  • The largest village had the greatest number of services
  • High-order services were found only in the largest village
  • Pedestrian counts increased with settlement size
  • A small village on the main road had more shops than a larger one
  • Two villages with very different populations had the same number of services

A good conclusion: (1) answers the enquiry question with a judgement on “to what extent”; (2) uses data from your results and secondary evidence (census populations); (3) explains using theory; (4) recognises other factors such as tourism and accessibility; and (5) notes the limits — only three villages on one day.

Success criterion 3

Evaluation: limitations, solutions and extensions

MethodProblem / limitationSolution
Services tallyDeciding whether a service is high- or low-order is subjective; some shops were closedAgree a classification list before the trip; record closed premises separately
Land use surveyCould not reach every street; buildings with more than one usePlan a route using satellite images; record mixed use as a separate category
Traffic and pedestrian countsOnly a few minutes at one time of day — a snapshot; counts were at different times of day at each village (e.g. lunch breaks), reducing reliability; the same vehicle may be counted twice; only vehicles, not people in them, were countedCount at the same times at every village and on several days; calculate a mean; note number plates or watch one direction each; record occupants too
InterviewsConvenience sample of only 2 people; language barrierInterview more people using stratified sampling; prepare questions in Thai
Secondary dataCensus data may be out of dateUse the most recent figures and note the date
  • Accuracy — how close a measurement is to the true value. Reliability — whether repeating the study would give the same results.
  • Extend the study: visit more villages and a larger town such as Pranburi or Hua Hin; visit on a weekend and in the tourist high season; add a questionnaire asking residents where they travel for different services to map spheres of influence.

Key terms

Anomaly
A result that does not fit the general pattern.
Conclusion
A summary that answers the enquiry question using evidence.
Accuracy
How close a measurement is to the true value.
Reliability
Whether repeating the study would give the same results.
Limitation
A weakness affecting the data or conclusions.
Exam-style question

Explain how the reliability of a rural enquiry could be improved. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each developed improvement (max 4), e.g. survey more settlements to increase the sample size; repeat on different days and times; use the same method/group for each site; larger questionnaire sample with stratified sampling; check results against secondary data.

Model answer: The reliability could be improved by surveying more settlements, because a larger sample makes the pattern clearer and reduces the effect of any unusual villages. We could also repeat the traffic counts and questionnaires on different days and at different times, so the results are not affected by one particular day, and use exactly the same methods at each site.
