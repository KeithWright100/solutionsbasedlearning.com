1. Our rural enquiry: question, location and the enquiry process

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Fieldwork · Planning the enquiry · Lesson 1 of 7

Learning Intention

To plan, organise and carry out fieldwork data collection.

Syllabus Statement

Paper 2, Section B fieldwork: investigating rural environments &mdash; the relationship between settlement size and the provision of services.

The stages of a geographical enquiry: introduction and planning, methods and data collection, data presentation, data analysis, conclusions and evaluation.

Success criterion 1

Our enquiry question

Enquiry question

“To what extent is there a relationship between rural settlement size and services in 3 villages in the Sam Roi Yot National Park, Thailand?”

In the exam you may be asked to state your enquiry question, so learn it word for word. A “to what extent” question means your conclusion must make a judgement about how strong the relationship is.

Success criterion 2

Location of the enquiry

  • We collected data in three small rural settlements in Sam Roi Yot National Park: Ban Khao Daeng, Ban Phu Yai Noi and Ban Khung Tanot.
  • Sam Roi Yot National Park is on the Gulf of Thailand coast in Prachuap Khiri Khan province, approximately 250 km south of Bangkok.
  • The closest major settlement is Hua Hin, approximately 47 km north of the national park.

Why did we choose this location?

  • It has several small rural villages of different sizes close together, so settlement size and services can be compared fairly.
  • The villages are safe and accessible by road and could all be visited in one day.
  • Good secondary data was available (census data, national park and tourism websites).
  • It links with our coastal fieldwork in the same national park, so the trip was efficient.
Success criterion 3

The enquiry process

StageIn our rural enquiry
1. Introduction and planningEnquiry question and hypothesis; settlement hierarchy theory; location; secondary research (census data and websites)
2. Methods and data collectionLand use survey, services tally, pedestrian and traffic counts, interviews, photographs; risk assessment and equipment
3. Data presentationPie charts, divided bar graphs, annotated photos, word clouds, proportional symbols maps
4. Data analysisCompare the villages; describe and explain patterns and anomalies
5. ConclusionsAccept or reject the hypothesis; answer “to what extent”
6. EvaluationLimitations of methods and data; improvements; extensions

Activity: which stage?

Match each task to the enquiry stage.

  • Using census data to find each village’s population
  • Writing a hypothesis based on settlement hierarchy
  • Counting pedestrians for 2 minutes
  • Interviewing a shopkeeper
  • Drawing a proportional symbols map
  • Annotating photographs back in class
  • Deciding whether to accept the hypothesis
  • Suggesting we visit on a weekend as well as a weekday

Key terms

Rural settlement

National park
A protected area of countryside.
Fieldwork
