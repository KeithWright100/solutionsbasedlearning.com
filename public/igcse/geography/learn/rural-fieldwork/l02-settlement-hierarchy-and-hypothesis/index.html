2. Settlement hierarchy: the theory behind our hypothesis

Success criterion 1

Settlement hierarchy

Settlement hierarchy is the relationship between the size of a settlement and the number and type of services it has. It is often shown as a pyramid: conurbations and big cities are at the top and hamlets and isolated dwellings are at the bottom.

  • Threshold population — the minimum number of customers needed for a service to be profitable or worthwhile.
  • Range — the maximum distance people are willing to travel to use a service.
  • Sphere of influence — the area around a settlement that it serves; larger settlements have larger spheres of influence.

Success criterion 3

Our hypothesis and the theory behind it

Each student wrote their own hypothesis. A typical one is:

Example hypothesis

“The larger the population of a village, the greater the number and variety of services it will have, including more high-order services.”

Theory: we would expect the larger the settlement size (the bigger the population), the greater the number and type of services there would be. A larger settlement should also have more high-order and specialist services, because these have a higher threshold population and so need a settlement with a bigger sphere of influence to attract enough customers.

Key terms

Settlement hierarchy
The ranking of settlements by size and the number and type of services.
Threshold population
The minimum number of people needed to support a service.
Range
The maximum distance people will travel to use a service.
Sphere of influence
The area served by a settlement.
Low-order service
A service used often, with a low threshold, e.g. a grocery shop.
High-order service
A service used rarely, with a high threshold, e.g. a hospital.
Hypothesis
A testable statement that can be accepted or rejected using data.
