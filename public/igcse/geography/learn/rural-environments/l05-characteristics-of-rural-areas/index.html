5. Characteristics of rural areas

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Environments · Farming and rural areas · Lesson 5 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand human patterns and processes.

Syllabus Statement

Characteristics of a rural environment: landscape, climate, settlement, population, land use, employment, accessibility, management (development or conservation).

Success criterion 1

Urban, suburban and rural

  • Urban — built-up areas such as towns and cities with high population density and mainly secondary and tertiary jobs.
  • Suburban — residential areas on the edge of a town or city, usually at lower density, from which many people commute.
  • Rural — the countryside: areas with low population density, small settlements (hamlets and villages) and land used mainly for farming, forestry, conservation or recreation.

Between town and country is the rural–urban fringe, where land uses compete (new housing, retail parks, golf courses, farmland).

Success criterion 2

Key characteristics of rural areas

CharacteristicWhat to look for
LandscapeRelief (mountains, plains), natural vegetation, lakes and rivers
ClimateTemperature and rainfall, which affect farming and tourism
SettlementDispersed farms, hamlets and villages; nucleated or linear
PopulationLow density; often ageing in developed countries, young and growing in developing countries
Land useFarming, forestry, mining, tourism, conservation
EmploymentPrimary jobs (farming, forestry, fishing), tourism and, increasingly, home-working and commuting
AccessibilityDistance to towns, roads, public transport and internet access
ManagementDevelopment (new housing, roads, tourism) or conservation (national parks, protected areas)

Key terms

Rural
The countryside, with low population density and small settlements.
Urban
Built-up areas such as towns and cities.
Suburban
Residential areas on the edge of a town or city.
Rural–urban fringe
The zone where town meets countryside.
Accessibility
How easy a place is to reach.
