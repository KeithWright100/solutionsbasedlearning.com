6. How are rural areas changing in developed countries? UK case study (1)

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Environments · Rural change in a developed country (UK) · Lesson 6 of 11

Syllabus Statement

Factors leading to rural changes in a named developed country: rural isolation, decline in farm employment, tourist pressures, suburbanisation, counter-urbanisation, natural hazards and the negative multiplier effect.

Success criterion 1

Key terms

  • Urbanisation — the process whereby a growing percentage of a population lives in towns and cities, and the economy depends increasingly on manufacturing and services.
  • Suburbanisation — the outward spread of the urban area, often at lower densities than older parts of towns and cities.
  • Counter-urbanisation — the movement of people and employment from major cities to smaller towns and rural areas.
Success criterion 2

Different rural areas in the UK

  • Accessible (commuter) countryside — villages close to cities and main roads or railways, e.g. around London, Manchester or Leeds. Populations are growing as commuters move in.
  • Accessible tourist countryside — e.g. the Lake District and Peak District: popular National Parks under pressure from visitors and second homes.
  • Remote rural areas — e.g. upland Cumbria, mid-Wales, the Scottish Highlands: isolated, with declining farm employment, ageing populations and closing services.
Success criterion 3

Suburbanisation: causes and consequences

Causes: economic growth and a growing middle class wanting larger homes, gardens and quieter surroundings; better transport (motorways, car ownership) and the internet making commuting and home-working easier; government policies supporting home ownership; and a housing shortage — the UK population is growing and the number of households has risen by about 30% since 1971. A 2019 National Housing Federation report said England needed 340,000 new homes a year.

Consequences: greenfield land on the rural–urban fringe is built on; more car use and road building; out-of-town shopping centres and business parks; loss of farmland and habitats; longer commutes.

Success criterion 3

Counter-urbanisation: causes and consequences

Key terms

Urbanisation
A growing percentage of people living in towns and cities.
Suburbanisation
The outward spread of urban areas at lower densities.
Counter-urbanisation
Movement of people from cities to smaller towns and rural areas.
Commuter
Someone who travels from home to work in another place.
Second home
A house owned in addition to a main home, often for holidays.
Greenfield site
Land that has not been built on before.
