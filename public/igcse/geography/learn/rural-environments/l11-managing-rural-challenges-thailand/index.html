11. Managing rural challenges in Thailand

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Environments · Rural change in an emerging country (Thailand) · Lesson 11 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand the need for sustainable development when planning the future.

Syllabus Statement

Role of different groups of people (national and local government, IGOs, NGOs and local communities) in managing the social, economic and environmental challenges within the chosen rural environment.

Success criterion 2

Examples of projects

  • Royal Project Foundation (founded in 1969 by King Bhumibol) — helped hill-tribe farmers in northern Thailand replace opium with cash crops such as temperate fruit, vegetables, coffee and flowers, reducing poverty and deforestation and improving access to markets. Read more.
  • Operation Smile Thailand — an NGO that provides free surgery for children born with cleft lips and palates, many from remote rural families who could not otherwise reach or afford treatment. Website.
  • The Tom Yum Project (Bring the Elephant Home) — helps farmers living next to wild elephant habitats switch to crops that elephants do not like to eat, such as the ingredients of tom yum soup (lemongrass, chilli, galangal and kaffir lime), reducing human–elephant conflict and protecting farmers’ incomes. Website.
  • Maekok River Village Resort — a local business near Chiang Rai involved in community projects with nearby villages and schools. Website.
Success criterion 3

Research and presentation task

In groups, research one organisation and make a bold, eye-catching poster. You should:

  • explain the specific economic, social or environmental rural challenge it is working on;
  • identify what type of group it is (government, local, global, NGO, IGO or local community);
  • outline its goals and aims;
  • explain how it manages the problem, with examples of projects;
  • include plenty of pictures.

Be ready to present your research at the end of the lesson.

Key terms

IGO
Intergovernmental organisation whose members are countries, e.g. the UN.
NGO
Non-governmental organisation, e.g. a charity.
OTOP
One Tambon One Product: a Thai scheme promoting village products.
Human–wildlife conflict
When wild animals damage crops or property, or people harm wildlife.
Cooperative
A group of farmers or workers who share resources and profits.
