9. How can we make life in rural areas more sustainable?

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Environments · Rural change in a developed country (UK) · Lesson 9 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand the need for sustainable development when planning the future.

Syllabus Statement

The range of possible strategies aimed at making rural living more sustainable and improving the quality of life (soil and air quality, water supplies, crop yields, health, employment and housing) for the chosen rural environment.

Success criterion 1

What is sustainable rural living?

Sustainable rural living means meeting the needs of people in rural areas today — jobs, housing, services, a healthy environment — without stopping future generations from meeting theirs. It balances social, economic and environmental needs.

Geographers use Egan’s Wheel to assess how sustainable a community or project is. For example, a new leisure centre in a rural area makes it more sustainable if it meets some or most of the criteria; a whole new town should meet most or all of them.

Success criterion 2

Strategies for sustainable rural environments

Activity: social, economic or environmental?

Categorise each UK strategy.

  • Organic farming: organic fertilisers, cover crops and fewer agrochemicals improve soil and river water quality
  • Education and learning: charities run countryside and conservation programmes for schools and adults
  • Developing rural economies: grants and loans help farms diversify into farm shops, B&Bs and events
  • Improving accessibility: better public transport reduces isolation for older people without cars
  • Affordable rural housing: shared-ownership schemes help local people stay in their village
  • Increasing employment: eco-tourism brings visitors and jobs to remote areas
  • Reducing traffic congestion: park-and-ride and cycling schemes at honeypot sites
  • Ecosystem preservation: restoring hedgerows, woodland and peat bogs

Some strategies fit more than one category — e.g. eco-tourism is economic but can also protect the environment. Explain your choice.

Success criterion 3

Organic farming as a sustainable strategy

What organic farmers doBenefits
No agrochemicals or synthetic fertilisers; organic fertilisers (manure, compost)Cleaner rivers and lakes; less harmful run-off
Crop rotation and cover cropsReduced soil loss; more soil organic matter; better water-holding capacity
No GMOs; use of local resourcesLower energy and water use; fewer non-renewable inputs
Hedgerows and wildflower marginsGreater plant, animal and microbial biodiversity

But yields are usually lower and food costs more, so organic farming alone cannot feed everyone. Organic land is only a small share of UK farmland.

UK NGOs and rural sustainability

NGOAims and strategies
The Prince’s Countryside FundSupports family farms and rural communities with grants, business training and emergency help
ACRE (Action with Communities in Rural England)Helps communities keep village halls, plan affordable housing and improve services
National TrustProtects countryside, coastline and historic places; runs farms and visitor sites
CPRE (the countryside charity)Campaigns to protect green belt and countryside and for affordable rural housing on brownfield land
The Wildlife TrustsManage nature reserves, restore hedgerows, wetlands and peat, and run education programmes

Research task: choose one NGO and produce a slide that gives a brief overview of its aims; outlines one rural problem it tackles; explains its strategy; evaluates the strategy’s success; and includes facts and statistics. Be ready to share your findings with the class.

Key terms

Sustainable rural living
Meeting rural people’s needs today without harming future generations.
Egan’s Wheel
A model of eight criteria for sustainable communities.
NGO
Non-governmental organisation, e.g. a charity.
Eco-tourism
Tourism that protects the environment and benefits local people.
Affordable housing
Homes that local people on average incomes can afford to rent or buy.
