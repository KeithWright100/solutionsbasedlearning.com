3. Exploitation of the Amazon rainforest

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Environments · Ecosystems and biomes · Lesson 3 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand the complex relationship between people and the environment.

Syllabus Statement

Rural environments are natural ecosystems that are exploited by human activities.

Success criterion 1

Where is the Amazon?

The Amazon rainforest is in northern South America, in the basin of the Amazon River, mostly between about 5°N and 15°S. It covers around 5.5 million km² across nine countries; about 60% is in Brazil, with the rest in Peru, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Bolivia, Guyana, Suriname and French Guiana. It is the largest tropical rainforest in the world.

Success criterion 2

How is the Amazon being exploited?

  • Cattle ranching — the biggest cause of deforestation; ranching earns Brazil over US$6.9 billion a year.
  • Commercial farming — e.g. soya beans; Brazil is one of the world’s largest soya producers.
  • Logging — valuable hardwoods such as mahogany; much logging is illegal.
  • Small-scale and subsistence farming by settlers.
  • Road building — e.g. the Trans-Amazonian Highway opens up the forest to further development.
  • Mining — e.g. the Carajás complex, the world’s largest iron ore mine.
  • Hydroelectric power — e.g. the Belo Monte dam complex, which flooded large areas of forest.

Changing rates: deforestation in the Brazilian Amazon fell from about 13,000 km² in 2021 to about 5,800 km² in 2025, the fourth year of decline in a row (INPE PRODES), but large areas are still cleared every year.

Success criterion 3

Impacts of exploitation

Activity: social, economic or environmental?

Classify each impact of exploiting the Amazon.

  • Ranching and soya farming earn Brazil billions of dollars in exports
  • Mining provides jobs, e.g. at the Carajás complex
  • Illegal logging means governments lose tax income
  • Indigenous people lose their traditional farming and hunting lands
  • Conflict and violence between land-grabbers and local communities
  • New roads bring settlers, services and diseases to remote areas
  • Burning trees releases carbon dioxide, adding to global warming
  • Loss of habitats reduces biodiversity
  • Heavy rain on bare land causes soil erosion; mining pollutes rivers

SocialEconomicEnvironmental
Indigenous communities lose land, culture and livelihoods; conflict over land; new settlements and roads bring jobs but also diseaseFarming, mining, logging and HEP earn export income and create jobs; but illegal logging loses tax revenue and soils soon become infertileThe Amazon stores huge amounts of carbon: deforestation releases CO₂; about 80% of land animals live in tropical rainforests, so biodiversity is lost; soil erosion; mining pollutes rivers; changes to the water cycle and rainfall

Managing the rainforest sustainably includes forest reserves, selective logging, replanting, international agreements (e.g. Forest Stewardship Council timber), company policies, education and debt reduction in return for conservation.

Key terms

Deforestation
The cutting down and removal of forest.
Exploitation
Using natural resources for economic gain.
Indigenous people
The original inhabitants of an area.
Selective logging
Cutting only certain trees and leaving the rest standing.
Sustainability
Meeting the needs of today without compromising the needs of tomorrow.
Exam-style question

Explain two impacts of the exploitation of the Amazon rainforest. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each impact identified (max 2), e.g. loss of biodiversity; increased carbon emissions/climate change; soil erosion; displacement of indigenous people; economic growth and jobs.
  • 1 mark for each explanation/development.

Model answer: One impact is the loss of biodiversity: clearing forest for cattle ranching and soya destroys habitats, so many plant and animal species are threatened. Another is climate change: burning and cutting trees releases stored carbon dioxide and reduces the forest’s ability to absorb carbon. However, the activities also bring jobs and export income to Brazil.
