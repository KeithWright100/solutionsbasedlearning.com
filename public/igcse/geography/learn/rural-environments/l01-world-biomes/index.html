1. The world’s biomes

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Environments · Ecosystems and biomes · Lesson 1 of 11

Learning Intention

Syllabus Statement

Distribution and characteristics of the world’s biomes (tropical, temperate and boreal forests, tropical and temperate grasslands, deserts and tundra).

Success criterion 1

Key terms

  • Biosphere — the parts of the Earth occupied by living organisms (land, water and air).
  • Biome — a plant and animal community covering a large area of the Earth’s surface, e.g. tropical rainforest or desert.
  • Ecosystem — a community of plants and animals that interact with each other and their physical environment. Ecosystems can be any size, from a pond to a whole forest.
  • Biodiversity — the number and variety of living species found in a given area or ecosystem.

Success criterion 2

Where are the biomes?

Biomes are found in broad bands that roughly follow lines of latitude, because latitude controls how much energy from the Sun an area receives. Tropical rainforests lie close to the Equator (e.g. the Amazon, Congo basin, South-East Asia); hot deserts are found around the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn (e.g. Sahara, Arabian, Australian deserts); temperate deciduous forests are found in western Europe and the eastern USA; boreal forests (taiga) stretch across Canada, Scandinavia and Russia; and tundra lies around the Arctic Circle in northern Canada, Alaska and Siberia.

Other factors change the pattern locally: altitude (mountains are colder), distance from the sea (continental interiors are drier) and ocean currents and prevailing winds.

Key terms

Biome
A large-scale plant and animal community, e.g. tropical rainforest.
Biosphere
The parts of the Earth occupied by living organisms.
Ecosystem
A community of living things interacting with each other and their environment.
Biodiversity
The number and variety of species in an area.
Permafrost
Ground that stays frozen all year.
Deciduous
Trees that lose their leaves in winter.
Coniferous
Evergreen trees with needles and cones.
Exam-style question

Describe the climate and vegetation of the tropical rainforest biome. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for climate, e.g. hot all year (26–28°C); very high rainfall (over 2,000 mm); high humidity; little seasonal variation.
  • Up to 2 marks for vegetation, e.g. dense, layered forest (emergent, canopy, understorey, shrub layer); very high biodiversity; buttress roots and drip-tip leaves.

Model answer: The tropical rainforest has a hot, wet climate with temperatures of about 27°C all year and over 2,000 mm of rain, so there are no real seasons. The vegetation is dense and arranged in layers, from tall emergent trees to the canopy and understorey, and it has very high biodiversity, with plants adapted to the heavy rain, such as drip-tip leaves.
