2. What is an ecosystem? Why are ecosystems so valuable?

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Environments · Ecosystems and biomes · Lesson 2 of 11

Learning Intention

To explore the relationships between human activity and the environment.

Success criterion 1

Ecosystems as open systems

An ecosystem is an open system: energy and matter enter and leave it.

  • Inputs — solar radiation (energy), rainfall, gases, minerals from weathered rocks, soil nutrients.
  • Flows and transfers — plants (producers) use sunlight for photosynthesis; energy passes along food chains to consumers; decomposers break down dead material and return nutrients to the soil (the nutrient cycle); water moves through the water cycle.
  • Outputs — heat, water (run-off and evaporation), gases such as CO₂ and oxygen, and eroded soil.
Success criterion 2

Goods and services

  • Goods are material things or products that can be taken directly from an ecosystem and used, e.g. timber, food, fuelwood, medicines.
  • Services are the long-term benefits people gain from ecosystems. They are often grouped into four types:
    • Provisioning — supplying basic resources such as food and water.
    • Regulating — e.g. controlling the climate through the carbon cycle, or protecting coasts from storms.
    • Supporting — e.g. the nutrient cycle and the water cycle, and soil formation.
    • Cultural — recreational, spiritual and educational benefits such as tourism.

Activity: good or service?

Decide whether each benefit is a good or a service.

  • Timber for building houses
  • Fuelwood for cooking
  • Nuts and fruit collected from the forest
  • Plants used to make medicines
  • Absorbing carbon dioxide and regulating the climate
  • Recycling water through the water cycle
  • Tourists hiking and photographing wildlife
  • Mangroves protecting the coast from storm surges

Key terms

Open system
A system with inputs and outputs of energy and matter.
Goods
Material products taken directly from an ecosystem.
Services
Long-term benefits people gain from ecosystems.
Nutrient cycle
The movement of nutrients between soil, plants, animals and decomposers.
Decomposers
Organisms such as fungi and bacteria that break down dead material.
Exam-style question

Explain the difference between the goods and services provided by ecosystems, using examples. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: goods are physical products taken from an ecosystem.
  • 1 mark: example of a good, e.g. timber, food, medicines, fuelwood.
  • 1 mark: services are the benefits/functions that ecosystems provide.
  • 1 mark: example of a service, e.g. carbon storage, regulating the water cycle, flood protection, pollination, recreation.

Model answer: Goods are physical products that people take from ecosystems, such as timber, fish or medicines from the rainforest. Services are the useful functions that ecosystems carry out, such as storing carbon, regulating the water cycle, protecting coasts from flooding or providing places for recreation.
