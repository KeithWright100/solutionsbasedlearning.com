8. Diversification of farming

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Environments · Rural change in a developed country (UK) · Lesson 8 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand human patterns and processes.

Syllabus Statement

The diversification of farming to generate new income streams: GM crops, specialist crops and food, organic farming, recreation and leisure.

Success criterion 1

How has farming changed in developed countries?

Farming in the UK today is very different from 200 years ago, when it was a major part of the economy. Agriculture now accounts for only about 1% of the UK’s GDP and 1% of employment, and the UK grows only around 60% of the food it eats; the rest is imported.

  • Output has risen thanks to technology, mechanisation and specialisation, which have made farms far more efficient.
  • Farms have become fewer and larger, and many are run as businesses by agribusinesses.
  • Consumers and supermarkets expect food to be cheap and available all year, so supermarkets import food or buy from large agribusinesses rather than small traditional farms.
  • As a result, small traditional farms often find it hard to make a profit from crops and livestock alone.
Success criterion 2

What is farm diversification?

Farm diversification means a farm developing new sources of income alongside, or instead of, traditional farming, to become more profitable and sustainable. Common types:

  • Specialist crops and food — lavender, cut flowers, vineyards, cheese, ice cream, craft beer; selling through farm shops and farmers’ markets.
  • Organic farming — no synthetic fertilisers or pesticides, earning a higher price.
  • Recreation and leisure — holiday cottages, glamping and campsites, B&Bs, petting farms, wedding venues, fishing lakes, mazes and pick-your-own.
  • Energy — renting land for wind turbines or solar panels; biogas.
  • GM (genetically modified) crops — crops whose genes are changed for higher yields or pest resistance. GM crops are not grown commercially in the UK, but England’s Precision Breeding Act (2023) now allows gene-edited crops to be developed.

Key terms

Farm diversification
Developing new sources of income on a farm.
Agribusiness
Large-scale, commercial farming run like a big business.
Organic farming
Farming without synthetic fertilisers or pesticides.
GM crops
Crops whose genetic material has been altered.
Food miles
The distance food travels from producer to consumer.
Exam-style question

Explain why farmers in the UK may diversify. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: falling incomes from traditional farming (low prices, competition from imports, reduced subsidies).
  • 1 mark: diversification provides another source of income, e.g. farm shops, holiday cottages, campsites, renewable energy.
  • 1 mark: development, e.g. spreads risk so income is less affected by bad weather or low prices.

Model answer: Many UK farmers diversify because the income from traditional farming has fallen due to low prices, cheap imports and changes to subsidies. Using the land for other activities, such as holiday cottages, farm shops or wind turbines, provides an extra source of income and spreads the risk, so the farm is less affected by a bad harvest.
