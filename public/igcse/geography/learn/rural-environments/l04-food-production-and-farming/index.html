4. Food production and farming

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Rural Environments · Farming and rural areas · Lesson 4 of 11

Learning Intention

To understand human patterns and processes.

Syllabus Statement

How humans use, modify and change ecosystems and rural environments to obtain food through farming systems (arable/pastoral, commercial/subsistence, intensive and extensive).

Success criterion 3

Why farming differs from place to place, and how it changes ecosystems

Activity: human or physical factor?

Classify each factor that influences the type of farming.

  • Temperature — crops need a minimum temperature to grow
  • Precipitation — total rainfall and when it falls
  • Growing season — barley needs about 90 days, rice about 120
  • Relief and slope aspect — altitude and steepness
  • Soil type and fertility
  • Drainage — waterlogged land is poor for crops
  • Tradition — farms grow what they always have
  • Capital — money to invest in machinery and fertiliser
  • Transport costs to market
  • Government subsidies
  • Farm size and economies of scale
  • Market demand for products

To grow food, people modify ecosystems: forests and grasslands are cleared; wetlands are drained; hillsides are terraced; land is irrigated; hedgerows are removed to make bigger fields; fertilisers, pesticides and machinery are added; and new crop varieties and greenhouses extend the growing season. These changes raise yields but can cause soil erosion, water pollution and loss of biodiversity.

Exam-style question

Explain how a farm can be represented as a system. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: inputs, e.g. seeds, labour, fertiliser, water, machinery, sunlight.
  • 1 mark: processes, e.g. ploughing, sowing, irrigation, harvesting.
  • 1 mark: outputs, e.g. crops, livestock, milk, profit, waste; outputs may be sold or fed back as inputs (feedback).

Model answer: A farm can be seen as a system with inputs, processes and outputs. Inputs include seeds, fertiliser, water, labour and machinery. Processes are the activities carried out on the farm, such as ploughing, sowing and harvesting. Outputs are the products, such as crops, milk and meat, and the profit from them, some of which is reinvested in the farm.
