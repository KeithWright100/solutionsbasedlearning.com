Success criterion 2

What is a coral reef?

A coral reef is an underwater ridge built from the calcium carbonate skeletons of millions of tiny animals called coral polyps. Each polyp lives in partnership (symbiosis) with microscopic algae called zooxanthellae, which live inside its tissue. The algae photosynthesise and give the coral food and its colour; the coral gives the algae shelter and nutrients.

Reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor but support about a quarter of all marine species.

Success criterion 3

Global distribution

Coral reefs are found in a band roughly 30°N to 30°S. Major areas include the Coral Triangle (Indonesia, the Philippines, Papua New Guinea), the Great Barrier Reef off north-east Australia, the Caribbean, the Red Sea and the Indian Ocean (e.g. the Maldives and Sri Lanka). They are mostly found on the eastern sides of continents, where warm ocean currents flow; cold currents on western coasts keep the water too cool.

Success criterion 4

Reasons for the distribution

  • Temperature — water between about 18°C and 29°C; warmer water causes bleaching.
  • Light — clear water less than about 50 m deep, so sunlight reaches the algae for photosynthesis.
  • Salinity — salty sea water; reefs are absent near large river mouths, which bring fresh water.
  • Sediment — clear water with little sediment, which would block light and smother the coral.
  • Wave action — moderate waves bring oxygen and food, but very strong waves can break coral.
Success criterion 5

Types of reef

  • Fringing reef — grows close to the shore, separated by a narrow, shallow lagoon or none at all. The most common type, e.g. many reefs around Sri Lanka and the Red Sea.
  • Barrier reef — runs parallel to the coast but further offshore, separated from the land by a deep, wide lagoon, e.g. the Great Barrier Reef.
  • Atoll — a ring of coral around a central lagoon, formed when a volcanic island sinks while the reef keeps growing upwards, e.g. many islands of the Maldives.
Success criterion 6

Abiotic and biotic characteristics: the Great Barrier Reef

Abiotic means the non-living parts of an ecosystem; biotic means the living parts.

Abiotic

  • Warm water, roughly 23–29°C
  • Clear, shallow water with high light levels
  • Salty water of the Coral Sea
  • Low nutrient levels in the water
  • Wave energy and currents bringing oxygen and food

Biotic

  • Hundreds of species of hard and soft coral
  • Zooxanthellae algae living inside the polyps
  • About 1,500 species of fish, plus turtles, sharks, rays and dugongs
  • Food web: plankton → small fish → larger fish → sharks
  • Seagrass meadows and mangroves nearby

The Great Barrier Reef stretches about 2,300 km along the coast of Queensland and is made up of around 2,900 individual reefs. It has been a World Heritage Site since 1981.

Success criterion 7

The value of coral reefs

  • Biodiversity — one of the most species-rich ecosystems on Earth.
  • Fishing and food — reef fish feed millions of people.
  • Tourism — diving and snorkelling bring income and jobs (e.g. Queensland, the Maldives, Sri Lanka).
  • Coastal protection — reefs break up waves and reduce erosion and flooding.
  • Medicine and science — reef organisms are a source of new medicines.

Key terms

Coral polyp
A tiny animal that builds a calcium carbonate skeleton.
Zooxanthellae
Algae living inside coral that provide food by photosynthesis.
Abiotic
The non-living parts of an ecosystem, e.g. temperature and light.
Biotic
The living parts of an ecosystem, e.g. plants and animals.
Atoll
A ring-shaped coral reef around a lagoon.
Exam-style question

Describe the conditions needed for coral reefs to grow. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each condition (max 3): warm water of 23–29°C; clear water so sunlight reaches the coral; shallow water (less than about 50 m deep); salt water with normal salinity; low sediment levels; well-oxygenated water with wave action.

Model answer: Coral reefs need warm sea water between about 23°C and 29°C. The water must be clear and shallow, usually less than 50 m deep, so that sunlight can reach the algae that live in the coral and photosynthesise. Reefs also need salty, well-oxygenated water with little sediment, so they do not grow near river mouths.
