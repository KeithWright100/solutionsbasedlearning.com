10. Threats to coastal ecosystems

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Coastal Environments · 2.2 Coastal ecosystems · Lesson 10 of 15

Learning Intention

To describe and explain physical patterns and processes

Syllabus Statement

Distinctive ecosystems develop along stretches of coastline

How small- and large-scale coastal ecosystems are threatened by people and their activities (industrialisation, agricultural practices, tourism and deforestation).

Success criterion 1

Retrieval: conditions for the four ecosystems

Complete this from memory, then check Lesson 8:

EcosystemKey conditions
Coral reefsWarm (18–29°C), clear, shallow, salty water
MangrovesWarm, sheltered, muddy, tidal coasts
Sand dunesSand supply, wide beach, onshore wind, pioneer plants
Salt marshesSheltered, calm water, fine mud, regular tides

Any human activity that changes these conditions — temperature, light, sediment, salinity or shelter — threatens the ecosystem.

Success criterion 2

Threats from people and their activities

ActivityThreatsExamples
IndustrialisationPorts, factories and oil terminals destroy habitats; chemical and oil pollution; dredging stirs up sediment that blocks light; warm water from power stations.Port expansion and dredging near the Great Barrier Reef; oil spills.
Agricultural practicesFertiliser run-off causes algal blooms that block light; soil erosion adds sediment; clearing mangroves for shrimp farms and rice.Farm run-off into the Great Barrier Reef lagoon; shrimp farms in South-East Asia.
TourismTrampling of dunes; divers and boat anchors damaging coral; hotels built on dunes and mangroves; sewage and litter.Dune erosion on popular UK beaches; reef damage in Thailand and the Maldives.
DeforestationMangroves cut for timber, fuelwood and land; inland deforestation increases river sediment reaching reefs.Mangrove loss in the Sundarbans and Indonesia.

Threats operate at different scales: small-scale (e.g. trampling on one dune system) and large-scale (e.g. climate change causing mass coral bleaching across the whole Great Barrier Reef, as in 2016, 2017 and 2020).

Success criterion 3

Exam technique: the 8-mark 'Analyse' question

Analyse means: break something down into its parts, explain how they link together, and show which are most important.

  • Point — identify a threat (e.g. agricultural run-off).
  • Evidence — use specific facts from a named ecosystem.
  • Explain — show how it damages the ecosystem (use “this leads to…”, “as a result…”).
  • Link — connect threats together (e.g. deforestation increases sediment, which adds to pollution).
  • Judge — finish with which threat is most significant and why.

Aim for two or three well-developed points with named examples. The past paper question for this lesson will be added here.

Key terms

Industrialisation
The growth of industry, such as ports, factories and power stations.
Algal bloom
A rapid growth of algae caused by nutrient pollution, which blocks light.
Coral bleaching
When stressed coral expels its algae, turns white and may die.
Deforestation
The removal of trees, such as mangrove forests.
Analyse
Break down into parts, explain the links and judge their importance.
