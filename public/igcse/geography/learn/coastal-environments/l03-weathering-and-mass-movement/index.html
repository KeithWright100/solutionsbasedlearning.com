3. Weathering and mass movement

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Coastal Environments · 2.1 Coastal processes and landforms · Lesson 3 of 15

Syllabus Statement

Physical processes and human intervention give rise to characteristic coastal landforms:

Weathering (mechanical, chemical and biological) and mass movement (sliding and slumping)

Success criterion 1

Weathering and mass movement: definitions

  • Weathering is the breakdown of rock in situ (where it lies) by the weather, plants and animals, or chemical reactions. Unlike erosion, the rock is not moved.
  • Mass movement is the downhill movement of rock and soil under the influence of gravity.

Both are sub-aerial processes — they act on the cliff face above the sea. They weaken cliffs and supply loose material, which the waves then remove.

Success criterion 2

Mechanical, chemical and biological weathering

TypeProcessHow it works
Mechanical (physical)Freeze–thawRainwater enters cracks, freezes and expands by about 9%, putting pressure on the rock. When it thaws the water seeps deeper. Repeated many times, pieces of rock break off.
MechanicalSalt weatheringSea spray evaporates in cracks, leaving salt crystals that grow and force the rock apart.
ChemicalCarbonation / solutionRainwater absorbs carbon dioxide and becomes a weak carbonic acid, which slowly dissolves rocks such as chalk and limestone.
BiologicalRoots and animalsPlant roots grow into cracks and widen them; burrowing animals such as rabbits and birds loosen rock and soil.
Success criterion 3

Sliding and slumping

  • Sliding (landslide) — a large block of rock or soil slides downhill along a flat slip plane, often a layer of weaker rock or a crack running parallel to the slope.
  • Slumping (rotational slip) — common on cliffs made of soft rock such as clay. Heavy rain soaks into the clay, making it heavier and reducing friction. The saturated material slips down a curved slip surface, often leaving a series of steps on the cliff.

Undercutting by waves at the cliff base makes both types of mass movement more likely. The waves then remove the fallen debris, so the cliff stays steep and the cycle repeats.

Key terms

Weathering
The breakdown of rock in situ by the weather, plants and animals, or chemical reactions.
Mass movement
The downhill movement of material under gravity.
Freeze–thaw
Mechanical weathering caused by water freezing and expanding in cracks.
Slumping
A rotational slip of saturated soft rock down a curved surface.
Sliding
Movement of material along a flat slip plane.
