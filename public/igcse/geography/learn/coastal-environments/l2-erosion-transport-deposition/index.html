Coastal erosion, transportation and deposition

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Paper 1: Physical Geography · Coastal Environments · Lesson 2 of 6

SpecificationPAPER 1 · SECTION A
Skill FocusDescribe and explain processes
Reading Time30–40 minutes

In this lesson you will learn to…

  • Explain the four processes of coastal erosion.
  • Describe how sediment is transported, including longshore drift.
  • Explain why deposition happens.

Processes of erosion

Waves erode the coast in four main ways. You need to be able to name and explain each one.

ProcessWhat happens
Hydraulic actionThe force of waves hitting the cliff compresses air in cracks. When the wave retreats the air expands explosively, widening the crack.
Abrasion (corrasion)Waves throw sand and pebbles at the cliff, scraping and wearing it away like sandpaper.
AttritionRocks and pebbles carried by the waves knock into each other, becoming smaller and more rounded.
Solution (corrosion)Slightly acidic sea water dissolves soluble rocks such as chalk and limestone.

The rate of erosion depends on rock type (soft clay erodes far faster than hard granite), wave energy, the amount of beach protecting the cliff, and human activity.

Transportation and longshore drift

Material is moved by the sea in four ways: traction (large boulders rolled along the sea bed), saltation (pebbles bounced along), suspension (fine sand and silt carried in the water) and solution (dissolved minerals).

Waves usually approach the coast at an angle, driven by the prevailing wind. This creates longshore drift:

  1. The swash carries sediment up the beach at the same angle as the waves.
  2. The backwash drags it straight back down the beach, at right angles to the coast, because of gravity.
  3. This repeats, moving sediment along the beach in a zig-zag.

Longshore drift is important because it feeds sediment to beaches and spits further along the coast — and because groynes are built to trap it.

Deposition

Deposition is when the sea drops the material it is carrying. It happens when waves lose energy, for example:

  • in sheltered bays where wave energy is spread out,
  • where the coast changes direction and the water becomes calmer,
  • when constructive waves dominate (strong swash, weak backwash),
  • where there is plenty of sediment, e.g. from eroding cliffs or rivers.

Deposition creates the landforms you will study in Lesson 4: beaches, spits, bars and tombolos.

Key terms

Hydraulic action
Erosion caused by the force of waves compressing air in cracks.
Abrasion
Erosion caused by waves throwing sediment against the cliff.
Attrition
Sediment knocking together and becoming smaller and rounder.
Longshore drift
The zig-zag movement of sediment along a coast by swash and backwash.
Deposition
When the sea drops material because it has lost energy.
Exam practice

Explain the process of longshore drift. You may use a diagram. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Waves approach the coast at an angle because of the prevailing wind (1).
  • The swash carries sediment up the beach at that angle (1).
  • The backwash carries it straight back down at right angles due to gravity (1).
  • Repeated, this moves sediment along the coast in a zig-zag (1). A clearly labelled diagram can earn credit.
