Success criterion 1

The four coastal ecosystems

An ecosystem is a community of plants and animals interacting with each other and with their physical environment.

  • Coral reefs — underwater structures built from the skeletons of tiny animals called coral polyps, in warm, clear, shallow seas.
  • Mangroves — forests of salt-tolerant trees and shrubs growing in the muddy intertidal zone of tropical and subtropical coasts.
  • Sand dunes — hills of wind-blown sand at the back of beaches, colonised by plants such as marram grass.
  • Salt marshes — areas of mud and salt-tolerant plants that develop in sheltered, low-energy coastal areas such as estuaries and behind spits.
Success criterion 2

Where are they found?

  • Coral reefs: between about 30°N and 30°S, especially in South-East Asia (the “Coral Triangle”), Australia (Great Barrier Reef), the Caribbean, the Red Sea and the Indian Ocean (e.g. Sri Lanka, the Maldives). They are mostly absent from west coasts of continents where cold currents flow.
  • Mangroves: tropical and subtropical coasts, e.g. Indonesia (which has the most), Brazil, Nigeria, Australia and the Sundarbans of Bangladesh and India.
  • Sand dunes: on coasts worldwide where there is plenty of sand and onshore winds, e.g. north-west Europe, the Namib coast and Australia.
  • Salt marshes: mainly sheltered temperate coasts in the mid to high latitudes, e.g. the UK, northern Europe and the eastern USA.
Success criterion 3

Why are they found there?

EcosystemConditions needed
Coral reefsWarm water (about 18–29°C); clear, shallow water so sunlight reaches the algae; salty water away from river mouths; low pollution.
MangrovesWarm temperatures (no frost); sheltered, low-energy coasts; muddy sediment; tidal flooding with salt or brackish water.
Sand dunesLarge supply of sand; a wide beach that dries at low tide; strong onshore winds to blow sand inland; obstacles and pioneer plants to trap it.
Salt marshesSheltered, calm water (estuaries, behind spits); supply of fine mud; regular tidal flooding; temperate climate where mangroves cannot grow.
Success criterion 4

Why are they valuable?

EcosystemValue
Coral reefsVery high biodiversity; fish breeding grounds; tourism (diving, snorkelling); protect coasts by breaking up waves; source of medicines.
MangrovesProtect coasts from storm surges and erosion; nursery areas for fish and shrimp; timber, fuelwood and honey; store large amounts of carbon.
Sand dunesNatural sea defence and sand store for beaches; habitats for rare plants and animals; recreation.
Salt marshesAbsorb wave energy and reduce flooding; habitat for birds; filter pollutants; store carbon.

Key terms

Ecosystem
A community of plants and animals interacting with each other and their environment.
Coral reef
An underwater structure built by coral polyps in warm, clear, shallow seas.
Mangrove
Salt-tolerant trees growing in the intertidal zone of tropical coasts.
Sand dune
A hill of wind-blown sand colonised by plants.
Salt marsh
An area of mud and salt-tolerant plants in a sheltered coastal area.
Exam-style question

Explain why mangroves are found only in certain parts of the world. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: mangroves grow in tropical and subtropical areas between about 30°N and 30°S.
  • 1 mark: they need warm water/temperatures (above about 20°C) and cannot survive frost.
  • 1 mark: they need sheltered coastlines with low-energy waves and muddy, tidal conditions (estuaries, deltas) where their roots can anchor.

Model answer: Mangroves are found in tropical and subtropical areas, roughly between 30°N and 30°S, because they need warm temperatures and cannot survive frost. They also need sheltered, low-energy coastlines such as estuaries and deltas, where fine mud is deposited and the trees can anchor their roots in the tidal zone.
