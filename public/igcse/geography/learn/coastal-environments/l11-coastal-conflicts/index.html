11. Conflicts between users of the coast

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Coastal Environments · 2.3 People and coastal management · Lesson 11 of 15

Learning Intention

To explore the relationships between human activity and the environment

Syllabus Statement

Coastal environments are of great importance to people and need to be sustainably managed

Success criterion 1

Who uses the coast? Stakeholders

A stakeholder is a person or group with an interest in how an area is used or managed. On the coast they include:

StakeholderWhat they want
Local residentsHomes protected from erosion and flooding; jobs; a pleasant environment.
Tourism businesses (hotels, restaurants, dive centres)Attractive beaches, clear water, reefs and easy access; new development.
Fishing communitiesHealthy fish stocks, access to the beach for boats, mangroves and reefs as breeding grounds.
FarmersProtection of farmland from erosion and salt water.
Industry and portsFlat land, deep water, dredged channels, room to expand.
ConservationistsProtection of ecosystems and wildlife; natural processes allowed to work.
Local and national governmentEconomic growth, jobs and tax income, but also safety and sustainability; limited budgets.

The main conflict is often between development (using the coast for economic growth) and conservation (protecting it for the future).

Success criterion 2

A conflict matrix

A conflict matrix compares every pair of stakeholders to show where they agree (✓), disagree (✗) or have little interaction (–).

Tourism ✗✗✗
Fishing ✗✓
Industry ✗

Each symbol must be explained. For example:

  • Tourism ✗ Fishing — hotels take over beaches where fishermen land their boats, and tourist boats damage reefs where fish breed.
  • Tourism ✗ Conservation — hotels built on dunes and divers damaging coral, versus the need to protect habitats.
  • Fishing ✓ Conservation — both want healthy reefs and mangroves, because they are fish nurseries.
  • Residents ✓ / ✗ Tourism — tourism brings jobs, but also crowding, higher prices and pressure for coastal defences.
Success criterion 3

Case study: conflict on the south-west coast of Sri Lanka

The south-west coast of Sri Lanka (e.g. Hikkaduwa to Matara) is one of the country’s busiest coastal zones:

  • Tourism has grown rapidly, with hotels built close to the shore, often on beaches used by fishermen. Glass-bottomed boats, divers and souvenir collecting have damaged coral reefs, such as at Hikkaduwa.
  • Fishing communities need beach space for boats and depend on the reefs and lagoons for fish.
  • Coral and sand mining — coral has been mined to make lime for building, and sand has been taken from beaches and rivers. This removes natural protection and increases erosion.
  • Coastal defences built to protect hotels and the coastal road and railway can increase erosion further along the coast.
  • Conservationists and the government’s Coast Conservation Department aim to protect reefs, mangroves and lagoons and control building close to the shore.
Success criterion 4

Suggesting solutions

  • Zoning — separate areas for tourism, fishing and conservation (e.g. marine protected areas and no-anchor zones).
  • Setback zones — no new building within a set distance of the shoreline.
  • Enforcing bans on coral mining and illegal sand mining, and providing alternative incomes.
  • Ecotourism and visitor education — mooring buoys instead of anchors, reef-safe guidance for divers.
  • Community involvement — giving local people a say and a share of tourism income (Lesson 14).
  • Integrated Coastal Zone Management — planning the whole coastline together.

Think about who wins and who loses from each solution — there is rarely an answer that suits every stakeholder.

Key terms

Stakeholder
A person or group with an interest in how an area is used or managed.
Conflict matrix
A table comparing stakeholders to show where they agree or disagree.
Conservation
Protecting the natural environment for the future.
Development
Using an area for economic growth, e.g. tourism or industry.
Setback zone
An area near the shoreline where new building is not allowed.
Exam-style question

Explain one conflict between different users of a coastline. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: identify two stakeholders in conflict, e.g. tourism developers and fishermen / conservationists.
  • 1 mark: explain the conflict, e.g. hotel building destroys mangroves that protect fish breeding grounds.
  • 1 mark: development of consequence, e.g. lower fish catches reduce fishermen’s income, while hotels bring jobs and tax revenue.

Model answer: Tourism developers may want to build hotels on the coast, but this can conflict with local fishermen. Clearing mangroves and building on the beach destroys the breeding grounds of fish and can pollute the water, so catches fall and fishermen lose income. However, the developers argue that tourism creates jobs and brings money into the area.
