Success criterion 1

What is fieldwork?

Fieldwork is the process of collecting primary data outside the classroom to answer a geographical question.

  • It helps you develop enquiry skills: asking questions, collecting and handling data, and reaching evidence-based conclusions.
  • It helps you understand geographical concepts by seeing processes such as longshore drift happening in real places.
  • It lets you apply theory from lessons (e.g. how groynes trap sediment) to a real environment.

In the exam, Paper 1 Section B asks questions about your own fieldwork and about fieldwork in an unfamiliar situation, so you need to know your study in detail.

Success criterion 2

The enquiry process

StageWhat you do
1. Introduction and planningIdentify an enquiry question and hypotheses; locate the study area; research background information using secondary data (e.g. newspaper articles, maps, websites).
2. Methods and data collectionDesign your methods and list the equipment; risk assess the activities; collect qualitative and quantitative data in the field.
3. Data presentationProcess data (percentages, means) and present it in appropriate simple and complex graphs, maps and charts.
4. Data analysisDescribe and explain patterns and trends; identify and explain anomalies.
5. ConclusionsUse primary and secondary evidence to accept or reject each hypothesis and answer the enquiry question.
6. EvaluationEvaluate the strengths and limitations of your methods; suggest how to improve reliability and extend the study.
Success criterion 3

Our coastal enquiry

Fieldwork focusInvestigating the impact of human intervention on coastal processes.
Enquiry question“To what extent have coastal management methods impacted coastal processes on 3 beaches along the coast of Sam Roi Yot National Park, Thailand?”
LocationThree beaches in Sam Roi Yot National Park, Prachuap Khiri Khan, on the Gulf of Thailand: Pak Nam Pran (fishing village with a river mouth and breakwaters), Pranburi Forest Park beach (with offshore reef/breakwaters) and the beach south of Sam Roi Yot.
Hypotheses
  1. On beaches where coastal management methods have been used, the beach profile will be steeper.
  2. On beaches where coastal management methods have been used, the beach profile will be wider.
  3. On beaches where coastal management methods have been used, wave frequency and wave height will be lower.
Quantitative methodsBeach width; beach gradient; beach transect (vegetation and sediment); longshore drift; wave frequency
Qualitative methodsField sketches; photos
Secondary dataOnline newspaper reports; historical photographs; Sam Roi Yot National Park website; Tourism Authority of Thailand

A hypothesis is a statement you can test with data and then accept or reject. Good hypotheses are specific and measurable.

Key terms

Fieldwork
Collecting primary data outside the classroom.
Enquiry question
The overall question an investigation sets out to answer.
Hypothesis
A testable statement that can be accepted or rejected using data.
Primary data
Data you collect yourself.
Secondary data
Data collected by someone else, e.g. maps or newspaper reports.
Exam-style question

State the six stages of a geographical enquiry. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for every two correct stages (max 3): planning (question and hypotheses); collecting data; presenting data; analysing data; drawing conclusions; evaluating the enquiry.

Model answer: The six stages are: (1) plan the enquiry, including the question, hypotheses and risk assessment; (2) collect primary and secondary data; (3) present the data using maps and graphs; (4) analyse the data to describe and explain patterns; (5) draw conclusions and accept or reject the hypotheses; (6) evaluate the methods and results.
