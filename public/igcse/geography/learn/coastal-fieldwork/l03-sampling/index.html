Success criterion 1

Sampling methods

We cannot measure everything, so we take a sample. The whole group being studied is called the population.

MethodHow it worksExample
Systematic (interval)Samples are taken at regular intervalsA quadrat every 10 m along the beach; gradient every 5 m
StratifiedThe area or population is divided into groups (strata) and each is sampled in proportion to its sizeIf 60% of a beach is sand and 40% shingle, take 6 samples on sand for every 4 on shingle
RandomEvery point has an equal chance of being chosen, using random numbers (not just guessing)Random number tables or a calculator to choose grid points

Samples can be taken at points (individual sites), along lines (a transect) or in areas (quadrats).

Success criterion 2

Why sample, and how to avoid bias

  • Why sample? It is quicker and cheaper than measuring everything; a well-chosen sample gives a similar average to the whole population; it lets you cover a larger area and compare sites fairly.
  • Bias happens when the sample is not representative, e.g. choosing the most interesting spots, only sampling at one time of day, or only asking one type of person.
  • To reduce bias: use a sampling strategy; sample at the same time and in the same way at each site; drop the quadrat over your shoulder without looking; take repeat readings and calculate a mean.
Success criterion 3

Sampling in our coastal enquiry

  • Transect — a line from the water’s edge (where the swash breaks) straight up to the back of the beach. Beach width, gradient and the beach transect were all measured along it, so the data can be combined into one profile.
  • Systematic sampling — gradient every 5 m (ranging poles 5 m apart) and a quadrat every 10 m along the transect.
  • Repeat readings — wave frequency and longshore drift were repeated three times and averaged.
  • Same method at each of the three sites so the beaches can be compared fairly.

Limitation: systematic sampling can miss features between the sampling points (e.g. a strip of vegetation between two quadrats).

Key terms

Sample
A small part of the population that is measured.
Systematic sampling
Sampling at regular intervals.
Stratified sampling
Sampling each group in proportion to its size.
Random sampling
Sampling where every point has an equal chance, using random numbers.
Transect
A line along which measurements are taken.
Bias
When results are not representative because of how the sample was chosen.
