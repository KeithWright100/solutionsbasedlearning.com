7. Evaluating the enquiry

Learning Intention

Paper 1, Section B fieldwork: investigating the impact of human intervention on coastal processes.

Success criterion 1

Problems and limitations

Ask yourself: Did we collect enough data? How accurate and reliable was it? Were there problems that affected data collection? Was the time well organised?

  • Accuracy — how close a measurement is to the true value.
  • Reliability — whether you would get the same results if you repeated the study.

Example limitations from our study

  • It was hard to measure beach width accurately — a subjective decision had to be made about where the shoreline ‘started’ and the beach ‘ended’.
  • The three sites were visited at different times of day, so the tide had changed, making beaches appear wider or narrower.
  • Systematic sampling means sections of the beach between sampling points were ‘missed out’, which may be misleading.
  • Only one transect per beach and one visit, so results may not be representative.
Success criterion 2

Solutions

MethodProblem / limitationSolution
Beach widthUnclear start and end points; tide changesAgree fixed markers (e.g. high-water mark, vegetation line); visit all sites at the same state of tide
Beach gradientPoles not held upright; clinometer misreadUse a spirit level on the poles; two people read each angle; average foresight and backsight
Beach transectEstimating % cover is subjectiveSame person estimates at every site; take photos of each quadrat to check later
Longshore driftFloat affected by wind; lost floatsRepeat more times; record wind speed and direction; use a weighted float
Wave frequencyHard to decide what counts as one wave; boatsAgree a rule (count only breaking waves); two counters; ignore readings affected by boats
Success criterion 3

Extending the investigation

  • Take more transects at each beach (e.g. either side of groynes) and visit more sites.
  • Repeat the study at different times of year (e.g. during the north-east and south-west monsoons) to see seasonal change.
  • Add new methods: sediment size and shape analysis, wave height, wind data.
  • Use more secondary data: historical photos and satellite images to measure change in the coastline over time.
  • Carry out interviews with fishermen and park staff about changes they have seen.

Exam tip: link each limitation to a solution and explain how it would improve accuracy or reliability. Write clearly, with accurate spelling, punctuation and grammar.

Key terms

Accuracy
How close a measurement is to the true value.
Reliability
Whether repeating the study would give the same results.
Limitation
A weakness that affects the quality of the data or conclusions.
Representative
A sample that fairly reflects the whole area or population.
Exam-style question

Suggest two ways the accuracy of a coastal enquiry could be improved. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each improvement (max 2), e.g. take more readings/more transects; repeat on different days and tides; use more accurate equipment (e.g. digital clinometer); calibrate and use the same team for measurements; increase sample size.
  • 1 mark for each explanation of why it improves accuracy/reliability.

Model answer: First, we could measure more transects along the beach, because a larger sample would reduce the effect of anomalies and give a more representative picture. Second, we could repeat the measurements on different days and at the same point in the tidal cycle, so the results are not affected by one day’s conditions.
