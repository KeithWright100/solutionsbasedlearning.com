Success criterion 2

Quantitative and qualitative data

  • Quantitative data can be measured or counted and shown as numbers, e.g. beach gradient in degrees. It is easy to analyse statistically and show in graphs. In human enquiries, closed questions and environmental-quality scores (e.g. 1–5) are quantitative.
  • Qualitative data is recorded in words, pictures or diagrams, e.g. field sketches, photos, interviews and open questions. It gives depth and context but is more subjective and can be interpreted differently by different people.

Activity: quantitative or qualitative?

Sort each method.

  • Beach width measured with a tape (m)
  • Beach gradient with a clinometer (°)
  • Waves breaking per minute
  • Distance a ball moves in 1 minute (LSD)
  • % vegetation in a quadrat
  • Annotated field sketch of the beach
  • Photographs of groynes and sea walls
  • Interview with a local fisherman
  • Notes on the condition of the breakwaters

Success criterion 3

Pilot surveys

A pilot survey is a small trial carried out before the main data collection. It is used to:

  • check that the right sites and areas are being studied;
  • find out about the location (e.g. where the defences are, where it is safe to work);
  • test the methods and equipment and how long each takes, saving time later;
  • check questionnaire questions, layout and timing;
  • make the investigation more representative.
Success criterion 4

Risk assessment

Before collecting data we must carry out a risk assessment: a careful look at what could cause harm and how to reduce it.

  • A hazard is anything with the potential to cause injury or harm.
  • Risk is the likelihood of that harm happening, taking into account how serious it would be.
HazardRiskHow to reduce it
Waves and tides when measuring LSDBeing knocked over or swept awayOnly go knee-deep (about 3 m out); check tide times; work in pairs; teacher supervision
Strong sun and heatSunburn, dehydration, heatstrokeSun cream, hats, water, shaded breaks
Slippery rocks, breakwaters and groynesSlips, cuts and broken bonesDo not climb on structures; wear proper shoes
Litter, glass and sharp shellsCuts to hands and feetWear shoes; do not pick up sharp objects
Jellyfish and other marine lifeStingsCheck conditions; carry first aid; avoid touching animals
Traffic and boats at Pak Nam PranAccidentsStay together; keep away from boat launch areas and roads

Key terms

Quantitative data
Data that can be measured or counted as numbers.
Qualitative data
Data recorded in words, pictures or diagrams.
Pilot survey
A small trial carried out before the main survey.
Hazard
Anything with the potential to cause harm.
Risk
The likelihood of harm happening and how serious it would be.
Risk assessment
Identifying hazards and how to reduce the risks.
