5. Presenting fieldwork data

Success criterion 1

Processing the data

  • Mean — add the values and divide by how many there are, e.g. wave counts of 10, 12 and 11 per minute give a mean of 11.
  • Percentages — quadrat squares convert directly to % cover (100 squares).
  • Mean gradient — average the foresight and backsight at each interval.
  • Range — the highest minus the lowest value; shows how varied the data is.

Averages make patterns easier to see and compare, but they hide extremes and the spread of the data. Tallying is a quick and simple way to record counts in the field.

Success criterion 2

Choosing graphs for coastal data

DataSuitable presentation
Beach width and gradientBeach profile (cross-section) drawn to scale for each site, side by side
Beach make-up (quadrats)Pie charts or divided bars overlaid on the profile at each 10 m point; kite diagram for vegetation along the transect
Wave frequency at three sitesBar chart (one bar per site)
Longshore drift direction and distanceArrows on a map, scaled to the distance travelled; or a bar chart
Location and defencesAnnotated maps, field sketches and photos

Success criterion 3

Evaluating presentation techniques

  • Beach profiles — very easy to compare the width and gradient of all three sites visually; can be annotated (e.g. top of the beach, defences). But the vertical scale is often exaggerated, and points between measurements are joined with straight lines.
  • Overlaid pie charts — clearly show how vegetation changes up the beach; but small pies are hard to read precisely.
  • Bar charts — simple to draw and compare; but show only means, not the spread of readings.
  • Field sketches and photos — show context and management; but are not measurements and can be selective.

Key terms

Mean
The total divided by the number of values.
Range
The difference between the highest and lowest values.
Beach profile
A cross-section showing the width and gradient of a beach.
Kite diagram
A graph showing how % cover changes along a transect.
Divided bar
A bar split into sections showing percentages.
