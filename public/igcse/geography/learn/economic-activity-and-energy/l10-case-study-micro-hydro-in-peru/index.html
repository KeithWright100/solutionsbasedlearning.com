10. Case study: micro-hydro in Peru

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Economic Activity and Energy · Sustainable energy · Lesson 10 of 10

Learning Intention

To apply knowledge and understanding to relevant case studies.

Syllabus Statement

A named example of a small-scale sustainable energy scheme in a developing country and its benefits for the local community.

Success criterion 1

Location and need

  • Chambamontera is a remote farming community in the Andes mountains of northern Peru, more than two hours by rough track from the nearest town, Jaén.
  • Families grew coffee and other crops but had no connection to the national electricity grid, which was too expensive to extend to such a remote, mountainous area.
  • People relied on kerosene lamps, candles and wood for light and cooking — expensive, smoky and a fire risk.
  • Most people depend on subsistence farming, with some small-scale coffee growing and livestock rearing. Nearly half the population lived on about US$2 a day.
  • The area had an energy deficit: lack of electricity for heat, light and power held back development. Because the population density is low, it was uneconomic to build an electricity grid to serve the area.
  • Steep slopes and fast-flowing rivers make the area ideal for small-scale hydro power.
Success criterion 2

How micro-hydro works

Water is diverted from the river into a pipe (penstock), falls downhill and spins a turbine connected to a generator, then flows back into the river. Because there is no large dam, the river and valley are not flooded.

Success criterion 3

Funding, impacts and sustainability

  • The scheme was set up with the NGO Practical Action and cost about US$51,000, funded by the Peruvian government, Japanese investment and the community itself. Each family contributed about US$750, with credit available to help them pay.
  • Local people were trained to run and maintain it; it is designed to last at least 25 years with low maintenance.

Activity: social, economic or environmental?

Key terms

Micro-hydro
A small hydroelectric scheme (usually under 100 kW) serving a local community.
Penstock
The pipe carrying water down to the turbine.
Run-of-river
Hydro power that uses the river’s flow without a large dam.
NGO
Non-governmental organisation, e.g. Practical Action.
Off-grid
Not connected to the national electricity network.
Appropriate technology
Technology suited to local needs, skills and money.
Case study

Chambamontera micro-hydro scheme, Peru

  • Where: remote Andean village in northern Peru, over 2 hours from Jaén; off-grid.
  • Who: Practical Action with the community, Peruvian government and Japanese funding; about US$51,000 total, about US$750 per family.
  • Benefits: lighting, refrigerated medicines, coffee processing, new businesses, less wood and kerosene burning.
  • Sustainable because: renewable, run-of-river, low-cost, locally built and maintained, lasting at least 25 years.
Exam-style question

Explain why the Chambamontera micro-hydro scheme is considered sustainable. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each developed point (max 4), e.g. uses renewable river water; small-scale with little environmental damage; low cost and maintained by the community (appropriate technology); provides electricity for lighting, refrigeration and small businesses; improves education and healthcare; reduces use of kerosene/wood.

Model answer: The micro-hydro scheme uses the flow of a local river, which is renewable and produces no emissions. It is small-scale, so it causes little environmental damage, and it uses simple technology that local people can maintain themselves. The electricity has improved quality of life, for example by providing lighting for studying and power for small businesses, and has reduced the need to burn kerosene and wood.
