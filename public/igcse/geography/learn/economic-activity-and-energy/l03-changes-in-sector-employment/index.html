3. Changes in sector employment

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Economic Activity and Energy · Changing employment · Lesson 3 of 10

Learning Intention

To describe and explain human patterns and processes.

Syllabus Statement

Reasons for the changes in the numbers of people employed in each economic sector, including the availability of raw materials, globalisation, mechanisation, demographic changes and government policies.

Success criterion 1

Five reasons why employment changes

ReasonHow it changes employmentExamples
1. Source of raw materialsResources run out or become too expensive to extract, so primary and linked secondary jobs declineUK coal production fell from over 200 million tonnes a year in the early 1900s to almost nothing; coal and steel towns in north-east England suffered unemployment
2. New technologies (mechanisation)Machines, robots and software replace workers but create new quaternary jobsCombine harvesters; robots on car lines; self-service checkouts; containerisation of shipping; cloud computing and home-working
3. GlobalisationManufacturing moves to countries with cheaper labour (deindustrialisation in HICs, industrialisation in NEEs)Nike manufacturing in Vietnam; call centres and IT outsourcing in India
4. Government policiesTaxes, investment in education, infrastructure and incentives shape which sectors growSingapore invested in education, ports and low taxes and became a high-income economy
5. Demographic and social changeRural–urban migration, education, ageing populations and rising incomes shift workers into servicesChina: millions moved from farms to city factories, then services grew; ageing populations need more health and care workers
Success criterion 1

Examples from the class research

  • China — rapid industrialisation and urbanisation moved millions from agriculture to manufacturing; with a growing middle class, services (retail, finance, technology) are now growing fastest.
  • USA — a post-industrial shift from manufacturing to services such as healthcare, education and finance; a large quaternary sector (e.g. Silicon Valley).
  • India — still many people in agriculture, but rapid growth in IT and business-process outsourcing thanks to globalisation, technology and an English-speaking workforce.
  • Germany — has kept a strong manufacturing base (cars, engineering) with a strong research (quaternary) sector.

Activity: which reason?

Match each change to the main reason behind it.

  • UK coal mines close as reserves become too expensive to extract
  • Self-service checkouts replace cashiers
  • Container cranes reduce the need for dock workers
  • A trainer company moves production to Vietnam
  • Call centres move from the UK to India
  • Low taxes and investment in education attract firms to Singapore
  • An ageing population increases demand for care workers
  • Millions of rural Chinese migrate to work in city factories

Success criterion 2

Research, present and apply

Research task: research one way in which demographic or social changes have affected the numbers of people employed in different sectors in a country of your choice. Summarise it on one slide for a whole-class presentation.

Exam practice: “Explain two reasons why the number of people employed in the secondary sector has decreased in many developed countries.” (4 marks) — make a point and develop it for each reason.

Key terms

Mechanisation
Replacing workers with machines.
Globalisation
The process by which countries become more connected through trade, communication and culture.
Deindustrialisation
The decline of manufacturing in a country or region.
Outsourcing
A firm paying another company, often abroad, to do part of its work.
Containerisation
Moving goods in standard steel containers, mechanising loading and unloading.
