9. Sustainable energy

Success criterion 1

Carbon and ecological footprints

  • Sustainable energy meets today’s needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs: it is low-carbon, affordable and secure.
  • A carbon footprint is the total amount of greenhouse gases (measured as tonnes of CO2-equivalent) produced by a person, organisation or country, directly and indirectly, in a year.
  • An ecological footprint is the area of productive land and sea needed to provide the resources a person uses and absorb their waste, measured in global hectares (gha).
  • If everyone lived like the average person in a high-income country such as the USA, we would need several Earths; the world as a whole currently uses resources faster than the Earth can renew them (about 1.7 Earths, Global Footprint Network).
  • A global hectare is a standard unit: a hectare (10,000 m2) of world-average biological productivity — its ability to produce food, fibre and timber and absorb CO2.
  • Biocapacity is the ability of an area to keep producing renewable resources and absorbing waste. An area is unsustainable when its ecological footprint exceeds its biocapacity.
  • Earth Overshoot Day marks the date when humanity’s demand for resources in a year exceeds what the Earth can regenerate that year. In 2024 it fell on 1 August.
  • There are only about 1.6–1.7 global hectares of biocapacity per person on Earth. A footprint of 6.3 gha would therefore be almost four times a fair “share”.

Activity: match the key word to its definition

Choose the key word for each definition.

  • How much demand human consumption places on the biosphere, measured in global hectares
  • A hectare of world-average biologically productive land, able to produce food, fibre and timber and absorb CO₂
  • The total CO₂ and other greenhouse gases linked to a person, product or country, measured in tonnes
  • The capacity of an area to keep producing renewable resources and absorbing waste
  • The date when humanity’s yearly demand for resources exceeds what Earth can regenerate in that year

Success criterion 3

Strategies at different scales

Activity: which scale?

Classify each strategy by scale.

  • Turning off lights and devices
  • Walking or cycling to school
  • Installing solar panels at home
  • A school or town solar or micro-hydro scheme
  • Better bus services and cycle lanes
  • Government subsidies for renewables
  • Building insulation standards
  • Banning new petrol and diesel car sales
  • The Paris Agreement (2015) to limit warming
  • Carbon trading between countries

The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are 17 global goals agreed by the United Nations in 2015 for 2030. SDG 7: Affordable and clean energy aims for universal access to affordable, reliable and modern energy, more renewables and doubled improvement in energy efficiency. SDG 13 (Climate action) and SDG 11 (Sustainable cities) are also linked to energy.

Evaluate: individual actions are cheap but small; national policies have the largest effect but may be unpopular or costly; global agreements are hard to enforce.

Key terms

Sustainable energy
Energy that meets present needs without compromising future generations.
Carbon footprint
The greenhouse gases produced by a person or group, in tonnes of CO₂e.
Ecological footprint
The area of land and sea needed to support a person’s lifestyle.
Energy efficiency
Using less energy to do the same task.
Paris Agreement
The 2015 international climate agreement.
Exam-style question

Explain two ways energy use can be made more sustainable. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each strategy (max 2), e.g. increasing renewable energy; improving energy efficiency (insulation, LED lighting); public transport and electric vehicles; government targets and taxes; reducing personal consumption.
  • 1 mark for each explanation of how it reduces resource use/emissions.

Model answer: One way is to increase the use of renewable energy, such as solar and wind, which reduces the burning of fossil fuels and so cuts carbon emissions. Another is to improve energy efficiency, for example by insulating homes and using LED lights, so less energy is needed for the same comfort, reducing people’s carbon footprint.
