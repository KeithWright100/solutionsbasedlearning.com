Success criterion 1

What is fracking?

Fracking (hydraulic fracturing) extracts shale gas trapped in tiny pores in shale rock. It transformed the USA into a major gas producer from the late 2000s. The UK has large shale deposits, especially the Bowland Shale beneath Lancashire, Yorkshire and the Midlands, but how much could be extracted economically is uncertain.

Success criterion 1

Fracking in the UK: a timeline

  • 2011 — Cuadrilla’s test fracking near Blackpool, Lancashire, triggers small earthquakes; fracking is paused.
  • 2018–2019 — fracking at Preston New Road, Lancashire, causes repeated tremors, including a magnitude 2.9 tremor in August 2019.
  • November 2019 — the government places a moratorium (temporary ban) on fracking in England.
  • 2022 — the ban is briefly lifted during the energy price crisis, then reinstated weeks later. Cuadrilla’s wells were later ordered to be sealed.
  • Fracking remains effectively banned in England; Scotland and Wales also oppose it.

Fast facts

  • Fracking is not new: the first attempt was in the USA in 1947.
  • About 99% of the fracking fluid is water and sand; the sand works like a car jack, propping the cracks open.
  • In 2015 the UK produced only about 45% of the gas it used; much of the rest is piped from Norway and Europe or shipped in as liquefied natural gas.
  • Each fracking well may use between 500 and 10,000 m3 of fresh water.
  • The industry claimed fracking could create around 60,000 jobs and £33 billion of investment, with reserves worth up to £1.5 trillion — but these are estimates and much of the gas may not be economic to extract.
  • Methane leaks (about 2% in some studies) matter because methane is a powerful greenhouse gas.
  • In Australia, fracking companies must use wastewater or recycled water to reduce freshwater use.

Policy can change — check the latest news before your exam.

Advantages and disadvantages

Activity: advantage or disadvantage?

Sort the arguments about fracking in the UK.

  • Reduces dependence on imported gas (energy security)
  • Could create jobs and tax income
  • Gas produces less CO₂ than coal
  • Local communities could receive payments
  • Earth tremors, e.g. magnitude 2.9 at Preston New Road
  • Risk of groundwater contamination from fracking fluids
  • Still a fossil fuel that adds to climate change
  • Heavy lorry traffic, noise and lights near homes
  • Uses 500–10,000 m³ of fresh water per well
  • Methane leaks add to global warming
  • Fear of property damage from repeated tremors
  • Landowners receive rent from fracking companies
  • May slow investment in renewables

Key terms

Fracking
Pumping water, sand and chemicals at high pressure to crack shale and release gas.
Shale gas

Moratorium
A temporary ban.
Stakeholder

Aquifer

Case study

Fracking at Preston New Road, Lancashire

  • What: shale gas exploration by Cuadrilla in the Bowland Shale.
  • Problem: repeated earth tremors, up to magnitude 2.9 (August 2019), plus local fears over water, traffic and property.
  • Response: moratorium in England from November 2019; briefly lifted in 2022 then reinstated; wells ordered to be sealed.
  • Debate: energy security and jobs versus climate change and local environmental impacts.
Exam-style question

Explain the possible advantages and disadvantages of fracking in the UK. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for advantages, e.g. increases energy security / reduces imports; creates jobs; gas produces less CO2 than coal.
  • Up to 2 marks for disadvantages, e.g. risk of groundwater contamination; small earthquakes (e.g. near Blackpool, 2011); large water use; still a fossil fuel contributing to climate change; local opposition.

Model answer: Fracking could improve the UK’s energy security by reducing gas imports and could create jobs, and gas releases less carbon dioxide than coal. However, injecting water and chemicals at high pressure can contaminate groundwater, and it caused small earthquakes near Blackpool in 2011. It is also still a fossil fuel, so it contributes to climate change, which is why many local people oppose it.
