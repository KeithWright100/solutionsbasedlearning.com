2. Factors affecting the location of economic activities

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Economic Activity and Energy · Economic sectors and location · Lesson 2 of 10

Learning Intention

To explore the changes which occur through time in places, landscapes and spatial distribution.

Syllabus Statement

The location of economic activity varies spatially and changes over time.

Factors affecting the location of economic activity in each economic sector and how these factors can change over time.

Success criterion 1

Locational factors

Activity: human or physical factor?

Classify each locational factor.

  • Skilled labour, e.g. for quaternary industries
  • Cheap labour, e.g. for clothes factories
  • Available capital (money to invest)
  • Market: nearness to customers
  • Supply network of nearby suppliers
  • Good housing, schools and hospitals for workers
  • Nearby universities for research
  • Transport links: roads, railways, airports
  • Good communications and broadband
  • Flat land that is easy to build on
  • Space to expand
  • Greenfield (unpolluted) land
  • Natural routes such as rivers and the coast
  • Available raw materials
  • Reliable water supply
  • Climate, e.g. sunshine for solar research

Other factors include government policies (grants, tax breaks, special economic zones) and a nice environment to attract skilled workers.

Success criterion 1

Different industries need different factors

IndustrySectorMost important factors
FarmingPrimaryClimate, soil, relief, water, market
Coal miningPrimaryLocation of the raw material (coal seams); transport to power stations
Iron and steelSecondaryOriginally near coal and iron ore; now at coastal sites to receive imported raw materials (e.g. Port Talbot, Wales)
Car manufacturingSecondaryLarge flat sites, motorway links, supply networks, skilled labour, markets, government grants (e.g. Nissan, Sunderland)
RetailingTertiaryAccessibility, customers, parking, footfall (city centres or out-of-town shopping centres)
Teaching and dentistryTertiaryWhere people live (the market)
App designQuaternarySkilled graduates, universities, fast broadband, pleasant environment (e.g. science parks, Silicon Valley)
Success criterion 2

How location changes over time

  • Raw materials run out or become expensive — UK coal mining moved, then closed; steelworks moved from coalfields to the coast to use imported ore and coal.
  • Footloose industries — modern light industries and services are not tied to raw materials and can locate almost anywhere with good transport and internet links, e.g. business parks near motorways and airports.
  • Globalisation — manufacturing has moved to countries with cheaper labour, e.g. clothing and electronics from the UK and USA to China, Vietnam and Bangladesh.
  • Technology — the internet allows online retail and home-working, so shops close on high streets while warehouses grow near motorways.
  • Government policy — grants and special economic zones attract firms to particular regions (e.g. the Eastern Seaboard of Thailand).

Key terms

Locational factor
A reason why an industry is located in a particular place.
Footloose industry
An industry not tied to raw materials that can locate in many places.
Greenfield site
Land that has not been built on before.
Market
The customers who buy a product or service.
Science park
A group of high-tech firms, often near a university.
