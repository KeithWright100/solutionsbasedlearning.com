10. Volcano case study: Mount Kīlauea 2018

Success criterion 1

Location

Kīlauea is on the south-east of the Island of Hawai‘i (the ‘Big Island’), the largest and youngest island of the US state of Hawai‘i, in the middle of the Pacific Ocean about 3,800 km south-west of California (about 19°N, 155°W). It is a shield volcano and one of the most active volcanoes in the world; it erupted almost continuously from 1983 to 2018. The 2018 eruption was in the Puna district, along the volcano’s lower East Rift Zone, in housing areas such as Leilani Estates.

Success criterion 2

How hotspots cause volcanoes

  • Hawai‘i is about 3,000 km from the nearest plate boundary, in the middle of the Pacific plate.
  • Underneath is a hotspot: a plume of unusually hot mantle rising from deep in the Earth. It melts the base of the plate and the magma rises to form volcanoes.
  • The hotspot stays still while the plate moves north-west, so a chain of islands forms. The oldest islands in the north-west are extinct and eroded; the Big Island, over the hotspot today, is active.
  • The magma is runny basalt, so eruptions are usually effusive, with long lava flows rather than explosions.
Success criterion 3

Impacts of the 2018 eruption

From 3 May 2018, 24 fissures opened in Leilani Estates, pouring out lava until early August. A magnitude 6.9 earthquake struck on 4 May, and the summit crater collapsed, sending ash about 9 km into the air.

Success criterion 5

Why do people live beside Kīlauea?

  • Cheap land — land in the Puna lava zones was sold cheaply, so people could afford homes.
  • Natural beauty and lifestyle — tropical climate, forests and coastline.
  • Fertile volcanic soils for farming, e.g. papaya and flowers.
  • Tourism and jobs — the volcano attracts millions of visitors to the national park.
  • Geothermal energy from the hot rock.
  • Low risk to life — eruptions are effusive and well monitored by the USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory, so people expect to be able to escape.
  • Family and cultural ties — in Hawaiian tradition the volcano is home to the goddess Pele.

Key terms

Hotspot
A plume of hot mantle that creates volcanoes in the middle of a plate.
Fissure
A long crack in the ground through which lava erupts.
Vog
Volcanic smog made of sulfur dioxide and other gases.
Laze
Acidic steam and glass particles formed when lava meets the sea.
Rift zone
A zone of cracks on the side of a volcano where magma erupts.
Exam-style question

Explain how hotspots cause volcanoes such as Kīlauea in Hawaii. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: a hotspot is a plume of very hot magma rising from deep in the mantle, away from plate boundaries.
  • 1 mark: the magma melts through the crust and erupts, forming a volcano.
  • 1 mark: the plate moves over the stationary hotspot, creating a chain of volcanic islands (the oldest furthest away); Kīlauea is currently above the hotspot.

Model answer: A hotspot is a column of very hot magma rising from deep within the mantle in the middle of a plate. The magma burns through the crust and erupts to form a shield volcano. The Pacific Plate moves slowly over the stationary hotspot, so a chain of islands forms; Kīlauea on Big Island is the youngest and currently sits above the hotspot, so it is active.
