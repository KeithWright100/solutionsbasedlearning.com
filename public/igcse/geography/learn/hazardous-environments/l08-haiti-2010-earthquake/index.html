8. Earthquake case study (LIC): Haiti 2010

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Hazardous Environments · Earthquakes · Lesson 8 of 12

Learning Intention

To understand the impacts of hazards on people and the environment.

Syllabus Statement

Causes of earthquake hazards.

Short and long-term impacts of earthquakes in a developing country.

Hazard management for earthquakes in a developing country.

Success criterion 1

Cause

  • When and where: 12 January 2010 at 16:53 local time. The epicentre was about 25 km west-south-west of the capital, Port-au-Prince, near Léogâne. The focus was very shallow, about 13 km deep.
  • Magnitude 7.0.
  • Plate boundary: a conservative boundary where the Caribbean plate moves east past the North American plate at about 20 mm a year, along the Enriquillo–Plantain Garden fault system. Stress built up for over 200 years before the rocks slipped.

Haiti was the poorest country in the Americas, with no building codes and many people living in densely packed, badly built homes in Port-au-Prince.

Success criterion 4

Hazard management before and after 2010

Before 2010After 2010
No building codes; cheap concrete without steel reinforcementHaiti’s first national building code (2012) and training for builders
Little monitoring of earthquakes; no earthquake planNew seismic monitoring stations and hazard mapping

However, enforcement remains weak: in August 2021 a magnitude 7.2 earthquake in south-west Haiti killed over 2,000 people, showing that the country is still very vulnerable.

Key terms

Port-au-Prince
The capital of Haiti, near the epicentre.
Conservative boundary
Where plates slide past each other, as between the Caribbean and North American plates.
Cholera
A water-borne disease spread by dirty water.
Relief camp
Temporary shelters for people made homeless.
Exam-style question

Explain why the 2010 earthquake had such a severe impact on Haiti. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each developed reason (max 4), e.g. Haiti is one of the poorest countries, so buildings were poorly constructed and collapsed; the epicentre was close to the capital Port-au-Prince and the focus was shallow; dense population in slums; weak emergency services and government; lack of preparation and building codes; damaged port and airport slowed aid.

Model answer: Haiti is the poorest country in the Americas, so most buildings were poorly built without earthquake-resistant design and collapsed easily. The epicentre was only about 25 km from the densely populated capital, Port-au-Prince, and the focus was shallow, so the shaking was intense. The government and emergency services were weak, and damage to the port and airport delayed aid, so over 200,000 people died.
