9. Volcanoes

Characteristics, distribution and measurement of volcanoes.

Success criterion 1

Where are volcanoes found?

  • There are about 1,350 potentially active volcanoes on land. Most lie in narrow belts along plate boundaries.
  • About three-quarters are around the Pacific Ring of Fire, at destructive boundaries — e.g. Japan (Mount Fuji), Indonesia (Merapi), the Philippines (Pinatubo), the Andes and the Cascades (Mount St Helens).
  • Others lie on constructive boundaries such as the Mid-Atlantic Ridge (Iceland) and the East African Rift (Nyiragongo).
  • A few form over hotspots in the middle of plates, such as Hawai‘i.
  • There are no volcanoes at conservative or collision boundaries.

Success criterion 3

Volcanic hazards and how eruptions are measured

  • Lava flows — destroy everything in their path, but usually move slowly enough for people to escape.
  • Pyroclastic flows — avalanches of hot gas, ash and rock moving at over 100 km/h and several hundred °C; the deadliest hazard.
  • Ash fall — collapses roofs, ruins crops, causes breathing problems and grounds aircraft.
  • Volcanic bombs — large lumps of rock thrown out of the volcano.
  • Lahars — mudflows of ash mixed with rain or melted snow; in 1985 a lahar from Nevado del Ruiz buried the town of Armero, Colombia, killing about 23,000 people.
  • Gases — e.g. sulfur dioxide and carbon dioxide, which can suffocate people and cause acid rain.

Eruptions are measured on the Volcanic Explosivity Index (VEI), from 0 (gentle) to 8 (super-eruption), based on the volume of material erupted and the height of the eruption column. Each step is about ten times bigger than the one below.

Success criterion 4

How do different countries manage volcanoes?

  • Monitoring — seismometers (small earthquakes as magma rises), tiltmeters and GPS (the ground swells), gas sensors (more sulfur dioxide) and satellites (heat).
  • Hazard maps and alert levels — showing danger zones and when to evacuate.
  • Evacuation — e.g. about 60,000 people were evacuated before Mount Pinatubo erupted in 1991, saving thousands of lives.
  • Diverting lava — barriers and channels (Etna, Italy) or cooling lava with sea water (Heimaey, Iceland, 1973).
  • Richer countries such as Iceland, Japan and the USA have well-funded volcano observatories and emergency plans. Poorer countries may have little monitoring and fewer resources to evacuate people, so they are more vulnerable — e.g. the 2021 eruption of Nyiragongo in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Key terms

Magma
Molten rock beneath the surface.
Lava
Molten rock that has reached the surface.
Pyroclastic flow
A fast, very hot avalanche of gas, ash and rock.
Lahar
A volcanic mudflow.
VEI
Volcanic Explosivity Index, measuring eruption size from 0 to 8.
Shield volcano
A wide, gently sloping volcano with runny lava.
Composite volcano
A steep volcano made of layers of ash and lava.
