7. Earthquake case study (HIC): Japan 2011

Success criterion 1

Cause

  • When and where: 11 March 2011 at 14:46 local time, about 70 km off the east coast of the Tōhoku region of north-east Japan, about 130 km from the city of Sendai. The focus was about 30 km deep.
  • Magnitude 9.0–9.1 — the most powerful earthquake ever recorded in Japan and one of the five strongest worldwide since records began. Shaking lasted about six minutes.
  • Plate boundary: a destructive boundary at the Japan Trench, where the oceanic Pacific plate subducts beneath the plate that northern Japan sits on (the North American/Okhotsk plate). The plates had been locked together; when they slipped, the sea floor was pushed up by several metres, triggering a huge tsunami.

Success criterion 4

Hazard management in Japan

  • Strict building codes (strengthened in 1981 and 2000): base isolators, flexible steel frames and counterweights — most buildings survived the shaking itself; the tsunami caused most deaths.
  • Earthquake Early Warning system run by the Japan Meteorological Agency, sending alerts to phones, TV and trains.
  • Education and drills: every year on 1 September (Disaster Prevention Day); school drills and emergency kits.
  • Tsunami defences: sea walls, evacuation routes, signs and towers. In 2011 many walls were overtopped because the tsunami was bigger than they were designed for.

Key terms

Tōhoku
The region of north-east Japan most affected in 2011.
Japan Trench
Where the Pacific plate subducts beneath northern Japan.
Fukushima Daiichi
The nuclear power plant damaged by the 2011 tsunami.
Exclusion zone
An area people are not allowed to live in because of danger.
