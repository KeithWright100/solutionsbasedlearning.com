5. Demographic transition model and population pyramids

Edexcel IGCSE Geography · Development and Human Welfare · Development and population · Lesson 5 of 9

Learning Intention

To understand human patterns and processes.

Syllabus Statement

How countries at different levels of development have differences in their demographic data (fertility rates, death rates, natural increase, population structures, maternal and infant mortality rates).

Success criterion 1

Interpreting a population pyramid step by step

Use “BSTAD” to interpret any pyramid, and always quote figures from the axes:

  • Base — how wide is it? “About 8% of the population are males aged 0–4.”
  • Sides — do the bars shrink quickly (high death rate) or stay the same (low death rate)?
  • Top — how many people are 65+? Are there more women than men at the top (women live longer)?
  • Anomalies — bulges, indents or a difference between males and females (e.g. male migrant workers in Qatar or the UAE).
  • Dependency — is it a youthful or ageing population?
Success criterion 2

Linking pyramids to the DTM

DTM stagePyramid shapeWhat it tells us
Stage 1Wide base, very narrow top, concave sidesHigh birth and death rates; short life expectancy
Stage 2Very wide base, triangular shapeHigh birth rate, falling death rate, rapid growth (e.g. Niger)
Stage 3Base starts to narrow, wider middleFalling birth rate, more people living longer (e.g. India, Kenya)
Stage 4Straight sides, wide topLow birth and death rates; stable population (e.g. UK)
Stage 5Narrow base, wide middle and topBirth rate below death rate; ageing and shrinking (e.g. Japan, Italy)

Youthful populations (stages 2–3) need schools, jobs and health care for mothers and babies. Ageing populations (stages 4–5) need pensions, care homes and more workers, sometimes from migration.

Countries at different levels of development have very different maternal mortality too: in Nigeria roughly 1 in 100 births leads to the mother’s death, compared with fewer than 1 in 10,000 in the UK. Lower maternal and infant mortality reflect better health care, nutrition, clean water and education — which is why these are such useful indicators of development.

Key terms

Population pyramid
A graph showing a population by age and sex.
Youthful population
A population with a high proportion of under-15s.
Ageing population
A population with a growing proportion of people aged 65+.
Dependency ratio
The number of dependents (young and old) compared with the working population.
Replacement level
A fertility rate of about 2.1, at which a population stays stable.
Exam-style question

Describe the shape of the population pyramid for a less developed country and explain what it shows. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: wide base.
  • 1 mark: explanation: high birth rate / large proportion of young dependants.
  • 1 mark: narrow top / concave sides.
  • 1 mark: explanation: high death rate and low life expectancy, so few elderly people.

Model answer: A less developed country usually has a pyramid with a wide base and a narrow top. The wide base shows a high birth rate and a large proportion of children. The sides narrow quickly and the top is small because the death rate is high and life expectancy is low, so few people live to old age.
