Success criterion 1

What is GDP?

Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the total value of all the goods and services produced in a country in one year. GDP per capita divides this by the population, giving the average value produced per person, in US dollars so that countries can be compared.

  • It can be measured by adding up everything that is produced, everything that is spent, or all the incomes earned — in theory all three give the same total.
  • PPP (purchasing power parity) adjusts for the cost of living: $1 buys much more in India than in Switzerland, so GDP per capita (PPP) is a fairer comparison.
  • GNI (Gross National Income) is similar but also includes income earned abroad by a country’s people and businesses. The World Bank uses GNI per capita for its income groups.

Examples of GDP per capita (approximate): USA about $85,000; UK about $50,000; Thailand about $7,500; Kenya about $2,000; Niger about $700.

Success criterion 2

Advantages and disadvantages of GDP

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Data is collected for almost every country, every year.It is an average: it hides inequality between rich and poor and between regions.
Easy to compare countries and see change over time.It ignores the informal economy (street sellers, subsistence farming), which is large in many developing countries.
Wealth pays for schools, hospitals and infrastructure, so it often links to quality of life.It says nothing about health, education, freedom or happiness.
Simple, single number that people understand.Exchange rates and the cost of living distort comparisons unless PPP is used; activities that damage the environment still add to GDP.

Success criterion 4

Why one indicator is never enough

  • Each indicator measures only one aspect of development; a country can score well on one and badly on another. For example, Saudi Arabia and Qatar have high incomes, but women’s rights and political freedoms are limited; Cuba and Sri Lanka have modest incomes but high life expectancy and literacy.
  • Averages hide inequality within a country (e.g. north and south Italy, or rich and poor areas of Nairobi).
  • Data may be out of date or unreliable, especially where there has been conflict.

That is why geographers use composite indices such as the Human Development Index (next lesson), which combine several indicators into one score.

Key terms

GDP
Gross Domestic Product: the total value of goods and services produced in a country in a year.
GDP per capita
GDP divided by the population.
PPP
Purchasing power parity: adjusts figures for differences in the cost of living.
Informal economy
Work that is not taxed or officially recorded, e.g. street selling.
Indicator
A measure used to show the level of development.
Composite index
A measure that combines several indicators into one score.
Exam-style question

Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of using GDP per capita to measure development. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: advantage identified, e.g. easy to compare countries; widely available data.
  • 1 mark: advantage developed.
  • 1 mark: disadvantage identified, e.g. it is an average so hides inequality; ignores the informal economy; does not measure quality of life.
  • 1 mark: disadvantage developed.

Model answer: An advantage of GDP per capita is that it is widely available for almost every country, so it is easy to compare countries’ wealth. A disadvantage is that it is an average, so it hides inequality: a country may have a high GDP per capita but most of the wealth may be held by a small number of people, and it says nothing about health or education.
