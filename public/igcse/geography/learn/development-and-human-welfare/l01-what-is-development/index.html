1. What is development and what factors contribute to a country's development?

Success criterion 1

What do we mean by development?

Development is the process of change that improves people’s lives. It used to be measured only by economic growth (how much money a country makes), but today geographers also look at social progress (health, education, equality) and political progress (freedom, human rights, honest government).

  • Economic development — growth in wealth, income and jobs, e.g. a rise in GDP per capita.
  • Social development — improvements in health care, education, housing, gender equality and access to clean water.
  • Political development — democracy, freedom of speech, the rule of law and low corruption.

Human welfare means the health, happiness and quality of life of people — whether their basic needs (food, water, shelter, health care, safety) are met and whether they have opportunities to improve their lives. A country can become richer without its people’s welfare improving, for example if the wealth is held by a small elite.

Success criterion 2

Factors that contribute to development and human welfare

FactorHow it helps development and welfare
EconomicJobs, wages, industry and trade create wealth; governments collect taxes to spend on schools, hospitals and infrastructure.
SocialEducation and health care create a skilled, healthy workforce; gender equality lets women work and lowers birth rates.
TechnologicalInternet, mobile phones, mechanised farming and medicine raise productivity and improve health (e.g. mobile banking in Kenya).
CulturalAttitudes to family size, women’s roles, education and religion affect birth rates, schooling and who can work.
Food securityWhen everyone has enough safe, nutritious food, people are healthier, children learn better and adults can work.
Water securityReliable, safe water reduces disease (e.g. cholera, diarrhoea) and saves time spent collecting water, especially for women and girls.

These factors are linked: for example, clean water improves health, which helps children attend school, which leads to better jobs and higher incomes.

Success criterion 3

The global pattern of development (World Bank, 2022)

The World Bank classifies every country by its Gross National Income (GNI) per capita. The groups used in 2022 (based on 2021 incomes) were:

GroupGNI per capitaExamples
High incomemore than $13,205USA, UK, Germany, Japan, Australia, Saudi Arabia
Upper-middle income$4,256–$13,205China, Brazil, Mexico, Thailand, South Africa
Lower-middle income$1,086–$4,255India, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, Vietnam
Low income$1,085 or lessNiger, Chad, Ethiopia, Afghanistan, Yemen

Describing the pattern: high-income countries are concentrated in North America, Western Europe, Australia and New Zealand, East Asia (Japan, South Korea) and the oil-rich Gulf states. Upper-middle-income countries are found across Latin America, much of Eastern Europe, China and parts of South-East Asia. Lower-middle-income countries are mainly in South Asia and parts of Africa. Low-income countries are concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa, especially the Sahel (e.g. Niger, Chad), plus a few conflict-affected countries elsewhere (e.g. Afghanistan, Yemen).

The thresholds rise every year with inflation: in 2026 the high-income threshold is more than $14,375.

Key terms

Development
The process of change that improves people’s lives economically, socially and politically.
Human welfare
The health, happiness and quality of life of people.
GNI per capita
The total income of a country’s people and businesses, including from abroad, divided by its population.
Food security
When all people always have access to enough safe, nutritious food.
Water security
Reliable access to enough safe water for drinking, sanitation and work.
Exam-style question

Explain two factors that contribute to a country’s level of development. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each factor (max 2), e.g. physical geography (climate, natural resources, landlocked); political stability/corruption; history (colonialism); education and health; trade and access to markets.
  • 1 mark for each explanation.

Model answer: One factor is natural resources: countries with oil or minerals can earn export income and invest in services, although this depends on good governance. Another factor is political stability. Countries affected by conflict or corruption find it hard to attract investment, and money is diverted from education and healthcare, slowing development.
