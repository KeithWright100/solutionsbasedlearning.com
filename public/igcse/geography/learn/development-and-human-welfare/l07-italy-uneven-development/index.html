7. Causes of uneven development within countries: Italy case study

Success criterion 1

Why are countries unequal inside?

Development is uneven within countries as well as between them. Common reasons are:

  • Core and periphery — investment, jobs and people concentrate in a core region (often around the capital or main ports), while remote areas fall behind.
  • Physical geography — mountains, poor soils, dry climates and hazards make some regions harder to farm, build in and reach.
  • Accessibility — regions near major markets and transport routes attract business.
  • History and politics — past government decisions, investment and conflict.
  • Cumulative causation — once a region is successful it attracts more investment and skilled workers (a ‘brain drain’ from poorer regions), so the gap grows.

Success criterion 3

Reasons for the differences

  • Physical — the North has the flat, fertile Po Valley, rivers for water and Alpine hydroelectric power. The South is mountainous (the Apennines), has a hotter, drier climate and poorer soils, and suffers from earthquakes and volcanoes (Vesuvius, Etna).
  • Location — the North is close to Europe’s core markets (France, Switzerland, Germany); the South is far from them, so transport costs are higher.
  • Historical — before unification in 1861 the South was a separate, mainly agricultural kingdom where land was held in huge estates. After unification, and again after 1945, industry grew mainly in the North.
  • Economic — the ‘industrial triangle’ of Milan, Turin and Genoa attracted car makers (e.g. Fiat), fashion, finance and engineering. Government spending through the Cassa per il Mezzogiorno (1950–1984) built roads and factories in the South, but some large plants such as the Taranto steelworks became ‘cathedrals in the desert’ with few linked jobs.
  • Social and political — organised crime (the Mafia in Sicily, the ‘Ndrangheta in Calabria and the Camorra in Campania) and corruption discourage investment. Many young, educated people leave for the North or abroad (brain drain), so the South loses skills.

Conclusion task: what impact do these differences have on people’s quality of life in the North and the South? Think about jobs, income, housing, services and opportunities for young people.

Key terms

Mezzogiorno
The South of Italy, including Sicily and Sardinia.
Industrial triangle
The area between Milan, Turin and Genoa where Italian industry grew.
Cassa per il Mezzogiorno
Government fund (1950–1984) to develop the South of Italy.
Brain drain
Skilled and educated people leaving a region or country.
Cumulative causation
A successful region attracts more investment, so the gap with poorer regions grows.
Exam-style question

Explain why the south of Italy is less developed than the north. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each developed reason (max 4), e.g. the north has better transport links to the rest of Europe; industry (cars, fashion) concentrated in the north (Milan, Turin); the south is more mountainous with poor soils and hot dry summers; organised crime and corruption deter investment; out-migration of young people from the south; lower levels of education.

Model answer: The north of Italy is closer to the rest of Europe and has good motorways and railways, so large industries such as car and fashion manufacturing have grown around Milan and Turin. The south (the Mezzogiorno) is more mountainous with poor soils and hot, dry summers, so it relies on low-paid farming. Organised crime and corruption discourage investment, and many young people migrate north for work, so the south has higher unemployment and lower incomes.
