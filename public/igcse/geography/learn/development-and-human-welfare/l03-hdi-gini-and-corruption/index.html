3. Measuring development: HDI, Gini coefficient and corruption

Success criterion 1

The Human Development Index (HDI)

The HDI was created by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in 1990. It combines three dimensions of development into one score between 0 and 1:

  • Health — life expectancy at birth.
  • Education — expected years of schooling for children, and mean (average) years of schooling for adults.
  • Standard of living — GNI per capita (PPP $).

Countries are grouped as very high (0.800+), high (0.700–0.799), medium (0.550–0.699) and low (below 0.550). In the 2025 report (2023 data), Iceland was top (0.972) and South Sudan bottom (0.388).

Strengths: it combines social and economic measures. Limitations: it does not measure inequality, freedom, corruption or the environment, and it is still an average for the whole country.

Success criterion 2

The Gini coefficient

The Gini coefficient measures inequality of income (or wealth) within a country. It is worked out from a Lorenz curve, which plots the share of income earned by the poorest 10%, 20%, 30% … of the population.

  • A score of 0 means perfect equality (everyone earns the same); 1 (or 100) means one person earns everything.
  • Most countries are between about 0.25 and 0.63. South Africa is one of the most unequal countries (about 0.63); Slovakia and Slovenia are among the most equal (about 0.24).

Limitation: a poor country can be very ‘equal’ because almost everyone is poor, so the Gini must be used alongside income and HDI. Some countries measure spending rather than income, which makes comparisons harder.

Success criterion 3

The Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI)

The CPI is published every year by the charity Transparency International. It ranks countries by how corrupt their public sector (government, police, courts, officials) is perceived to be.

  • It is built from surveys and expert assessments (e.g. business people and organisations such as the World Bank) — corruption is hidden, so it cannot be counted directly.
  • Scores run from 0 (highly corrupt) to 100 (very clean).
  • CPI 2025 covered 182 countries and territories: Denmark scored highest (89); Somalia and South Sudan lowest (9). Over two-thirds scored below 50.

Why it matters: corruption means money for schools, hospitals and roads is stolen or wasted, foreign companies are put off investing and aid may not reach the people who need it.

What do you notice? In general, countries with a high HDI also have high CPI scores (less corruption). But the Gini does not follow the same pattern: Niger and Norway have quite similar Gini scores even though their HDI is very different. This shows why we need several indices together.

Key terms

HDI
Human Development Index: combines life expectancy, education and income into a score from 0 to 1.
Gini coefficient
A measure of income inequality from 0 (equal) to 1 (unequal).
Lorenz curve
A graph showing how income is shared out across a population.
CPI
Corruption Perceptions Index: scores countries from 0 (very corrupt) to 100 (very clean).
Corruption
Dishonest use of power for personal gain, e.g. bribery.
Exam-style question

Explain why the Human Development Index (HDI) is a better measure of development than GDP alone. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: the HDI combines several indicators: life expectancy (health), education (years of schooling) and income (GNI per capita).
  • 1 mark: it therefore measures quality of life, not only wealth.
  • 1 mark: development, e.g. two countries with similar incomes can have very different health and education outcomes.

Model answer: The HDI is a composite measure that combines life expectancy, education (mean and expected years of schooling) and income per head into one score between 0 and 1. Because it includes health and education as well as wealth, it gives a better picture of people’s quality of life than GDP alone, which only measures economic output.
