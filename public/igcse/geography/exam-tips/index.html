IGCSE Geography

Examination Tips & Advice

How to read command words, manage your time, and structure answers that pick up every mark on offer — from one-mark definitions to the extended 8-mark question.

Extended answers

Develop your ability to answer 6, 8 and 12 mark exam questions

1. Know your command words

Every question is built around a command word. It tells you exactly what the examiner wants — get this wrong and you can write a page and still score low marks.

Command wordWhat it wants
State / IdentifyA short, precise fact. No explanation needed — and none rewarded.
DefineA clear, accurate meaning of a geographical term.
DescribeWhat a graph, map or photo shows. Use data and direction of change (e.g. “rose from 12°C to 19°C between June and August”). No reasons yet.
Explain / Suggest reasons forThe why. Give a cause, then say how it leads to the effect — a chain of reasoning, not just a description.
CompareSimilarities and differences, using linking words like “whereas” or “in contrast”. Never describe the two things separately.
Assess / Evaluate / To what extentWeigh up more than one side (e.g. costs vs benefits, or how far you agree) and reach a justified conclusion. This is where the extended-writing marks live.
Using Figure/Photo X…You must refer directly to that resource — quote data, name features, or describe what is visible.

2. Manage your time by the mark

Work on roughly one minute per mark, plus a couple of minutes spare to check your answers at the end. If a question is worth 2 marks, two short points is enough — don’t write four sentences on it and run out of time for the 8-mark question later in the paper.

  • Skim the whole paper first and note the marks for each question.
  • Spend the most time on the extended-writing question (usually 6–8 marks) — it’s where structure and depth are rewarded most.
  • For a “State two…” or “Give one reason…” question, stop once you’ve given that many points — extra points beyond what’s asked don’t earn extra marks and cost you time.

3. Structuring your answers

Short-answer and data-response questions

Match your answer length to the marks. For a describe question using a graph or map: state the overall trend, then quote specific data or a named location to back it up.

The extended (6–8 mark) question

Use a Point → Evidence → Explain → Link (PEEL) structure for each paragraph, and always finish with a conclusion that directly answers the question:

  • Point — make one clear point that answers the question.
  • Evidence — support it with a named example, place, or data from the resource booklet.
  • Explain — say how or why this supports your point (the geographical reasoning).
  • Link — tie it back to the question, especially the command word (e.g. “this is why the benefits outweigh the costs…”).

If the question says “with reference to a named example” or “case study”, you must name a real place — a generic, unnamed answer is capped well below full marks even if everything else is correct.

4. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Describing a resource instead of explaining it (or the reverse) — always check the command word before you start writing.
  • Forgetting to name a place, case study or example when the question asks for one.
  • Writing everything you know about a topic instead of directly answering the question asked.
  • Ignoring the resource booklet — many marks are only available if you use the map, graph, or photo you’ve been given.
  • Ending an extended answer without a conclusion that clearly answers “to what extent” or weighs up the question.

5. Final checklist before you submit

  • I’ve answered the command word, not just the topic.
  • I’ve used data or named examples wherever the question needs them.
  • My time matched the marks available for each question.
  • My extended answer has a clear structure and a conclusion.
  • I’ve checked I haven’t missed a question or part-question.
