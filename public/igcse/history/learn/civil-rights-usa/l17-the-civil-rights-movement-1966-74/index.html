17. The civil rights movement, 1966–74

Success criterion 1

Chicago and the Poor People’s Campaign

On 7 January 1966 the SCLC launched the Chicago Freedom Movement, focusing on housing, education and employment in Chicago’s ghettos. “Operation Breadbasket” boycotted businesses to make them employ more black people. Progress was slow; riots broke out in July and marchers were attacked with rocks and bottles in August. King reached an agreement with the mayor on public housing and fair mortgages, but it was not upheld.

The Poor People’s Campaign, announced in December 1967, aimed to help poor people of all races, demanding more welfare for the unemployed, a fair minimum wage and education for poor adults. Thousands set up shelters near Congress in June 1968, but without King the groups disagreed and many left before the planned march. It set up some free food programmes but was generally seen as a disaster.

Success criterion 2

The impact of King’s death

King was shot outside a motel by a white supremacist on 4 April 1968. Riots broke out across America, and it seemed his message of non-violence had been forgotten. King had mostly kept the movement’s different groups together; that unity was lost after his death.

Success criterion 3

Bussing and affirmative action

Johnson hoped government action would reduce extremism. The 1964 Act allowed Federal funding to be cut from schools resisting integration, and by 1968 60 per cent of Southern schools had desegregated.

PolicyUnder JohnsonUnder Nixon (1969–74)
BussingBuses took children between mainly black and mainly white areas to integrate schools. Highly controversial: some white families moved out of urban areas.Less committed to civil rights. Spoke against bussing on TV and tried, unsuccessfully, to overturn the 1971 Swann ruling that allowed it. In Milliken versus Bradley (1974) the Supreme Court said bussing was not allowed across school districts. By the end of 1974 Southern school integration had dropped to 40 per cent.
Affirmative actionSchools or businesses accepted African Americans even with lower qualifications. Very controversial among both black and white Americans.Continued it: the 1969 Philadelphia plan set building contractors targets, increasing black workers from 1 to 12 per cent. Declared constitutional in Griggs versus Duke Power Company (1971).

Nixon pushed the Equal Opportunity Act of 1972 through Congress, but generally did little for civil rights. Between 1968 and 1974 there were a few improvements in education and employment but little progress elsewhere: the movement was divided and leaderless, and there was no committed president. Many African Americans still lived in great poverty.

Key terms

Chicago Freedom Movement
The SCLC’s 1966 campaign against discrimination in Chicago.
Poor People’s Campaign
King’s 1967–68 campaign to help poor people of all races.
Bussing
Using buses to take children between mainly black and mainly white areas to integrate schools.
Affirmative action
Positive discrimination to give African Americans more equal opportunities in education and jobs.
Philadelphia plan
Nixon’s 1969 scheme setting building contractors targets to employ African Americans.
