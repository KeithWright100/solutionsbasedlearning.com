Success criterion 1

McCarthy’s rise and methods

Joseph McCarthy became Republican senator for Wisconsin in 1946. In February 1950, after the Hiss trial, he claimed to have a list of 205 members of the Communist Party working in the State Department; the number later fell to 57. The Tydings Committee found his accusations were untrue, but McCarthy called Senator Tydings “un-American” and Tydings lost his seat that year.

McCarthy’s claims explained why the Korean War and the Cold War were going badly, and the Republicans encouraged him to attack the Democrats in the 1952 election. After the Republicans won, President Eisenhower made him chairman of a Senate committee, and he led investigations into government departments for 2 years.

Methods:

  • Hearings, first private and then public, with bullying, aggressive questioning.
  • Attacking high-profile figures for publicity and trying to make them confess.
  • Using evidence fed to him by the FBI, and fabricating more.
  • Accusing critics of being communists or “soft on communism”.

Nobody accused by McCarthy was ever convicted of spying, but just being called for questioning could end a career.

Success criterion 2

McCarthy’s downfall

McCarthy’s accusations grew more extreme. He accused the popular war general George Marshall of helping the communist drive for world domination. In 1953 opinion polls still showed majority support, but this changed:

  • In spring 1954 the Army–McCarthy hearings were televised, and the public was shocked by his bullying.
  • The army sent evidence of McCarthy abusing his privileges to reporters, and more anti-McCarthy material appeared in the press.
  • On 9 March 1954 Ed Murrow devoted a whole episode of his TV show See It Now to criticising McCarthy.
  • Cold War tensions had eased slightly after the Korean War ended in July 1953.

On 2 December 1954 the Senate condemned McCarthy for “improper conduct” by 67 votes to 22. The media stopped reporting his work, and he died from alcoholism in 1957.

The impact of McCarthyism

Direct impactWider impact
Thousands lost their jobs or had their careers and lives damagedHysteria about the “enemy within” affected almost everyone
The State Department lost hundreds of talented peopleThousands of ordinary Americans gave the FBI information on people they suspected
Trade unions were seen as communist; fewer workers joined and strikes were rareMany people stopped talking about politics; politicians rarely campaigned for left-wing policies
The USA was seen abroad as intolerantAnxiety about communism lasted long after 1954; being “tough on communism” mattered in elections until the 1990s

Key terms

McCarthyism
The anti-communist hysteria of the 1950s.
Tydings Committee
A committee of Congress which found McCarthy’s 1950 accusations untrue.
Army–McCarthy hearings
Televised 1954 hearings into communism in the army, where the public saw McCarthy’s bullying.
See It Now
Ed Murrow’s TV show, which criticised McCarthy on 9 March 1954.
Condemned
Formally criticised; the Senate condemned McCarthy in December 1954.
