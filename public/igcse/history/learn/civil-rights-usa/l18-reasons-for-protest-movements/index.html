18. Why did protest movements grow in the 1960s?

Edexcel IGCSE History · A divided union: civil rights in the USA, 1945–74 · 4. Other protest movements: students, women, anti-Vietnam · Lesson 18 of 25

Learning Intention

To understand the reasons for the growth of protest movements in the USA in the 1960s.

Syllabus Statement

Reasons for the growth of protest movements in the 1960s.

Success criterion 1

The influence of civil rights

Many young people, black and white, had taken part in civil rights protests; the sit-ins of 1960–61 were effectively the first student protest of the decade. They used similar methods to protest about other parts of society. The civil rights movement also inspired other groups facing prejudice, such as women, Hispanic Americans and later Native Americans. One writer called it “the mother of all the movements of the sixties and seventies”.

Success criterion 2

Shared reasons

FactorExplanation
DisappointmentSociety changed in the Second World War (more women worked), but the 1950s saw a return to the pre-war situation. Kennedy’s election in 1960 promised change; his assassination in 1963 shocked many.
The teenagerIn the 1950s more teenagers rebelled and questioned their parents’ values, influenced by figures like Elvis Presley and James Dean. As they went to college in the 1960s, they formed protest groups.
The mediaFor the first time most homes had televisions. Coverage of Vietnam from 1965, of racism and of the protests themselves raised awareness, though it also created opposition.
Role of governmentSince Roosevelt’s 1930s policies, many expected the Federal government to tackle injustice, so they protested to make it act.
Baby boomersA post-war rise in the birth rate meant young people were a large share of the population.
Music and the artsSingers such as Bob Dylan and bands like the Beatles promoted alternative lifestyles.
ProsperityWealth was mainly enjoyed by white Protestant men, highlighting those left out.
Protest elsewhereProtests abroad, such as Paris in May 1968, inspired Americans.
Success criterion 3

Specific reasons

  • Women: the contraceptive pill and time-saving domestic appliances led more women to question their role.
  • Students: greater awareness of what they saw as unfair university rules.
  • Anti-war protestors: the horrors of the Vietnam War and US actions in it.

Key terms

Protest movement
A group campaigning to change society or government policy.
Baby boomers
People born in the rise in the birth rate after the Second World War.
Teenager
A young person in their teens; a separate youth culture emerged in the 1950s.
Counter-culture
A way of life rejecting traditional values, such as that of the hippies.
Contraceptive pill
A form of birth control taken by women, available from 1960.
