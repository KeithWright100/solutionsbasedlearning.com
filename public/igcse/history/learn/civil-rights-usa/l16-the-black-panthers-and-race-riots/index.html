Success criterion 1

The Black Panthers

Huey Newton and Bobby Seale set up the Black Panther Party in October 1966 in Oakland, California. Although it never had more than about 2,000 members in 25 cities, it was the best known and most feared Black Power group. Members wore black berets, trousers and leather jackets and often carried guns. Unlike most Black Power groups, it would work with white people who shared its beliefs.

Its aims were set out in a ten-point plan, including freedom to run the black community, full employment, decent housing, education teaching true black history, an end to police brutality and trials by juries from the black community. The media focused on “patrolling the pigs”: following police to prevent abuse of African Americans. Shoot-outs brought negative publicity, and the FBI watched members closely because of their socialist beliefs.

In the ghettos they:

  • organised medical clinics giving free healthcare;
  • ran breakfast clubs for children before school;
  • provided free shoes for poor families;
  • ran black history classes;
  • encouraged co-operation between gangs to reduce violence.
Success criterion 2

Race riots

In July 1964, just 2 weeks after the Civil Rights Act, a large riot took place in New York. Over the next 4 years there were 329 major riots in 257 cities: over 200 people were killed, tens of thousands arrested, and billions of dollars of damage done.

The largest was in Watts, Los Angeles, on 11–17 August 1965. Marquette Frye resisted arrest for drink-driving and rumours spread that police had attacked his family. Over 44,000 National Guard troops enforced a curfew. After 6 days, 34 people had died, over 1,000 were injured and nearly 4,000 arrested.

No group organised the riots; most rioters were young African Americans. City authorities blamed Black Power, but the 1968 Kerner Report blamed:

  • frustration and anger at poor ghetto conditions caused by segregation and discrimination;
  • the failure of police and city authorities to respond to problems;
  • unfair treatment and violence by police, which made the riots worse.

It warned the nation was “moving toward two societies, one black, one white — separate and unequal”. Its recommended social programmes were never followed up after Nixon became president in January 1969.

Key terms

Black Panther Party
A Black Power group set up in Oakland, California, in October 1966.
Ten-point plan
The Black Panthers’ list of aims, from employment to an end to police brutality.
Patrolling the pigs
The Panthers’ campaign of following police to prevent abuse.
Watts riot
The largest riot, in Los Angeles, 11–17 August 1965.
Kerner Report
The 1968 report into the causes of the race riots.
