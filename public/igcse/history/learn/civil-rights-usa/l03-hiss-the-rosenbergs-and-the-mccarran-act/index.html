3. Alger Hiss, the Rosenbergs and the McCarran Act

Success criterion 1

Alger Hiss

Alger Hiss worked in the State Department and had been an important adviser to President Roosevelt. In 1948, during a HUAC hearing, he was named as a member of a communist group. He denied being a communist, but Richard Nixon, a member of HUAC, insisted Hiss had been leaking information to the Soviet Union.

Hiss went to trial in 1949 and in January 1950 was sentenced to 5 years in prison for lying to the court. He was never convicted of spying, but many people thought he must have been a spy. Because Hiss had worked at the highest level of government, this seemed to prove Hoover right: communist spies were everywhere.

Success criterion 2

The Rosenbergs

DateEvent
February 1950Klaus Fuchs is arrested in Britain for passing information on the atomic bomb to the Soviets. He confesses, is sentenced to 14 years and names other spies, including David Greenglass.
1950Greenglass is arrested in the USA and names his sister and brother-in-law, Ethel and Julius Rosenberg.
March 1951The Rosenbergs deny all charges at their trial. The evidence is weak, but they are found guilty and sentenced to death.
19 June 1953The Rosenbergs are executed.

The weak evidence led to large protests against their sentence, but most Americans blamed the Rosenbergs for helping the Soviets build atomic bombs, which they thought had led to North Korea’s invasion of South Korea.

Success criterion 3

The impact: the McCarran Act

News of the Soviet atomic bomb arrived during the Hiss trial. Together these made US fears of communists greater than ever. In August 1950 Congress passed the McCarran Internal Security Act, making all communist organisations register with the government. It was strengthened in 1952:

  • Communists were not allowed US passports.
  • Communists were not allowed to work in certain jobs.
  • In an emergency, anyone suspected of “subversion” could be held in a detention camp without trial.

President Truman tried to stop the act, but few in Congress agreed with him. People became suspicious of colleagues and neighbours and afraid of being accused themselves.

Key terms

Alger Hiss
A State Department official sentenced in 1950 for lying about communist links.
Klaus Fuchs
A scientist arrested in Britain in 1950 for passing atomic secrets to the Soviets.
Ethel and Julius Rosenberg
A couple executed in 1953 for spying for the Soviet Union.
McCarran Act
The 1950 Internal Security Act making communist organisations register with the government.
Subversion
Trying to weaken or destroy a government or political system.
