8. Little Rock, 1957

Edexcel IGCSE History · A divided union: civil rights in the USA, 1945–74 · 2. Civil rights in the 1950s · Lesson 8 of 25

Learning Intention

To understand why school desegregation was resisted in the South, the events at Little Rock and their significance.

Syllabus Statement

Opposition to the civil rights movement. Events at Little Rock, 1957.

Success criterion 1

Southern resistance

By 1957 not one school in the Deep South had been integrated. Southern senators signed the Southern Manifesto, saying Brown was an abuse of the Supreme Court’s power. State governors supported White Citizens’ Councils and some closed public schools completely. President Eisenhower did nothing to enforce the decision until Little Rock forced him to act.

Success criterion 2

The Little Rock Nine

DateEvent
1957A Federal Court rules that the all-white Central High School in Little Rock, Arkansas, must integrate. 25 African American students are selected, but threats mean only nine still want to go: the Little Rock Nine.
3 SeptemberGovernor Orval Faubus announces on television that state troops will stop the students entering, “for their own protection”.
4 SeptemberThe nine are surrounded by a screaming white mob and turned away by troops. Elizabeth Eckford, aged 15, faces the crowd alone because her family had no telephone to get the message.
12 SeptemberEisenhower meets Faubus. A Federal judge orders the troops removed.
23 SeptemberPolice get the students in, but a riot outside means they are sent home.
24–25 SeptemberEisenhower sends 1,200 Federal troops and takes command of the state troops. On 25 September the nine finally attend classes.

The nine were protected by the army all year but still faced harassment. In September 1958 Faubus closed all Little Rock high schools; a vote showed 72 per cent of citizens were against integration. The Supreme Court stood by Brown, and schools reopened in August 1959. Significant integration in Little Rock only came in the 1970s.

Success criterion 3

The significance of Little Rock

  • It was the only time the president directly intervened to enforce school integration, but it showed the Federal government could overrule state governments.
  • Publicity was international: it damaged the USA’s reputation and made it look hypocritical when it criticised other countries. Eisenhower said “our enemies are gloating over this incident”.
  • It showed campaigners that Supreme Court victories were not enough to end segregation, and that media coverage was essential.

Key terms

Southern Manifesto
A statement by Southern senators that the Brown decision was an abuse of the Supreme Court’s power.
Little Rock Nine
The nine African American students who integrated Central High School in 1957.
Orval Faubus
The Governor of Arkansas who used state troops to stop integration.
Integrate
Become part of a group or society, or help someone to do this.
Federal troops
Soldiers under the command of the president rather than a state governor.
