25. The impact of Watergate

Edexcel IGCSE History · A divided union: civil rights in the USA, 1945–74 · 5. Nixon and Watergate · Lesson 25 of 25

Learning Intention

To understand the impact of Watergate on Nixon, on US politics and the new laws that were passed.

Syllabus Statement

The impact of the Watergate scandal on Nixon, US politics and new laws.

Success criterion 1

The impact on Nixon

Watergate ended Nixon’s political career. He said he resigned for the good of the country, but impeachment seemed certain. Thanks to Ford’s pardon he never faced trial, but many believed he planned the burglary as well as covering it up.

The recordings revealed how he spoke about others, including verbal attacks on minorities such as Jews and Italians. He never apologised and seemed to believe presidents should not be answerable to the law.

His achievements, such as setting up the Environment Protection Agency, enforcing school desegregation and improving relations with China and the Soviet Union, were ignored. People called him “Tricky Dicky”. He later regained some respect as an author and foreign-affairs expert, but he often tops polls as the worst US president.

Success criterion 2

The impact on US politics and the media

  • Over 30 officials received prison sentences, causing a massive change in government staff.
  • The Republicans lost many seats at the next elections. In 1976 the Democrats chose the little-known Jimmy Carter, seen as honest and not part of “the Washington set”; some historians argue voters have valued honesty over capability ever since.
  • Americans lost trust in government. Politicians published financial records and opened meetings to prove honesty, but fewer people voted or wanted to become politicians.
  • The media: Woodward and Bernstein were praised and journalists looked for more scandals, which made politicians more accountable, but some published “scandals” without evidence.
  • The suffix “-gate” is now used for scandals worldwide.
Success criterion 3

New laws

YearLawWhat it did
1973House and Senate Open Meeting RulesAll committee meetings to be open to the public
1973War Powers ActStopped a president going to war without Congress’s approval
1974Election Campaign ActSet limits on campaign spending and contributions
1974Freedom of Information ActGave people the right to see government documents in which they featured
1974Privacy ActSet rules on how government could collect information on individuals
1974Congressional Budget Control ActSet rules on how the president could use government money

These laws aimed to stop another Watergate and limit presidents acting without Congress. Restoring faith in government was harder to measure, but Watergate removed the “myth” of the president’s honesty and morality.

Key terms

Tricky Dicky
A nickname for Nixon after Watergate (“Dicky” is short for Richard).
War Powers Act
A 1973 law stopping a president going to war without Congress’s approval.
Election Campaign Act
A 1974 law limiting election campaign spending and contributions.
Freedom of Information Act
A 1974 law giving people the right to see government documents about them.
-gate
A suffix added to the names of scandals since Watergate.
