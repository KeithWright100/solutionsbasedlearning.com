9. The Civil Rights Act, 1957

Edexcel IGCSE History · A divided union: civil rights in the USA, 1945–74 · 2. Civil rights in the 1950s · Lesson 9 of 25

Learning Intention

To understand the events leading to the 1957 Civil Rights Act, the opposition to it and its significance.

Syllabus Statement

Success criterion 1

Pressure for a law

By 1957 the Supreme Court had acted on education and transport, but the president and Congress had not. Truman’s civil rights bill of 1948 had been blocked by Republicans and Dixiecrats (pro-segregation Southern Democrats).

Republican President Eisenhower declared support for civil rights but seemed unwilling to act. He believed changing the law would not work until attitudes changed, and he respected states’ rights. However, Brown, the Montgomery Bus Boycott and the violent reactions to them put pressure on him and Congress to pass a Federal law.

Success criterion 2

Opposition and terms

A bill in 1956 failed because of Southern resistance. Leading Dixiecrat Strom Thurmond later spoke in a filibuster for 24 hours and 18 minutes. In 1957 a revised bill was weakened but, partly thanks to Lyndon Johnson, the Democrats’ leader in the Senate, it passed. Eisenhower signed it on 9 September 1957. It aimed to increase the number of African American voters by:

  • setting up the US Commission on Civil Rights to investigate how African Americans were prevented from voting;
  • allowing Federal courts to prosecute states that tried to prevent people from voting.

Significance

The 1957 and 1960 Acts only increased black voter registration by 3 per centLed to the 1960 Civil Rights Act, with Federal inspections of voter registration and penalties for obstructing it

By the end of the 1950s there were signs that a civil rights movement, rather than a few separate events, was building, but there was still a long way to go.

Key terms

Dixiecrats
A separate, pro-segregation party formed by Southern Democrats in 1948.
Filibuster
A tactic to stop a vote on a bill, usually by talking until the debate’s time limit is reached.
Civil Rights Act 1957
The first Federal civil rights law in 82 years, aimed at increasing African American voters.
US Commission on Civil Rights
A body set up in 1957 to investigate how African Americans were prevented from voting.
Civil Rights Act 1960
A law introducing Federal inspections of voter registration and penalties for obstruction.
