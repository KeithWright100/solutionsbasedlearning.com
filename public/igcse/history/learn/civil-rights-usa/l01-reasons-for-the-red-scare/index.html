Success criterion 1

How the US government works

The USA is a federation of individual states. Each state has its own government, with a governor, a legislature and a supreme court, and controls most of the laws that apply within that state, such as education and the death penalty. The Federal government, based in Washington DC, governs the whole country. People defending state laws against a Federal law often used the cry of “states’ rights”.

BranchWho
Legislative (makes laws)Congress: the Senate and the House of Representatives
Executive (carries out laws)The president, helped by the vice president, the cabinet and government departments (the president’s administration)
Judicial (evaluates laws)The Supreme Court and other Federal courts, which make sure laws are constitutional

The system was designed to limit the power of each group. Laws passed by Congress can be vetoed by the president or the Supreme Court, but Congress can impeach the president and Supreme Court judges. An amendment to the Constitution needs a two-thirds majority in both Houses of Congress and must be ratified by three-quarters of the states. Key amendments include the 14th (1868: equal protection of the law for all citizens) and the 15th (1870: African American men given the vote).

Success criterion 2

Capitalism, communism and the first Red Scare

The USA is a capitalist democracy: governments are chosen in regular elections and businesses are privately owned, with big differences in wealth. In a communist state, the only candidates are communists and businesses are owned and run by the government, usually with more equality.

After communists took over Russia in 1917, a wave of anti-communist violence led to the first Red Scare of 1919–20. Thousands of suspected communists were arrested and some Russian immigrants were sent back to Russia. Fear of communism reduced after 1920 but never disappeared. Some Russian communists had aimed for world revolution, while the USA wanted a capitalist world to trade with. Communists were known as “reds”, after the main colour of the Soviet flag.

Decide which system each statement describes.

Success criterion 3

The Cold War increases fear

In 1945 the USA and the Soviet Union emerged as the two most powerful nations. Their distrust developed into the Cold War, and a series of events seemed to show that communism was spreading.

DateEvent
1945Communist governments set up in Eastern European countries liberated by the Red Army. The USA tests and uses an atomic bomb without telling the Soviet Union.
1946Communists in Greece try to take over the government; the USA sends money to remove them.
March 1947The Truman Doctrine promises help to any country resisting a communist takeover; the Marshall Plan gives millions of dollars of aid to Europe.
1948–49Stalin blocks supply routes into West Berlin; the Western allies fly supplies in until he reopens them in May 1949.
1949Communists win the Chinese civil war. In August the Soviet Union tests its own atomic bomb.
1950–53Communist North Korea invades South Korea; UN forces, mostly American, fight until July 1953.

By 1950 all of Eastern Europe had communist governments. The Soviet atomic bomb shocked Americans: their biggest enemy now had a weapon of huge destruction. Many Americans, including people in government, became convinced the Soviet Union wanted world domination, and that a communist takeover of the USA itself was possible.

Key terms

Federal government
The government for the whole USA, based in Washington DC.
States’ rights
The idea that each state should control its own affairs without interference from the Federal government.
Impeach
Prosecute a person in government for misconduct while in office.
Capitalism
A system in which businesses belong mostly to private owners, not the government.
Communism
A system in which the government controls the production of goods and there is no privately owned property.
Red Scare
The fear of communists (“reds”) in the USA.
