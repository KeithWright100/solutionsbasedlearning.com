10. The sit-ins and the freedom rides

Success criterion 1

The Greensboro sit-ins

On 1 February 1960, four African American students sat at the whites-only lunch counter in Woolworth’s in Greensboro, North Carolina, and waited to be served until the shop closed. The next day they returned with 25 more students; on 3 February 80 more joined, and by 4 February there were over 300.

Sit-ins spread across the South and even to some Northern states: over 70,000 people took part. Protestors were insulted, spat at and had food thrown at them, but never responded with violence. Thousands were arrested, leading to the tactic of “jail not bail”. The NAACP, CORE and the SCLC organised boycotts of shops and trained students to cope with harassment.

Ella Baker arranged a meeting at Shaw University on 15 April 1960, where the Student Nonviolent Co-ordinating Committee (SNCC, “snick”) was set up.

Success criterion 2

The significance of the sit-ins

  • Most businesses, including Woolworth’s, desegregated their eating facilities; by the end of 1960 over 420 towns and cities had some desegregated lunch counters.
  • They were highly visible and gained huge publicity and sympathy; even Eisenhower said he was “deeply sympathetic”.
  • They were the first truly mass protest and the first with significant numbers of white protestors.
  • They started the student protests of the 1960s and led to the SNCC.
  • They were more confrontational than boycotts and could be used at beaches, hotels and libraries.
Success criterion 3

The freedom rides

In December 1960 the Supreme Court confirmed that interstate transport and its facilities, such as waiting rooms, should be desegregated. In 1961 CORE planned freedom rides to show these rulings were not being followed; founder James Farmer admitted they aimed to create a crisis that would force the government to act.

DateEvent
4 May 1961The first freedom riders leave Washington DC on two buses heading south.
14 May 1961At Anniston, Alabama, a mob led by a local Klan leader attacks the first bus; it is firebombed outside the city. The second bus’s passengers are beaten, and beaten again in Montgomery, where police chief “Bull” Connor tells police to do nothing.
20 May 1961Hundreds of Klan members attack riders and the press in Montgomery; it is the riders who are arrested.
Summer 1961Over 60 freedom rides; over 300 riders jailed in Jackson, Mississippi, alone.
End of 1961President Kennedy threatens to send US marshals; states integrate bus facilities and the rides end.

Key terms

Sit-in
A protest in which people refuse to leave a place until their demands are considered or agreed to.
CORE
The Congress of Racial Equality, a mostly Northern civil rights group with many white members.
SCLC
The Southern Christian Leadership Council, a civil rights group linked to Martin Luther King.
SNCC
The Student Nonviolent Co-ordinating Committee, set up in April 1960.
Freedom rides
1961 bus journeys organised by CORE to test the desegregation of interstate transport.
