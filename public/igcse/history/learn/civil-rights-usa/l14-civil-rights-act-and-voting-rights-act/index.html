14. The Civil Rights Act 1964 and Voting Rights Act 1965

Success criterion 1

The Civil Rights Act, 1964

The bill was struggling when Kennedy was assassinated. Johnson used the shock of his death, and every method he could, to persuade and bully Congress. Dixiecrats made a 54-day filibuster attempt, but Johnson signed the Act on 2 July 1964.

  • Segregation and discrimination in public places, education and businesses banned.
  • Voter registration tests had to be fair and the same for black and white people.
  • Discrimination in employment banned; the Equal Opportunities Commission set up to investigate it.
  • The Federal government could remove funding from state projects that discriminated.

It also banned discrimination on the basis of gender, religion or national origin. It ended legal segregation, but was hard to enforce, especially in the Deep South. Many Southerners were angered while many African Americans felt it did not go far enough.

Success criterion 2

The Voting Rights Act, 1965

Johnson had removed voting sections from the 1964 bill so it would pass, but Freedom Summer and Selma showed the need for action. He got the bill through Congress in record time; it became law on 6 August 1965.

  • One fair national test for voter registration for everyone.
  • States banned from setting their own voting qualifications unless approved by the Federal government.
  • Federal examiners appointed to supervise voter registration.

Many historians believe it was even more important than the 1964 Act: African Americans began to elect their own representatives, and politicians had to consider their issues to win their votes.

Success criterion 3

Why was legislation passed?

Truman said in 1947 that he wanted civil rights laws, but it took nearly 20 years. The protests were essential:

Protests were…As a result, this…The effect was…
Well led and organised; highly visible with large numbers; within the law; targeted at areas likely to get an extreme reactionHighlighted the issues; showed the numbers supporting civil rights; provoked violence showing opponents did not operate within the lawIncreased awareness and support; huge publicity in the USA and abroad, putting pressure on the Federal government to act

Other factors included the attitudes of Kennedy and Johnson and Johnson’s political skill, more members of Congress wanting to “do the right thing”, and more radical activists who seemed to threaten violence if laws were not passed.

Key terms

Civil Rights Act 1964
The law that banned segregation and discrimination in public places, education, businesses and employment.
Voting Rights Act 1965
The law setting one fair national voting test and Federal supervision of voter registration.
Equal Opportunities Commission
The body set up in 1964 to investigate discrimination in employment.
Federal examiners
Officials appointed to supervise voter registration in the states.
Legal segregation
Segregation required by law, which the 1964 Act ended.
