24. The Watergate scandal and Ford’s pardon

Edexcel IGCSE History · A divided union: civil rights in the USA, 1945–74 · 5. Nixon and Watergate · Lesson 24 of 25

Learning Intention

To understand how the break-in became a scandal, why Nixon resigned and why Gerald Ford pardoned him.

Syllabus Statement

Key features of the Watergate scandal. Why Gerald Ford gave a presidential pardon.

Success criterion 1

From break-in to scandal

On 8 January 1973 the trial of the five burglars, Hunt and Liddy began, for conspiracy, burglary and wiretapping; all seven were convicted. On 19 March 1973, before sentencing, burglar James McCord wrote to Judge Sirica claiming leading White House officials had pressured the defendants to plead guilty and lie in court. This really started the scandal.

  • 17 April: Nixon announced a new investigation led by special prosecutor Archibald Cox.
  • 30 April: Nixon sacked Dean and Haldeman; Ehrlichman resigned.
  • The Senate set up the Select Committee on Presidential Campaign Activities on 7 February, led by Sam Ervin. Its hearings, televised from May, gripped the nation. In 5 days of testimony, John Dean revealed that the president was involved in the cover-up. Nixon refused to be questioned.
Success criterion 2

The recordings and resignation

A witness revealed that all conversations in Nixon’s office had been recorded since 1971. Cox and the committee fought for access; Nixon refused, citing national security. He released edited scripts in October 1973 and more in April 1974, with swearing replaced by “expletive deleted”, which shocked the public.

DateEvent
24 July 1974The Supreme Court orders Nixon to supply all the recordings.
5 August 1974The “smoking gun”: a recording from 23 June 1972 shows Nixon tried to stop the FBI investigating the break-in, proving he covered it up from the start.
9 August 1974Before he can be impeached, Nixon resigns. Gerald Ford becomes president.
Success criterion 3

Ford’s pardon

Ford said “our long national nightmare is over”. His calm and honesty helped restore some confidence. But a month later, on 8 September 1974, he pardoned Nixon for any crimes he may have committed, so Nixon would never face a criminal trial.

Reasons for the pardonWhy it was controversial
Ford believed it was the only way the USA could move on, rather than facing months or years of trialsMillions wanted Nixon brought to justice
He said Nixon would be “cruelly and excessively penalized” and his concern was the country’s futureThings remained unknown, such as whether Nixon knew of the burglary in advance
Some suspected Ford and Nixon had made a deal (Ford denied it); it was one reason Ford lost the 1976 election

Key terms

Special prosecutor
A lawyer appointed to investigate a particular case, such as Archibald Cox.
Select Committee on Presidential Campaign Activities
The Senate committee, led by Sam Ervin, whose televised hearings exposed Watergate.
Expletive deleted
The phrase that replaced swearing in the released scripts of Nixon’s recordings.
Smoking gun
The 23 June 1972 recording proving Nixon covered up Watergate from the start.
Pardon
Officially allow someone to go free without being punished for a crime.
