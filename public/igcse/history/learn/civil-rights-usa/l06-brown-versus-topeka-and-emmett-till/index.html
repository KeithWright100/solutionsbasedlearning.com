Success criterion 1

Brown versus Topeka

State spending per pupil could be over four times more for white pupils, so from the 1940s the NAACP (National Association for the Advancement of Colored People) challenged segregated education through the courts.

DateEvent
1951Linda Brown’s parents apply for her to attend the white-only Sumner Elementary school in Topeka, Kansas, much closer to home. The Board of Education rejects her because she is black.
1951–52With NAACP support, the case is rejected by the local court because of Plessy versus Ferguson. It goes to the Supreme Court, combined with four similar cases.
December 1952The judges cannot agree, so a retrial is needed. Earl Warren becomes Chief Justice.
17 May 1954NAACP lawyers led by Thurgood Marshall argue separate schools damage black children psychologically. The Supreme Court rules unanimously that school segregation is unconstitutional.
1955A second ruling says desegregation should happen “with all deliberate speed”.

In the Deep South the day of the decision became known as “Black Monday”. The first White Citizens’ Council was set up in Indianola, Mississippi, and many more followed, organising protests to resist integration. Many members joined the Ku Klux Klan, which revived across the South, burning crosses outside homes and beating, lynching and shooting victims. From January 1956 it also used bombs.

Success criterion 3

The murder of Emmett Till

Emmett Till, aged 14, from Chicago, visited relatives in Money, Mississippi, in summer 1955. On 24 August, after a dare, he went into Bryant’s grocery store; Carolyn Bryant claimed he flirted with her. Four days later he was taken from his great-uncle’s house by her husband Roy Bryant and Roy’s half-brother J.W. Milam, beaten, shot and thrown into the river.

His mother, Mamie Bradley, insisted his body was sent to Chicago and left the coffin open. Photographs shocked many white people as well as African Americans. The all-white, all-male jury took just over an hour to find the two men “not guilty”. Months later they confessed in a magazine article, knowing they could not be tried again.

Impact: it was the publicity that made the case different. Many Northern whites saw extreme Southern racism for the first time, and many young African Americans identified with Till and were motivated to join later protests. Some historians see it as the catalyst for the civil rights movement.

Key terms

NAACP
National Association for the Advancement of Colored People, a civil rights campaign group.
Brown versus Topeka
The 1954 Supreme Court case which ruled school segregation unconstitutional.
Deep South
Georgia, Alabama, South Carolina, Mississippi and Louisiana, where segregation covered all aspects of life.
White Citizens’ Councils
Groups set up after 1954 to resist integration.
Ku Klux Klan
A racist organisation, started in 1865, that believes in the superiority of white Protestant Christians.
White flight
White people leaving areas with large numbers of black residents.
