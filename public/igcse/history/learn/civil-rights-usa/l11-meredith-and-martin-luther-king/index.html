Success criterion 1

The James Meredith case

DateEvent
May 1961James Meredith, an African American, is rejected by the University of Mississippi (“Ole Miss”). The NAACP helps him take the case to court.
June 1962The Supreme Court orders the university to admit him, but it refuses. The governor says no school will be integrated while he is governor.
September 1962The state legislature passes a law barring anyone convicted of a “felony offense”; Meredith is suspended because of an earlier conviction for “false voter registration”.
29–30 September 1962Kennedy orders those obstructing the law to desist and sends hundreds of Federal marshals to escort Meredith. A riot follows: two civilians die and 300 are injured. Over 2,000 Federal troops stop the rioting.
1 October 1962Meredith registers at the university, guarded for the next year until he graduates.

Significance: the case showed the president would take direct action to enforce Supreme Court rulings. After this, African American students were rarely prevented from attending integrated universities, though they often had a difficult time.

Success criterion 2

Martin Luther King

King was born in 1929 into a middle-class family, followed his father as a Baptist minister and gained a doctorate at Boston University. The Montgomery Bus Boycott made him well known. His qualities included:

  • being well educated and a passionate, well-spoken speaker;
  • continually emphasising non-violence;
  • working with people of all races;
  • as a Baptist minister, the respect of many African Americans and Christians, as he emphasised Christian values and the Bible.

Inspired by Gandhi, he believed in non-violent direct action: boycotts and marches that were often confrontational and dangerous. He knew publicity was essential, so planned protests he knew would provoke a violent response. He was arrested and imprisoned many times. His speeches were his most important contribution, winning white support and showing respect for the Constitution.

Success criterion 3

King’s later work and importance

After 1965 King turned to discrimination in the North, starting in Chicago in 1966. He became frustrated that protest and negotiation made little progress in ending discrimination. His views became more socialist, which lost him some support, and he focused on poverty, unemployment and housing for all Americans. On 4 April 1968, while supporting a workers’ strike in Memphis, Tennessee, he was assassinated. He never gave up non-violence.

Today roads and schools are named after him and Martin Luther King Day is a public holiday. However, people can exaggerate his importance and forget the many others involved in civil rights protests.

Key terms

Ole Miss
The nickname of the University of Mississippi.
Federal marshals
Federal law officers sent by the president to enforce court rulings.
Non-violent direct action
Peaceful but confrontational protest, such as boycotts and marches.
Mahatma Gandhi
Indian campaigner whose non-violent disobedience inspired King.
Assassinate
Murder an important person.
