Success criterion 1

Richard Nixon

Nixon was elected to the House of Representatives in 1947 and became well known during the Red Scare for sending Alger Hiss for trial. He was elected to the Senate in 1950, was Eisenhower’s Vice President (1952–60), narrowly lost to Kennedy in 1960, and became president on 20 January 1969.

He was very intelligent and hard-working, but could be deeply suspicious, even paranoid, holding resentments against people he thought were against him while rewarding loyalty. His campaign chairman remembered him as “extraordinarily sensitive to criticism” and “frantic over leaks”. These characteristics led to his fall.

Success criterion 2

The Plumbers and CREEP

DateEvent
June 1971The New York Times publishes an article based on the Pentagon Papers, secret documents about the Vietnam War leaked by Daniel Ellsberg. Nixon is furious.
1971Nixon sets up the “White House Plumbers” to stop leaks. In September some break into the office of Ellsberg’s psychiatrist looking for information to discredit him.
1972Nixon sets up the Committee to Re-elect the President (CREEP), directed by former Attorney General John Mitchell. A secret fund pays the Plumbers to spy on and damage Democratic rivals.
1972The Plumbers persuade a newspaper to publish damaging stories about Edmund Muskie’s wife; he breaks down in tears and loses the Democratic primaries.
28 May 1972Bugging devices are installed in the Democratic National Committee offices in the Watergate complex.
17 June 1972Five men are arrested trying to repair the bugs.
Success criterion 3

Uncovering the truth

The break-in got little attention at first, but Bob Woodward and Carl Bernstein of the Washington Post found connections to CREEP. Mitchell denied any link, but with help from sources inside the FBI they found more evidence linking the break-in to CREEP and then the White House. They kept the story in the public eye and persuaded Democrats in Congress that more investigation was needed.

The FBI quickly found links to CREEP and that Howard Hunt and G. Gordon Liddy (both Plumbers) had planned the break-in. Over 2 years it revealed a campaign to spy on and sabotage Democrats, financed by donations to CREEP from people who thought they were funding a legal campaign.

At first most people believed the burglars acted alone. When Nixon said White House lawyer John Dean had found no White House staff involved, people believed him, and Nixon easily won the November 1972 election.

Key terms

Pentagon Papers
Secret documents about the Vietnam War leaked to the New York Times in 1971.
White House Plumbers
A group set up by Nixon to stop leaks, which also spied on his opponents.
CREEP
The Committee to Re-elect the President, set up in 1972.
Bugging device
A small electronic device for secretly listening to conversations.
Primaries
Elections that decide which person will be a party’s candidate for president.
