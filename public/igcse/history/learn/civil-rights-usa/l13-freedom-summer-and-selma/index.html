Success criterion 1

The Freedom Summer, 1964

Mississippi had the lowest percentage of African Americans registered to vote: fewer than 7 per cent. Before the November 1964 presidential election, the NAACP, CORE and SNCC launched Freedom Summer.

  • A new party, the Mississippi Freedom Democratic Party (MFDP), had over 80,000 members and 68 elected to join the national Democratic convention, challenging Mississippi’s all-white Democratic Party.
  • The MFDP ran classes to help people pass voter registration tests and opened 30 Freedom Schools teaching black history and politics.
  • The SNCC recruited around 1,000 volunteers, mostly young, white college students from well-off Northern families, knowing violence against them would make headlines.

The Klan attacked campaigners and bombed churches and homes. On 21 June 1964, three CORE members, Michael Schwerner, Andrew Goodman and James Chaney, were arrested by a policeman who was a Klan member. After their release they disappeared; their bodies were found six weeks later.

Success criterion 3

Selma and voting rights, 1965

Dallas County, Alabama, had an even worse rate of black voter registration than Mississippi. In January 1965 local groups invited King and the SCLC to Selma. Two months of protests led to violence, many arrests and the murder of a protestor.

DateEvent
7 March 1965“Bloody Sunday”: a march to Montgomery is stopped at the Edmund Pettus Bridge; state troops and police attack with tear gas, horses, clubs and electric cattle sticks. Footage is broadcast worldwide.
9 March 1965A second march is called off because King will not let marchers confront the police.
17 March 1965President Johnson submits a voting rights bill to Congress.
21–25 March 1965Escorted by the Alabama National Guard, under Johnson’s control, the march takes place; King leads around 25,000 people to Montgomery.
August 1965Congress passes the Voting Rights Act.

Some historians believe Selma is where the movement began to split, as many protestors, including SNCC members, wanted to confront the police and began to question non-violence.

Key terms

Freedom Summer
The 1964 campaign to increase African American voter registration in Mississippi.
MFDP
The Mississippi Freedom Democratic Party, set up during Freedom Summer.
Freedom Schools
Schools opened in 1964 to teach black history and politics.
Bloody Sunday
7 March 1965, when police attacked marchers at the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma.
Capitol
A building where a legislature or parliament meets.
