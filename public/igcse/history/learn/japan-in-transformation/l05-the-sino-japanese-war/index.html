5. The Sino-Japanese War, 1894–95

Success criterion 1

Rivalry over Korea

Korea, the “Hermit Kingdom”, paid tribute to China. Many Japanese, especially ex-samurai, wanted to intervene. In 1876 Japan used gunboat diplomacy, as Perry had, to force Korea open: the Treaty of Kanghwa opened three ports and recognised Korea as independent, to detach it from China. The army under Yamagata Aritomo believed Japan needed some control of Korea for its security, even if that meant war.

In 1894 the Tong Hak rebellion broke out in Korea. Both China and Japan sent troops; both refused to leave. On 23 July 1894 Japanese troops seized the king’s palace and made him declare independence from China. Two days later Japan sank a Chinese troopship, the Kowshing.

Success criterion 2

The war

DateEvent
1 August 1894War declared.
16–17 September 1894Quick Japanese victories at Pyongyang and over Chinese warships at the Yalu River.
21 November 1894Port Arthur in Manchuria falls after a nearly 3-month siege.
12 February 1895The Chinese fleet collapses at Weihaiwei.
17 April 1895The Treaty of Shimonoseki ends the war.

Japan won decisively on land and sea, surprising the world; a British officer praised its army as admirably organised and equipped. But at Port Arthur Japanese soldiers massacred up to 60,000 Chinese over several days, damaging Japan’s international reputation.

Success criterion 3

The Treaty of Shimonoseki

  • China recognised Korea as independent.
  • The Liaotung Peninsula, Formosa (Taiwan) and the Pescadores Islands went to Japan.
  • Four new Chinese ports opened to Japanese trade.
  • Japan gained most-favoured nation rights in China.
  • China paid an indemnity of 200 million taels.

Key terms

Hermit Kingdom
A name for Korea because of its policy of isolation.
Tong Hak
A Korean religious group whose 1894 rebellion led to war.
Port Arthur
A Manchurian fortress captured by Japan in 1894 and again in 1905.
Treaty of Shimonoseki
The 1895 treaty that ended the Sino-Japanese War.
Indemnity
Money paid as compensation, for example after losing a war.
