11. The army takes control, 1932–36

Learning Intention

To understand how the army gained control of Japanese politics between 1932 and 1936.

Syllabus Statement

The growth of militarism: the May 1932 incident, army factions and the 26 February 1936 coup.

Success criterion 1

The May 1932 incident

By March 1932 parliament was losing influence and power was passing to extreme right-wing nationalists. Prime Minister Inukai Tsuyoshi (from December 1931) tried to restore civilian control of the army and opened secret talks with China. On 15 May 1932 ultranationalist army cadets and naval officers shot him dead at his official residence.

This ended more than a decade of party government. Admirals Saito (1932–34) and Okada (1934–36) led moderate governments, but gave the army time to sort out its internal differences.

Army factions

Imperial Way faction (Kodo-ha)Control faction (Tosei-ha)
Supported by many young officers; more extremeWider support among senior officers; more conservative
Wanted a “Showa Restoration”, influenced by Kita Ikki, rejecting individualism, liberalism, materialism and communismDisapproved of the Imperial Way faction’s radical excesses and insubordination
Called for a coup to “liberate” the emperor and let the army ruleFavoured expansion into China to increase wealth
Saw Manchuria as a buffer against the Soviet Union; did not want war in ChinaWanted good relations with the Soviet Union and was eager for war with China

In 1934 Control faction officers took key posts. In August 1935 Colonel Aizawa of the Imperial Way faction murdered General Nagata, a Control faction leader.

Success criterion 3

The 26 February 1936 coup

During Aizawa’s trial, on 26 February 1936 over 1,400 soldiers of the Imperial Way faction seized central Tokyo, crying “Revere the Emperor, destroy the traitors”. They killed the Lord Keeper of the Privy Seal Saito Makoto, Finance Minister Takahashi and General Watanabe, and demanded a Showa Restoration.

Martial law was declared. The emperor was outraged, saying killing his trusted subjects was “like gently strangling me with floss-silk”. On 28 February he ordered loyal troops to crush the rebels, who were deserted by their men and surrendered. Their leaders, Aizawa and Kita Ikki were executed. The tragic irony was that the emperor they claimed to serve opposed them.

The failed coup discredited the Imperial Way faction; the Control faction now dominated the army and soon the whole nation.

Key terms

Militarism
The belief that a country should build strong armed forces and use them to expand.
May 1932 incident
The assassination of Prime Minister Inukai by ultranationalist officers.
Imperial Way faction
The radical army faction (Kodo-ha) that wanted a Showa Restoration.
Control faction
The more conservative army faction (Tosei-ha) that came to dominate after 1936.
Coup d’état
A violent takeover of government.
