4. The effects of the Meiji Restoration

Success criterion 1

Meiji imperialism

During the Meiji Restoration Japan pursued the ideal of “rich country, strong military” (fukoku kyohei). It grew from a closed feudal state into an empire in East Asia. By 1910 it had:

  • acquired Formosa (Taiwan) as a colony;
  • gained extensive economic control in southern Manchuria;
  • won the southern half of Sakhalin;
  • annexed Korea.

By Emperor Meiji’s death in 1912, Japan had strengthened itself through war and industry to become a modern nation and an imperialist power.

Success criterion 2

Changing Western views

Foreign experts (yatoi) who came to supervise factories and train technicians often looked down on the Japanese. Under the unequal treaties and extraterritoriality, foreigners were governed by their own consulates, outside Japanese law. In 1881 an English newspaper in Japan predicted Japan would never become wealthy.

By 1904 the London Times admitted the West had failed to take Japan seriously, seeing it as a land of “pretty dolls” in paper houses. In less than 50 years Japan had gone from a feudal society to a world power able to defeat a European power in a modern war.

Success criterion 3

The plight of women

Improving women’s status was not one of the reforms. The liberal reformer Fukuzawa Yukichi wrote that a woman had “no property, no rights, and no children”.

DateEvent
1882Women forbidden to make political speeches.
1889Women banned from all political activity, even listening to speeches; the new constitution gave them no political rights.
1898The Civil Code gave the head of the family absolute authority over property, residence, marriage and divorce; wives could not take legal action.

Brothels were allowed by the government. Poor peasant families, mainly in Kyushu, were often forced to sell daughters into prostitution, many abroad as karayuki-san, trapped by debt and threats. The ideal of women as “good wives and wise mothers” (ryosai kembo) was deeply rooted, despite women’s contribution to Japan’s industrial revolution.

Key terms

Fukoku kyohei
“Rich country, strong military”, the Meiji ideal.
Imperialism
Building an empire by taking control of other countries.
Yatoi
Foreign experts employed in Meiji Japan.
Extraterritoriality
The right of foreigners to be governed by their own consulates, outside local law.
Ryosai kembo
“Good wives and wise mothers”, the traditional ideal for women.
