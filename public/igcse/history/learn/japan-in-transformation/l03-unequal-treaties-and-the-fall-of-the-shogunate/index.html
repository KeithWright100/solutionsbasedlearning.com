3. The unequal treaties and the fall of the shogunate

Success criterion 1

The unequal treaties

Defenceless, the shogun had to accept Kanagawa, reversing centuries of excluding foreigners. Many believed he had lost his right to rule.

In July 1858 the first American consul, Townsend Harris, persuaded Japan to sign the Treaty of Edo (Harris Treaty): formal diplomatic relations, five more ports opened, and a “most favoured nation” clause. Similar treaties followed with Britain, France, Holland and Russia. Many Japanese saw them as “unequal”:

  • Japan had been bullied into signing;
  • foreign countries did not give Japan equal privileges;
  • they clearly favoured other nations.

A British journalist called them “a triumph of might over right”.

Success criterion 2

“Revere the emperor and expel the barbarians”

Opposition centred on the Satsuma and Choshu clans of south-west Japan, enemies of the Tokugawa since their defeat at Sekigahara. Backed by merchant families, they called for power to be restored to the emperor and foreigners expelled: Sonno Joi.

Samurai activists, the shi-shi (“men of determination”), saw the emperor as a god and were outraged the shogun had signed treaties without his consent. From 1860 to 1863 they murdered Western advisers and attacked Western ships. Western fines strained the shogun’s finances, and Western gunboats bombarded Satsuma and Choshu. Clan leaders learned that Japan must acquire Western power to protect itself.

Success criterion 3

The fall of the shogun

DateEvent
1866Satsuma and Choshu form a secret alliance with Tosa and Hizen.
1867Emperor Komei dies; his young successor Mutsuhito takes the name Meiji, “enlightened rule”.
Late 1867The last shogun, Keiki, surrenders his powers to the emperor, but his supporters fight imperial forces.
1868Edo falls, is renamed Tokyo and becomes the capital. The Meiji Restoration begins.

Key terms

Unequal treaties
Treaties forced on Japan that favoured Western nations.
Townsend Harris
The first American consul to Japan, who negotiated the 1858 Treaty of Edo.
Sonno Joi
“Revere the emperor and expel the barbarians”, the opposition’s slogan.
Shi-shi
“Men of determination”: samurai activists who attacked foreigners.
Meiji Restoration
The return of power to the emperor in 1868; Meiji means “enlightened rule”.
