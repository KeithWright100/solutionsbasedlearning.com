9. The era of party government, 1918–29

Success criterion 1

The political elites

Under the Meiji Constitution the prime minister and cabinet were not usually from the majority party in the parliament (the Diet) and did not have to resign if they lost votes. Only the emperor, advised by the genro, chose the prime minister. The army and navy could bring down a government by refusing to provide a minister. Power was divided among elites, and the parties, the Seiyukai and Kenseikai (Minseito from 1927), were not usually at the centre of politics. In the 1920s, however, parties formed a series of governments.

Success criterion 2

Hara and Kato

Prime ministerKey features
Hara Kei (Seiyukai), 1918–21After the rice riots, the first commoner and first majority-party leader to be prime minister. Limited reforms: reduced central control of towns and villages, single-member electorates, and the vote for small landowners (tax qualification cut from ten to three yen). Ruthlessly suppressed strikes. Stabbed to death at Tokyo station, November 1921.
Non-party cabinets, 1922–24Admirals Kato and Yamamoto and Count Kiyoura led governments not based on parties.
Kato Komei (Kenseikai), 1924–26Universal Manhood Suffrage Act (1925): all men over 25 could vote, increasing voters from 3 million to 14 million. Peace Preservation Law (1925) outlawed groups seeking radical change, aimed at the press and Communist Party. Health insurance; army and military budget cut.
Success criterion 3

The end of the decade

Emperor Taisho died on 25 December 1926. Hirohito became emperor, taking the name Showa, “enlightenment and peace”, and hoping for progress and friendship with all nations.

  • A banking crisis in April 1927 brought down Wakatsuki’s government; 36 banks failed.
  • Tanaka Giichi (1927–29) followed an aggressive policy towards China and resigned after lying to the emperor about army officers’ part in assassinating the Manchurian warlord Zhang Zuolin in 1928.
  • Hamaguchi Osachi’s hopes were destroyed by the Great Depression, and Japan entered the “Dark Valley” of the 1930s.

Key terms

The Japanese parliament.
Genro
The elder statesmen who advised the emperor on choosing prime ministers.
Universal Manhood Suffrage Act
The 1925 law giving all men over 25 the vote.
Peace Preservation Law
The 1925 law outlawing groups seeking radical political change.
Showa
The name of Hirohito’s reign from 1926, meaning “enlightenment and peace”.
