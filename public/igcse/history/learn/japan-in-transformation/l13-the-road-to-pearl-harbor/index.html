13. The road to Pearl Harbor

Edexcel IGCSE History · Japan in transformation, 1853–1945 · 5. Japan at war, 1937–45 · Lesson 13 of 15

Learning Intention

To understand why relations between Japan and the USA broke down and why Japan attacked Pearl Harbor.

Success criterion 1

Strained relations

President Franklin D. Roosevelt wanted to focus on helping Britain and France, so at first he kept selling Japan oil, scrap iron and steel, but ended the commercial treaty in January 1940. When Holland and France fell to Germany in 1940, Japan saw a chance to expand into the Dutch East Indies and French Indo-China; the US Pacific fleet moved to Pearl Harbor, Hawaii.

  • July 1940: the USA stopped selling aviation fuel and top-grade scrap metal.
  • August 1940: Foreign Minister Matsuoka announced the Greater East Asia Co-prosperity Sphere, really a slogan for empire building.
  • September 1940: Japan built bases in Indo-China with the permission of Vichy France; the USA cut off all scrap iron but still sold oil.
Success criterion 2

The Tripartite Pact and deadlock

On 27 September 1940, urged by General Tojo Hideki, Japan signed the Tripartite Pact with Germany and Italy: each would help the others if attacked by a country not yet in their wars, meaning the USA. Hirohito believed it would harm Japan and later said it caused the war with the USA.

Talks in Washington from April 1941 between Ambassador Nomura and Secretary of State Cordell Hull reached deadlock over the Pact and the US demand that Japan leave China. When Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941, Japan chose to expand into South East Asia rather than attack Russia.

Success criterion 3

7 December 1941

Hirohito sanctioned the decision, later saying that refusing would have caused a rebellion. Shortly before 8 a.m. on Sunday 7 December 1941, Japanese carrier planes, under Admiral Yamamoto Isoroku, sank five US battleships, damaged three more and destroyed over half the aircraft at Pearl Harbor. Radar spotted the planes but they were thought to be friendly. 2,403 Americans died.

Roosevelt called it “a date which will live in infamy” and asked Congress to declare war. Hirohito said Japan was fighting for “self-preservation and self-defence”. Many Japanese were excited; others, like one naval officer, felt a chill, knowing Japan should not fight. Japan had roused a “sleeping giant”.

Key terms

Greater East Asia Co-prosperity Sphere
Japan’s 1940 plan for an Asian bloc under its control.
Tripartite Pact
The 1940 alliance of Japan, Germany and Italy.
Embargo
An official ban on trade, such as the US oil embargo of 1941.
Pearl Harbor
The US naval base in Hawaii attacked by Japan on 7 December 1941.
Tojo Hideki
The army general who became prime minister in October 1941.
