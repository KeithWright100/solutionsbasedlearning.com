14. The war in the Pacific, 1941–45

Success criterion 1

Japanese victories

In the six months after Pearl Harbor the Allies could not stop Japan. Guam and the Gilbert Islands fell; within a month Japan landed in the Philippines, Hong Kong surrendered, Manila fell and Malaya was overrun.

The British base at Singapore, built at a cost of over £60 million, had guns that faced the sea and could not defend against a land attack. Japan invaded Malaya on 8 December 1941, and on 15 February 1942 General Percival surrendered 130,000 British and Commonwealth troops to General Yamashita. Churchill called it “the worst disaster and the biggest capitulation in British history”. By May 1942 Japan had conquered most of the western and southern Pacific.

Success criterion 2

The turning point

  • Doolittle Raid (18 April 1942): 16 US bombers struck Tokyo and other cities, astonishing the Japanese.
  • Coral Sea (4–8 May 1942): a carrier battle blocked Japan’s attempt to take Port Moresby and cut links to Australia; Japan lost two carriers.
  • Midway (3–6 June 1942): US codebreakers (“Magic”) let Admiral Nimitz learn Yamamoto’s plans. Japan lost all four carriers for one US carrier, ending threats to Hawaii. The Japanese public were never told the truth.
Success criterion 3

The Allied advance

General Douglas MacArthur used island-hopping, seizing key islands to approach Japan. By October 1944 the Allies were back in the Philippines.

DateEvent
25 October 1944First planned suicide missions (tokkotai) at Leyte Gulf; about 2,530 pilots died on such missions by August 1945.
19 February–16 March 1945Iwo Jima: 26,000 US casualties; the 25,000-strong Japanese garrison fought to the death.
March 1945The first big fire-bomb raid on Tokyo may have killed over 200,000.
1 April–June 1945Okinawa, the last major land battle: 49,000 Allied casualties and 110,000 Japanese military dead.

Japan faced shortages of materials, trained men and constant air raids, and its wooden cities burned easily.

Key terms

Island-hopping
The Allied strategy of capturing key islands to advance towards Japan.
Midway
The June 1942 battle in which Japan lost four aircraft carriers.
Magic
The US breaking of Japan’s secret codes.
Tokkotai
Japanese suicide attack squadrons, often called kamikaze.
Capitulation
Surrender.
