2. Commodore Perry and the Treaty of Kanagawa

Success criterion 1

Renewed foreign pressure

By the late 18th century Western interest in the Pacific and the China trade grew. Russia tried to establish relations in 1792 and 1805, and British ships visited in 1797 and 1808. In 1825 the shogunate ordered foreign ships driven away “without second thought”. But the USA would not take no for an answer.

Success criterion 2

The “black ships”, 1853

On 8 July 1853 Commodore Matthew Perry led four coal-powered warships, painted black, into Uraga Bay near Edo. The Japanese had never seen such ships; at first some thought they were on fire. Edo was thrown into turmoil.

Perry delivered a letter from President Millard Fillmore asking that:

  • the USA and Japan live in friendship and trade;
  • American ships be supplied with coal and other supplies;
  • shipwrecked American sailors be protected.

The letter was polite but contained a veiled threat; Perry demanded these things “as a right, and not as a favour”. The shogun asked for time, and Perry said he would return within a year. The shogunate faced a dilemma: refusal meant war, but agreement would cause unrest at home.

Success criterion 3

The Treaty of Kanagawa, 1854

Perry returned with a larger fleet in February 1854. On 31 March 1854 the Treaty of Kanagawa was signed. Japan agreed to:

  • friendly, permanent relations with the USA;
  • open two ports, Shimoda and Hakodate, to American ships;
  • protect shipwrecked American sailors;
  • accept an American consul at Shimoda;
  • give the USA any privileges given to other nations in future.

Perry set off events that would end Japan’s isolation for ever.

Activity: letter or treaty?

Was each point in Fillmore’s 1853 letter or the 1854 Treaty of Kanagawa?

  • A request that the two countries trade
  • Shimoda and Hakodate opened to Americans
  • A request for coal for American ships
  • An American consul to live at Shimoda
  • Polite words containing a veiled threat
  • The USA to receive any privileges given to others later

Key terms

Commodore Perry
The US naval commander who arrived in Japan in 1853.
Black ships
The name for Perry’s coal-powered warships.
Treaty of Kanagawa
The 1854 treaty opening two Japanese ports to the USA.
Consul
An official who represents his country’s citizens in a foreign city.
Gunboat diplomacy
Using the threat of naval force to get what you want.
