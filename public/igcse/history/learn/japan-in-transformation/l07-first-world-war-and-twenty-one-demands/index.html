7. The First World War and the Twenty-One Demands

Success criterion 1

Japan enters the war

Emperor Meiji was succeeded in 1912 by his son Yoshihito, whose reign was called Taisho; his ill-health made him a figurehead. Japan saw the war in Europe as an opportunity.

DateEvent
7 August 1914Britain asks Japan to help destroy German warships in Chinese waters.
15 August 1914Japan demands Germany hand over its lease of Jiaozhou Bay and its interests in Shandong.
23 August 1914With no reply, Japan declares war on Germany.
3 September 1914Japanese marines land in Shandong, violating Chinese neutrality.
7 November 1914Germany surrenders its territory in Shandong (Qingdao) to Japan.
Success criterion 2

The Twenty-One Demands

With the West busy in Europe, Foreign Minister Kato, with the approval of the genro (elder statesmen) and emperor, sent Chinese President Yuan Shikai 21 demands in five groups, threatening retaliation:

GroupDemand
1. ShandongChina to accept Japan’s arrangements for the former German territories.
2. South Manchuria and Inner MongoliaJapan’s leases and railway rights extended to 99 years; Japanese free to live, work and mine there.
3. Yangtse ValleyJoint control of the Han Ye Ping coal and iron company.
4. CoastNo harbours or islands to be leased to any country but Japan.
5. MiscellaneousChina to use Japanese political, financial and military advisers and buy Japanese arms; joint police control in key areas.
Success criterion 3

China’s response and Japan’s justification

China bitterly resented the demands, especially group 5, an attack on its independence. Yuan held out for nearly four months. The genro, irritated by Kato’s forceful methods, removed the main points of group 5. On 25 May 1915 China signed treaties accepting most of groups 1 to 4.

Yamagata Aritomo justified the demands: Japan’s dominance in Manchuria was needed for its own growth and security and to protect China and Asians from Russia, for the “co-existence and co-prosperity” of China and Japan.

Key terms

Taisho
The name of Emperor Yoshihito’s reign, 1912–26, meaning “great righteousness”.
Genro
Japan’s elder statesmen, who advised the emperor.
Twenty-One Demands
Japan’s 1915 demands for control over parts of China.
Shandong
The Chinese province where Germany had a leased territory, taken by Japan in 1914.
Yuan Shikai
The Chinese president who received the Twenty-One Demands.
