Success criterion 1

The Triple Intervention and Russia

On 23 April 1895, six days after Shimonoseki, Russia, France and Germany forced Japan to give up the Liaotung Peninsula in exchange for 30 million taels. Japan felt humiliated by these “three world bullies” and determined never to be so weak again.

In 1898 Russia forced China to lease it the Liaotung Peninsula and built the South Manchurian Railway to Dairen and Port Arthur, alarming Japan. When the anti-foreign Boxer Rebellion (1900) besieged Peking and Chinese forces attacked Manchuria, Russia sent troops and took control of Manchuria. The Boxers were crushed by an international army that included Japanese troops.

Success criterion 2

The Anglo-Japanese Alliance, 1902

Russia’s advance worried Britain and Japan. On 30 January 1902 they signed an alliance:

  • recognising Britain’s special interests in China and Japan’s in Korea;
  • each could protect its interests if threatened;
  • each would stay neutral if the other went to war to protect them;
  • each would help the other if a third power joined that war;
  • it would last five years.

With Britain as an ally, Japan was in a much stronger position. In 1903 it offered to recognise Russia’s rights in Manchuria if Russia recognised Japan’s interests in Korea, but Russia would not fully agree.

Success criterion 3

The war and the Treaty of Portsmouth

DateEvent
8 February 1904Japan torpedoes the Russian fleet at Port Arthur without warning; war is declared on 10 February.
May 1904Victory at the Yalu River gives Japan control of all of Korea.
2 January 1905General Nogi captures Port Arthur after a five-month siege.
March 1905Mukden falls after a two-month battle.
27–28 May 1905Admiral Togo destroys the Russian fleet at Tsushima after its 7-month voyage from the Baltic.
5 September 1905The Treaty of Portsmouth, arranged by US President Theodore Roosevelt, ends the war.

It was the first time an Asian power had beaten an established European nation. Under the treaty Russia recognised Japan’s interests in Korea, transferred the Liaotung lease and the South Manchurian Railway, and gave Japan southern Sakhalin.

Key terms

Triple Intervention
Russia, France and Germany forcing Japan to give up the Liaotung Peninsula in 1895.
South Manchurian Railway
A Russian railway to Dairen and Port Arthur, later controlled by Japan.
Boxer Rebellion
An anti-foreign movement that besieged Peking in 1900.
Anglo-Japanese Alliance
The 1902 alliance between Britain and Japan.
Treaty of Portsmouth
The 1905 treaty ending the Russo-Japanese War.
