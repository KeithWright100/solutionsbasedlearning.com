1. Tokugawa Japan before 1853

Success criterion 1

Feudal Japan

From 1603 to 1868 Japan was ruled from Edo (later Tokyo) by the Tokugawa shogunate, while the emperor stayed in Kyoto. The emperor was the centre of loyalty for all Japanese but had no political power; the shogun was the military ruler who governed in his name.

ClassRole
DaimyoPowerful feudal lords controlling areas called han; divided into fudai (hereditary lords) and tozama (outer lords).
SamuraiProfessional warriors (“those who serve”) following Bushido, the way of the warrior: absolute loyalty, bravery and honour.
PeasantsMost of the population; gave part of their crop as tax to the daimyo.
ArtisansLow on the social scale, though their work was admired.
Merchants (chonin)Despised as unproductive, but became wealthy money lenders in Edo and Osaka.
OutcastsBeggars and those doing “unclean” work.

Loyalty, a legacy of Confucian obedience to authority, held the system together. Women were expected to obey their parents, then husband, then sons.

Success criterion 2

Closing Japan

Portuguese traders arrived in 1543, followed by missionaries such as Francis Xavier (1549). Rulers came to see Christianity as a threat: Hideyoshi ordered missionaries out in 1587, and the Tokugawa expelled priests in 1614 and persecuted Christians.

  • In 1636 an edict banned Japanese from leaving or returning on pain of death.
  • The mainly Christian Shimabara Rebellion (1637–38) was crushed; about 37,000 died.
  • The Portuguese were barred from 1639. After 1641 the only Europeans allowed were the Dutch, confined to the tiny island of Deshima in Nagasaki harbour, with one ship a year. Trade with China was also allowed.

This “closed door” policy, shakoku, lasted until 1853.

Success criterion 3

Decline of the Tokugawa system

  • Long peace left the samurai with no military role; many became administrators or scholars, and many fell into debt and poverty, some becoming lordless ronin.
  • Merchants grew rich as trade and towns expanded, and daimyo became heavily indebted to them, partly because they had to live in Edo every other year.
  • Poorer peasants suffered rising taxes, famine and debt; uprisings became more frequent.
  • Through Dutch books some scholars studied Western science, and others studied the time when emperors had actually ruled.

The shogunate was not about to collapse in 1853, but it had been weakened.

Key terms

Shogun
The military ruler of Japan who governed in the emperor’s name.
Daimyo
Powerful feudal lords who controlled areas of Japan.
Samurai
Professional warriors loyal to their lord.
Bushido
“The way of the warrior”, the samurai code of loyalty, bravery and honour.
Shakoku
Japan’s “closed door” policy of isolation.
Deshima
The island in Nagasaki harbour where Dutch traders were confined.
