17. War Communism, 1918–21

Edexcel IGCSE History · Russia and the Soviet Union, 1905–24 · 5. War Communism and the New Economic Policy · Lesson 17 of 20

Learning Intention

To understand the features of War Communism, why it was introduced and its effects.

Syllabus Statement

The reasons for, nature and effects of War Communism.

Success criterion 1

Features

War Communism lasted from June 1918 to March 1921. The state, not private owners, decided what to produce and how to share it out.

  • Peasants could not sell crops: the state requisitioned them, leaving a little to live on. Hoarders could be shot.
  • Food rationing in cities, with soldiers and workers getting more.
  • A central planning body, the Supreme Economic Council (Vesenkha), set targets.
  • Factories with more than 10 workers were nationalised.
  • Strikes were banned and labour conscription forced everyone of working age to work.
Success criterion 2

Reasons

  • Industry: by 1917 output was 60 per cent of 1913, transport was disrupted, banks stopped lending, Brest-Litovsk cost 40 per cent of industrial areas, and workers were leaving the cities.
  • Food: farmland was lost to Germany and the Whites, and peasants grew little to sell because there was nothing to buy. Grain collected rose from under 1 million tonnes in 1918 to 6 million in 1920, but it was not enough.
  • Ideology: state control and no private profit matched communist ideas; money was replaced with tokens. Some historians see the war as an excuse to move to communism; others see emergency measures.
Success criterion 3

Effects

Requisitioning left peasants with little, sometimes not even seed; farm production fell to 37 per cent of 1913; famines broke outHalf of Moscow’s population and almost three quarters of Petrograd’s left for the countryside, 1918–20

Peasants still preferred the Reds to the Whites, who would return land to landlords. Once the war was won, protests grew rapidly.

Key terms

War Communism
The system of state control of industry and agriculture, June 1918–March 1921.
Central planning
When the state controls the economy and decides what is produced.
Kulak
A Bolshevik term for a rich peasant, meaning “closed fist”.
Black market
Illegal buying and selling of goods in short supply.
Labour conscription
Forcing everyone of working age to work for the state.
