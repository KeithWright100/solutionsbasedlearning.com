12. The Bolshevik takeover, October 1917

Edexcel IGCSE History · Russia and the Soviet Union, 1905–24 · 3. The Provisional Government and the Bolshevik revolution · Lesson 12 of 20

Learning Intention

To understand the key events of the Bolshevik takeover and the reasons for Bolshevik success.

Syllabus Statement

The Bolshevik takeover of power and the reasons for Bolshevik success, including the roles of Lenin and Trotsky.

Success criterion 1

Key events

DateEvent
10 OctoberLenin returns secretly from Finland and argues for an armed takeover.
OctoberFearing a coup, Kerensky tries to send pro-Bolshevik units away. The Soviet sets up the Military Revolutionary Committee (MRC); by 21 October most regiments are loyal to it.
24 OctoberKerensky closes Bolshevik newspapers and orders the MRC’s arrest. Trotsky uses the MRC to take bridges, army headquarters and the telegraph office.
24–25 OctoberRed Guards seize key areas with almost no opposition.
25–26 OctoberBolshevik soldiers enter the Winter Palace and arrest the Provisional Government.
26 OctoberLenin forms a Bolshevik government, the Council of People’s Commissars.

Other parties walked out of the Soviet in protest; Trotsky said they belonged in “the dustbin of history”. Elections to the Constituent Assembly, promised for November, put pressure on Lenin to act first, as the SRs were likely to win.

Success criterion 2

Lenin and Trotsky

Role of LeninRole of Trotsky
Changed the party’s view that Russia was not ready for revolutionOrganised the takeover in Petrograd, following Lenin’s strategy, with little opposition
Gave simple, effective slogans such as “Peace, Land and Bread”A hero since 1905 and a brilliant speaker
Convinced divided Bolsheviks to seize power by force and planned the strategyAs president of the Soviet, controlled the MRC and made it look like the soviets, not just the Bolsheviks, were taking power
Success criterion 3

Failures of the Provisional Government

  • The Kornilov Revolt had weakened Kerensky, and support went to the Bolsheviks (and the SRs in the countryside).
  • The army and navy had lost discipline, so the government could not keep order.
  • Peasants were seizing land and the government could not stop it, losing landowners’ support.
  • German advances threatened Petrograd; the Bolsheviks claimed Kerensky planned to surrender it.
  • Kerensky failed to crush the Bolsheviks after the July Days, underestimated them, and failed to hold elections or bring in reforms.

Historians disagree: some see a Bolshevik conspiracy or coup by a small minority; others stress deeper social conflicts that pushed the Bolsheviks into a strong position.

Key terms

Military Revolutionary Committee
The MRC, set up by the Petrograd Soviet to bring together soviet-supporting soldiers.
Winter Palace
The former royal palace where the Provisional Government was arrested.
Council of People’s Commissars
The Bolshevik government formed on 26 October 1917 (Sovnarkom).
Coup d’état
When a small group, usually backed by security forces, overthrows a government.
Dustbin of history
Trotsky’s phrase for the parties that walked out of the Soviet.
