19. The New Economic Policy

Success criterion 1

Crisis in 1921

The economy was barely functioning. Famine in the Volga region in 1920–21 affected 20 million people, and about 5 million died.

With the war won, the Bolsheviks could no longer blame the Whites. Three quarters of Petrograd’s factories were on strike by the end of 1920, and regions such as Tambov were in revolt. At the 10th Party Congress in March 1921, Lenin said urgent steps were needed to give the peasants what they wanted.

Success criterion 2

Key features

  • The free market returned: peasants could sell produce at their own price, paying a 10 per cent tax.
  • Grain requisitioning ended.
  • The state kept big factories, but businesses with fewer than 20 workers could be private and make profits.
  • 20,000 foreign experts came from the USA and Canada, 1920–25, paid more than workers.
  • Anyone could open a shop; traders became known as “Nepmen”.
Success criterion 3

Effects and opposition

  • Farm production rose quickly; food reached the towns; a new currency, the chervonets, came in 1922 and rationing ended.
  • Small industry grew; Britain made trade deals.
  • The “scissors crisis” of 1923: food prices fell while manufactured goods stayed expensive; the government lowered industrial prices.
  • Peasant revolts ended (partly crushed by the Red Army) and city life recovered, but inequality grew.

Lenin called it one step back to leap forward, a “peasant Brest-Litovsk”, and reportedly threatened to resign if the party rejected it. Many Bolsheviks disliked it because it looked like capitalism, favoured peasants over workers, and created rich Nepmen and kulaks.

Key terms

NEP
The New Economic Policy, introduced in 1921, which brought back some capitalism.
Nepmen
Traders who made profits under the NEP.
Scissors crisis
The 1923 problem of falling food prices and high prices for manufactured goods.
Free market
A system where people can buy and sell freely at prices they choose.
Peasant Brest-Litovsk
Lenin’s description of the NEP as a painful but necessary retreat.
