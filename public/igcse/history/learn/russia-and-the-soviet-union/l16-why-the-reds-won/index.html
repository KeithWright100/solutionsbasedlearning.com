16. Why the Reds won the Civil War

Edexcel IGCSE History · Russia and the Soviet Union, 1905–24 · 4. Bolshevik consolidation of power and the Civil War · Lesson 16 of 20

Learning Intention

To understand the reasons for the Bolshevik victory in the Civil War, including the Red Terror.

Success criterion 1

White weaknesses

In 1918 the Whites seemed certain to win, with experienced generals and foreign support. But:

  • there was no single leader, because they did not share the same aims (monarchists wanted the tsar back, SRs the Constituent Assembly), so attacks were badly co-ordinated;
  • they were unpopular with peasants, as many wanted to take back land;
  • they were spread out on the edges of Russia, far from industry, railways and population, making supply and communication hard;
  • they were much smaller: a maximum of 250,000 soldiers, and peasants would not leave their regions to fight for them.

Red strengths

StrengthExplanation
A huge armyOver 3 million by 1920, over 5 million by 1921, fighting on up to 16 fronts.
TrotskyCreated the Red Army, imposed discipline and toured the fronts in an armoured train to boost morale.
Central locationControl of industry, railways and population; shorter supply lines.
PropagandaStriking posters and slogans warned that the Whites would take peasant land and foreigners would invade.
War CommunismLenin put the state in charge of production so the army was fed.
AlliancesThe Bolsheviks never had to fight all their opponents at once.
Success criterion 3

The Red Terror

After the attempt on Lenin, the Cheka arrested and executed suspected opponents without trial. Nearly 300,000 people were killed between 1918 and 1920. When the Red Army captured an area, the Cheka punished anyone thought to have helped the Whites, so that people were too frightened to oppose the Bolsheviks. Both sides were brutal; some historians argue White brutality also pushed people towards the Reds.

  • Control of central Russia’s railways
  • Peasants feared losing their land
  • Effective propaganda posters
  • A maximum of 250,000 soldiers

Key terms

Red Terror
The Cheka’s campaign of arrests and executions against Bolshevik opponents.
Propaganda
Information, often false or one-sided, used to make people agree with a group.
Conscription
Making people serve the state, for example in the army.
Armoured train
The train Trotsky used to travel between fronts.
Bourgeois revolt
A Bolshevik term describing the Whites as capitalists rebelling against Bolshevik rule.
