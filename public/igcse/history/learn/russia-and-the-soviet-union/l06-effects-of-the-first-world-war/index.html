6. The effects of the First World War on Russia

Success criterion 1

Defeat and the tsar takes command

Russia supported Serbia in 1914, which meant war with Austria and Germany. At first the war boosted the tsar: people were patriotic and Russia’s huge army advanced. But at Tannenburg (August 1914) Germany captured or killed 122,000 Russians, and defeats at the Masurian Lakes followed. By the end of 1915 Russia had lost 2 million men.

The army was badly supplied: one third had no rifles, and shells, winter uniforms, boots and food were short. In August 1915 Nicholas took personal command, despite no military experience and against his ministers’ advice; he would now be blamed directly for defeats.

Success criterion 3

Political effects

The Fourth Duma was suspended in August 1914. As the war worsened, deputies demanded a “ministry of national confidence”. When the tsar refused, about half formed the Progressive Bloc, which became a centre of opposition. When the duma met again in November 1916 it attacked the government and Rasputin’s influence. Respect and support for the tsar collapsed.

Key terms

Tannenburg
A crushing German victory over Russia in August 1914.
Mobilisation
Gathering and equipping an army and moving it towards the enemy.
Inflation
A rise in prices.
Requisitioning
Taking something, such as food, by official order.
Progressive Bloc
A group of about half the duma deputies who opposed the tsar’s government during the war.
