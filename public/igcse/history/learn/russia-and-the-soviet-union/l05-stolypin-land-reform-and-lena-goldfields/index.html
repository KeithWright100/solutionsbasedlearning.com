5. Stolypin’s land reform and the Lena Goldfields

Edexcel IGCSE History · Russia and the Soviet Union, 1905–24 · 1. Tsarist rule in Russia, 1905–14 · Lesson 5 of 20

Learning Intention

To understand Stolypin’s land reform, how successful it was, and the impact of the Lena Goldfields massacre.

Syllabus Statement

Stolypin’s land reform and the growth of opposition: the Lena Goldfields strike.

Success criterion 1

The “wager on the strong”

Stolypin and the landlords did not want to give peasants more land, arguing that the old-fashioned communes, which made all farming decisions in the village, would waste it.

His plan let individual peasants leave the commune and set up their own family farms. Keeping all their profits, they would use modern methods and grow more. His “wager on the strong” was that successful farmers would stop attacking landlords and support the government. He also encouraged migration to Siberia: 3.5 million peasants moved there between 1905 and 1917.

Success criterion 2

How successful was it?

  • There were far more applications than expected, and the reform was complex.
  • Many peasants still farmed as if in a commune, with scattered strips and shared grazing.
  • By May 1915 only 14 per cent of peasants taking up the reform had enclosed family farms.
  • Its political aim failed: in 1917 peasant unrest, organised by the communes, swept rural Russia again.

Stolypin was hated by liberals and radicals, and was assassinated by an SR at the opera in September 1911. Some see him as a great reformer who could have saved Russia; others think he had upset almost everyone by 1911.

Success criterion 3

The Lena Goldfields massacre, 1912

Gold miners on the Lena River in Siberia worked in terrible conditions. A protest over rotten horsemeat led to a strike, organised with Bolshevik help. When a huge crowd brought demands to the managers, troops fired: between 200 and 500 workers were killed.

Strikes had fallen from thousands in 1905 to a few hundred in 1911; after the massacre there were nearly 2,000 strikes. A duma investigation was led by Alexander Kerensky, who became famous for his reports. It showed none of the reasons for discontent had gone away.

Activity: success or failure?

Does each point suggest the land reform succeeded or failed?

  • 3.5 million peasants moved to Siberia for land
  • Only 14 per cent created enclosed family farms by 1915
  • Far more applications than the government expected
  • Peasants kept farming scattered strips as before
  • Peasant unrest swept the countryside again in 1917
  • Some peasants became independent landowning farmers

Key terms

Commune
The peasant community that owned and shared land and made farming decisions.
Wager on the strong
Stolypin’s gamble that successful individual farmers would support the government.
Lena Goldfields massacre
The 1912 shooting of striking gold miners in Siberia.
Proletarian
A member of the working class with nothing to live on except wages.
Alexander Kerensky
The duma deputy who investigated the Lena Goldfields massacre.
