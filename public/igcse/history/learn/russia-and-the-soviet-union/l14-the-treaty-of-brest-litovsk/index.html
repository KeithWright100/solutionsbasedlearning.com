14. The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk

Edexcel IGCSE History · Russia and the Soviet Union, 1905–24 · 4. Bolshevik consolidation of power and the Civil War · Lesson 14 of 20

Learning Intention

To understand why the Bolsheviks signed the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, its terms and its consequences.

Syllabus Statement

The reasons for the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk and its consequences.

Success criterion 1

Negotiations

After the Decree on Peace there was a ceasefire. Talks began at Brest-Litovsk on 22 December 1917; Britain, France and the USA did not attend, so Russia negotiated alone. Lenin gave the job to Trotsky, who expected a German revolution and dragged talks out for 2 months. None came. Trotsky then said Russia would stop fighting but give no land or compensation. Germany restarted its invasion and threatened Petrograd; Moscow became the capital.

Lenin demanded peace at any price: Russia could not fight, defeat would end the revolution, and he could not fight Germany and a civil war at once.

Success criterion 2

The terms, 3 March 1918

Russia gave up Ukraine, the Baltic provinces, Finland, parts of Poland and Georgia, losing:

LossShare
Coal mines and iron ore74 per cent
Industry50 per cent
Railway network26 per cent
Farmland27 per cent
Population26 per cent (62 million people)

Russia also paid Germany 300 million gold roubles. Lenin said Germany’s “knees are on our chest”, but the peace was a respite to prepare for the fight against the bourgeoisie.

Success criterion 3

Consequences

  • It was seen as shameful: half the empire and many key resources were lost.
  • Food shortages worsened; by June 1918, 1 million people had left Petrograd.
  • Soldiers were pleased the war was over, but most Russians were devastated.
  • Conservatives were convinced they must fight the Bolsheviks: it was a major cause of the Civil War.
  • The Bolshevik Party itself was split.
  • Britain, France and the USA, now facing Germany alone in the west, supported the Whites.

Key terms

Treaty
A formal written agreement between countries.
Brest-Litovsk
The German army headquarters where Russia and Germany negotiated peace.
Ceasefire
An agreement to stop fighting for a time.
Imperialist
A system in which one country rules many others.
Respite
A short break from something difficult.
