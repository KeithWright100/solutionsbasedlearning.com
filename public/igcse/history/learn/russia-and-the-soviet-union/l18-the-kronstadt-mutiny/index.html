18. The Kronstadt Naval Mutiny, 1921

Success criterion 1

The “reddest of the red”

The Kronstadt naval base guarded Petrograd. Its sailors rioted in February 1917, took part in the July Days, defended Petrograd from Kornilov, helped seize power in October and helped close the Constituent Assembly. Trotsky called them the “reddest of the red”.

Success criterion 2

Reasons and demands

On 28 February 1921 sailors on the battleship Petropavlovsk mutinied, and over 15,000 sailors joined. The bread ration had been cut again in January 1921, workers were striking in Moscow and Petrograd, and Bolshevik officials enjoyed more food and better homes. The sailors were furious that the Bolsheviks were betraying the revolution by imprisoning and executing people without trial. They demanded:

  • new soviet elections by secret ballot;
  • freedom of speech for workers, peasants, anarchists and socialist parties;
  • release of socialist political prisoners;
  • equal rations for all workers;
  • peasants’ right to do as they wished with their land.
Success criterion 3

Crushing the mutiny and its significance

Trotsky sent 50,000 Red Army soldiers across the frozen Gulf of Finland. After fierce fighting the base was retaken by 17 March. About 8,000 sailors escaped to Finland and about 500 were executed.

With peasant revolts in the countryside and opposition from workers, soldiers and sailors who should have been loyal, Lenin realised War Communism was putting Bolshevik control at risk. He called Kronstadt “the flash which lit up reality” and abandoned War Communism for a new policy.

Key terms

Kronstadt
The naval base guarding Petrograd whose sailors mutinied in 1921.
Petropavlovsk
The battleship where the Kronstadt Mutiny began.
Bureaucrat
Someone in a bureaucracy who applies official rules very strictly.
Reddest of the red
Trotsky’s description of the loyal Kronstadt sailors.
Secret ballot
Voting in private so no one knows how you voted.
