4. Nicholas II and the dumas, 1906–14

Success criterion 1

The Fundamental Laws, 1906

By April 1906, when the Fundamental Laws were set out, the tsar was back in control. They set up the State Duma of around 500 elected deputies, but limited its power:

  • an upper house, the State Council, could block anything; half its members were chosen by the tsar;
  • the tsar controlled the armed forces and foreign policy, chose ministers, could veto laws and dissolve the duma;
  • under Article 87 he could pass laws when the duma was not sitting;
  • only he could change the Fundamental Laws.

Nicholas hated limits on his sacred power, but the opening of the First Duma on 27 April 1906 was celebrated and he made a speech to the deputies.

Success criterion 2

The first four dumas

After 1905 many parties formed, from the ultra-conservative Union of the Russian People to the RSDP. In the middle were the liberal Kadets (Constitutional Democrats) and the more conservative Octobrists.

DumaDatesKey features
First DumaApril–July 1906Dominated by Kadets and Trudoviks (a peasant party), who demanded land reform and more power for the duma. Dissolved after 10 weeks.
Second DumaFebruary–June 1907222 socialists, mostly SRs, elected. Even more of a threat; dissolved.
Third DumaNovember 1907–June 1912On 3 June 1907 Stolypin changed the election rules: conservatives held 287 of 443 seats. It lasted its full term.
Fourth DumaNovember 1912–February 1917Also conservative, with stronger nationalist parties; opposition too weak to change policy.
Success criterion 3

The impact on opposition

  • Liberals were very disappointed by the quick return to autocracy and shocked by the dishonest change to election rules; they realised the tsar would never give up power willingly.
  • Revolutionary parties learned they would not achieve their aims through elections.
  • Repression meant revolutionaries could hardly operate; factory owners could sack strikers.
  • Extreme nationalist groups, the Black Hundreds, killed over 3,000 people, mostly Jews, in 1905–06, with links to the police.

Key terms

Fundamental Laws
The 1906 laws setting up the duma and limiting its powers.
Article 87
The rule allowing the tsar to pass laws when the duma was not sitting.
Veto
The right to reject a decision made by parliament.
Dissolve
End a parliament so that a new one must be elected.
Kadets
The Constitutional Democrats, a liberal party.
Octobrists
A more conservative party, named after the October Manifesto.
