Success criterion 1

Nicholas II and autocracy

By 1905 the Romanov family had ruled Russia for nearly 300 years. Nicholas II, aged 37, had never been prepared to rule by his father. He was devoted to his family but weak, found decisions hard, had no patience for detail and few strong ideas, except that he must pass on his powers to his son, Alexei (born 1904).

Nicholas ruled as an autocrat: there were no controls on his power. He chose ministers from the aristocracy and senior Church leaders, and commanded the armed forces, who swore personal loyalty to him. Unlike Britain, where people could vote out a government, Russians could do nothing to change how they were ruled. People rarely criticised the tsar, assuming instead that his ministers gave bad advice.

Success criterion 2

Reasons for discontent

GroupReasons for discontent
PeasantsAround 85 per cent of the population. Population growth meant they needed more land but were too poor to buy it; they were overtaxed. Famines in the 1890s and 1901 killed thousands, and the government even banned the word “famine” from newspapers.
Industrial workersIndustry grew fast from the 1890s in huge city factories. Conditions were dangerous, hours long, pay low and rules strict; workers lived in overcrowded, dirty barracks where disease spread.
Middle classesMostly liberals with no say in government. They feared radical revolution and wanted a constitution guaranteeing rights under a fair legal system.
Non-RussiansOnly 44 per cent of the 125 million people in the empire (1897) were Russian. “Russification” pressured others to speak Russian and adopt Russian culture; some wanted independence.

Success criterion 3

The radical threat and the police state

In 1881 Nicholas’s grandfather, Alexander II, was assassinated, and terrorists, mostly from the Socialist Revolutionary Party (SRs), killed ministers in the early 1900s. The Russian Social Democratic Party (RSDP), formed in 1898, followed Karl Marx. It split into the Mensheviks, who thought a workers’ revolution was a long way off, and the Bolsheviks, led by Vladimir Ulyanov (Lenin), who wanted to lead a revolution as soon as the time was right. Political parties were illegal.

To protect the regime, the police had great power. Censorship stopped radical ideas being published, and suspects were imprisoned or exiled to Siberia. The secret police, the Okhrana, spied on suspects and its agents pretended to be revolutionaries. Captured terrorists were usually executed, including Lenin’s brother, Alexander.

Key terms

Autocrat
A ruler who has absolute power over a country.
Romanovs
The family that had ruled Russia for nearly 300 years by 1905.
Russification
A policy pressuring non-Russians to speak Russian and adopt Russian culture.
Okhrana
The tsar’s secret police, who watched suspected revolutionaries.
Bolsheviks
The part of the RSDP, led by Lenin, that wanted to lead a workers’ revolution as soon as possible.
Socialist
Someone who thinks the state should own important industries and share their wealth.
