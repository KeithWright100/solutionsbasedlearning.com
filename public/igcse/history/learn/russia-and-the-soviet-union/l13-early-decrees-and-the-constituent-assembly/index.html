13. The early decrees and the Constituent Assembly

Success criterion 1

Problems after October

In 1917 the Bolsheviks controlled only Petrograd, Moscow and the area between, and Moscow took weeks of fighting. Their promises were hard to keep:

  • Peace: Russia’s allies would not negotiate, so a separate deal with Germany would be very costly.
  • Land: giving peasants land would strengthen the SRs, not the Bolsheviks, who believed in state farms.
  • Bread: taking food from peasants would be hard if they controlled the land.
  • All power to the soviets: this meant the Bolsheviks giving up control.

In fact the party went on to rule as a one-party dictatorship as authoritarian as tsarism.

Success criterion 2

The early decrees

DecreeTerms
Decree on LandPrivate ownership of land abolished; landlords’ and the tsar’s land, and from December Church land, given to peasants. Often just made existing takeovers legal.
Decree on PeaceCalled for peace talks and an immediate armistice, without giving up land or paying compensation.
Decree on NationalitiesPeoples of the old empire could have their own governments, though under Bolshevik control.
Decrees on workers’ rights8-hour day and 48-hour week (12 November); unemployment insurance (14 November); workers’ control of factories (27 December).

Other measures: civil marriage and divorce, women equal to men and able to own property, army ranks and titles abolished, the opposition press banned, and a new secret police, the Cheka, set up. Government was run by Sovnarkom, entirely Bolshevik. In December 1922 the soviet republics became the USSR.

Success criterion 3

The Constituent Assembly

Elections were held on 12 November 1917. The SRs won 53 per cent; the Bolsheviks had just under a quarter, doing well in the cities and among soldiers but badly in the countryside.

At its first meeting on 5 January 1918, the Assembly rejected a Bolshevik proposal giving all power to the soviets. Lenin said it was dominated by counter-revolutionaries; the Bolsheviks walked out, and the next day Red Guards stopped it reopening. It was dissolved after one day. Soon all opposition parties, including the SRs, Mensheviks and Kadets, were banned and their leaders arrested.

Key terms

Decree
An official order from the ruler that has the same power as a law.
Sovnarkom
The Council of People’s Commissars, the Bolshevik government.
Cheka
The Bolshevik secret police, set up in 1917.
Armistice
A truce; an end to fighting for an agreed time.
USSR
The Union of Soviet Socialist Republics, formed in December 1922.
