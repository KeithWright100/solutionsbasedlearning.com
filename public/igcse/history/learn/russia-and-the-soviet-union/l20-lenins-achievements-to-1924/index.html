20. Lenin’s achievements to 1924

Success criterion 1

Illness and death

Lenin suffered strokes in May 1922, December 1922 (after which he retired) and March 1923 (which left him unable to speak). He died on 21 January 1924, aged 53. Stress, his workaholic habits (14-hour days) and the injuries from Kaplan’s attack probably contributed.

A cult of Lenin grew: his words were treated as sacred, his image was everywhere, Petrograd was renamed Leningrad, and his embalmed body was put on display; 100,000 people visited in the first 6 weeks.

Success criterion 2

Key achievements

AchievementWhy it mattered
The April ThesesChanged the party’s aims and gave it clear slogans and more support.
Marxism-LeninismRethought Marxism to fit Russia, where Marxists had thought socialism was years away.
The October RevolutionMasterminded the seizure of power.
Brest-LitovskDemanded peace at any price, helping the Bolsheviks hold on to power.
Single-party ruleRefused to share power; the Soviet Union became a dictatorship.
War CommunismUnpopular, but helped win the Civil War.
The NEPPersuaded the party to retreat from communism when War Communism threatened to bring them down.

When the Bolsheviks took power most people expected them to last weeks; by 1924 they fully controlled the Soviet Union, and whoever succeeded Lenin, it would remain communist.

Success criterion 3

Interpretations

Views of Lenin differ. A British newspaper in 1924 called him an “arch-traitor” whose fanaticism caused millions of deaths, though perhaps sincere in his beliefs. Stalin said Lenin had led the workers and peasants in throwing off landlords and capitalists. Some argue he simply saw how easy it was to seize power; others that the revolution would not have happened without him. Some say he truly believed socialism would help ordinary people; others that he wanted power at all costs.

Key terms

Cult
The worship of a person, as happened with Lenin after his death.
Leningrad
The new name for Petrograd after Lenin’s death.
Marxism-Leninism
Lenin’s version of Marx’s ideas adapted to Russia.
Obituary
A newspaper article about the life of someone who has just died.
Dictatorship
Rule by one person or party with complete power.
