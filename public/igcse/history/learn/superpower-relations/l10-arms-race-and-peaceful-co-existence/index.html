10. The nuclear arms race, new leaders and peaceful co-existence

Success criterion 1

The nuclear arms race

DateEvent
1945USA develops the atomic bomb
1949Soviet Union develops the atomic bomb (helped by spies such as George Koval)
1952USA tests the hydrogen bomb, 1,000 times more powerful than the atomic bomb
1953Soviet Union tests a hydrogen bomb
1957USA develops the ICBM, able to hit a target about 5,000 km away; months later the Soviet Union tests its first ICBM

Each development by one side was quickly matched by the other: reaction and counter-reaction.

Success criterion 2

Impact: deterrence

Both sides spent huge sums on armies, navies, submarines and conventional and nuclear missiles, hoping military superiority would help in any disagreement. But from the early 1950s both could destroy the world many times over. Weapons were now developed to make sure the other side would not go to war at all: they acted as a deterrent. In 1957 George Kennan argued there could never be a “limited” nuclear war.

Success criterion 3

New leaders and peaceful co-existence

  • Dwight Eisenhower became US president in January 1953. He and his secretary of state, John Foster Dulles, were strongly anti-communist, but he was aware of the dangers of nuclear weapons and prepared to negotiate.
  • Stalin died on 5 March 1953. By 1956 Nikita Khrushchev emerged as leader, criticised Stalin’s policies and suggested peaceful co-existence with the West, believing communism was so superior that capitalism would eventually collapse anyway.

Hope was helped by clearly defined borders in Europe, the end of the Korean War in July 1953 and the cost of armed forces. An agreement on Austria and the Geneva Summit (July 1955) created a co-operative atmosphere, although no agreement was reached on disarmament or Germany. The thaw was short-lived: West Germany joined NATO in May 1955 and the Hungarian Uprising followed in 1956.

Key terms

Hydrogen bomb
A nuclear weapon about 1,000 times more powerful than the atomic bomb.
ICBM
An inter-continental ballistic missile that can carry a nuclear weapon thousands of kilometres.
Deterrent
A force that prevents something from happening; nuclear weapons deterred war.
Peaceful co-existence
Khrushchev’s idea of the two systems living side by side in peace.
Geneva Summit
A 1955 meeting that improved the atmosphere between East and West.
