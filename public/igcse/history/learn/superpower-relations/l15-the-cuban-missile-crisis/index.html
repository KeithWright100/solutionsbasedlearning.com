15. The Cuban Missile Crisis, 1962

Success criterion 1

Why missiles in Cuba?

On 14 October 1962 a U-2 spy plane photographed Soviet missile launch sites in Cuba that could reach most US cities. Khrushchev had promised he would not do this. His possible reasons:

  • NATO had missiles in Turkey, close to the Soviet Union; the USA should feel what it was like to be under threat, or the Turkish missiles might be removed;
  • the Berlin Wall was seen by critics as a failure; outwitting Kennedy would restore his reputation;
  • he feared another American attack on Cuba.

The missiles made little practical difference, but a huge political one. McNamara warned they would be ready in 2 weeks.

Success criterion 2

The Thirteen Days, 16–28 October

DateEvent
16 OctoberKennedy sets up ExComm, which meets every day for 13 days
22 OctoberKennedy rejects an attack and announces a naval blockade (“quarantine”) on TV; 54 nuclear bombers are prepared
23–24 OctoberKhrushchev orders the Soviet ships to turn around; Dean Rusk: “We are eyeball to eyeball, and I think the other guy just blinked!”
26 OctoberKhrushchev offers to remove the missiles if the USA promises not to invade Cuba
27 OctoberA second telegram demands removal of US missiles in Turkey; a U-2 is shot down over Cuba. Kennedy ignores the Hawks and accepts the first offer; Robert Kennedy secretly agrees to remove the Turkish missiles
28 OctoberKhrushchev agrees to remove the missiles
Success criterion 3

Outcome and consequences

War was avoided because Khrushchev refused to break the blockade, Kennedy refused to bomb Cuba, neither leader wanted war, and the USA agreed to move its missiles from Turkey.

  • Both sides wanted to avoid future misunderstandings: the hotline (1963) and later the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968).
  • Kennedy’s popularity rose, as the Turkey deal was secret; he was more confident with the Soviet Union.
  • Khrushchev claimed victory for guaranteeing Cuba’s security, but the Soviet military felt humiliated; this contributed to his dismissal in 1964.
  • It showed the dangers of brinkmanship.

Activity: who gained what?

Who gained from each part of the outcome?

  • The missiles were removed from Cuba
  • The USA promised not to invade Cuba
  • US missiles were secretly removed from Turkey
  • Kennedy’s popularity rose at home
  • Kennedy was seen as standing up to the Hawks and Khrushchev
  • Castro’s communist Cuba was protected

Key terms

ExComm
Kennedy’s Executive Committee that met during the crisis.
Blockade (quarantine)
Using ships to stop other ships reaching Cuba.
Hawks and Doves
Hawks supported going to war; Doves looked for peaceful solutions.
Brinkmanship
Pushing a disagreement to the brink of war.
Hotline
A direct communication link between Washington and Moscow set up in 1963.
