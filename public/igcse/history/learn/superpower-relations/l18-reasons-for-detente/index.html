18. Reasons for détente

Success criterion 1

Why the USA wanted détente

Relations were still poor in the 1960s: in 1968 the invasion of Czechoslovakia led President Johnson to cancel a meeting with Brezhnev. But by the end of the decade there was a genuine attempt at détente, a more lasting period of peace.

  • Vietnam: Nixon, elected in 1968, wanted to end a war that had cost around $170 billion and almost 60,000 American lives and showed military power did not always work.
  • Problems at home: inequality led to massive urban riots in 1968 after the assassination of Martin Luther King; less spending on weapons meant more for social problems.
  • Nixon’s ambitions: he wanted to reduce tension and open relations with China, and gain public approval.
  • China: China tested an atomic bomb in 1964; if the USA was friendly with China, the Soviet Union might be pushed into an agreement.
Success criterion 2

Why the Soviet Union wanted détente

  • It now felt equal to the USA in nuclear weapons, so could negotiate from strength; Vietnam showed the USA could be beaten and might accept Soviet control of Eastern Europe.
  • Economic problems: it spent almost 30% of its GDP on defence (the USA 13%) and wanted to improve living standards.
  • It was falling behind in new technology such as computers and microchips and hoped the West might share technology.
  • It was worried about a possible friendship between the USA and China.
Success criterion 3

China and Ostpolitik

China and the Soviet Union signed a treaty of friendship in 1950, but Mao felt badly treated in Moscow. Relations declined under Khrushchev: Mao accused him of cowardice over Cuba and criticised the invasion of Czechoslovakia. In 1969 Chinese and Soviet troops fought on their border, so Mao turned towards the USA.

Ping-pong diplomacy: in April 1971 friendly contact at the World Table Tennis Championships in Japan led to the US team being invited to China. Henry Kissinger then visited Beijing and met Zhou En-lai. In February 1972 Nixon met Mao and they signed the Shanghai Communique, with the USA accepting “only one China”.

Ostpolitik: West German chancellor Willy Brandt (elected 1969) built links between East and West Europe, and other Western European nations followed, boosting détente.

Key terms

Détente
A more lasting period of peace between groups that were previously hostile.
Ping-pong diplomacy
The improvement in US-China relations that began with a table tennis tournament in 1971.
Shanghai Communique
The 1972 agreement between Nixon and Mao on Taiwan and better relations.
Ostpolitik
Willy Brandt’s policy of building links with Eastern Europe.
Henry Kissinger
Nixon’s national security advisor and a key figure in détente.
