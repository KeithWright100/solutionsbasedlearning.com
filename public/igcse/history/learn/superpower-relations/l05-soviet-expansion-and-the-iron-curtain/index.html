5. Soviet expansion in Eastern Europe and the Iron Curtain

Learning Intention

Success criterion 1

Creating satellite states

In 1944–45 the Red Army freed many Eastern European countries from the Nazis. Stalin did not want to give them up, as they were a useful buffer zone.

CountryHow communists took control
Baltic StatesLithuania, Latvia and Estonia were conquered in 1940 and treated as part of the Soviet Union
PolandThe Red Army waited until the 1944 Warsaw Uprising had been crushed, then installed a pro-communist government. After “free” elections in 1947 the London Poles had to flee or face prison
HungaryCommunists did not win enough seats in 1945; in 1947 voters were threatened and a communist government elected. One-party state by 1949
Bulgaria, Romania, East Germany“Free” elections, but voters were frightened into voting communist
CzechoslovakiaThe only democracy in Eastern Europe from 1919. In February 1948, communists backed by the Soviet Union overthrew President Benes and set up a government under Klement Gottwald
Yugoslavia and AlbaniaCommunist, but never occupied by the Red Army, so more independent

Truman saw this as evidence that the Soviet Union wanted to spread communism worldwide.

The telegrams of 1946

Success criterion 3

The Iron Curtain speech, March 1946

In March 1946 Churchill, no longer prime minister, gave a speech at Fulton, Missouri, in the USA. He described an “iron curtain” that had descended across Europe from Stettin in the Baltic to Trieste in the Adriatic, with Eastern Europe under increasing control from Moscow.

Because Churchill was speaking in the USA, Stalin thought the speech reflected American views too. Together with the Novikov Telegram, it led the Soviet Union to strengthen its forces and step up anti-Western propaganda, making hostility worse.

Key terms

Satellite state
A country under the political, economic and military control of the Soviet Union.
Iron Curtain
Churchill’s term for the imaginary line dividing communist Eastern Europe from the West.
Long Telegram
Kennan’s 1946 report on Soviet attitudes to the US government.
Novikov Telegram
The Soviet ambassador’s 1946 report on US attitudes.
Containment
The US policy of preventing the spread of communism.
Embassy
The building where diplomats from a foreign country are based.
