3. The Tehran and Yalta Conferences

Success criterion 1

Tehran, November 1943

The Big Three first met in Tehran to plan a winning strategy. They agreed:

  • the USA and Britain would open a second front in Western Europe, easing pressure on the Soviet Union;
  • Stalin would declare war on Japan once the war in Europe was over;
  • Germany should surrender completely and remain weak after the war;
  • Poland would receive land from Germany, but the Soviet Union could keep land it seized from Poland in 1939 (important to Stalin, who wanted to secure his western border);
  • an international body should be set up to settle disputes, laying the ground for the United Nations.

Effects: Stalin was pleased with the second front. Churchill was less pleased because he wanted it in the Balkans, but Roosevelt sided with Stalin. Roosevelt also seemed at times to see British colonialism as more of a threat than the Soviet Union, so there was tension between the USA and Britain; the “Big Three” looked like it was becoming the “Big Two”.

Success criterion 2

Yalta, February 1945

By Yalta, the Soviets controlled most of Central and Eastern Europe, and Stalin was determined to keep it as a cushion against future German invasions. The Big Three agreed:

IssueAgreement
GermanySplit into four zones (USA, Britain, France, Soviet Union); $20 billion reparations, half to the Soviet Union; the Nazi Party banned and war criminals prosecuted
United NationsTo be set up, first meeting 25 April 1945. Stalin wanted all 16 Soviet republics to be members; only Russia, Ukraine and Belarus were admitted
JapanStalin would join the war against Japan 3 months after Germany’s defeat
Eastern EuropeGovernments to be decided in free elections
PolandBorders returned to their 1921 position, with free elections. Stalin expected a pro-communist government; Britain supported the non-communist London Poles
Success criterion 3

The effects of the conferences

Roosevelt and Stalin were pleased with agreement on free elections and the United Nations, but Poland became a difficult issue. Churchill’s telegram to Stalin in April 1945 warned that a world split into two sides would be a disaster, while Stalin complained in May 1945 that the American attitude had cooled once Germany was clearly defeated.

Key terms

Tehran Conference
November 1943 meeting of the Big Three to plan war strategy.
Yalta Conference
February 1945 meeting of the Big Three to plan post-war Europe.
Reparations
Payments made after a war by the defeated country to the victors as compensation.
United Nations
The global organisation set up in 1945 to try to maintain world peace.
The London Poles
A non-communist Polish government in exile, based in London.
Colonialism
Control of a country by a more powerful one.
