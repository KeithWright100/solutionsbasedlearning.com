Success criterion 1

The Korean War, 1950–53

After 1945 Korea was split at the 38th parallel: the Soviet-backed North and the US-backed South, each claiming to rule the whole country. Truman feared a domino effect: if South Korea fell to communism, others would follow.

DateEvent
25 June 1950North Korea invades South Korea. The Soviet Union is not attending the UN, so the Security Council agrees to support South Korea
1950A UN force from 16 countries, mostly American and commanded by General MacArthur, pushes the North Koreans back across the 38th parallel and captures Pyongyang
Late 1950As MacArthur nears China, Mao sends 200,000 troops; UN forces are pushed back to the 38th parallel
April 1951Truman dismisses MacArthur after disagreements over how the war should be fought
July 1953The war ends with the border still at the 38th parallel

Truman believed the Soviet Union had told North Korea to invade, but Stalin was not keen; it was the North Korean leader, Kim Il-sung, who pushed for it. The human cost was huge: over 30,000 American soldiers and around a million South Korean civilians died.

Success criterion 2

The impact of the war

  • The USA committed itself to supporting other Asian countries resisting communism, strengthened its forces in Europe, encouraged Greece and Turkey to join NATO and tripled its defence budget.
  • In September 1954 SEATO (Southeast Asia Treaty Organization) was formed to stop communism spreading in Southeast Asia.
  • The Soviet Union increased the Red Army from 2.8 million (1950) to almost 5.6 million soldiers (1955).
  • Suspicion grew on both sides and helped fuel the nuclear arms race.
Success criterion 3

The Warsaw Pact, 1955

In May 1955 West Germany joined NATO. There was now a real danger of an armed, powerful Germany on the borders of Soviet-controlled Eastern Europe. Within a week the Soviet Union formed the Warsaw Pact, a communist defensive military alliance of the Soviet Union, Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Albania and East Germany (the “Eastern Bloc”). The leadership was entirely Soviet. Europe was now, in reality, two Europes.

Key terms

38th parallel
The border between North and South Korea.
Domino effect
The idea that if one country became communist others would follow, like falling dominoes.
SEATO
The Southeast Asia Treaty Organization, formed in 1954 to contain communism.
Warsaw Pact
The Soviet-led military alliance of communist countries formed in 1955.
Eastern Bloc
The communist countries of the Warsaw Pact.
