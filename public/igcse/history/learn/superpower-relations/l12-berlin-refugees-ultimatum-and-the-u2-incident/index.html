12. Berlin 1958–61: refugees, the ultimatum and the U-2 incident

Edexcel IGCSE History · A world divided: superpower relations, 1943–72 · 4. Three crises: Berlin, Cuba and Czechoslovakia · Lesson 12 of 19

Learning Intention

To understand why Berlin became a crisis again from 1958 and why the summits failed.

Syllabus Statement

The U2 incident (1960) and its effects on the Paris Summit Conference. Reasons for the construction of the Berlin Wall in 1961, including the refugee problem.

Success criterion 1

The refugee problem

West Germany received Marshall Aid and became wealthy in the 1950s; East Germany had a low standard of living, strict limits on freedom and a secret police. In 1953 there were riots, put down by Soviet forces.

East Germans could escape simply by crossing from East to West Berlin. By 1958, 3 million (over a sixth of the population) had left, including skilled engineers, technicians and teachers. This damaged East Germany’s economy and was a propaganda disaster for communism: given a choice, people preferred the West.

Success criterion 2

The Berlin Ultimatum and the summits

In November 1958 Khrushchev demanded that the West recognise East Germany. His Berlin Ultimatum (27 November) demanded that Berlin be demilitarised, Western troops withdrawn and Berlin become a “free city”. Otherwise, in 6 months he would hand control of the routes into Berlin to East Germany, forcing the West to deal with it as a real country. Neither side wanted war, so talks followed.

SummitWhat happened
Geneva, May 1959Foreign ministers met; proposals on both sides, but no agreement. Eisenhower invited Khrushchev to the USA
Camp David, September 1959First face-to-face meeting of Eisenhower and Khrushchev. No agreement on Berlin, but the Ultimatum was withdrawn and a Paris summit agreed
Paris, May 1960Wrecked by the U-2 incident (see below)
Vienna, June 1961Khrushchev, thinking the new president Kennedy inexperienced after the Bay of Pigs failure, renewed the Ultimatum. Kennedy refused concessions and increased defence spending by over $2 billion
Success criterion 3

The U-2 incident, May 1960

On 1 May 1960 the Soviets shot down an American U-2 spy plane. The USA claimed it was a weather plane, but the captured pilot, Gary Powers, admitted spying. Eisenhower was embarrassed but refused to apologise, saying such flights were necessary. Khrushchev, who had publicly committed himself to peaceful co-existence, walked out and the Paris Summit ended with no decisions.

Activity: which summit?

Match each event to the summit.

  • The Berlin Ultimatum was withdrawn
  • Khrushchev walked out over the U-2 spy plane
  • Khrushchev renewed the Berlin Ultimatum
  • First face-to-face meeting of Eisenhower and Khrushchev
  • Kennedy met Khrushchev after the Bay of Pigs
  • Ended with no decisions after Eisenhower refused to apologise

Key terms

Refugee
A person who leaves their home to escape danger or hardship.
Ultimatum
A final demand, often backed up with a threat.
Free city
A city with its own independent government.
U-2
An American high-altitude spy plane.
Summit
A meeting between heads of government.
