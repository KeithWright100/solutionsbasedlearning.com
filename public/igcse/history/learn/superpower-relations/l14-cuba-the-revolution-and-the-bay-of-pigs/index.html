14. Cuba: the revolution and the Bay of Pigs

Success criterion 1

The Cuban Revolution, 1959

In January 1959 revolutionaries led by Fidel Castro and Che Guevara overthrew Fulgencio Batista’s pro-American government. American businesses owned much of Cuba’s land, oil refineries, electricity, telephones and railways, and invested heavily in sugar. Castro was a nationalist who did not want US control.

DateEvent
May 1959Cuba takes over all foreign-owned land; the USA refuses to recognise the compensation scheme
February 1960Castro signs an agreement with Khrushchev: the Soviets buy sugar and give aid, with a secret clause for arms
July–October 1960Eisenhower cuts sugar purchases, then bans all trade with Cuba
January 1961The USA breaks off diplomatic relations

A pro-Soviet regime just 145 km from the US mainland alarmed the USA.

Success criterion 2

The Bay of Pigs, April 1961

Kennedy backed a CIA plan, agreed under Eisenhower, for trained Cuban exiles to invade so it would look like a Cuban counter-revolution. On 17 April 1961 about 1,400 exiles landed at the Bay of Pigs. It was a complete failure:

  • the volunteers had little military experience against an experienced army;
  • the USA could not send ground forces or air strikes without revealing its involvement;
  • Castro knew about the plans and had 20,000 soldiers waiting;
  • most Cubans supported Castro and did not join the invaders.
Success criterion 3

The effects

  • A public humiliation for the USA, which could not deny involvement. It looked as if the USA was doing what it accused the Soviets of: building an empire.
  • Khrushchev claimed it showed the popularity of communism.
  • Castro declared himself a communist and asked for Soviet protection; in September 1961 Khrushchev announced he would provide arms to Cuba.
  • Kennedy warned Khrushchev not to use Cuba as a base to threaten the USA; Khrushchev assured him he would not.
  • Khrushchev saw Kennedy as weak and inexperienced, which shaped the Vienna Summit.

Activity: why did it fail?

Is each a reason the Bay of Pigs failed, or not?

  • Castro had 20,000 soldiers waiting
  • The USA sent a huge air force to help
  • Most Cubans supported Castro
  • The exiles had little military experience
  • The invasion was kept completely secret
  • The USA could not openly send its own forces

Key terms

Fidel Castro
The revolutionary leader of Cuba from 1959.
Nationalist
Someone who wants their country to be independent and free from foreign control.
CIA
The Central Intelligence Agency, the US spy organisation.
Exiles
People forced or choosing to live outside their own country.
Bay of Pigs
The failed US-backed invasion of Cuba in April 1961.
