4. Potsdam, Truman and Stalin, and the atomic bomb

Success criterion 1

A changed situation

The Potsdam Conference (July–August 1945) took place only months after Yalta, but much had changed:

  • Roosevelt died in April 1945 and was replaced by Harry S. Truman, who took a “get tough” approach and was much more suspicious of Stalin. He believed the Soviets wanted to spread communism worldwide.
  • Churchill lost the 1945 election; the new prime minister, Clement Attlee, was new to diplomacy and wanted to return home quickly.
  • Germany had surrendered in May 1945, removing the common enemy.
  • The USA had developed an atomic bomb; Truman delayed the conference until it was ready, believing it gave him an edge.
  • The United Nations had been created in June 1945.
Success criterion 2

Decisions and disagreements at Potsdam

AgreedDisagreed
Germany divided into four zones, but the economy run as a wholeTruman objected to Soviet control of the countries it had liberated, seeing the Red Army as an army of occupation
Berlin also divided into four zones, though it was deep inside the Soviet zoneTruman objected to the arrangements and borders for Poland and wanted less communist influence
Each power to take reparations from its own zone; the Soviet Union could also take a quarter of the industrial equipment from the other zonesNo agreement on the government of Eastern Europe

Stalin saw control of Eastern Europe as a reasonable defensive measure to secure the Soviet Union. Truman saw it as communist aggression. In 1945–46 this basic disagreement turned the wartime alliance into peacetime hostility.

Success criterion 3

The impact of the atomic bomb

On 6 August 1945 the USA dropped an atomic bomb on Hiroshima and on 9 August on Nagasaki; over 120,000 Japanese civilians were killed. Some historians argue the USA used the bombs partly to strengthen its position against the Soviet Union.

  • It made Truman more confident at Potsdam and made Western Europe feel safer under American protection.
  • It had the opposite effect to what the USA hoped: Stalin became even more determined to create a buffer zone in Eastern Europe.
  • The US monopoly did not last: the Soviet Union tested its own bomb in August 1949. An arms race began, although the terrible consequences may also have made both sides more reluctant to go to war.

Key terms

Potsdam Conference
July–August 1945 meeting where the Allies agreed to divide Germany and Berlin.
Harry S. Truman
US president from April 1945, who took a “get tough” approach to Stalin.
Army of occupation
Troops that stay in a country to control it.
Atomic bomb
A nuclear weapon first used by the USA on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in 1945.
Arms race
Competition between the superpowers to have more and better weapons.
