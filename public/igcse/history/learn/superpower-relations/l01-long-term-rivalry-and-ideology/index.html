1. Long-term rivalry and ideological differences

Learning Intention

To understand why the Soviet Union and the West distrusted each other long before 1945.

Syllabus Statement

Long-term rivalry between the Soviet Union and the West and the ideological differences between Communism and Capitalism.

Success criterion 1

From Grand Alliance to Cold War

In April 1945, Soviet and American soldiers met at the River Elbe in Germany. Since 1941 the USA, Britain and the Soviet Union had co-operated in a Grand Alliance to defeat Nazi Germany. The USA and Britain sent the Soviet Union weapons, ships, aircraft and food to resist the German invasion.

Even as allies they were suspicious of each other. Stalin was determined that Soviet forces should enter Berlin before the Americans, and around 70,000 Soviet soldiers died in the attack. Within a year of victory, the two superpowers (the USA and the Soviet Union) were in a Cold War: not a military conflict, but a war of words, using propaganda, spying, loans and aid, the threat of war and an arms race.

Success criterion 2

Capitalism versus communism

The basic reason for mistrust was that the two sides had very different ideologies (sets of shared beliefs). The Soviet Union feared the West wanted to impose its beliefs on it; the West feared the Soviet Union wanted to spread communism worldwide.

Soviet Union (communism)USA and Britain (capitalism)
PoliticsSingle-party ruleFree elections with a choice of parties
Social structureClassless society, everyone equalSome people have more power than others (family, wealth, education, achievement)
EconomyAll property owned by the statePrivate ownership and competition between businesses
RightsRights of all workers more important than individual rightsIndividual freedoms valued, but limited by majority opinion

Success criterion 3

Distrust from 1917 to 1941

DateEvent
1917The Bolsheviks (communists) take power in Russia and make peace with Germany, dropping out of the First World War.
1918–20Britain, France and the USA send forces to support the Bolsheviks’ opponents in the civil war. They fail, but the Bolsheviks are left in no doubt that the West wants them overthrown.
1922Russia becomes the Soviet Union. Relations with the West stay poor in the 1920s and 1930s.
1938Britain, France and Italy allow Hitler to take parts of Czechoslovakia. Stalin is not even consulted and concludes the West would be happy for Germany to attack the Soviet Union.
August 1939The Nazi-Soviet Pact: Hitler and Stalin agree to split Poland. Britain and France feel betrayed.
June 1941Hitler invades the Soviet Union, which only then joins the West on the same side.

Key terms

Superpowers
The two most powerful nations at this time: the USA and the Soviet Union.
Cold War
A war of words, propaganda, spying and threats rather than direct fighting between the superpowers.
Ideology
A set of shared beliefs about how a country should be governed.
Capitalism
The belief that everyone should be free to own property and businesses and make money.
Communism
The belief that all property should belong to the state so everyone has a fair share.
Grand Alliance
The wartime alliance of Britain, the USA and the Soviet Union, 1941–45.
