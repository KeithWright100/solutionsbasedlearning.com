2. Tensions in the Grand Alliance and the Big Three

Success criterion 1

The Big Three

Roosevelt (USA), Churchill (Britain) and Stalin (Soviet Union) are called the Big Three. Their suspicions of each other shaped relations from 1941.

LeaderRoleAttitudes
Franklin D. RooseveltPresident of the USA, 1933–45Believed strongly in democracy but formed an alliance with Stalin. Wanted Soviet support against Japan, so was not always as tough with Stalin as Churchill wanted. Believed peace needed the Soviet Union to be accepted as a superpower and partner.
Winston ChurchillPrime minister of Britain, 1940–45 and 1951–55A Conservative from an aristocratic family who believed strongly in the British Empire. Deeply suspicious of Stalin and saw his role as stopping Soviet expansion.
Joseph StalinLeader of the Soviet Union, 1920s–1953Strengthened one-party rule and cut back individual rights. Convinced the West wanted to destroy communism. Wanted to keep Germany weak and create a “buffer zone” of pro-Soviet countries.

Success criterion 2

Disagreements during the war

  • The second front. Stalin wanted Britain and the USA to open a second front in France to take pressure off the Soviet Union on the Eastern Front. It was not opened until 1944. Stalin believed the delay was deliberate, so the Soviet Union would be weakened by fighting Germany alone.
  • Fears about each other’s aims. Churchill and Roosevelt feared Stalin wanted to install communism in the Eastern European countries Germany had conquered. Stalin feared they wanted Germany and the Soviet Union to fight a long war that would leave both weak.
  • The atomic bomb. The Americans kept their atomic bomb secret from Stalin until the very last moment before using it on Japan in August 1945. Stalin worried the USA might use such bombs against the Soviet Union in the future.
Success criterion 3

The Soviet Union and Eastern Europe

The Soviet Union suffered enormously in what Stalin called the Great Patriotic War: over 11 million Soviet soldiers and nearly 17 million civilians were killed. Stalin believed these sacrifices gave him the right to control Eastern Europe as satellite states that would provide raw materials and protect the Soviet Union from future attacks from the West.

The USA, in contrast, believed victory over Germany was a victory for democracy, and that people in Europe should have the same rights and freedoms as Americans, to stop communism spreading. These opposing views made it almost impossible to agree how post-war Europe should be governed.

Key terms

The Big Three
Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin, the leaders of the Grand Alliance.
Second front
A new centre of fighting against Germany in Western Europe, which Stalin wanted the Allies to open.
Buffer zone
An area of friendly countries protecting a nation from attack.
Satellite state
A nation that was once independent but is now under the control of another.
The Great Patriotic War
Stalin’s name for the Soviet fight against Nazi Germany.
