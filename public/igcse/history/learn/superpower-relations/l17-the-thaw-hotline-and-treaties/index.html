17. The thaw: the Hotline and the arms treaties

Learning Intention

To understand why the superpowers agreed to limit nuclear weapons in the 1960s.

Success criterion 1

MAD and the lessons of Cuba

By the 1960s both superpowers could destroy each other several times over. Many believed this Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD) would keep the peace, because no one would risk a war that would also destroy their own country. But the Cuban Missile Crisis showed leaders could lose control of events and war could break out almost by accident, so both sides looked for ways to reduce the threat.

Success criterion 2

The Hotline and the Test Ban Treaty, 1963

The Hotline (agreed 20 June 1963) was a direct communication link between the White House and the Kremlin for use in emergencies. It was not a red telephone but a telegraph link along a 16,000 km cable. Nixon and Brezhnev used it in 1971 during a war between India and Pakistan.

The Limited Test Ban Treaty (1963): as the dangers of radioactive fallout became clearer (for example the crew of the fishing boat Lucky Dragon fell ill after a 1954 US test), the USA, the Soviet Union and Britain agreed not to test nuclear weapons in the atmosphere, under water or in outer space. A further 113 countries signed.

Success criterion 3

Outer Space and Non-Proliferation

TreatyTermsSignificance
Outer Space Treaty, 1967No nuclear weapons or weapons of mass destruction in orbit or on planets; the Moon used only for peaceful purposes; no one can own the Moon; space used for the benefit of allDrawn up by the USA, Soviet Union and Britain; signed by 12 others. Stopped the arms race spreading into space
Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty, 1968Nuclear weapon states (USA, Soviet Union, China, France, Britain) would work towards disarmament and not help others develop weapons; non-nuclear states agreed not to develop themSigned by the USA, Soviet Union, Britain and 59 other states. Key terms

Thaw
An easing of tension between East and West.
MAD
Mutually Assured Destruction: the belief that nuclear war would destroy both sides, so neither would start one.
Hotline
A direct communication link between Washington and Moscow.
Fallout
Radioactive particles spread after a nuclear explosion.
Non-proliferation
Stopping the spread of nuclear weapons.
