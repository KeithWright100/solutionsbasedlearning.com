6. The Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, Cominform and Comecon

Success criterion 1

The Truman Doctrine, 1947

After the war many European countries were in ruins, and communism looked attractive to poorer people. Britain was nearly bankrupt and in 1947 announced it could no longer support the Greek government against communist guerrillas.

On 12 March 1947 Truman told Congress the USA would give $400 million in aid to Greece and Turkey. He described two ways of life: democracy, with majority rule and freedom, and communism, where a minority forced its will on the majority through terror, a controlled press and fixed elections. The USA would send troops and economic aid to countries resisting communism. This was the Truman Doctrine: the USA abandoned isolationism for containment and set itself up as leader of the fight against communism.

Success criterion 2

The Marshall Plan

Announced by US secretary of state George Marshall in June 1947, the Marshall Plan was the practical outcome of the Truman Doctrine: economic aid so that poverty would not lead to communism.

  • Between 1948 and 1952 the USA gave $13.7 billion (on top of $13 billion already given).
  • Sixteen countries benefited; Britain received more than a quarter of the total ($3,297 million), followed by France and West Germany.
  • Aid included direct help such as nets for Norwegian fishermen and food for hungry people.
  • Eastern European countries could only accept if they agreed to a review of their finances, which Stalin would not allow, so in practice they did not benefit.
Success criterion 3

The Soviet response

Stalin saw the Truman Doctrine as the USA extending its influence and weakening the United Nations. He called the Marshall Plan “dollar imperialism”: using economic power to divide Europe and build an American economic empire.

OrganisationSet upPurpose
Cominform22 September 1947A political organisation of Communist parties across Europe. It let Stalin direct and control satellite governments, rejected the Marshall Plan, spread propaganda and ensured loyalty to Moscow
Comecon25 January 1949An economic organisation (Soviet Union, Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Poland, Romania; East Germany and Albania joined later) to rival the Marshall Plan, support economic development and discourage trade with the West

Effects: any remaining belief in a Grand Alliance was gone; Stalin’s suspicions grew; Western Europe was tied to the USA; Europe was divided into two economic and political camps.

Key terms

Truman Doctrine
Truman’s 1947 promise to help countries resisting communism.
Isolationism
Staying apart from the affairs of other countries.
Marshall Plan
US economic aid to help Europe recover and resist communism, 1948–52.
Dollar imperialism
The Soviet term for using financial aid to win support and control.
Cominform
The Communist Information Bureau, set up by Stalin in 1947.
Comecon
The Council for Mutual Economic Assistance, set up in 1949.
