18. Living standards and poverty

Cambridge IGCSE Economics · 5 Economic development · Syllabus 5.1–5.2 · Part 1 of 2 in this topic

Learning Intention

To compare living standards using real GDP per head and the HDI, and to explain the causes of poverty and the policies to reduce it.

Syllabus Statement

5.1 Living standards: real GDP per head and the HDI (components, advantages and disadvantages); reasons for differences in living standards and income distribution within and between countries. 5.2 Poverty: absolute and relative poverty; causes of poverty; policies to alleviate poverty and redistribute income.

Success criterion 1

Indicators of living standards

The standard of living is the social and economic wellbeing of people in a country.

Real GDP per head = real GDP ÷ population. It is the most widely used single indicator: higher real GDP per head means the average person can buy more goods and services. GDP must be divided by population (China’s GDP is far larger than Sweden’s, but its GDP per head is lower) and adjusted for inflation (real, not nominal).

Easy to calculate and widely availableIgnores how income is distributed: the average may hide great inequalityConsiders health and education as well as incomeStill ignores income distribution
Shows the average person’s purchasing powerIgnores quality of life: pollution, working hours, freedomA wider (composite) measure, so better reflects wellbeingIgnores gender inequality, human rights and political freedom

Exam tip: The specimen paper asks why the HDI is more useful than real GDP per head: because it includes a range of measures (health and education as well as income).

Success criterion 2

Why living standards and income distribution differ

ReasonExplanation
ProductivityMore productive and skilled workers earn more.
EducationHigher educational attainment raises earning potential.
Role of governmentProgressive taxes and benefits redistribute income; mixed economies redistribute more than market economies.
Population size and structureRapid population growth lowers GDP per head; crowded cities have high rents and congestion.
Distribution and composition of GDPHigh GDP may go mainly to the rich, or into military spending rather than services.
Regional differencesIncomes vary within countries: London’s median earnings are much higher than in Wales or Northern Ireland.
General price levelHigh inflation (e.g. in Venezuela) reduces real incomes.
FreedomCivil, political and economic freedoms affect wellbeing.
Success criterion 3

Absolute and relative poverty

Poverty exists when people lack enough income and wealth for a basic standard of living.

TypeMeaning
Absolute povertyExtreme poverty: people cannot afford the basic needs for survival (food, clean water, shelter, clothing). The World Bank’s international poverty line is $2.15 a day. Example: about 65% of Burundi’s population in 2023.
Relative povertyBeing poor compared with the average person in the same society: a person with a much lower income than others in their country, even if they can afford basic needs. It exists in rich countries too.

Causes of poverty:

  • Unemployment: no earned income; low skills and poor health keep productivity low.
  • Low wages: many unskilled workers; low GDP per head.
  • Illness and poor healthcare: low life expectancy and lost working days; low health spending.
  • Age: children (child labour where families are very poor) and elderly people without pensions.
  • Environmental factors: droughts, floods, hurricanes and deforestation destroy crops, homes and livelihoods.
  • Other causes: low literacy, high population growth, poor infrastructure, low foreign direct investment, high public debt, reliance on low-value primary products, corruption and instability.

Key terms

Standard of living
The social and economic wellbeing of people in a country.
Real GDP per head
Real GDP divided by the population.
Human Development Index (HDI)
A composite measure of development combining health (life expectancy), education (years of schooling) and income.
Absolute poverty
When people cannot afford the basic needs for survival; living below $2.15 a day.
Relative poverty
Being poor compared with the average member of the same society.
Income distribution
How a country’s income is shared among its people.
Redistribution of income
Policies that transfer income from richer to poorer people.
Paper 2-style question (c)

Analyse how a decrease in investment may increase poverty. [6]

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for understanding investment and 1 mark for poverty, remaining marks for analysis (or up to 6 for analysis with implicit understanding).
  • Investment is spending on capital goods (1); less investment means output falls (1), unemployment rises (1), incomes fall (1), people struggle to buy necessities (1), increasing absolute poverty (1).
  • Less investment in education / training / healthcare (1) reduces skills and productivity (1), lowering some wages (1), widening the gap between rich and poor (1), increasing relative poverty (1).
  • Firms use older equipment (1), raising average costs (1) and prices (1), reducing purchasing power (1).
Paper 2-style question (d)

Discuss whether or not the HDI is a better measure of living standards than real GDP per head. [8]

Show mark guidance
  • Level 3 (6–8): a reasoned discussion that accurately considers both sides of the argument, with clear, logical analysis and accurate use of terms; may point out uncertainties and reach a justified conclusion.
  • Level 2 (3–5): a reasoned discussion with some clear analysis, but it may lack depth or be one-sided.
  • Level 1 (1–2): relevant definitions or statements that are not developed.
  • Why it is better: a composite measure including health and education as well as income; shows whether income is turned into longer, healthier, better-educated lives; less affected by a few very rich people.
  • Why it may not be: still ignores income distribution, gender inequality, freedom, pollution and the environment; uses averages; real GDP per head is simpler, more up to date and directly measures purchasing power.
