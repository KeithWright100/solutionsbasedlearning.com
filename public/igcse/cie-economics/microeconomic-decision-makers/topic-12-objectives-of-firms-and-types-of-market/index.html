12. Objectives of firms and types of market

Cambridge IGCSE Economics · 3 Microeconomic decision-makers · Syllabus 3.6.5, 3.7 · Part 5 of 5 in this topic

Learning Intention

To explain the different objectives of firms and compare the effects of competitive and monopoly markets on price, quality, choice and profit.

Syllabus Statement

3.6.5 Objectives of firms: survival, social welfare, profit maximisation and growth. 3.7 Types of markets: characteristics, advantages and disadvantages of competitive markets and monopoly markets, and their effects on price, quality, choice and profit.

Success criterion 1

Objectives of firms

ObjectiveExplanation
SurvivalStaying in business, vital for new firms and in difficult trading times. In the long run firms need to be profitable to survive.
Social welfareConcern for workers, customers, communities and the environment; acting ethically (Patagonia gives 1% of sales to environmental groups). Improves the firm’s image.
Profit maximisationMaking the largest possible difference between total revenue and total cost. Profit = TR − TC. The main goal of most private sector firms, and the reward for risk-taking.
GrowthIncreasing the size of the firm, usually its sales revenue and market share. Brings economies of scale and customer loyalty.

Objectives can conflict: social welfare goals (fair trade, no animal testing) may raise costs and reduce profit, as The Body Shop found when it closed its US stores in 2024 after falling sales and rising costs. Public sector organisations usually aim to provide a service rather than make a profit.

Success criterion 2

Competitive markets

Market structure describes the number and size of firms, the type of competition and the barriers to entry. Barriers to entry are obstacles that stop new firms joining a market (high start-up costs, patents, economies of scale of existing firms, large advertising budgets, legal restrictions).

A competitive market has many firms, none of which can control market price, with low barriers to entry (for example a fresh food market).

Effect of many firms onExplanation
PriceFirms are price takers: the price is set by market demand and supply, and competition keeps prices low.
QualityFirms compete on quality and customer service (non-price competition); some sell identical (homogeneous) products.
ChoiceWide choice; firms differentiate products through branding, design and slogans.
ProfitProfits are relatively low for each firm because they are shared among many rivals.
Success criterion 3

Monopoly markets

A monopoly is a market with a single supplier, which has the power to influence market supply and price. A pure monopoly is the only supplier (the US Federal Reserve issuing banknotes; India’s IRCTC selling train tickets). In practice, firms with a large market share (EssilorLuxottica in eyewear, Apple and Samsung in smartphones) have monopoly power.

  • Single supplier with no close substitutes.
  • Price maker: can set its own price, but cannot control demand, so a higher price still means lower sales.
  • High barriers to entry keep rivals out.
  • Imperfect knowledge: trade secrets and confusing pricing.
Effect of one firm onExplanation
PriceLikely to be higher; output restricted (demand is often price inelastic because there are no substitutes).
QualityMay be poor: little incentive to improve without competition.
ChoiceLittle or none for consumers.
ProfitCan be high in the long run because barriers to entry protect the firm.

Exam tip: Do not say a monopolist can charge “any price it wants”. It controls supply, not demand: if it raises the price, consumers buy less.

Success criterion 4

Evaluating competition and monopoly

Advantages of competitive marketsDisadvantages of competitive markets
Lower prices and higher outputSome firms fail and close
Better quality and customer serviceSmall firms may not afford research and development
More choice for consumersToo much choice wastes consumers’ time
Innovation to stay ahead of rivalsMoney spent on advertising could be used elsewhere; profits shared among many firms
Advantages of monopolyDisadvantages of monopoly
Huge economies of scale may allow lower prices and large outputMay restrict output and charge higher prices
Large profits fund research and development and innovationLess incentive to innovate or be efficient without rivals
Natural monopolies avoid wasteful duplication (water pipes, rail tracks, electricity grids)High barriers to entry stop new firms competing
Can compete with large foreign firmsConsumers have little choice and imperfect knowledge

Governments may intervene against monopolies acting against the public interest: blocking mergers (South Korea blocked a merger of two exam-preparation firms in 2024), regulating prices, or requiring common standards (the EU’s USB-C charger law).

Key terms

Survival
The objective of staying in business.
Social welfare
Business activity concerned with the wellbeing of workers, customers, society and the environment.
Profit maximisation
Making the largest possible difference between total revenue and total cost.
Growth
Increasing the size of a firm, usually its sales and market share.
Market structure
The characteristics of a market: number and size of firms, type of competition and barriers to entry.
Barriers to entry
Obstacles that prevent new firms from entering a market.
Competitive market
A market with many firms, none able to control the market price.
Price taker
A firm that must accept the price set by the market.
Monopoly
A market with a single supplier that can influence market supply and price.
Price maker
A firm with the power to set its own price.
Natural monopoly
An industry where one firm can supply the market at lower cost than several firms, e.g. water pipes.
Paper 2-style question (c)

Analyse why a monopoly may charge a higher price than firms in a competitive market. [6]

Show mark guidance
  • A monopoly is the single supplier / has market power / is a price maker (1).
  • There are no close substitutes (1), so demand is price inelastic (1).
  • High barriers to entry stop new firms entering (1), so there is no competition to push the price down (1).
  • It can restrict supply / output (1) to raise the price and maximise profit (1).
  • In a competitive market, firms are price takers and a high price would lose customers to rivals (1).
Paper 2-style question (d)

Discuss whether or not a monopoly is always bad for consumers. [8]

Show mark guidance
  • Level 3 (6–8): a reasoned discussion that accurately considers both sides of the argument, with clear, logical analysis and accurate use of terms; may point out uncertainties and reach a justified conclusion.
  • Level 2 (3–5): a reasoned discussion with some clear analysis, but it may lack depth or be one-sided.
  • Level 1 (1–2): relevant definitions or statements that are not developed.
  • Bad for consumers: higher prices and lower output; poor quality and little choice; less incentive to innovate; imperfect knowledge; high profits at consumers’ expense.
  • Not always bad: economies of scale may lower costs and prices; profits fund research and development and new products; natural monopolies avoid wasteful duplication; governments can regulate monopolies.
