20. Specialisation, free trade, globalisation and MNCs

Cambridge IGCSE Economics · 6 International trade and globalisation · Syllabus 6.1, 6.2.1–6.2.3 · Part 1 of 4 in this topic

Learning Intention

To understand why countries specialise and trade, the advantages and disadvantages of free trade, the causes and effects of globalisation, and the role of multinational companies.

Syllabus Statement

6.1 Specialisation and free trade: specialisation by country, its basis, advantages and disadvantages; definition, advantages and disadvantages of free trade. 6.2 Globalisation: definition; causes and consequences of changes in globalisation; the role of MNCs and their advantages and disadvantages to host and home countries.

Success criterion 1

Specialisation by country

Specialisation by country is when a country concentrates on producing particular goods or services, usually those it can produce at low cost or with its best resources: textiles in Bangladesh and India, rice in Thailand and Vietnam, timber in Sweden, tourism in Caribbean islands. The basis is the best allocation of scarce resources: Sweden could grow tropical fruit, but it is cheaper to import it and specialise in timber.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Efficiency: resources are used where they are most productive, raising GDPOver-specialisation: if demand falls (Sheffield steel) there is regional and structural unemployment
Higher labour productivity and better qualityVulnerable to shocks: Liberia relies on agriculture for about 70% of GDP, so bad weather hits hard
Larger output: the PPC shifts outwardsLess variety for consumers
Economies of scale and lower pricesBoring, repetitive work: high labour turnover and low labour mobility
Greater international competitiveness and tradeHigher labour costs for highly specialised workers
Success criterion 2

Free trade

International trade is the exchange of goods and services between countries (exports and imports). Free trade is international trade without restrictions such as tariffs or quotas.

Advantages of free tradeDisadvantages of free trade
Access to goods and resources a country cannot produce itselfJob losses in domestic industries that cannot compete
Lower prices: cheaper imports and lower costs of tradingWider income inequality: skilled workers benefit most
Greater choice for consumers and firmsPossible exploitation of workers where labour standards are low
Larger markets, so firms gain economies of scaleEnvironmental damage from more production and transport
Competition forces domestic firms to become more efficient and improve qualityGreater dependence on other countries
Better international relations and cooperationInfant industries may struggle to grow

Exam tip: The specimen paper defines international trade as “the exchange of goods (and services) between countries”: two marks for two points.

Success criterion 3

Globalisation

Globalisation is the process by which the world’s economies become increasingly interconnected and interdependent through trade, investment, migration and the flow of ideas and culture.

Effect onExplanation
International tradeMore trade as barriers fall (China joining the WTO in 2001); trade wars reduce it.
CompetitionMore rivals from abroad (Amazon, Alibaba, Huawei) force firms to innovate and cut costs.
The environmentMore production and transport raise emissions; but global agreements (Paris Agreement) and greener MNC practices can help.
MigrationWorkers move to better-paid jobs (free movement in the EU); restrictions reduce it (Brexit).
Income distributionSkilled workers and large firms gain most; less-skilled workers in rich countries may lose jobs to lower-wage countries.
Economic developmentAccess to markets, FDI and technology can speed development; less globalisation limits it.
Success criterion 4

Multinational companies (MNCs)

A multinational company (MNC) is a firm that operates in two or more countries (Apple, Toyota, Samsung, Coca-Cola). The host country is where it sets up operations; the home country is where its headquarters are.

Advantages for the host countryDisadvantages for the host country
Creates jobs and raises incomes and living standardsMay pay low wages and offer poor working conditions
Brings investment (FDI), technology, skills and trainingCan force less competitive local firms to close
Increases output, exports and tax revenueProfits may be sent back to the home country
Economies of scale may lower pricesVery powerful: may pressure governments for subsidies and tax breaks
Increases competitionMay deplete natural resources and pollute; may leave suddenly (Standard Chartered left five African countries in 2020)
Advantages for the home countryDisadvantages for the home country
Profits are returned (repatriated) to the home countryJobs may be moved abroad, raising unemployment at home
Access to new markets and lower costs makes the firm more competitiveLess tax revenue if production moves overseas
Can avoid trade restrictions by producing inside a trade bloc (Nissan in the EU)Reputation damage if overseas practices are unethical

Activity: host or home country?

Is each effect of an MNC most likely to affect its host country or its home country?

  • New factory jobs where the MNC sets up
  • Profits sent back to the headquarters country
  • Local firms forced to close by competition
  • Factory jobs lost when production moves abroad
  • Transfer of new technology to local workers
  • Higher tax revenue from repatriated profits

Key terms

Specialisation by country
When a country concentrates on producing particular goods or services, usually where it has low costs.
Over-specialisation
Relying too much on a narrow range of products, increasing risk.
International trade
The exchange of goods and services between countries.
Free trade
International trade without restrictions such as tariffs or quotas.
Globalisation
The process by which economies become increasingly interconnected and interdependent.
Multinational company (MNC)
A firm that operates in two or more countries.
Host country
The country where an MNC sets up operations.
Home country
The country where an MNC has its headquarters.
Foreign direct investment (FDI)
Investment by firms in production facilities in another country.
