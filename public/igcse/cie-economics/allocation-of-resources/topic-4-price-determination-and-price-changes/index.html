4. Price determination and price changes

Success criterion 1

The price mechanism

The price mechanism is the system of relying on the market forces of demand and supply to allocate resources. The private sector answers what, how and for whom to produce. Its features:

  • No government interference: private individuals and firms own resources and are free to allocate them.
  • Allocation by price: a high price encourages supply; a low price encourages buying. Goods go to those willing and able to pay.
  • Financial incentives: resources move to their most profitable use; unprofitable products stop being made.
  • Competition gives firms opportunities and consumers choice, innovation and quality.

Prices act as signals: rising prices tell firms to produce more of a good; falling prices tell them to produce less.

Success criterion 2

Market equilibrium

Market equilibrium is where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied. The equilibrium price (market-clearing price) is where the demand and supply curves intersect; at this price there is neither a shortage nor a surplus.

Success criterion 3

Disequilibrium: shortages and surpluses

Market disequilibrium occurs when quantity demanded does not equal quantity supplied.

SituationResultNameWhat happens next
Price below equilibriumQuantity demanded > quantity suppliedShortage (excess demand)Price is pushed up; demand contracts and supply extends until equilibrium.
Price above equilibriumQuantity supplied > quantity demandedSurplus (excess supply)Firms cut prices to sell stock (e.g. summer clothes on sale in autumn).

Worked example: excess supply

In the schedule above, at a price of $30 quantity supplied is 8,000 and quantity demanded is 6,000 a week.

Excess supply = 8,000 − 6,000 = 2,000 units a week. Over four weeks, suppliers would build up 8,000 unsold units.

Success criterion 4

Causes and consequences of price changes

Equilibrium price and quantity change when demand or supply shifts.

ChangeEquilibrium priceEquilibrium quantity
Demand increases (shift right)RisesRises
Demand decreases (shift left)FallsFalls
Supply increases (shift right)FallsRises
Supply decreases (shift left)RisesFalls

Effect of price changes on sales: a price rise usually reduces sales because fewer consumers are willing or able to buy; a price cut usually increases sales by making the product affordable to more customers. How much sales change depends on price elasticity (Topic 5).

Example: in 2024 the price of coffee beans reached a record high because droughts in Brazil and Vietnam reduced supply while rising incomes in Asia increased demand: both shifts pushed the price up.

Exam tip: In a “draw a diagram” question, show the original and new curves, label both equilibria (P₁, Q₁ and P₂, Q₂) and draw arrows showing the direction of the shift.

Activity: what happens to price?

Does each change cause the equilibrium price to rise or fall?

Key terms

Price mechanism
The system of using the market forces of demand and supply to allocate resources.
Market equilibrium
Where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, with no shortage or surplus.
Equilibrium price
The price at which demand and supply are equal; the market-clearing price.
Market disequilibrium
Where quantity demanded is not equal to quantity supplied.
Shortage (excess demand)
When quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied because the price is below equilibrium.
Surplus (excess supply)
When quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded because the price is above equilibrium.

Paper 2-style question (c)

Analyse how a decrease in the supply of a product affects its market. [6]

Show mark guidance
  • Identify a cause of the decrease in supply, e.g. higher costs or bad weather (1).
  • Supply curve shifts to the left (1).
  • At the original price there is a shortage / excess demand (1).
  • Price rises (1).
  • Demand contracts / quantity demanded falls (1).
  • New equilibrium at a higher price and lower quantity (1).
