2. Production possibility curves

Success criterion 1

What a PPC shows

A production possibility curve (PPC) shows the maximum combinations of two goods or services that an economy can produce in a period of time when all its resources are used fully and efficiently. It shows the productive capacity of the economy.

The curve slopes downwards because resources are scarce: to produce more of one good, the economy must produce less of the other. It is usually drawn bowed outwards (concave to the origin) because resources are not equally suited to producing both goods, so the opportunity cost rises as more of one good is produced.

Exam tip: Label both axes with the two goods (and units if given), label the curve “PPC” and mark the origin O. Diagrams in Paper 2 earn marks for accurate labels.

Success criterion 2

Points inside, on and beyond the PPC

PositionSignificance
On the curve (A, B, C, D)All resources are fully employed and used efficiently. More of one good means less of the other.
Inside the curve (F)Some factors of production are unemployed or used inefficiently (spare capacity). Output of both goods could rise with no opportunity cost.
Beyond the curve (E)Unattainable with current resources and technology. It could only be reached after economic growth shifts the PPC outwards.

For an economy to be on its PPC, two conditions must hold: all resources are used (no unemployment of factors of production) and resources are used efficiently (each factor is put to its best use).

Success criterion 3

Movements along a PPC and opportunity cost

A movement along a PPC means resources are reallocated from one good to the other. Moving from X to Y increases consumer goods from A to B, but the opportunity cost is the fall in producer goods from C to D.

This choice matters for the future. Producer (capital) goods such as machinery increase future productive capacity, so moving resources towards consumer goods raises living standards now but may slow future economic growth.

Activity: movement or shift?

Does each change cause a movement along the PPC or a shift of the PPC?

  • Moving workers from farming to making clothes
  • Discovering a large new oil field
  • A government switching spending from roads to hospitals
  • A war destroying factories
  • Improved technology raising productivity
  • A firm making more bread and fewer cakes with the same resources

Success criterion 4

Shifts of the PPC and economic growth

An outward shift of the PPC shows economic growth: an increase in productive capacity, so the economy can produce more of both goods.

Cause of an outward shiftExamples
Increase in the quantity of factors of productionDiscovery of new resources, land reclamation, net immigration of labour, more capital goods through investment.
Increase in the quality of factors of productionEducation and training improve labour skills; better healthcare; technological advances and improved production techniques raise productivity.

An inward shift shows a fall in productive capacity, for example after a major natural disaster, a pandemic, or a war that destroys farmland, factories and infrastructure. Flooding in low-lying Bangladesh, for example, destroys crops, homes, roads and railways.

Consequences of an outward shift: more output and income, higher living standards and potentially lower unemployment. However, growth is not always good: it can cause pollution, use up non-renewable resources and widen inequality (see Topic 4).

Key terms

Production possibility curve (PPC)
A curve showing the maximum combinations of two goods or services an economy can produce when all resources are used fully and efficiently.
Productive capacity
The maximum output an economy can produce with its resources.
Spare capacity
When some resources are unemployed or used inefficiently, shown by a point inside the PPC.
Unattainable point
A combination beyond the PPC that cannot be produced with current resources.
Movement along a PPC
A reallocation of resources from one good to another, which has an opportunity cost.
Shift of a PPC
A change in productive capacity: outward shows economic growth, inward a fall in capacity.
Producer (capital) goods
Goods used to produce other goods and services, such as machinery.
Consumer goods
Goods bought by households for their own use.

Draw a PPC diagram to show the effect of a major earthquake that destroys factories and roads. [4]

  • Direction of the shift shown with an arrow or labels PPC₁ and PPC₂ (1).
Paper 2-style question (c)

Analyse how an economy can move from a point inside its PPC to a point on its PPC. [6]

Show mark guidance
  • A point inside the PPC means some resources are unemployed or used inefficiently (1–2).
  • Reducing unemployment, e.g. by increasing total demand or training workers so they can fill vacancies (1–2).
  • Using resources more efficiently, e.g. allocating factors to their best use (1).
  • This increases output of one or both goods (1) without any opportunity cost (1).
