10. The minimum wage

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.2 Business Economics · 1.2.6 Government intervention · Lesson 10 of 10

Learning Intention

To evaluate the effects of a minimum wage on workers, firms and the economy.

Syllabus Statement

Reasons for a minimum wage; its advantages and disadvantages; labour market diagrams showing the introduction and increase of a minimum wage; effects on wages and employment.

Success criterion 1

Why have a minimum wage?

A minimum wage is the lowest hourly wage that employers can legally pay workers. Thailand, the UK and many other countries set one, often with different rates by age or region.

  • To reduce poverty among low-paid workers and raise their living standards.
  • To protect workers from exploitation by powerful employers.
  • To give people an incentive to work rather than rely on benefits.
  • To reduce income inequality.
Success criterion 2

Minimum wage diagrams

To have any effect, the minimum wage must be set above the equilibrium wage.

  • At the minimum wage, the supply of labour extends (more people want jobs) to Qs.
  • The demand for labour contracts (firms hire fewer workers) to Qd.
  • The gap Qs − Qd is an excess supply of labour — unemployment.
  • Increasing the minimum wage further widens this gap.

Exam tip: draw the minimum wage as a horizontal line above equilibrium, and label Qd and Qs clearly.

Success criterion 3

Assessing the minimum wage

GroupPossible benefitsPossible costs
WorkersHigher pay for the lowest paid; less poverty; more motivationSome may lose jobs or have hours cut
FirmsMore motivated staff and lower labour turnoverHigher wage costs; lower profits; may raise prices or automate
The economyHigher spending by low-paid workers; less spent on benefitsPossible unemployment; cost-push inflation; may reduce competitiveness of exports

The size of the effect depends on how far above equilibrium the minimum wage is set and on the state of the economy: in a booming economy firms may absorb the cost; in a recession job losses are more likely.

Activity: benefit or cost?

Decide whether each is a likely benefit or cost of raising the minimum wage.

  • Low-paid workers have more to spend
  • Small firms cut staff hours
  • Fewer workers need state benefits
  • Restaurants raise menu prices
  • Staff turnover falls
  • Firms replace cashiers with self-service tills

Key terms

Minimum wage
The lowest wage per hour that employers can legally pay.
Excess supply of labour
When more people want to work at a wage than firms want to employ: unemployment.
Exploitation
Paying workers unfairly low wages or treating them unfairly.
Cost-push inflation
Rising prices caused by higher costs of production, such as wages.
Automation
Replacing workers with machines or technology.
Exam-style question

Using a labour market diagram, explain the likely effect on employment of a large increase in the minimum wage. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: labelled diagram (Wage rate, Quantity of labour) with DL, SL and equilibrium.
  • 1 mark: minimum wage shown above the equilibrium wage.
  • 1 mark: Qd and Qs identified, showing excess supply of labour.
  • 1 mark: explanation: higher wage costs mean firms demand fewer workers, so employment falls and unemployment rises.
