6. Competition and the size of firms

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.2 Business Economics · 1.2.4 Business competition · Lesson 6 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand the effects of competition and why some firms grow large while others stay small.

Syllabus Statement

Advantages and disadvantages of competition for firms, efficiency, consumer choice, quality, innovation and price; advantages and disadvantages of large and small firms; factors influencing the growth of firms and reasons firms remain small.

Success criterion 1

The effects of competition

A competitive market has many firms trying to win the same customers.

AreaEffect of more competition
EfficiencyFirms must keep costs low to survive, so resources are used efficiently.
Consumer choiceMany firms offer a wider range of products.
QualityFirms improve quality and service to attract customers.
InnovationFirms develop new products and processes to stay ahead.
PriceCompetition pushes prices down towards costs.

For firms, competition is a challenge: profit margins are squeezed and weaker firms may close. But it also pushes firms to become more efficient and innovative, which can help them compete in global markets.

Success criterion 2

Large and small firms

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Large firmsEconomies of scale and lower unit costs; can fund research and innovation; strong brands; can diversify riskDiseconomies of scale; may be slow to respond to change; may abuse market power with high prices
Small firmsFlexible and quick to adapt; personal service; can serve niche markets; owners close to customers and staffHigher average costs; harder to raise finance; vulnerable to large competitors and downturns
Success criterion 3

Why firms grow – and why some stay small

Factors influencing the growth of firms:

  • Economies of scale — lower unit costs make growth attractive.
  • Access to finance — firms that can borrow or sell shares can afford to expand.
  • Spreading risk — growing into new products or markets reduces dependence on one.
  • Taking over competitors — mergers and takeovers increase market share.
  • Government regulation — competition law may block mergers; red tape can hold growth back.

Reasons firms remain small:

  • Size of the market — a small local market (e.g. a village hairdresser) limits sales.
  • Niche market — specialist products (handmade guitars, rare teas) have limited demand.
  • Lack of finance — small firms find it hard to borrow.
  • Aims of the entrepreneur — many owners prefer control and a good work-life balance to growth.

Activity: growth or staying small?

Decide whether each factor is more likely to help a firm grow or keep it small.

  • A bank offers a large low-interest loan
  • The owner wants to keep full control
  • Bulk discounts become available at higher output
  • The product serves a very small niche
  • Buying out a rival firm
  • The firm serves only one small town

Key terms

Competition
Rivalry between firms selling similar products.
Innovation
Developing new products or new ways of producing.
Niche market
A small, specialised segment of a market.
Merger
When two firms agree to join together.
Takeover
When one firm buys control of another.
Exam-style question

Explain two reasons why a small bakery may choose to remain small. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each valid reason (max 2): e.g. small local market; niche product; lack of finance; owner’s aims (control, lifestyle).
  • 1 mark for each explanation linked to a bakery: e.g. a village bakery can only sell to local residents, so expanding would not increase sales; the owner prefers to know all customers personally.
