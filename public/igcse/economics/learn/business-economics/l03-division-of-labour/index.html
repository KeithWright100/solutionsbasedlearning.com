3. Division of labour

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.2 Business Economics · 1.2.2 Productivity and division of labour · Lesson 3 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand specialisation of workers and its advantages and disadvantages for workers and businesses.

Syllabus Statement

The meaning of division of labour; its advantages and disadvantages to workers and to businesses.

Success criterion 1

What is division of labour?

Division of labour is when the production process is broken down into separate tasks, and each worker specialises in one task.

The economist Adam Smith famously described a pin factory where 10 workers, each doing one step, could make about 48,000 pins a day — far more than if each worker made whole pins alone. Today, car assembly lines, fast-food kitchens and hospitals all use division of labour.

Success criterion 2

Effects on workers

Advantages to workersDisadvantages to workers
Workers become highly skilled and quick at their taskWork can become repetitive and boring, reducing motivation
Less training is needed, so it is easier to find a jobNarrow skills make it harder to change jobs if demand falls (low occupational mobility)
Higher productivity can lead to higher wagesWorkers may be replaced by machines that can do simple tasks
Success criterion 3

Effects on businesses

Advantages to businessesDisadvantages to businesses
Higher productivity and outputBored workers may be absent more or leave (higher labour turnover)
Lower cost per unitInterdependence: if one stage stops, the whole line stops
Less time wasted moving between tasksQuality may fall if workers lose interest
Workers can be matched to the tasks they are best atProducts become standardised, with less flexibility

Activity: advantage or disadvantage?

Decide whether each statement is an advantage or a disadvantage of division of labour.

  • Workers become faster at one task
  • Repetitive work lowers motivation
  • Training costs are lower
  • A breakdown at one stage halts production
  • Output per worker rises
  • Workers find it harder to change jobs

Key terms

Division of labour
Breaking production into separate tasks, with each worker specialising in one task.
Specialisation
Concentrating on producing a limited range of goods or tasks.
Labour turnover
The rate at which workers leave a business and need replacing.
Interdependence
When each stage of production depends on the others.
Occupational mobility
The ease with which workers can change jobs.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether division of labour is always beneficial for a car manufacturer. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks for explaining benefits, e.g. higher productivity and lower unit costs as workers become skilled at one task.
  • Up to 2 marks for explaining drawbacks, e.g. boredom raises absence and labour turnover; one breakdown can stop the whole assembly line.
  • Up to 2 marks for a reasoned conclusion, e.g. the benefits outweigh the costs for mass production, but the firm may need job rotation to keep workers motivated.
