1. Production and the sectors of the economy

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.2 Business Economics · 1.2.1 Production · Lesson 1 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand the factors of production and how the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors change as economies develop.

Syllabus Statement

The factors of production (land, labour, capital, enterprise); the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors; changes in the relative importance of the sectors over time in developing and developed economies.

Success criterion 1

The factors of production

Production is the process of turning inputs into goods and services that satisfy wants. It needs four factors of production:

FactorMeaningRewardExample
LandNatural resources: soil, water, minerals, forests, fishRentA palm-oil plantation in Malaysia
LabourHuman effort, physical and mentalWagesNurses, factory workers, software engineers
CapitalMan-made goods used to produce other goodsInterestTractors, robots, delivery vans, computers
EnterpriseThe skill of organising the other factors and taking risksProfitA founder starting a food-delivery app

Exam tip: capital means physical equipment in economics, not money in a bank account.

Success criterion 2

The three sectors

  • Primary sector — extracting raw materials from the land or sea: farming, fishing, forestry, mining, oil extraction.
  • Secondary sector — manufacturing and construction: turning raw materials into finished goods, e.g. car assembly, steel making, building houses.
  • Tertiary sector — providing services: retail, banking, tourism, education, health care, transport.

Success criterion 3

How sectors change over time

The relative importance of each sector changes as an economy develops (sometimes called sectoral change).

  • Developing economies usually have a large share of employment in the primary sector (often subsistence farming). As they industrialise, workers move into manufacturing (secondary), attracted by higher wages, often in cities.
  • Developed economies have most employment and output in the tertiary sector. Manufacturing declines (deindustrialisation) as it moves to lower-cost countries and as machines replace workers, while rising incomes increase demand for services such as tourism, finance and health care.
  • Primary output may still rise even as employment falls, because farming becomes more mechanised and productive.

Key terms

Production
Turning inputs (factors of production) into goods and services.
Primary sector
Extraction of raw materials, e.g. farming, fishing, mining.
Secondary sector
Manufacturing and construction.
Tertiary sector
The provision of services.
Deindustrialisation
The decline of manufacturing in an economy.
Exam-style question

Explain why the proportion of workers in the tertiary sector usually increases as an economy develops. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: rising incomes increase demand for services such as tourism, health care and financial services (high income elasticity).
  • 1 mark: mechanisation of farming and manufacturing means fewer workers are needed in the primary and secondary sectors.
  • 1 mark: manufacturing may relocate to lower-cost countries, so workers move into service jobs.
