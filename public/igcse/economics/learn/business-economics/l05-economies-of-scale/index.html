5. Economies and diseconomies of scale

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.2 Business Economics · 1.2.3 Costs, revenue and profit · Lesson 5 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand why average costs change as a business grows, using the LRAC curve.

Syllabus Statement

Internal economies of scale (purchasing, marketing, technical, financial, managerial, risk-bearing); external economies of scale; diseconomies of scale (bureaucracy, communication, control, distance between management and workers); the long-run average cost curve.

Success criterion 1

Internal economies of scale

Economies of scale are the cost advantages a business gains as it grows: average cost falls as output increases. Internal economies come from the growth of the firm itself.

TypeExplanationExample
Purchasing (bulk buying)Large firms buy inputs in bulk and negotiate discounts from suppliers.A supermarket chain pays less per kilo of rice than a corner shop.
MarketingAdvertising costs are spread over a larger output; national campaigns become affordable.One TV advert promotes products sold in 2,000 stores.
TechnicalLarge firms can afford specialist, high-capacity machinery and production lines.A car plant using robots to weld 1,000 cars a day.
FinancialLarge firms can borrow more cheaply because banks see them as less risky; they can also sell shares.A multinational borrowing at a lower interest rate than a small café.
ManagerialLarge firms employ specialist managers (finance, marketing, HR), improving efficiency.Hiring a qualified finance director.
Risk-bearingLarge firms can diversify into different products and markets, so a fall in one does not threaten the firm.A company selling phones, TVs and home appliances in many countries.
Success criterion 2

External economies of scale

External economies of scale are cost savings that come from the growth of the industry or area in which a firm is located, not from the firm itself.

  • Skilled labour — a pool of trained workers develops locally, reducing training costs (e.g. software engineers in Bangalore).
  • Improved infrastructure — governments build better roads, ports and broadband where an industry is concentrated.
  • Access to suppliers — specialist suppliers locate nearby, cutting transport costs and delivery times.
  • Similar businesses nearby — firms share ideas, research centres and services (e.g. the tech cluster in Silicon Valley or the car industry around Bangkok’s Eastern Seaboard).
Success criterion 3

Diseconomies of scale and the LRAC curve

Diseconomies of scale occur when a firm grows too large and average cost starts to rise.

  • Bureaucracy — more layers of management, paperwork and rules slow decisions.
  • Communication problems — messages pass through many people and get delayed or distorted.
  • Lack of control — it is harder to monitor what every department and worker is doing.
  • Distance between top management and workers — workers feel unvalued, motivation falls and absence rises.

Activity: economy or diseconomy?

Decide whether each statement describes an economy or a diseconomy of scale.

  • A bulk discount on raw materials
  • Decisions take weeks because of layers of management
  • Borrowing at a lower interest rate
  • Workers never see senior managers and feel ignored
  • Employing a specialist marketing team
  • Instructions are misunderstood between sites

Key terms

Economies of scale
Falling average costs as a firm increases its scale of output.
Internal economies of scale
Cost savings from the growth of the firm itself.
External economies of scale
Cost savings from the growth of the industry or area.
Diseconomies of scale
Rising average costs when a firm grows too large.
LRAC curve
A curve showing how average cost changes as the scale of production changes.
Exam-style question

Using an LRAC diagram, explain how a supermarket chain may benefit from economies of scale as it opens more stores. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: correctly labelled LRAC diagram (Average cost, Output) with a U-shape.
  • 1 mark: show average cost falling as output rises (economies of scale).
  • 1 mark: identify a relevant economy, e.g. purchasing/bulk buying.
  • 1 mark: explain how it lowers average cost, e.g. suppliers give discounts on large orders, so cost per unit falls.
