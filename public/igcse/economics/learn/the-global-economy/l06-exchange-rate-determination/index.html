6. How exchange rates are determined

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.2 The Global Economy · 2.2.3 Exchange rates · Lesson 6 of 7

Learning Intention

To understand how the demand for and supply of a currency determine its exchange rate.

Syllabus Statement

Definition of an exchange rate; how interest rates, speculation, and imports and exports of goods and services affect the demand for and supply of a currency; supply and demand diagrams showing the determination of, and changes in, exchange rates.

Success criterion 1

What is an exchange rate?

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another, e.g. $1 = 33 Thai baht, or £1 = $1.30. In a floating exchange rate system, the rate is set by the demand for and supply of the currency on the foreign exchange market.

Converting: if $1 = 33 baht, a 6,600 baht hotel room costs an American tourist 6,600 ÷ 33 = $200.

Success criterion 2

Demand for and supply of a currency

Demand for baht comes from people who need baht to buy Thai things; supply of baht comes from Thais selling baht to buy foreign currency.

FactorEffect on the baht
Exports of goods and servicesForeign buyers of Thai rice, cars and tourism need baht → demand for baht rises.
ImportsThai firms buying foreign goods sell baht to buy dollars → supply of baht rises.
Interest ratesHigher Thai interest rates attract foreign savers (‘hot money’), who buy baht → demand rises.
SpeculationIf speculators expect the baht to rise, they buy it now → demand rises (expectations can be self-fulfilling).
Foreign investmentFDI into Thailand requires baht → demand rises.
Success criterion 3

Exchange rate diagrams

The equilibrium exchange rate is where demand for the currency equals supply.

ChangeExchange rate
Demand for currency risesRises (appreciation)
Demand for currency fallsFalls (depreciation)
Supply of currency risesFalls (depreciation)
Supply of currency fallsRises (appreciation)

Activity: baht up or down?

Decide whether each event is likely to make the baht appreciate or depreciate.

  • A record number of foreign tourists visit Thailand
  • Thai consumers buy many more imported goods
  • The Bank of Thailand raises interest rates
  • Speculators expect the baht to fall
  • A Japanese firm builds a large factory in Thailand
  • Thai interest rates fall well below US rates

Key terms

Exchange rate
The price of one currency in terms of another.
Floating exchange rate
An exchange rate determined by market demand and supply.
Foreign exchange market
The market where currencies are bought and sold.
Speculation
Buying or selling currency to profit from expected changes in its value.
Hot money
Money moved between countries to earn higher interest rates.
Exam-style question

Using a supply and demand diagram, explain the likely effect on the value of a country’s currency of a rise in its interest rates. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: correctly labelled diagram (price of currency, quantity of currency) with D and S.
  • 1 mark: demand shifted right (D to D1).
  • 1 mark: new higher exchange rate shown (appreciation).
  • 1 mark: explanation: higher interest rates attract foreign savers, who must buy the currency to deposit money.
