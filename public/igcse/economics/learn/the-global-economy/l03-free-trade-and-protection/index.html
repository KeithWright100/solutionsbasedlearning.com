3. Free trade and protection

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.2 The Global Economy · 2.2.2 International trade · Lesson 3 of 7

Learning Intention

To understand the benefits and costs of free trade and why governments use protection.

Syllabus Statement

Benefits of free trade (lower prices and more choice for consumers, lower input costs and wider markets for businesses) and its costs (foreign competition harming domestic firms, unemployment); reasons for protection (dumping, employment, infant industries, tariff revenue, protecting consumers, reducing current account deficits); retaliation.

Success criterion 1

The benefits of free trade

Free trade means trade between countries without barriers such as tariffs and quotas.

  • Consumers — lower prices (cheaper imports and more competition) and more choice.
  • Businesses — lower input costs (cheaper imported raw materials and components) and access to wider markets, allowing economies of scale.
  • Countries can specialise in what they produce most efficiently.
Success criterion 2

The costs of free trade

  • Foreign competition can harm domestic businesses that cannot match lower prices, leading to closures.
  • This can cause unemployment, especially structural unemployment in declining industries.
  • Countries may become dependent on imports of vital goods such as food or medicines.
Success criterion 3

Why governments protect industries

Protectionism is the use of barriers to restrict imports and protect domestic producers.

ReasonExplanation
Prevent dumpingDumping is selling goods abroad below cost price; it can destroy domestic firms.
Protect employmentSaving jobs in industries threatened by imports.
Infant industriesNew industries need time to grow and gain economies of scale before facing global competition.
Tariff revenueTariffs raise money for the government, important for some developing countries.
Protect consumersBanning unsafe or low-quality imports (e.g. contaminated food, unsafe toys).
Reduce a current account deficitFewer imports improve the balance of exports minus imports.

Retaliation: if one country imposes tariffs, its trading partners may respond with tariffs of their own (a trade war), harming exporters in both countries and reducing world trade.

Activity: argument for free trade or for protection?

Decide whether each argument supports free trade or protection.

  • Consumers pay lower prices for imported goods
  • A new solar panel industry needs time to grow
  • Firms can buy cheaper imported components
  • A foreign firm is selling steel below cost
  • Exporters gain access to bigger markets
  • Jobs in local textile factories are at risk

Key terms

Free trade
International trade without barriers such as tariffs or quotas.
Protectionism
Using barriers to restrict imports and protect domestic industries.
Dumping
Selling goods abroad at below the cost of production.
Infant industry
A new industry that is not yet able to compete internationally.
Retaliation
When a country responds to trade barriers by imposing its own.
Exam-style question

Explain two reasons why a government may protect a domestic industry. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for each valid reason (max 2): e.g. infant industry; prevent dumping; protect jobs; raise tariff revenue; protect consumers; reduce current account deficit.
  • 1 mark for each explanation, e.g. a new industry needs time to gain economies of scale before it can compete with established foreign firms.
