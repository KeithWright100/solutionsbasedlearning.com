2. Multinational corporations and FDI

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.2 The Global Economy · 2.2.1 Globalisation · Lesson 2 of 7

Learning Intention

To understand why MNCs locate overseas and evaluate their impact on host economies.

Syllabus Statement

Definitions of MNCs and foreign direct investment (FDI); reasons for locating overseas (economies of scale, natural resources and cheaper materials, lower transport and communication costs, access to customers); impacts on host economies (jobs, infrastructure, skills, capital, tax revenue, tax avoidance, environmental damage, repatriation of profits).

Success criterion 1

Why do MNCs locate overseas?

A multinational corporation (MNC) is a business with its headquarters in one country and operations (production or services) in several others. Foreign direct investment (FDI) is investment by a firm in one country in productive assets in another, e.g. building a factory or buying a foreign business.

  • Economies of scale — producing for a global market lowers average costs.
  • Natural resources and cheaper materials — locating near oil, minerals or cheap labour and land lowers costs.
  • Lower transport and communication costs — producing close to markets cuts delivery costs and avoids tariffs.
  • Access to customers — being inside large or fast-growing markets (e.g. ASEAN, India) increases sales.

Example: Japanese car makers such as Toyota and Honda have large factories in Thailand, serving both Thai buyers and export markets across Asia and Australia.

Success criterion 2

Benefits to the host economy

  • Jobs — direct jobs in factories and offices, and indirect jobs for local suppliers.
  • Infrastructure — MNCs (and governments trying to attract them) invest in roads, ports and power.
  • Skills — workers are trained in new techniques and management.
  • Capital — FDI brings machinery and technology the country could not afford alone.
  • Tax revenue — MNCs pay corporation tax and their workers pay income tax.
Success criterion 3

Costs to the host economy

  • Tax avoidance — MNCs may use transfer pricing to declare profits in low-tax countries.
  • Environmental damage — pollution, deforestation and waste, especially where regulation is weak.
  • Profits sent abroad (repatriation) — profits flow back to the home country rather than being reinvested locally.
  • Local firms may be out-competed; MNCs may leave suddenly if costs rise elsewhere; many senior jobs go to foreign staff.

Activity: benefit or cost for the host country?

Decide whether each is a benefit or a cost of an MNC for the host economy.

  • Local workers are trained in new technology
  • Profits are sent back to the parent company abroad
  • A new port is built near the factory
  • Waste chemicals pollute a local river
  • Local firms win contracts to supply parts
  • Profits are declared in a tax haven

Key terms

Multinational corporation (MNC)
A business that operates in more than one country.
Foreign direct investment (FDI)
Investment by a firm in productive assets in another country.
Host country
The country in which an MNC locates its operations.
Repatriation of profits
Sending profits back to the MNC’s home country.
Tax avoidance
Legally reducing tax paid, e.g. by declaring profits in low-tax countries.
Exam-style question

Assess the impact of FDI by MNCs on a developing economy. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks: benefits, e.g. jobs, skills, capital and infrastructure, tax revenue.
  • Up to 2 marks: costs, e.g. profits repatriated, environmental damage, tax avoidance, competition for local firms.
  • Up to 2 marks: reasoned judgement, e.g. depends on government regulation and how far MNCs use local suppliers and workers.
