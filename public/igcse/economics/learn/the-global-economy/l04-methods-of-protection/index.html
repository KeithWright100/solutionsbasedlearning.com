4. Methods of protection: tariffs, quotas and subsidies

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.2 The Global Economy · 2.2.2 International trade · Lesson 4 of 7

Learning Intention

To use diagrams to analyse how tariffs, quotas and subsidies restrict trade, and evaluate each method.

Syllabus Statement

Tariffs, quotas and subsidies as methods of protection; their advantages and disadvantages; supply and demand diagrams showing the impact of a tariff, a quota and a subsidy.

Success criterion 1

Tariffs

A tariff is a tax on imported goods. It raises the price of imports, making domestic goods relatively cheaper.

  • Domestic producers sell more (Q1 → Q3), protecting jobs.
  • Consumers pay a higher price and buy less (Q2 → Q4).
  • Imports fall, and the government gains tariff revenue (tariff × remaining imports).
Success criterion 2

Quotas

A quota is a physical limit on the quantity (or value) of a good that can be imported in a period, e.g. 100,000 cars a year.

Unlike a tariff, a quota raises no revenue for the government; foreign firms that hold import licences may gain from the higher price.

Success criterion 3

Subsidies and evaluation

A subsidy to domestic producers lowers their costs so they can compete with imports without raising the price to consumers.

MethodAdvantagesDisadvantages
TariffRaises revenue; protects jobs; reduces importsHigher prices for consumers; less choice; may cause retaliation; inefficient firms protected
QuotaCertain limit on importsNo government revenue; higher prices; foreign firms may profit; retaliation
SubsidyConsumer prices stay low; protects domestic output and jobsCosts taxpayers money (opportunity cost); may protect inefficient firms; may break WTO rules

Activity: tariff, quota or subsidy?

Identify the method of protection.

  • A 25% tax on imported steel
  • Only 50,000 tonnes of foreign rice may enter each year
  • Government pays local farmers $50 per tonne produced
  • An extra import duty on foreign cars
  • A limit on the number of imported TVs
  • Grants to domestic solar panel makers

Key terms

Tariff
A tax on imports.
Quota
A physical limit on the quantity of imports.
Subsidy
A payment from government to producers to lower their costs.
World price
The price of a good on international markets.
Tariff revenue
Money raised by the government from taxing imports.
Exam-style question

With the help of a diagram, explain the effect of a tariff on domestic producers and consumers. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: correctly labelled axes, domestic D and S.
  • 1 mark: world price line and world price + tariff line.
  • 1 mark: quantities showing domestic supply and demand before and after.
  • 1 mark: domestic producers supply more at the higher price.
  • 1 mark: consumers pay a higher price and buy less.
  • 1 mark: imports fall / government gains tariff revenue.
