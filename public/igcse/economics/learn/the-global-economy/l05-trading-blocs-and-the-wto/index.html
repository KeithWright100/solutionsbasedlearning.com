5. Trading blocs, the WTO and trade patterns

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.2 The Global Economy · 2.2.2 International trade · Lesson 5 of 7

Learning Intention

To understand trading blocs, the role of the WTO and how trade patterns differ between countries.

Syllabus Statement

Definition and examples of trading blocs; their impact on members and non-members; the role of the World Trade Organization and actions it takes; trade patterns of developed and developing countries.

Success criterion 1

Trading blocs

A trading bloc is a group of countries that agree to reduce or remove trade barriers between themselves.

BlocKey features
ASEAN (Association of Southeast Asian Nations)11 members including Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia and Singapore; free trade area (AFTA)
European Union (EU)27 members; single market with free movement of goods, services, capital and people; common external tariff
USMCAUSA, Mexico and Canada free trade agreement
MercosurSouth American bloc including Brazil and Argentina
BenefitsDrawbacks

Success criterion 2

The World Trade Organization (WTO)

The WTO is an international organisation with over 160 member countries that promotes free trade and sets the rules for international trade.

  • Negotiates agreements to reduce tariffs, quotas and subsidies.
  • Settles disputes between members, e.g. accusations of dumping or unfair subsidies.
  • Can authorise retaliation (e.g. allowing a country to impose tariffs) when a member breaks the rules.
  • Monitors members’ trade policies and supports developing countries with technical help.

Trade patterns

Main exportsManufactured goods, high-tech products, financial and business servicesPrimary products (crops, minerals, oil) and low-cost manufactured goods
Main importsRaw materials, energy, low-cost consumer goodsCapital goods, machinery, high-tech goods
Trading partnersMostly other developed countries and large emerging economiesOften developed countries and, increasingly, China and other emerging economies
RisksCompetition from lower-cost producersDependence on a few commodities whose prices are volatile

Many emerging economies, such as Vietnam and Thailand, have shifted from primary exports towards manufactured goods, moving up the value chain.

Key terms

Trading bloc
A group of countries that agree to reduce or remove trade barriers between them.
Free trade area
A bloc in which members remove tariffs on trade with each other.
Common external tariff
The same tariff charged by all bloc members on imports from outside.
World Trade Organization (WTO)
The international body that promotes free trade and settles trade disputes.
Trade pattern
The types of goods a country exports and imports, and with whom it trades.
Exam-style question

Explain one benefit and one drawback for a country of joining a trading bloc. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: benefit identified, e.g. tariff-free access to a larger market.
  • 1 mark: explained, e.g. exporters sell more and gain economies of scale.
  • 1 mark: drawback identified, e.g. more competition / loss of control over trade policy.
  • 1 mark: explained, e.g. inefficient domestic firms may close, causing job losses.
