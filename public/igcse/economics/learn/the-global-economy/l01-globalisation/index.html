1. Globalisation

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.2 The Global Economy · 2.2.1 Globalisation · Lesson 1 of 7

Learning Intention

To understand the causes and impacts of globalisation.

Syllabus Statement

Globalisation as the increased integration and interdependence of economies; causes (fewer tariffs and quotas, reduced transport and communication costs, the increased significance of MNCs); impacts (living standards, consumer choice, prices, communication costs, closure of traditional industries, the environment) on countries, governments, producers, consumers, workers and the environment.

Success criterion 1

What is globalisation?

Globalisation is the increased integration and interdependence of the world’s economies: more trade in goods and services, more movement of capital, people and ideas across borders. A smartphone might be designed in the USA, use minerals from Africa, components from Japan and Korea, and be assembled in China or Vietnam.

Causes of globalisation:

  • Fewer tariffs and quotas — trade agreements and the WTO have reduced barriers to trade.
  • Reduced transport costs — container ships, larger aircraft and better ports make shipping goods cheap.
  • Reduced communication costs — the internet, mobile phones and video calls let firms manage operations worldwide.
  • Increased significance of multinational corporations (MNCs) — firms such as Toyota, Samsung and Unilever produce and sell in many countries.

Success criterion 3

Who wins and who loses?

  • Countries — developing countries can grow through exports and FDI, but may become dependent on MNCs or a few exports.
  • Governments — gain tax revenue and growth, but find it harder to tax mobile MNCs and to control their own economies.
  • Producers — access to bigger markets and cheaper inputs, but face more foreign competition.
  • Consumers — lower prices and more choice.
  • Workers — new jobs in exporting industries, but job losses in uncompetitive industries and possible poor conditions in low-wage factories.
  • The environment — more emissions from transport and production.

Activity: cause or impact?

Decide whether each is a cause or an impact of globalisation.

  • Container shipping cuts the cost of moving goods
  • A shoe factory in Europe closes due to cheap imports
  • Countries agree to remove tariffs
  • Consumers can buy fruit from around the world all year
  • Video calls make managing overseas offices easy
  • Higher carbon emissions from air freight

Key terms

Globalisation
The increased integration and interdependence of the world’s economies.
Interdependence
When countries rely on each other for goods, services and investment.
Multinational corporation (MNC)
A business that operates in more than one country.
Tariff
A tax on imports.
Quota
A physical limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported.
Exam-style question

Discuss the impact of globalisation on workers in a developed economy. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks: positive impacts, e.g. jobs in exporting and high-skilled service industries; lower prices raise real wages.
  • Up to 2 marks: negative impacts, e.g. closure of traditional manufacturing industries; structural unemployment; downward pressure on wages.
  • Up to 2 marks: reasoned judgement, e.g. depends on workers’ skills and on government retraining policy.
