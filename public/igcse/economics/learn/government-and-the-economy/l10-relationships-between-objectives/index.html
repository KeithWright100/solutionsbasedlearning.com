10. Conflicts between objectives and policies

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.1 Government and the Economy · 2.1.3 Relationships between objectives · Lesson 10 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand trade-offs between macroeconomic objectives and assess which a government should prioritise.

Syllabus Statement

The trade-offs between unemployment and inflation, economic growth and inflation, and economic growth and the environment; the relationship between inflation and the current account; how policies aimed at one objective may conflict with another.

Success criterion 1

Unemployment, growth and inflation

  • Unemployment vs inflation — policies that reduce unemployment (tax cuts, lower interest rates, more government spending) increase total demand. As spare labour is used up, firms compete for workers and wages rise, causing demand-pull and cost-push inflation. Policies to reduce inflation tend to increase unemployment.
  • Growth vs inflation — rapid growth driven by demand can exceed the economy’s capacity, so prices rise. A boom often brings higher inflation.
Success criterion 2

Growth, the environment and the current account

  • Growth vs the environment — more production and consumption can mean more pollution, congestion, deforestation and use of finite resources. Policies to protect the environment (taxes, regulation) may raise costs and slow growth.
  • Inflation vs the current account — if a country’s inflation is higher than its trading partners’, its exports become less competitive and imports relatively cheaper, worsening the current account. Higher incomes during growth also suck in imports.
PolicyPossible conflict
Cutting interest rates to reduce unemploymentInflation rises; currency falls
Raising interest rates to cut inflationUnemployment rises; growth slows; stronger currency hurts exports
Expansionary fiscal policy to boost growthInflation, larger deficit, more imports
Environmental taxesHigher costs, slower growth, possible job losses
Success criterion 3

Which objective should come first?

The right priority depends on the situation:

  • In a recession with high unemployment and low inflation, governments usually prioritise growth and jobs.
  • When inflation is high (e.g. well above target), controlling it usually comes first, because it harms living standards and competitiveness.
  • Where pollution threatens health, environmental protection may be prioritised, often using supply-side measures that allow greener growth.

Supply-side policies can reduce conflicts, because they increase capacity, allowing growth and lower unemployment without inflation.

Activity: which objective may suffer?

Decide which objective is most likely to be harmed by each policy.

  • Cutting interest rates during a boom
  • Raising interest rates sharply
  • Relaxing pollution rules to attract factories
  • Large tax cuts when unemployment is already low
  • Big cuts in government spending
  • Subsidising coal power to lower energy prices

Key terms

Trade-off
When achieving one objective means sacrificing some of another.
Conflict of objectives
When a policy that helps one objective harms another.
Demand-pull inflation
Inflation caused by demand growing faster than capacity.
Sustainable growth
Growth that does not harm future generations’ ability to meet their needs.
Spare capacity
Unused resources in the economy.
Exam-style question

Assess whether a government should prioritise reducing unemployment or reducing inflation. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks: explain the trade-off: policies to cut unemployment raise demand and may increase inflation.
  • Up to 2 marks: arguments for each priority, e.g. unemployment causes poverty and wasted resources; high inflation erodes living standards and competitiveness.
  • Up to 2 marks: reasoned judgement based on the situation, e.g. prioritise jobs in a recession with low inflation; prioritise inflation if it is well above target.
