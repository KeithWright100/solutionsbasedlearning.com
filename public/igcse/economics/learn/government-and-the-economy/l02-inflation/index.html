2. Low and stable inflation

Success criterion 1

Measuring inflation

Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level over time. Deflation is a sustained fall in the general price level. Most governments target low and stable inflation, often around 2%.

Inflation is measured using the Consumer Price Index (CPI):

  1. A survey of households finds what a typical family buys.
  2. A basket of several hundred goods and services is chosen.
  3. Each item is given a weight according to the share of income spent on it.
  4. Prices are collected every month from shops and websites.
  5. The index is compared with a base year (= 100).

Inflation rate (%) = (CPI this year − CPI last year) ÷ CPI last year × 100

Example: CPI rises from 120 to 126 → inflation = 6 ÷ 120 × 100 = 5%.

Success criterion 2

Causes of inflation

  • Demand-pull inflation — total demand in the economy grows faster than the economy can produce: “too much money chasing too few goods”. Causes include rising consumer spending, low interest rates, tax cuts, higher government spending and booming exports.
  • Cost-push inflation — rising costs of production push up prices: higher wages, higher oil and energy prices, more expensive imported raw materials (e.g. after a fall in the exchange rate) or higher indirect taxes.

Inflation and interest rates: central banks usually raise interest rates to reduce inflation, because borrowing becomes more expensive and saving more attractive, so spending falls.

Activity: demand-pull or cost-push?

Decide which type of inflation each event is most likely to cause.

  • World oil prices double
  • A large cut in income tax boosts spending
  • Workers win a 10% pay rise across industry
  • Interest rates fall to record lows
  • The currency falls, making imported materials dearer
  • A surge in demand for exports

Success criterion 3

The impact of inflation

AreaEffect of high inflation
PricesThe cost of living rises; people on fixed incomes lose purchasing power.
WagesWorkers demand higher wages to keep up, which can cause a wage-price spiral.
ExportsDomestic goods become less price competitive abroad, so exports fall.
UnemploymentFalling exports and lower confidence may lead to job losses.
Menu costsFirms must spend time and money changing price lists, menus and websites.
Shoe-leather costsPeople and firms spend time moving money to find the best interest rates.
Uncertainty and confidenceUnpredictable prices make planning hard, reducing business and consumer confidence.
InvestmentUncertainty discourages firms from investing in new capital.

Deflation can also be harmful: consumers delay purchases expecting lower prices, so demand, output and jobs fall.

Key terms

Inflation
A sustained increase in the general price level.
Deflation
A sustained fall in the general price level.
Consumer Price Index (CPI)
A weighted index of the prices of a basket of goods and services bought by typical households.
Demand-pull inflation
Inflation caused by total demand growing faster than the economy’s capacity.
Cost-push inflation
Inflation caused by rising costs of production.
Menu costs
The costs to firms of changing prices.
Exam-style question

Explain how a rise in world oil prices may cause inflation in a country. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: oil is a key input / raises firms’ costs of production (energy, transport).
  • 1 mark: firms pass higher costs on to consumers as higher prices.
  • 1 mark: this is cost-push inflation; the general price level rises (possibly a wage-price spiral as workers demand higher wages).
