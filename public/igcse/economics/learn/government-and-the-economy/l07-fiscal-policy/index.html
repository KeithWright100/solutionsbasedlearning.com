7. Fiscal policy

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.1 Government and the Economy · 2.1.2 Government policies · Lesson 7 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand how governments use taxation and spending to influence the economy.

Syllabus Statement

Definition of fiscal policy; sources of government revenue (direct and indirect taxes); main areas of government expenditure; fiscal deficit and surplus and their impact; the effect of fiscal policy on growth, inflation, unemployment and the current account.

Success criterion 1

Government revenue and spending

Fiscal policy is the use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy.

Revenue sourceMeaningExamples
Direct taxesTaxes on income and wealth, paid directly to the governmentIncome tax, corporation tax (on company profits)
Indirect taxesTaxes on spending, collected by sellersVAT, excise duties on fuel, alcohol and tobacco, import tariffs

Main areas of government expenditure: health care, education, social protection (pensions and benefits), defence, transport and infrastructure, and interest on government debt.

Success criterion 2

Deficits and surpluses

  • A fiscal (budget) deficit is when government spending is greater than tax revenue in a year. The gap is financed by borrowing, which adds to the national debt.
  • A fiscal (budget) surplus is when tax revenue is greater than government spending.
Possible benefitsPossible problems
Fiscal deficitCan boost demand, growth and jobs, especially in a recessionAdds to national debt; future interest payments have an opportunity cost; may cause inflation
Fiscal surplusCan repay debt and help control inflationHigher taxes or lower spending reduce demand and may slow growth
Success criterion 3

Fiscal policy and the objectives

Expansionary fiscal policy means higher spending and/or lower taxes. Contractionary fiscal policy means lower spending and/or higher taxes.

ObjectiveExpansionary policyContractionary policy
Economic growthRises: more demand and outputSlows
UnemploymentFalls: firms hire more workersRises
InflationMay rise (demand-pull)Falls
Current accountMay worsen: higher incomes increase importsMay improve: lower spending on imports

Activity: expansionary or contractionary?

Decide whether each policy is expansionary or contractionary.

  • Cutting the rate of income tax
  • Raising VAT from 7% to 10%
  • Building new high-speed railways
  • Cutting spending on defence
  • Raising unemployment benefits
  • Increasing corporation tax

Key terms

Fiscal policy
The use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy.
Direct tax
A tax on income or wealth, e.g. income tax.
Indirect tax
A tax on spending, e.g. VAT.
Fiscal deficit
When government spending exceeds tax revenue.
Fiscal surplus
When tax revenue exceeds government spending.
National debt
The total amount the government owes from past borrowing.
Exam-style question

Explain how a cut in income tax may affect unemployment. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: households have higher disposable income.
  • 1 mark: consumer spending increases, raising demand for goods and services.
  • 1 mark: firms increase output and employ more workers, so (cyclical) unemployment falls.
