5. Protecting the environment and government controls

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.1 Government and the Economy · 2.1.1 Macroeconomic objectives · Lesson 5 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand how business activity damages the environment and evaluate the controls governments use.

Syllabus Statement

How business activity damages the environment (visual pollution including litter, noise, air and water pollution); taxation, subsidies, regulation, fines, pollution permits and provision of parks to protect the environment; the advantages and disadvantages of regulation, legislation, fines and pollution permits as government controls.

Success criterion 1

How business damages the environment

TypeExamples
Visual pollution (including litter)Ugly buildings, billboards, quarries, packaging and fast-food litter spoil the landscape.
Noise pollutionAirports, factories, construction sites and road traffic disturb local residents.
Air pollutionFactory chimneys, power stations and vehicles release smoke, particles and greenhouse gases.
Water pollutionChemical waste, fertiliser run-off and sewage enter rivers and seas, harming wildlife and drinking water.
Success criterion 2

Policies to protect the environment

  • Taxation — taxes on polluting activities (fuel duty, carbon taxes, landfill taxes) make firms and consumers pay for external costs.
  • Subsidies — support for clean alternatives such as electric buses, solar power and recycling.
  • Regulation — legal limits on emissions or noise, bans on single-use plastics.
  • Fines — penalties for breaking environmental rules, e.g. illegal dumping.
  • Pollution permits — a cap on total emissions, with permits that firms can trade.
  • Provision of parks — governments create and protect green spaces and national parks for public enjoyment and wildlife.
Success criterion 3

Assessing government controls

ControlAdvantagesDisadvantages
RegulationClear rules; can target the most harmful activities directlyNeeds inspectors to monitor; raises firms’ costs
LegislationLegally binding, with strong penaltiesSlow to introduce and change; may not keep up with new technology
FinesDeters bad behaviour; raises revenueMust be large enough to deter; offenders must be caught
Pollution permitsCaps total pollution; market rewards firms that cut emissionsDifficult to set the right cap; wealthy firms may simply buy permits

The best control depends on the situation: a ban suits very harmful pollution; taxes or permits suit pollution from many sources, where firms can choose the cheapest way to cut emissions.

Activity: type of pollution

Identify the main type of pollution in each case.

  • Smoke from a coal-fired power station
  • A factory pipes chemicals into a river
  • Night flights over a housing estate
  • Billboards along a scenic coast road
  • Litter around a fast-food restaurant
  • Fertiliser run-off reaching the sea

Key terms

Air pollution
Harmful substances released into the air.
Visual pollution
Unattractive sights that spoil the environment, including litter.
Legislation
Laws passed by government.
Carbon tax
A tax on the carbon content of fuels to reduce emissions.
Pollution permit
A tradable licence allowing a set amount of pollution.
Exam-style question

Evaluate the use of pollution permits to reduce air pollution from factories. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks: explain how permits work: a cap on total emissions; firms that cut pollution can sell spare permits.
  • Up to 2 marks: advantages: total pollution is limited; gives an incentive to invest in cleaner technology.
  • Up to 2 marks: disadvantages and judgement, e.g. hard to set the cap; too many permits make them cheap; effective only if monitored.
