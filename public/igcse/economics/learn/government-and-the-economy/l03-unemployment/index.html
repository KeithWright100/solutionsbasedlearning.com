3. Low unemployment

Success criterion 1

Measuring unemployment

Unemployment is when people who are willing and able to work and actively seeking work cannot find a job.

The International Labour Organization (ILO) measure uses a labour force survey. A person is unemployed if they are without a job, have actively looked for work in the past four weeks and are available to start within two weeks. Because it uses the same definition everywhere, it allows international comparisons.

Unemployment rate (%) = number unemployed ÷ labour force × 100

The labour force is everyone employed plus everyone unemployed. Example: 1.5 million unemployed out of a labour force of 30 million = 5%.

Success criterion 2

Types of unemployment

TypeCauseExample
Cyclical (demand-deficient)Caused by a fall in total demand during a downturn or recession.Factory workers laid off as car sales fall in a recession
StructuralCaused by long-term decline of an industry, often due to new technology or foreign competition; workers lack the skills for new jobs.Coal miners after mines close; bank clerks replaced by online banking
SeasonalCaused by regular changes in demand at certain times of year.Ski instructors in summer; beach-resort staff in the rainy season
FrictionalShort-term unemployment while people move between jobs.A graduate searching for a first job
VoluntaryPeople choose not to work at the current wage, e.g. if benefits are close to wages.Someone turning down low-paid jobs while searching for better pay

Activity: which type?

Identify the type of unemployment in each case.

  • A typist replaced by voice-recognition software
  • A Christmas shop assistant in January
  • Builders laid off during a recession
  • A nurse who has resigned and starts a new job next month
  • Shipyard workers after the yard closes due to cheaper Asian rivals
  • Mango pickers outside the harvest season

Success criterion 3

The impact of unemployment

  • Output — the economy produces less than it could (a point inside the PPC).
  • Scarce resources wasted — labour is a scarce resource left unused.
  • Poverty — unemployed people and their families lose income.
  • Government spending on benefits rises (an opportunity cost: less for schools and hospitals).
  • Tax revenue falls — less income tax and less spending (so less VAT).
  • Consumer confidence falls as people fear losing their jobs, so spending falls further.
  • Business confidence falls, so firms invest less.
  • Society — higher crime, poorer health, loss of skills and family stress, especially with long-term unemployment.

Key terms

Unemployment
When people willing and able to work and seeking work cannot find a job.
ILO measure
The internationally agreed survey-based measure of unemployment.
Cyclical unemployment
Unemployment caused by a lack of demand in a downturn or recession.
Structural unemployment
Unemployment caused by the long-term decline of an industry.
Frictional unemployment
Short-term unemployment between jobs.
Labour force
Everyone employed plus everyone unemployed.
Exam-style question

Explain two impacts of rising unemployment on a government’s finances. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: government spending on unemployment benefits rises.
  • 1 mark: development, e.g. this has an opportunity cost: less money for education or health.
  • 1 mark: tax revenue falls, as fewer people pay income tax.
  • 1 mark: development, e.g. lower spending also reduces indirect tax revenue (VAT), which may increase the budget deficit.
