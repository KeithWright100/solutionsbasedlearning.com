9. Supply-side policy

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.1 Government and the Economy · 2.1.2 Government policies · Lesson 9 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand how supply-side policies raise productivity and the productive capacity of the economy.

Syllabus Statement

Definition of supply-side policy; effects on productivity and total output; privatisation, deregulation, education and training, regional policy for areas of high unemployment, infrastructure spending, lower business taxes and lower income tax; impact on the macroeconomic objectives.

Success criterion 1

What are supply-side policies?

Supply-side policies aim to increase the productive capacity of the economy by improving the quantity and quality of factors of production and making markets work more efficiently. They raise productivity and total output, shifting the PPC outwards.

Success criterion 2

Types of supply-side policy

PolicyHow it increases output
PrivatisationSelling state-owned firms to the private sector; the profit motive encourages efficiency.
DeregulationRemoving unnecessary rules and barriers to entry, increasing competition (e.g. opening airline routes to budget carriers).
Education and trainingImproves human capital and labour productivity; reduces structural unemployment.
Regional policyGrants, tax breaks or infrastructure to attract firms to areas of high unemployment.
Infrastructure spendingBetter roads, ports, railways and broadband cut firms’ costs and attract investment.
Lower business taxesLower corporation tax leaves firms with more profit to invest and attracts foreign firms.
Lower income taxIncreases the reward from working, encouraging people to work, work longer or seek promotion.
Success criterion 3

Assessing supply-side policies

  • Economic growth — higher productive capacity allows sustained, long-term growth.
  • Inflation — more output and lower costs reduce inflationary pressure, so growth can happen without prices rising.
  • Unemployment — training and regional policy reduce structural unemployment.
  • Current account — more productive firms become more competitive internationally, raising exports.

Limitations: they often take many years to work (e.g. education); they can be expensive; tax cuts may mainly benefit high earners and increase inequality; privatisation and deregulation can lead to job losses or lower safety standards.

Activity: which supply-side policy?

Match each action to the supply-side policy it represents.

  • Free coding courses for unemployed adults
  • Building a new deep-sea port
  • Cutting corporation tax from 25% to 20%
  • Allowing new firms to run bus routes
  • Rolling out 5G broadband to rural areas
  • Expanding apprenticeships in engineering

Key terms

Supply-side policy
Policies that increase the productive capacity of the economy.
Deregulation
Removing rules and barriers to increase competition.
Regional policy
Policies to attract firms and jobs to areas of high unemployment.
Infrastructure
Basic facilities such as roads, ports, railways and telecommunications.
Productive capacity
The maximum output an economy can produce.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether spending on education and training is the best policy to reduce unemployment. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks: explain how training reduces structural unemployment and raises productivity.
  • Up to 2 marks: limitations, e.g. slow to take effect; costly; will not solve cyclical unemployment caused by low demand.
  • Up to 2 marks: reasoned judgement, e.g. best for structural unemployment, but fiscal or monetary policy is needed for cyclical unemployment.
