1. Economic growth and the economic cycle

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 2.1 Government and the Economy · 2.1.1 Macroeconomic objectives · Lesson 1 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand how economic growth is measured, the stages of the economic cycle and the effects of growth.

Syllabus Statement

Definition of economic growth; GDP as a measure and its limitations; the economic cycle (boom, downturn, recession, recovery) and its effects on growth, inflation and unemployment; the impact of economic growth on employment, living standards, poverty, productive potential, inflation and the environment.

Success criterion 1

Measuring economic growth

Economic growth is an increase in the output of goods and services in an economy over time. It is measured by the percentage change in real gross domestic product (GDP).

GDP is the total value of all goods and services produced in a country in a year. Real GDP has been adjusted for inflation, so it shows changes in actual output rather than just higher prices.

Growth rate (%) = (GDP this year − GDP last year) ÷ GDP last year × 100

Example: real GDP rises from $500bn to $515bn → growth = 15 ÷ 500 × 100 = 3%.

Limitations of GDP:

  • It ignores the informal economy (unrecorded cash work) and unpaid work such as caring.
  • It says nothing about how income is distributed.
  • It ignores quality of life: leisure time, health, pollution and stress.
  • It is not adjusted for population size unless GDP per capita is used.
  • Comparing countries is hard because of different currencies and prices.
Success criterion 2

The economic cycle

Economies do not grow smoothly. Real GDP fluctuates around a long-term trend in the economic (business) cycle.

StageEconomic growthInflationUnemployment
BoomFastRising (demand-pull pressure)Low
DownturnSlowingStill high, starting to easeStarting to rise
RecessionNegative (two quarters of falling real GDP)Low or fallingHigh
RecoveryPicking upStarting to riseFalling
Success criterion 3

The impact of economic growth

  • Employment — more output needs more workers, so unemployment falls.
  • Standards of living — higher incomes allow people to consume more goods and services.
  • Poverty — jobs and higher tax revenue (for public services and benefits) can reduce poverty.
  • Productive potential — growth led by investment increases the economy’s capacity (an outward shift of the PPC).
  • Inflation — if demand grows faster than capacity, prices rise.
  • The environment — more production can mean more pollution, congestion and depletion of resources.

Activity: which stage of the cycle?

Decide which stage of the economic cycle each description fits best.

  • Firms struggle to find workers and prices rise quickly
  • Real GDP has fallen for two quarters in a row
  • Unemployment starts to fall after a slump
  • Growth slows and some firms delay investment
  • Consumer confidence is at a record high
  • Many businesses close and job losses are widespread

Key terms

Economic growth
An increase in the output of goods and services over time, measured by real GDP.
GDP
Gross domestic product: the total value of goods and services produced in a country in a year.
Real GDP
GDP adjusted for inflation.
Recession
Two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP.
Economic cycle
The pattern of boom, downturn, recession and recovery in an economy.
Exam-style question

Discuss whether economic growth is always beneficial for a country. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Up to 2 marks: benefits, e.g. higher employment, rising living standards, more tax revenue for public services.
  • Up to 2 marks: costs, e.g. environmental damage, inflation, unequal distribution of gains.
  • Up to 2 marks: reasoned judgement, e.g. sustainable growth with protection of the environment is beneficial; rapid growth that causes inflation and pollution may not be.
