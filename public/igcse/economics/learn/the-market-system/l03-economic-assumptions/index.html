3. Economic assumptions

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.1 The Market System · 1.1.2 Economic assumptions · Lesson 3 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand the assumptions economists make about consumers and producers, and why real behaviour may differ.

Syllabus Statement

The assumption that consumers aim to maximise their benefit and businesses aim to maximise profit, and reasons why consumers and producers may not do so.

Success criterion 1

The traditional assumptions

Economic models start from simple assumptions about how people behave:

  • Consumers aim to maximise their benefit (sometimes called utility or satisfaction) from the money they spend. They compare the benefit of each purchase with its price.
  • Businesses aim to maximise profit — the difference between total revenue and total costs.

These assumptions make it possible to predict, for example, that consumers buy more of a good when its price falls and that firms produce more when prices rise.

Success criterion 2

Why consumers may not maximise benefit

Real people do not always behave ‘rationally’:

ReasonExplanationExample
Inaccurate calculationConsumers may not have the time, information or skill to compare every option, so they misjudge the benefits.Buying an expensive phone contract without comparing other deals
HabitPeople keep buying the same products out of routine rather than thinking about whether there is a better choice.Always buying the same brand of cereal
Copying others (herd behaviour)Consumers follow friends, influencers or trends rather than their own needs.Buying a product because it is popular on social media
Success criterion 3

Why producers may not maximise profit

Some businesses have other aims as well as, or instead of, profit:

  • Maximising revenue or sales — managers may be rewarded for growth or market share rather than profit, and a bigger business can give them more status.
  • Caring for customers — good service and fair prices build loyalty and a strong reputation, even if they cost money in the short run.
  • Charitable work and social responsibility — firms may support local communities, donate to charity or protect the environment.
  • Survival — a new business or one in a recession may simply aim to break even.

Activity: profit-maximising or another aim?

Decide whether each action is mainly about maximising profit.

  • Cutting costs by switching to a cheaper supplier
  • Selling at a low price to win the largest market share
  • Donating a share of profits to a local hospital
  • Raising prices where customers have few alternatives
  • Offering free repairs to keep customers happy

Key terms

Rational behaviour
Making choices that give the greatest benefit for the cost.
Benefit (utility)
The satisfaction a consumer gains from consuming a good or service.
Profit
Total revenue minus total costs.
Revenue maximisation
Aiming for the highest possible sales revenue rather than profit.
Herd behaviour
Copying the choices of other people.
Exam-style question

Explain one reason why a business may not aim to maximise profit. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a valid reason, e.g. managers aim to maximise sales or revenue.
  • 1 mark for explanation, e.g. managers’ pay or status may be linked to the size of the business.
  • 1 mark for development, e.g. lower prices increase sales and market share but reduce profit per unit.
