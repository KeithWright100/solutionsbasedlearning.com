2. Production possibility curves

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.1 The Market System · 1.1.1 The economic problem · Lesson 2 of 10

Learning Intention

To use a production possibility curve to show scarcity, choice, opportunity cost and economic growth.

Syllabus Statement

Production possibility curves (PPC): maximum productive potential, fully employed and unemployed resources, opportunity cost, possible and unobtainable production, and positive and negative economic growth shown by shifts of the PPC, with their causes.

Success criterion 1

What a PPC shows

A production possibility curve (PPC), also called a production possibility frontier, shows the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce when it uses all of its resources fully and efficiently.

  • On the curve (A) — all resources are fully employed; this is the economy’s maximum productive potential.
  • Inside the curve (B) — possible, but some resources are unemployed or used inefficiently (e.g. during a recession).
  • Outside the curve (C) — unobtainable with current resources and technology.
Success criterion 2

Opportunity cost on a PPC

Because the economy is already using all its resources on the curve, producing more of one good means producing less of the other. The amount given up is the opportunity cost.

The PPC is usually drawn bowed outwards (concave) because resources are not equally suited to producing both goods: as more of one good is made, ever larger amounts of the other must be given up.

Success criterion 3

Economic growth and the PPC

An increase in the quantity or quality of the factors of production shifts the whole curve.

Causes of positive growth (outward shift)Causes of negative growth (inward shift)
New technology and innovationNatural disasters destroying land and capital
More investment in capital (machinery, infrastructure)War or conflict
Better education and training (higher-quality labour)A fall in the working population, e.g. emigration
Discovery of new natural resourcesDepletion of natural resources
Immigration increasing the labour forceDisease or a pandemic reducing the workforce

Exam tip: a movement from a point inside the curve to a point on the curve is a recovery in output using unemployed resources — it is not a shift of the PPC.

Key terms

Production possibility curve
A curve showing the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with its resources fully employed.
Productive potential
The maximum output an economy can produce.
Unemployed resources
Factors of production that are not being used, shown by a point inside the PPC.
Positive economic growth
An increase in productive potential, shown by an outward shift of the PPC.
Negative economic growth
A fall in productive potential, shown by an inward shift of the PPC.
Exam-style question

With the help of a production possibility curve, explain how an increase in investment in new technology affects an economy. (4 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a correctly labelled PPC (axes for two goods).
  • 1 mark for showing an outward shift from PPC1 to PPC2.
  • 1 mark for explaining that technology increases the quality/productivity of factors of production.
  • 1 mark for linking this to positive economic growth: more of both goods can now be produced.

Model answer: New technology increases the productivity of capital and labour, so the economy can produce more with the same resources. This increases its productive potential, shifting the PPC outwards from PPC1 to PPC2. Combinations that were previously unobtainable can now be reached, which shows positive economic growth.
