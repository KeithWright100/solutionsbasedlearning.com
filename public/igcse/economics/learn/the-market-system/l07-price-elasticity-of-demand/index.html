7. Price elasticity of demand

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.1 The Market System · 1.1.4 Elasticity · Lesson 7 of 10

Learning Intention

To calculate and interpret price elasticity of demand and explain its link with total revenue.

Syllabus Statement

Price elasticity of demand (PED): definition, formula and calculation; interpretation of values (perfectly inelastic, inelastic, unitary, elastic, perfectly elastic); factors influencing PED; the relationship between PED and total revenue.

Success criterion 1

Calculating PED

Price elasticity of demand (PED) measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price.

PED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price

% change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100

Worked example: the price of a cinema ticket rises from $10 to $12 (+20%). Tickets sold fall from 1,000 to 900 (−10%). PED = −10 ÷ 20 = −0.5.

PED is almost always negative because price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions. Economists usually look at the size of the number, ignoring the sign.

Success criterion 2

Interpreting PED values

PED (ignoring sign)DescriptionMeaning
0Perfectly inelasticQuantity demanded does not change at all when price changes (vertical curve).
Between 0 and 1Price inelasticQuantity changes by a smaller % than price, e.g. petrol, cigarettes, salt.
1Unitary elasticityQuantity changes by the same % as price.
Greater than 1Price elasticQuantity changes by a larger % than price, e.g. a particular brand of crisps.
Infinity (∞)Perfectly elasticAny price rise causes demand to fall to zero (horizontal curve).
Success criterion 3

Factors that influence PED

  • Availability of substitutes — more close substitutes → more elastic (one brand of cola is elastic; water in general is inelastic).
  • Degree of necessity — necessities and addictive goods are inelastic; luxuries are more elastic.
  • Percentage of income spent — goods taking a small share of income (e.g. matches) are inelastic; expensive items (e.g. cars) are more elastic.
  • Time — demand becomes more elastic over time as consumers find alternatives.
Success criterion 4

PED and total revenue

Total revenue (TR) = price × quantity sold. Firms use PED to decide pricing.

If demand is…Price rise → TRPrice cut → TR
Inelastic (PED < 1)RisesFalls
Elastic (PED > 1)FallsRises
Unitary (PED = 1)No changeNo change

Example: price rises from $4 to $5 and sales fall from 100 to 90. TR rises from $400 to $450, so demand is price inelastic.

Key terms

Price elasticity of demand
The responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price.
Price inelastic
When the % change in quantity demanded is smaller than the % change in price (PED between 0 and 1).
Price elastic
When the % change in quantity demanded is greater than the % change in price (PED greater than 1).
Unitary elasticity
When the % change in quantity demanded equals the % change in price (PED = 1).
Total revenue
Price multiplied by quantity sold.
Exam-style question

The price of a magazine rose from $5 to $6 and sales fell from 2,000 to 1,500 copies a week. Calculate the PED for the magazine. Show your working. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: % change in quantity = (1,500 − 2,000) ÷ 2,000 × 100 = −25%.
  • 1 mark: % change in price = (6 − 5) ÷ 5 × 100 = +20%.
  • 1 mark: PED = −25 ÷ 20 = −1.25 (price elastic).

Model answer: PED = −25% ÷ 20% = −1.25. Because the value is greater than 1 (ignoring the sign), demand for the magazine is price elastic.
