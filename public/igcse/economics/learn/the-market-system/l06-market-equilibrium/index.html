6. Market equilibrium

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.1 The Market System · 1.1.3 Demand, supply and equilibrium · Lesson 6 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand how demand and supply interact to set the equilibrium price and quantity, and how this changes.

Syllabus Statement

Market equilibrium price and quantity; how shifts in demand and supply affect equilibrium; excess demand and excess supply and how market forces remove them.

Success criteria

Success criterion 1

Equilibrium

Market equilibrium is where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied. At this price there is no pressure for the price to change.

Success criterion 2

Excess supply and excess demand

If the price is not at equilibrium, market forces (the price mechanism) push it back.

  • Excess supply (surplus): at P1, firms cannot sell all their stock. They cut prices; demand extends and supply contracts until equilibrium is restored.
  • Excess demand (shortage): at P2, consumers queue or cannot buy. Firms raise prices; demand contracts and supply extends until equilibrium is restored.
Success criterion 3

Changes in equilibrium

When demand or supply shifts, a new equilibrium is reached.

ChangeEquilibrium priceEquilibrium quantity
Demand increases (right)RisesRises
Demand decreases (left)FallsFalls
Supply increases (right)FallsRises
Supply decreases (left)RisesFalls

Exam method: (1) decide which curve shifts and why; (2) decide the direction; (3) draw the new curve; (4) read off the new price and quantity; (5) explain the process (e.g. excess demand at the old price pushes the price up).

Activity: what happens to price?

For each event in the market for coffee, decide what happens to the equilibrium price.

  • Frost destroys a third of Brazil’s coffee crop
  • A new machine halves the cost of harvesting coffee
  • Coffee shops become fashionable with students
  • The price of tea falls sharply
  • A new tax is placed on coffee imports
  • A report claims coffee damages health

Key terms

Market equilibrium
Where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
Equilibrium price
The price at which demand equals supply; the market-clearing price.
Excess demand
When quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied at a given price (a shortage).
Excess supply
When quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded at a given price (a surplus).
Price mechanism
The way changes in price respond to demand and supply to allocate resources.
Exam-style question

Using a demand and supply diagram, explain the likely effect of a hurricane that destroys banana plantations on the market for bananas. (6 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark: identify that the hurricane reduces the supply of bananas.
  • 1 mark: explain that at the original price there is now excess demand.
  • 1 mark: explain that price rises, demand contracts until the new equilibrium.
  • 1 mark: correctly labelled axes (Price, Quantity) with original D and S and equilibrium.
  • 1 mark: supply shifted left (S to S1).
  • 1 mark: new equilibrium showing higher price (P1) and lower quantity (Q1).
