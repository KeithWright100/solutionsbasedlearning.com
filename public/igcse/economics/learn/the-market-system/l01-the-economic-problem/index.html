1. The economic problem and opportunity cost

Edexcel IGCSE Economics · 1.1 The Market System · 1.1.1 The economic problem · Lesson 1 of 10

Learning Intention

To understand why scarcity forces every economic agent to make choices.

Syllabus Statement

The economic problem: limited resources and unlimited wants, creating the need to make choices; opportunity cost and how it affects consumers, producers and government; the four factors of production.

Success criterion 1

Scarcity: the basic economic problem

Human wants are unlimited: as soon as one want is satisfied, another appears. But the resources available to satisfy those wants are finite (scarce). This is the economic problem.

Because resources are scarce, every individual, business and government has to make choices: what to produce, how to produce it and who gets it. Economics is the study of how these choices are made.

  • Needs are things we must have to survive, such as food, water, shelter and clothing.
  • Wants are things we would like to have, such as a new phone, a holiday or designer trainers.

Even the richest countries face scarcity: a government with a huge budget still cannot build every hospital, school and road that people would like.

Success criterion 2

Opportunity cost

Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative given up when a choice is made. It is not the money spent — it is what you could have had instead.

Economic agentChoice madeOpportunity cost
ConsumerA student has $40 and buys a concert ticket instead of a pair of trainers.The trainers
Producer (business)A farmer uses a field to grow rice instead of sugar cane.The sugar cane that could have been grown
GovernmentA government spends $50 million on a new motorway instead of a new hospital.The hospital (and the health care it would have provided)
WorkerA school leaver goes to university instead of taking a full-time job.Three years of wages

Opportunity cost helps decision-makers compare options. A rational consumer, producer or government should choose the option whose benefits are greatest compared with what has to be given up.

Activity: who faces the opportunity cost?

Decide which economic agent faces each choice.

  • Choosing between a cinema trip and saving for a new bike
  • A factory deciding whether to make more cars or more vans
  • Spending tax revenue on defence rather than on education
  • A bakery using its budget on a new oven instead of extra staff
  • A family choosing a cheaper flat so they can afford a car
  • Cutting a tax instead of building new railway lines

Success criterion 3

The factors of production

Goods and services are produced using four factors of production. Each earns a reward.

FactorWhat it isReward
LandAll natural resources: soil, minerals, oil, water, fish, forestsRent
LabourThe human effort, physical and mental, used in productionWages
CapitalMan-made goods used to make other goods, e.g. machinery, tools, factories, computersInterest
EnterpriseThe entrepreneur who organises the other factors and takes the riskProfit

An increase in the quantity or quality of factors of production (for example more workers, better-trained workers or new technology) increases what an economy can produce.

Key terms

Scarcity
When there are not enough resources to satisfy everyone’s wants.
Economic problem
Unlimited wants but finite resources, so choices must be made.
Opportunity cost
The value of the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
Factors of production
The resources used to make goods and services: land, labour, capital and enterprise.
Entrepreneur
A person who organises the factors of production and takes the risk of running a business.
Exam-style question

Explain how opportunity cost affects a government when it decides how to spend its budget. (3 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • 1 mark for a correct definition or identification: opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up.
  • 1 mark for applying it to government: e.g. spending on a new hospital means a school cannot be built.
  • 1 mark for development: e.g. the government must compare the benefits of each option, so some services go without or taxes/borrowing must rise.

Model answer: Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up. Because a government has a limited budget, choosing to spend $50 million on a new motorway means it cannot spend that money on a new hospital. The government must therefore weigh up which option gives greater benefit to society, and the hospital care that is lost is the opportunity cost of the motorway.
