2. Economic integration

IB Economics Revision Guide · Unit 4: The global economy · Topics · Topic 2 of 5

Success criterion 1

Preferential trade agreements

  • Bilateral: between two countries (e.g. the UK–Australia free trade agreement, 2023).
  • Regional: between countries in a region (e.g. the CPTPP; the African Continental Free Trade Area).
  • Multilateral: among many countries, through the World Trade Organization. WTO members must apply the most-favoured nation principle (no discrimination between trading partners), with exceptions for trading blocs and developing countries.

Exam tip: Rules of origin are needed in a free trade area so that goods cannot be imported through the member with the lowest tariff and then moved tariff-free to other members.

Real-world example: In the euro crisis (2010–12) Greece, Spain and others could not devalue or cut interest rates to deal with recession and high debt, and had to rely on austerity and support from the EU and IMF.

Success criterion 5

The World Trade Organization

The WTO (founded 1995, about 166 members) aims to promote free trade by reducing trade barriers. Its functions: providing a forum for trade negotiations (rounds such as the Doha Round), settling trade disputes between members, and monitoring members’ trade policies.

Its influence is limited by the difficulty of reaching agreement when all members must agree (especially on agriculture and services), the unequal bargaining power of rich and poor members, the growth of regional agreements, and the paralysis of its Appellate Body since 2019 because the USA blocked new appointments.

Key terms

Trading bloc
A group of countries that agree to reduce or remove trade barriers between them.
Free trade area
A bloc in which members remove trade barriers between themselves but keep their own barriers against others.
Customs union
A free trade area with a common external tariff.
Common market
A customs union with free movement of labour and capital.
Monetary union
A group of countries sharing a single currency and central bank.
Trade creation
HL: the replacement of higher-cost domestic production by lower-cost imports from a bloc member.
Trade diversion
HL: the replacement of lower-cost imports from outside a bloc by higher-cost imports from a member.
World Trade Organization (WTO)
An international organisation that promotes free trade and settles trade disputes.
Exam-style question

Evaluate the view that joining a customs union always benefits a country. (15 marks)

Show mark guidance
  • Level 5 (13–15): relevant terms defined; correct, fully explained diagram(s); real-world examples used effectively; a synthesis or evaluation that is balanced and supported by appropriate reasoning (for example short run v long run, stakeholders, priorities, pros and cons).
  • Level 4 (10–12): mostly accurate theory and diagram(s), real-world examples, and an evaluation that is not fully balanced.
  • Level 3 (7–9): some theory and a diagram, a limited real-world example and evaluation that is mostly asserted.
  • Levels 1–2 (1–6): little relevant theory, no or incorrect diagram and no real evaluation.
  • Define customs union and common external tariff.
  • Benefits: larger market, economies of scale, trade creation (HL), bargaining power. Costs: trade diversion (HL), loss of sovereignty, competition for weaker industries.
  • Evaluate with a real example (e.g. the EU customs union, Brexit, Mercosur).

Model answer: A customs union removes tariffs between members and sets a common external tariff. Firms gain access to a larger market and economies of scale, and consumers can buy from the lowest-cost producer in the union (trade creation). The union also negotiates trade deals as a bloc, with more bargaining power. However, the common external tariff may cause trade diversion if cheaper producers outside the union are replaced; the member loses control of its trade policy; and less competitive industries may shrink. Whether a country benefits depends on how much it trades with other members and how high the external tariff is.

  • May 2022 · P1 HL · Q3(b)Discuss the pros and cons for a country of joining a trading bloc, using real examples.[15]

    How to answer

    • Use common market features: free movement of goods, services, labour and capital.
    • Weigh market access, FDI and competition against job losses in uncompetitive sectors, labour emigration and loss of policy freedom.
